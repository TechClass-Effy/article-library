# **PASSAGE B — JOHN 20:28**

## **John 20:28 — Geneva 1599:**

*"Then Thomas answered, and said unto him, Thou art my Lord, and my God."*

---

## **Context (John 20:24-29)**

Thomas had been absent when the risen Christ appeared to the disciples. When told, he said: *"Except I see in his hands the print of the nails... I will not believe."* (v. 25).

Eight days later, Jesus appears again with Thomas present. He offers His hands and side for Thomas to touch, and says: *"be not faithless, but believing."* (v. 27).

Thomas responds: **Ὁ Κύριός μου καὶ ὁ Θεός μου** — *"My Lord and my God."*

---

## **Careful Examination**

### **The Grammatical Form**

**ὁ Κύριός μου καὶ ὁ Θεός μου** — both nouns have the article and the possessive *"my."*

**This is direct address to Jesus.** Thomas is not making a general theological statement; he is addressing the risen Christ standing before him.

### **The Content of the Claim**

Thomas calls Jesus:
- **ὁ Κύριός μου** — *"my Lord."*
- **ὁ Θεός μου** — *"my God."*

**"My Lord"** is the same title used throughout the NT — Ps 110:1 language, the risen and enthroned Lord.

**"My God"** is more striking. What does Thomas mean by this?

---

## **Under Our Framework**

### **The Name-Authority Reading**

Under your two insights, Thomas's confession resolves beautifully:

**Thomas is confessing:**
1. **"My Lord"** — Acknowledging Jesus as the risen enthroned Lord (Ps 110:1) — the One raised and given all authority by the Father.
2. **"My God"** — Acknowledging that in the risen Jesus, the Father's Name and fullness are manifest. As the Only-Begotten who bears the Father's Name and in whom the fullness of Godhead dwells bodily (Col 2:9), Jesus can be addressed with the divine designation.

**Thomas is NOT confessing:**
- That Jesus is identical Person with the Father.
- That Jesus is the sole God to the exclusion of the Father.

**Thomas is confessing that the risen Christ is the visible manifestation of the Father** — the One in whom the Father's Name dwells, the Image of the invisible God, the fullness of Godhead bodily — and therefore addressable with divine titles.

### **The Immediate Context Confirms This**

Just prior in John 20:17, Jesus said to Mary Magdalene:

### **John 20:17 — Geneva 1599:**

*"...go to my brethren, and say unto them, I ascend unto my Father, and to your Father, and to my God, and your God."*

---

### Observation of decisive importance

**Just eleven verses before Thomas's confession, the risen Jesus explicitly identifies the Father as HIS OWN GOD.**

*"My God and your God."*

**Jesus has a God. That God is the Father.** This is spoken **by the risen Christ** — after the resurrection, in His glorified state.

**Whatever Thomas means by "my God" in v. 28, it cannot contradict what Jesus said in v. 17.**

**Under our maxims, the honest reading is:**

**Thomas addresses the risen Christ with the highest reverence — as "my Lord and my God" — recognizing that in Him the Father's Name and fullness dwell bodily. But Jesus Himself has just declared that the Father is His God.**

**Two distinct realities are affirmed:**
1. **The Father is Jesus' God** (v. 17) — the eternal supremacy of the Father.
2. **Jesus can be addressed as "my Lord and my God"** (v. 28) — because He bears the Father's Name and manifests His fullness.

**These are not contradictions.** Under your Name-authority framework, they are perfectly complementary:

- Jesus, as the Only-Begotten begotten in Spirit, has the Father as His own God.
- Jesus, bearing the Father's Name and manifesting the Father's fullness bodily, can be addressed with divine titles by those who recognize Him.

**The Father remains the One God supreme.** The Son bears the Father's Name and shares His Spirit-nature. Thomas's confession acknowledges this — without collapsing Persons or displacing the Father.

---

### Jesus' Response Confirms

### **John 20:29 — Geneva 1599:**

*"...Thomas, because thou hast seen me, thou believest: blessed are they that have not seen, and have believed."*

---

### Observation

**Jesus does not correct Thomas.** He accepts the confession. But He also does not turn it into a metaphysical statement of tri-personal deity. He simply notes: *"Because you have seen Me, you have believed."*

Thomas's confession is accepted **as an act of faith in the risen Christ** — the One in whom the Father's Name and fullness dwell.

**This is the highest confession of faith, and it fits perfectly under our framework.**
