## **Completing the Lord's Prayer Pattern**

The pattern Jesus gave provides the believer with **six categories** of prayer:

| Category | Petition | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Adoration** | *"Our Father... hallowed be Thy Name"* | Worshipping the Father and reverencing His Name |
| **Kingdom** | *"Thy kingdom come"* | Submitting to and praying for His reign |
| **Surrender** | *"Thy will be done"* | Yielding our will to His |
| **Provision** | *"Give us this day our daily bread"* | Asking for daily needs |
| **Confession/Forgiveness** | *"Forgive us our debts... as we also forgive"* | Maintenance of fellowship through confession |
| **Deliverance** | *"Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil"* | Asking for protection from sin and the evil one |

**This is the architecture of healthy prayer.** Notice the priority: **God's glory and kingdom first** (three petitions), **our needs second** (three petitions). The believer is taught to think God-first, kingdom-first, and only then bring his own needs.

## **Praying in the Spirit**

We examined under Romans 8 that the Spirit intercedes for us in our weakness. But the believer is also commanded to **pray in the Spirit:**

### **Ephesians 6:18 — Geneva 1599:**

***"And pray always with all manner prayer and supplication in the Spirit: and watch thereunto with all perseverance and supplication for all Saints,"***

### **Jude 20-21 — Geneva 1599:**

***"But, ye beloved, edify yourselves in your most holy faith, praying in the holy Ghost, And keep yourselves in the love of God..."***

---

### Observations

**To pray in the Spirit** means:
- Praying in the **Spirit's empowerment** rather than in the flesh's eloquence.
- Praying in **alignment with the Spirit's leading** rather than according to our own desires alone.
- Praying with **the Spirit interceding through us** — sometimes with words, sometimes with groanings beyond words (Rom 8:26).
- Praying with **the Spirit's witness** that we are children, freely calling on the Father (Rom 8:15-16).

This is not a special technique; it is the **normal mode** of Christian prayer. The believer prays in the Spirit because the Spirit indwells him and prays through him.

## **The Modes of Prayer**

The NT presents several distinct modes of prayer the believer is to practice:

### **1. Petition (αἴτησις) — Asking for Specific Needs**

### **Matthew 7:7-11 — Geneva 1599:**

***"Ask, and it shall be given you: seek, and ye shall find: knock, and it shall be opened unto you..."***

The Father invites our requests. **Three present imperatives** — *ask, seek, knock* — all in continuous tense. We are to keep asking, keep seeking, keep knocking. The Father delights to give good gifts to His children.

### **2. Thanksgiving (εὐχαριστία)**

### **1 Thessalonians 5:18 — Geneva 1599:**

***"In all things give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus toward you."***

In **all things** — not for all things in the sense of celebrating evil, but **in the midst of all things** — give thanks. Thanksgiving is the will of God for the believer.

### **3. Intercession (ἔντευξις) — Praying for Others**

### **1 Timothy 2:1-2 — Geneva 1599:**

***"I exhort therefore, that first of all, supplications, prayers, intercessions, and giving of thanks be made for all men: For Kings, and for all that are in authority..."***

The believer is to intercede for **all men** — for the lost, for fellow believers, for rulers and authorities, for enemies, for the suffering. **Intercession is one of the highest privileges of the Christian life.**

### **4. Confession (ὁμολογία)**

We will examine this fully in its own section, but it is a major mode of prayer — agreeing with God about our sin.

### **5. Adoration / Worship**

The lifting up of the heart in praise to the Father — which we will examine more fully in the worship section.

### **6. Lament**

The honest expression to the Father of grief, distress, perplexity, even questioning — modeled in the Psalms and by the Son in Gethsemane and on the cross. The believer is not required to pretend to feelings he does not have; he is invited to bring his real state before the Father.

## **The Posture of Prayer**

### **Persistence**

### **Luke 18:1 — Geneva 1599:**

***"And he spake also a parable unto them, to this end, that they ought always to pray, and not to wax faint,"***

The parable of the persistent widow teaches that the believer is to pray **always** and **not faint.** Some answers require persistent asking over time. The Father is not slow; He is wise — and He uses delayed answers to deepen our dependence.

### **Faith**

### **Mark 11:24 — Geneva 1599:**

***"...Whatsoever ye desire when ye pray, believe that ye shall have it, and it shall be done unto you."***

Prayer is to be offered in faith — trusting the Father's character and promises. Not faith in our prayers, but faith **in the One to whom we pray.**

### **According to His Will**

### **1 John 5:14-15 — Geneva 1599:**

***"And this is that assurance, that we have in him, that if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us..."***

Prayer is to be **according to His will.** Not every request is granted — but every request offered in submission to His will is heard. The believer prays *"Thy will be done"* — confident that the Father's will is best, even when His will differs from our preferences.

### **In Jesus' Name**

### **John 14:13-14 — Geneva 1599:**

***"And whatsoever ye ask in my Name, that will I do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son. If ye shall ask any thing in my Name, I will do it."***

Prayer is offered **in Jesus' Name** — that is, on the basis of His person, His mediation, and His authority. Not as a magical formula appended to prayers, but as **the recognition that all access to the Father is through the Son.**

### **With Thanksgiving**

### **Philippians 4:6-7 — Geneva 1599:**

***"Be nothing careful, but in all things let your requests be showed unto God in prayer, and supplication with giving of thanks. And the peace of God which passeth all understanding, shall preserve your hearts..."***

Prayer with thanksgiving is the antidote to anxiety. The promise: **peace that surpasses understanding will guard the heart.**

## **Practical Implications for the Modern Believer**

**1. Establish a regular time of prayer** — daily, ideally in the morning. Not as legalistic duty, but as joyful communion. The believer who waits to "feel like" praying will rarely pray.

**2. Use the Lord's Prayer pattern** — let Jesus' template shape the architecture of your daily prayer. Adoration → Kingdom → Surrender → Provision → Confession → Deliverance.

**3. Pray Scripture back to the Father** — pray through the Psalms, pray through Paul's prayers (Eph 1:15-23; 3:14-21; Phil 1:9-11; Col 1:9-12). Let the Word shape your prayers.

**4. Pray with persistence** — do not abandon prayer when answers delay. The Father uses delay to deepen us.

**5. Pray for others** — keep a list of those for whom you intercede: family, brethren, enemies, rulers, missionaries, the lost.

**6. Pray throughout the day** — *"pray without ceasing"* (1 Thess 5:17) means maintaining a continuous attitude of God-consciousness, with frequent brief turning of the heart to Him.

**7. Pray with thanksgiving** — even in trial, give thanks for what you have received, for who He is, for the hope set before you.

**8. Pray honestly** — bring your real state. The Father does not need pious phrases; He desires the honest heart.
