# **PART 7 — CONFESSION OF SIN**

The believer is not sinless. Failure occurs. The discipline of confession is the means by which fellowship with the Father is maintained.

## **The Foundation: 1 John 1:9**

### **1 John 1:9 — Geneva 1599:**

*"If we acknowledge our sins, he is faithful and just, to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness."*

---

### Observations

**(1)** **ὁμολογῶμεν** — *"confess"* — literally *"to say the same thing"* (ὁμό + λέγω). To confess sin is to **agree with God about it** — naming it for what He calls it, not minimizing or excusing.

**(2)** The promise is twofold:
- **ἀφῇ** — *forgive* — the legal release from the debt of sin.
- **καθαρίσῃ** — *cleanse* — the removal of the stain from the conscience and life.

**(3) The basis** — *"He is faithful and just."* Forgiveness is not because God is sentimental or lenient, but because **He is faithful to His promise** and **just toward the propitiation Christ made.**

---

## **The Distinction: Position vs. Fellowship**

A vital theological distinction must be held:

**Positionally**, the believer's sins — past, present, and future — were dealt with at the cross. He is forgiven, justified, accepted in the Beloved. This is not undone by daily sin.

**Practically (in fellowship)**, daily sin grieves the indwelling Spirit (Eph 4:30) and disrupts the believer's experiential communion with the Father. Confession restores the experiential fellowship.

**1 John 1:9 addresses the practical/fellowship dimension** — the believer's daily walk, his conscience, his communion. The position is secure; the fellowship requires confession.

---

## **The Pattern of Daily Confession**

The believer should make confession a daily, regular practice:

1. **At the close of the day** — review the day before the Lord, confessing known sin.
2. **At the moment of conviction** — when the Spirit convicts of specific sin, confess immediately.
3. **Before the Lord's Supper** — self-examination per 1 Cor 11.
4. **Before significant prayer** — clearing the channels.

---

## **Confession to Other Believers**

### **James 5:16 — Geneva 1599:**

*"Acknowledge your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed: for the prayer of a righteous man availeth much, if it be fervent."*

---

### Observation

Some sins should be confessed to other trusted believers — for prayer, accountability, and healing. This is not the priestly confessional of Rome (per maxim #3), but the genuine confession of brethren to one another for mutual support.

This is particularly helpful for besetting sins, sins of pride that need to be exposed in humility, or sins against specific persons that require their forgiveness.

---

## **Practical Implications**

**1. Confess sin promptly** — do not let unconfessed sin accumulate.

**2. Be specific in confession** — name the sin clearly, not in vague generalities.

**3. Receive forgiveness by faith** — believe the promise of 1 John 1:9. Do not wallow in self-condemnation after God has forgiven.

**4. Make restitution where required** — confession may need to be accompanied by reconciliation with those wronged.

**5. Confess corporate sin** — sometimes the believer confesses on behalf of his nation, family, or community (as Daniel did in Dan 9).

**6. Do not let the enemy use confession against you** — once forgiven, you are forgiven. The accuser will try to bring back what God has remembered no more.
