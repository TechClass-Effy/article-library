## **CATEGORY 3 — SUFFERING IN THE FLESH AS DYING WITH CHRIST DAILY**

A more interior, ongoing form: the daily mortification of the flesh and the bearing about of Christ's death in the body.

---

### **2 Corinthians 4:8-11:**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"We are afflicted on every side, yet are we not in distress: we are in doubt, but yet we despair not. We are persecuted, but not forsaken: cast down, but we perish not. Everywhere we bear about in our body the dying of the Lord Jesus, that the life of Jesus might also be made manifest in our bodies. For we which live, are always delivered unto death for Jesus' sake, that the life also of Jesus might be made manifest in our mortal flesh."*

---

### Observations of profound significance

**(1)** The apostolic ministry is described in **four pairs of paradoxes:** afflicted but not crushed, perplexed but not despairing, persecuted but not forsaken, struck down but not destroyed. The believer experiences the reality of pressure, perplexity, persecution, and being knocked down — yet is sustained.

**(2)** **πάντοτε τὴν νέκρωσιν τοῦ Ἰησοῦ ἐν τῷ σώματι περιφέροντες** — *"always bearing about in the body the dying of Jesus."*

**νέκρωσις** — *"the dying, the state of being put to death."* The believer **always carries about** the dying of Jesus in his body. This is more than physical suffering; it is a continual experiential identification with the Son's crucifixion.

**(3) The purpose:** *"that the life of Jesus might also be manifested in our body."* The carrying-about of Christ's dying is unto the manifestation of Christ's life. **Death precedes life — even in the believer's daily experience.** The pattern of the Son becomes the pattern of His people.

**(4)** **ἀεὶ γὰρ ἡμεῖς οἱ ζῶντες εἰς θάνατον παραδιδόμεθα διὰ Ἰησοῦν** — *"always we who live are delivered unto death for Jesus' sake."* The verb **παραδιδόμεθα** is the same verb used of Jesus being delivered up for our sins (Rom 4:25; 8:32). Paul applies it to the believer: **we are continually being delivered up to death for His sake.**

---

### **Romans 8:36 — A citation of Psalm 43:23 LXX (Ps 44:22):**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"As it is written, For thy sake are we killed all day long: we are counted as sheep for the slaughter."*

---

### Observation

Paul cites Psalm 43:23 LXX (44:22 MT) — the psalmist's lament that God's people are slaughtered all day long for His sake. **Paul applies this to the church.** This is the believer's lot: continually delivered up to death-circumstances for the Lord's sake — though, as Paul will declare in v. 37, **"in all these things we are more than conquerors through Him that loved us."**

---

## **CATEGORY 4 — SUFFERING THROUGH THE GROANINGS OF THE FALLEN CREATION**

A different facet again: the suffering that comes simply from being part of a creation under bondage to corruption — yet borne with hope.

---

### **Romans 8:18-25:**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"For I count that the afflictions of this present time are not worthy of the glory, which shall be showed unto us. For the fervent desire of the creature waiteth when the sons of God shall be revealed... For we know that every creature groaneth with us also, and travaileth in pain together unto this present. And not only the creature, but we also which have the first fruits of the Spirit, even we do sigh in ourselves, waiting for the adoption, even the redemption of our body."*

---

### Observations

**(1)** **τὰ παθήματα τοῦ νῦν καιροῦ** — *"the sufferings of the present time."* Paul speaks of an entire era of suffering — *this present age* until the Son's appearing.

**(2)** **πᾶσα ἡ κτίσις συστενάζει καὶ συνωδίνει** — *"the whole creation groans together and travails in pain together."* The entire creation suffers — under the curse of Genesis 3. The believer is not exempt from the conditions of the cursed creation.

**(3)** **καὶ αὐτοὶ ἐν ἑαυτοῖς στενάζομεν** — *"we ourselves also groan within ourselves."* The believer groans — even possessing the firstfruits of the Spirit — awaiting the full redemption (the resurrection of the body).

**(4)** **The disease, decay, weakness, frailty, and mortality of our bodies are part of this suffering.** We were redeemed; our spirits were sealed; but our bodies still bear the corruption of Adam's race, awaiting the **ἀπολύτρωσις τοῦ σώματος** — *the redemption of the body* (Rom 8:23) — which comes at His appearing.

**(5)** This is a **groaning with hope** — not despair. The sufferings are **οὐκ ἄξια** — *not worthy* — to be compared with the coming glory.

---

## **CATEGORY 5 — SUFFERING THROUGH GENUINE LOVE FOR CHRIST'S BODY**

A pastoral and brotherly form of suffering: the burden borne for the sake of fellow believers.

---

### **2 Corinthians 11:28-29:**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Beside the things which are outward, I am cumbered daily, and have the care of all the Churches. Who is weak, and I am not weak? who is offended, and I burn not?"*

### **Colossians 1:24:**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Now rejoice I in my sufferings for you, and fulfill the rest of the afflictions of Christ in my flesh, for his body's sake, which is the Church,"*

---

### Observations

**(1)** Paul speaks of bearing in his body **the afflictions of Christ for the Body's sake**. He does not mean the atoning sufferings of Christ are incomplete; he means that **Christ's sufferings, which continue in His Body, are filled up in the apostle's flesh for the Body's benefit.** The Body of Christ continues to suffer in this age — and faithful ministers bear suffering on behalf of their brethren.

**(2)** Paul's sufferings include **the daily burden of care for the churches** — the emotional, pastoral, intercessory weight of bearing his brethren's burdens. He weeps with them, sympathizes with them, intercedes for them. **When others stumble, he burns; when others are weak, he is weak with them.**

---

## **CATEGORY 6 — SUFFERING THROUGH SELF-DENIAL AND CROSS-BEARING**

Jesus' own call to discipleship.

---

### **Matthew 16:24-25:**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Jesus then said to his disciples, If any man will follow me, let him forsake himself: and take up his cross, and follow me. For whosoever will save his life, shall lose it: and whosoever shall lose his life for my sake, shall find it."*

### **Luke 9:23:**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"And he said to them all, If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me."*

---

### Observations

**(1)** Three commands: **deny self, take up the cross, follow.** Each one a suffering.

**(2)** **Luke adds καθ᾽ ἡμέραν** — *"daily."* The cross-bearing is not a one-time act but a daily reality. Each day brings its own self-denial, its own cross.

**(3)** **The "cross" was a Roman instrument of execution.** When Jesus said "take up your cross," His hearers heard *"go to the place of dying."* The cross-bearer was a condemned man walking to his execution. Jesus calls every disciple to this posture: **to live as one already given over to death, that he might live unto the Lord.**

**(4)** **The paradox:** saving one's life loses it; losing one's life for Christ's sake finds it. The believer must die to find life. This is a daily reality, not merely an eschatological one.

---

## **CATEGORY 7 — SUFFERING DOWNWARD MOBILITY FOR HIS SAKE**

The willing surrender of worldly status, position, possessions, or recognition for the sake of identification with Christ.

---

### **Hebrews 11:24-26:**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"By faith Moses when he was come to age, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter, And chose rather to suffer adversity with the people of God, than to enjoy the pleasures of sins for a season, Esteeming the rebuke of Christ greater riches than the treasures of Egypt: for he had respect unto the recompense of the reward."*

### **Philippians 3:7-8:**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"But the things that were vantage unto me, the same I counted loss for Christ's sake. Yea, doubtless I think all things but loss for the excellent knowledge sake of Christ Jesus my Lord, for whom I have counted all things loss, and do judge them to be dung, that I might win Christ,"*

---

### Observations

**(1) Moses gave up royalty and the treasures of Egypt** — choosing rather to **συγκακουχεῖσθαι τῷ λαῷ τοῦ Θεοῦ** — *"to suffer affliction together with the people of God."*

**(2) He esteemed τὸν ὀνειδισμὸν τοῦ Χριστοῦ — "the reproach of Christ"** as greater riches than the treasures of Egypt. This is remarkable — Moses, in advance of the incarnation, bore "the reproach of Christ" by his identification with God's people. **The reproach of identification with Christ is a treasure.**

**(3) Paul echoes this:** All his Jewish pedigree, his pharisaical standing, his learning, his accomplishments — **all counted as loss, even as σκύβαλα (refuse, dung)** — for the surpassing worth of knowing Christ.

**(4)** This is a real suffering — the loss of what the world esteems, willingly endured for the sake of Christ.

---

## **CATEGORY 8 — SUFFERING THE CHASTENING OF THE FATHER**

A facet often misunderstood — the Father's loving discipline of His children.

---

### **Hebrews 12:5-11, selections:**


### **1599 Geneva (selections):**
*"...My son, despise not the chastening of the Lord, neither faint when thou art rebuked of him. For whom the Lord loveth, he chasteneth: and he scourgeth every son that he receiveth... If ye endure chastening, God offereth himself unto you as unto sons: for what son is it whom the father chasteneth not? If therefore ye be without correction, whereof all are partakers, then are ye bastards, and not sons... Now no chastising for the present seemeth to be joyous, but, grievous: but afterward, it bringeth the quiet fruit of righteousness, unto them which are thereby exercised."*

---

### Observations

**(1)** The Father chastens those He loves. Chastening is **evidence of sonship**, not exclusion from it.

**(2)** This chastening is sometimes experienced through circumstances — adversity, loss, trial — which the Father uses to correct, train, and conform us to His Son's image.

**(3) Not all such suffering is for specific sin** — sometimes it is for general training in holiness. Even the Son was perfected through what He suffered (Heb 5:8). The believer's path is the same pattern.

---

# **BRINGING THIS HONESTLY INTO THE MODERN BELIEVER'S LIFE**

Now we must ask honestly: **how does this present itself in the life of a modern believer in 2024-2025 in (presumably) the Western world?**

Per our maxims, we must not pretend the modern Western believer faces what believers in many places still face (imprisonment, beating, execution for their faith). But we also must not dismiss the real sufferings he or she does endure as somehow not counting. Let me address this carefully.

---

## **1. Open Persecution in Much of the World — Real and Present**

We must first acknowledge: **in many parts of the world today, believers face Category 1 persecution in the most explicit form.** In North Korea, in parts of China, in Saudi Arabia, in Iran, in Afghanistan, in Nigeria, in Eritrea, in Pakistan, in India, in numerous other places — believers today are:

- **Imprisoned** for their faith.
- **Tortured** for their faith.
- **Killed** for their faith.
- **Separated from families** for their faith.
- **Denied employment, education, civil rights** for their faith.
- **Watching their children sold or trafficked** for their faith.
- **Driven from their homes and villages** for their faith.

The believer's suffering with Christ has **not lessened** in our day; in many ways, statistics suggest that more believers are being killed for their faith in this present age than in any previous generation. **The Western reader must remember the global body of Christ.** We are in fellowship with these brethren — **"remember those in bonds as bound with them"** (Heb 13:3).

---

## **2. Increasing Cultural Hostility in the Western World**

The modern Western believer faces what is perhaps best described as **soft persecution** — but persecution nonetheless. Specifically:

### **Reviling and slander (Matt 5:11):**

- Being mocked or ridiculed in media, education, or public discourse for biblical convictions.
- Being labeled with stigmatizing words (*"bigot," "hateful," "intolerant," "phobic"*) for holding to plain biblical teaching on creation, marriage, gender, sexual morality, the exclusivity of Christ, the authority of Scripture.
- Having one's character, motives, or intelligence impugned because of one's faith.

This is real **ὀνειδισμός** — *reproach* — for the Name. Not on the scale of imprisonment, but real nonetheless, and increasing in intensity.

### **Loss of social, professional, or educational standing:**

- Believers who openly affirm biblical convictions in workplaces, universities, professional bodies, government, military, or media increasingly face:
- Discrimination in hiring and promotion.
- Pressure to participate in or affirm what conscience forbids.
- Loss of jobs, contracts, licenses, or platforms for refusing.
- Exclusion from professional associations.
- Censorship on social media and digital platforms.

This is real **παίδων** — *suffering loss* — and corresponds to what Moses chose: identification with God's people over the treasures and standing of Egypt (Heb 11:24-26).

### **Legal and civic pressures:**

- In some Western jurisdictions, believers face legal action for biblical speech regarded as "hate speech."
- Christian schools, universities, charities, and businesses face mounting legal restrictions for adhering to biblical convictions.
- Parents face challenges over the upbringing of their children in faith.
- Conscience protections are eroding.

### **Family rupture:**

Jesus warned that the Gospel would divide families (Matt 10:34-37). Many modern believers — particularly converts from unbelieving, hostile religious, or ideological backgrounds — experience:

- Estrangement from parents, siblings, or extended family.
- Loss of marriage relationships when an unbelieving spouse rejects the believer's conversion.
- Loss of children who reject the faith and reject the parent.
- Loss of friendships of long standing.

This is a deep, ongoing suffering — and is borne **for His sake**.

---

## **3. The Internal Sufferings of the Faithful Walk (Category 3 — Daily Dying)**

The modern believer who walks faithfully experiences daily what Paul called *"always bearing about in the body the dying of the Lord Jesus"* (2 Cor 4:10). This includes:

### **Mortification of the flesh:**

- The daily putting to death of sinful desires, habits, thought-patterns (Col 3:5; Rom 8:13).
- Saying no to legitimate pleasures when they would compromise fidelity to Christ.
- Choosing the harder right over the easier wrong.

This is **a real suffering** — the flesh resists, the world tempts, and yielding to the Spirit costs.

### **The conflict within (Rom 7, Gal 5):**

- The ongoing tension between the indwelling Spirit and the remaining flesh.
- The grief of besetting sins, repeated failures, and slow growth.
- The earnest desire for holiness, paired with painful awareness of imperfection.

This conflict itself is a form of suffering — the very fact that we groan over our remaining sinfulness is part of our co-suffering with Christ.

### **The cost of standing firm in truth:**

- Refusing to compromise on the Father's truth when it would be socially easier to soften it.
- Speaking the truth in love when silence would be more comfortable.
- Disciplining children, congregations, or oneself when permissiveness would be easier.
- Confronting sin in a brother (Matt 18) when avoidance would be easier.

Each of these is a small daily cross.

---

## **4. The Sufferings of the Fallen Creation (Category 4)**

Every believer endures the sufferings common to all in the fallen creation — but bears them **with hope**:

- **Bodily afflictions:** illness, injury, disability, the weakness of aging, chronic conditions, mental suffering, depression, anxiety.
- **The grief of bereavement:** the death of loved ones, the experience of separation and loss.
- **Economic hardship:** poverty, job loss, financial pressure, debt.
- **Relational pain:** broken relationships, betrayal, loneliness, marital difficulty, the heartbreak of wayward children.
- **Natural disasters, accidents, the violence of others** — all the consequences of a creation under the curse.

The believer is **not exempted** from any of these. What is changed is the **relation** to them: the believer suffers **with hope**, **as a child of the Father**, **in fellowship with the Son who also suffered in a fallen world**.

These are not "punishments." They are the conditions of life in this age — and the Father uses them all for the conformity of His children to the image of His Son (Rom 8:28-29).

---

## **5. The Burden of Brotherly Love (Category 5)**

The faithful believer who genuinely loves the Body of Christ will inevitably suffer:

- **For wayward brethren** — burdened in prayer, grieved over their drift.
- **With suffering brethren** — entering into their pain, weeping with those who weep (Rom 12:15).
- **In intercession** — the long, often hidden labor of prayer for the saints.
- **In service** — pouring out time, resources, energy for the building up of the Body.
- **Through false teachers and divisions** — the heartbreak of watching the Body of Christ fractured by those who would mislead or divide.
- **In the work of the Gospel** — the burden of evangelism, the weight of rejection by those one labors to win.

---

## **6. The Daily Cross-Bearing of Discipleship (Category 6)**

Every believer is called to **daily** self-denial (Luke 9:23). This includes:

- **Denying self-will** in submission to the Father's will.
- **Denying the desire for ease** in choosing the path of obedience.
- **Denying the desire for vindication** in entrusting wrongs to the Father.
- **Denying the desire for approval** in choosing fidelity to Christ over the praise of men.
- **Denying lawful pleasures** when they would crowd out devotion.

The cross is taken up **καθ᾽ ἡμέραν** — *daily*. There is no Christian life without it.

---

# **WHAT IS NOT "SUFFERING WITH CHRIST"**

Per our maxims, we should also note honestly what does **not** qualify as suffering with Christ — lest the modern believer claim credit where Scripture gives none.

### **Peter's distinction (1 Peter 4:15-16):**

**1599 Geneva:** *"But if any man suffer as a Christian, let him not be ashamed... But let none of you suffer as a murderer, or as a thief, or an evil doer, or as a busybody in other men's matters."*

**Suffering brought on by one's own foolishness, sin, lawlessness, or meddling does not qualify.** A believer who suffers consequences for his own actions is not thereby "suffering with Christ" — he is suffering for his own folly.

This includes:
- Suffering caused by our own sin or sinful patterns.
- Suffering caused by our own pride, contentiousness, or unkindness — which we may dress up as "persecution for righteousness' sake" when in fact it is reaction to our own carnality.
- Suffering caused by our own poor judgment, indolence, or irresponsibility.
- Self-imposed suffering (asceticism not commanded by God) — Paul rebukes this in Col 2:20-23.

The modern believer must be honest in self-examination: **is this truly persecution for the Name and the truth, or am I merely facing the consequences of my own actions or attitudes?**

---

# **THE GIFTS THAT ACCOMPANY THE SUFFERING**

Scripture is careful to surround the doctrine of co-suffering with **comfort, promise, and assurance** — not to soften the reality but to sustain the believer.

### **Romans 8:18:**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"For I count that the afflictions of this present time are not worthy of the glory, which shall be showed unto us."*

The sufferings of this present age are **not worthy of comparison** with the coming glory.

### **2 Corinthians 4:17:**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"For our light affliction which is but for a moment, causeth unto us a far most excellent and an eternal weight of glory:"*

The present afflictions are working out **a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory.** The pain is producing the glory.

### **Philippians 1:29:**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"For unto you it is given for Christ, that not only ye should believe in him, but also suffer for his sake,"*

**Both faith in Christ AND suffering for Him are gifts** — **ἐχαρίσθη** — *"have been graciously given"* — from the same hand of grace. The suffering is a divine gift, not a divine punishment.

### **1 Peter 4:12-13:**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Dearly beloved, think it not strange concerning the fiery trial, which is among you to prove you, as though some strange thing were come unto you: But rejoice, inasmuch as ye are partakers of Christ's sufferings, that when his glory shall appear, ye may be glad and rejoice."*

**Do not think it strange.** The fiery trial is normative, not abnormal. **Rejoice** — for you are sharing in His sufferings, and you will share in His glory.

# **GRAND SYNTHESIS — MODERN BELIEVER'S SUFFERINGS WITH CHRIST**

Bringing it together, the modern believer's suffering with Christ takes these honest forms:

| Category | Modern Form |
|---|---|
| **Overt persecution** | Real and present for many globally; emerging in soft form in Western nations |
| **Reviling for the Name** | Mockery, slander, stigmatization, social rejection |
| **Bearing about His dying** | Daily mortification of the flesh, denial of sinful desires |
| **Groanings of the fallen creation** | Illness, loss, bereavement, the consequences of living in a cursed world |
| **Burden for the Body** | Pastoral care, intercession, sympathy with hurting brethren, grief over the church's divisions |
| **Daily cross-bearing** | Self-denial, submission to the Father's will, the cost of obedience |
| **Downward mobility** | Loss of status, career, opportunity, family for the sake of Christ |
| **The Father's chastening** | The discipline that conforms us to the Son's image |

# **A FINAL OBSERVATION — THE THEOLOGICAL HEART OF IT**

Romans 8:17 must be read in the context of Romans 8:18-39 — the most exalted passage on the believer's security in Christ. Paul has just said the conditional *"if we suffer with Him."* Within the next 22 verses he declares:

- The sufferings are not worthy to be compared with the coming glory.
- All things work together for good.
- Those whom the Father foreknew, He predestined, called, justified, and glorified.
- **If God is for us, who can be against us?**
- He who did not spare His own Son will freely give us all things.
- **No one can lay any charge against God's elect.**
- **Christ Himself intercedes for us at the Father's right hand.**
- **Nothing — not tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril, sword, death, life, angels, principalities, powers, things present, things to come, height, depth, nor any other creature — can separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord.**

**This is the framework in which the call to co-suffering is given.** The Father is not asking us to bear sufferings He has not foreseen, not allowed, not measured, not woven into His glorious purpose. **Every suffering we endure with Christ is endured in His unfailing love and unbreakable purpose.**

The Beloved Son suffered for the joy set before Him (Heb 12:2). We suffer with Him for the joy set before us — the coming glory, the unveiled face of the Father, the eternal abode in His house, the inheritance reserved in heaven that cannot fade away.

**Christ has made our sufferings holy.** What He endured, He sanctified. What we endure in fellowship with Him is now caught up into His own purpose. We are not merely sufferers; we are **co-sufferers with the Beloved**, **for the eternal weight of glory** that no eye has seen.
