## **The Suffering Servant — Psalm 21 LXX (22 MT) and Isaiah 52:13—53:12; Their Cross-Witnesses; the Synthesis**

> A.

We now proceed to **Psalm 21 LXX (22 MT) and Isaiah 52:13-53:12** — the two most-cited prophetic posits of the Son's suffering, death, and vindication. These are bound to the Son by **extensive and explicit** NT citation, so we will be on firm ground.
Per our refined modus operandi, I will:
1. Examine each verse of each passage.
2. Cross-reference every explicit NT citation and allusion.
3. Note honestly where the text strictly establishes a claim and where it does not.
4. Let the whole counsel of the God-breathed Word speak.

---

# **PART ONE: PSALM 21 LXX (22 MT)**

## **Psalm 21:1 LXX (22:1 MT) — The Cry of Forsakenness**

### **NETS:**

> God, my God, attend to me; why did you forsake me? Far away from my deliverance are the words of my transgressions. — **Psalm 21:1 LXX (22:1 MT), NETS**
---

### **Cross-witnesses — explicit NT citation:**

> And about the ninth hour Jesus cried with a loud voice, saying, Eli, Eli, lamasabachthani? that is, My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me? — **Matthew 27:46, 1599 Geneva**
> And at the ninth hour Jesus cried with a loud voice, saying, Eloi, Eloi, lamma-sabachthani? which is by interpretation, My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me? — **Mark 15:34, 1599 Geneva**
---

### Observations

**(1)** Jesus' own dying cry from the cross is **a direct quotation of Psalm 21:1 LXX (22:1 MT).** This is the strongest possible witness: **Jesus Himself identifies His suffering with this Psalm.** He does not merely quote a phrase from Scripture; He recites the opening line, claiming the entire Psalm as His own.

**(2)** **Ὁ Θεός μου** — *"My God."* The Christ on the cross addresses the Father as **My God.** This is consistent with John 20:17 — *"I ascend to My Father and your Father, and My God and your God."* The Son has a God; that God is the Father. **Even at the climax of His suffering, the Son's monotheistic confession is preserved.**

**(3)** **ἐγκατέλιπές με** — *"You have forsaken Me."* The Father forsakes the Son in this moment. This is a real abandonment — not a feigned one. The Son experiences true forsakenness while bearing sin.

**(4)** Note the LXX reads **οἱ λόγοι τῶν παραπτωμάτων μου** — *"the words of my transgressions"* — where the Hebrew has *"the words of my roaring."* The LXX makes the "transgressions" language explicit. Yet — the Son had no transgressions of His own (Heb 4:15; 1 Pet 2:22). The "transgressions" must be the sins **He bore on behalf of others** (Isa 53:6, 11-12), which we shall examine shortly.

---

## **Psalm 21:6-8 LXX (22:6-8 MT) — The Mocking**

### **NETS:**

> But as for me, I am a worm and not human, a reproach of mankind and despised by people. All who saw me mocked at me; they talked with the lips; they moved the head: “He hoped in the Lord; let him rescue him; let him save him, because he wanted him,” — **Psalm 21:6-8 LXX (22:6-8 MT), NETS**
---

### **Cross-witnesses:**

> And they that passed by, reviled him, wagging their heads ... Likewise also the high Priests mocking him, with the Scribes and Elders, and Pharises, said, He saved others, but he cannot save ... He trusted in God, let him deliver him now, if he will have him. — **Matthew 27:39-43, 1599 Geneva**
---

### Observation

The mockers' words at the cross — **"He trusted in God; let Him deliver Him if He will have Him"** — are nearly a verbatim quotation of Psalm 21:8 LXX. The Synoptic writers, by inspiration, record the historical mocking and the prophetic word matching it. **Two witnesses (Matthew and the Psalmist) testify to the same event.**

---

## **Psalm 21:14-15 LXX (22:14-15 MT) — Physical Agony**

### **NETS:**

> Like water I was poured out, and all my bones were scattered; my heart became like wax melting within my belly; my strength was dried up like a potsherd, and my tongue is stuck to my throat, and to death’s dust you brought me down, — **Psalm 21:14-15 LXX (22:14-15 MT), NETS**
---

### **Cross-witness:**

> After, when Jesus knew that all things were performed, that the Scripture might be fulfilled, he said, I thirst. — **John 19:28, 1599 Geneva**
---

### Observation

John explicitly states that Jesus' cry **"I thirst"** was **"that the Scripture might be fulfilled."** Psalm 21:15 LXX speaks of His tongue cleaving to His jaws. John binds Jesus' suffering directly to this prophetic Psalm.

---

## **Psalm 21:16 LXX (22:16 MT) — The Piercing of Hands and Feet**

### **NETS:**

> because many dogs encircled me, a gathering of evildoers surrounded me. They gouged my hands and feet; — **Psalm 21:16 LXX (22:16 MT), NETS**
---

### Observations

**(1)** **ὤρυξαν χεῖράς μου καὶ πόδας** — *"they pierced/dug my hands and feet."* The verb **ὀρύσσω** means to dig, to pierce, to bore through. The LXX rendering is unambiguous: hands and feet are **pierced**.

**(2) This is a remarkable witness.** The LXX was translated centuries before crucifixion was known as a Jewish or Christian theme. The reading "they pierced my hands and feet" is in the **pre-Christian Greek text**. The Masoretic Hebrew has a contested reading (כָּאֲרִי — *"like a lion"* vs. כָּאֲרוּ — *"they pierced"*) that has been used by some to obscure the prophecy. The LXX preserves the unambiguous prophetic reading. **Per our maxim #1, we let God be true** — and the God-breathed text Jesus and the Apostles used reads **ὤρυξαν**.

---

### **Cross-witnesses — the fulfillment:**

> Behold mine hands and my feet: for it is I myself: handle me, and see: for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have. And when he had thus spoken, he showed them his hands and feet. — **Luke 24:39-40, 1599 Geneva**
> Except I see in his hands the print of the nails ... I will not believe ... he said to Thomas, Put thy finger here, and see mine hands, and put forth thine hand, and put it into my side — **John 20:25-27, 1599 Geneva**
---

### Observation

The risen Jesus shows His **pierced hands and feet** — fulfilling Ps 21:16 LXX exactly. Two NT witnesses (Luke and John) record the same proof.

---

## **Psalm 21:18 LXX (22:18 MT) — Garments Divided and Lots Cast**

### **NETS:**

> they divided my clothes among themselves, and for my clothing they cast lots. — **Psalm 21:18 LXX (22:18 MT), NETS**
---

### **Cross-witness — the most explicit citation in the NT:**

> Then the soldiers when they had crucified Jesus, took his garments, (and made four parts, to every soldier a part) and his coat: and the coat was without seam, woven from the top throughout. Therefore they said one to another, Let us not divide it, but cast lots for it, whose it shall be. This was that the Scripture might be fulfilled, which saith, They parted my garments among them, and on my coat did cast lots. So the souldiers did these things in deede. — **John 19:23-24, 1599 Geneva**
---

### Observation

John quotes Psalm 21:18 LXX **verbatim** as the fulfilled Scripture. This is one of the most precise prophecy-fulfillment correspondences in all of Scripture: the *exact* manner of dividing garments and casting lots is foretold and historically fulfilled.

**This is also recorded by all four Gospels** — Matthew 27:35, Mark 15:24, Luke 23:34, John 19:23-24 — making it a quadruple witness.

## **Psalm 21:22 LXX (22:22 MT) — The Vindicated One's Praise**

### **NETS:**

> I will tell of your name to my kindred; in the midst of an assembly I will sing a hymn to you: — **Psalm 21:22 LXX (22:22 MT), NETS**
---

### **Cross-witness — explicit NT citation:**

> For he that sanctifieth, and they which are sanctified, are all of one: wherefore he is not ashamed to call them brethren, Saying, I will declare thy Name unto my brethren: in the midst of the Church will I sing praises to thee. — **Hebrews 2:11-12, 1599 Geneva**
---

### Observation

The author of Hebrews **explicitly cites Psalm 22:22 as words spoken by the Christ to the Father**. This is decisive: **the entire Psalm is the voice of the Christ**, and the resurrected Son declares the Father's Name to His brethren in the midst of the Church. The Psalm moves from forsakenness (v. 1) to vindication and proclamation (v. 22) — i.e., **death and resurrection** are the arc of the Psalm.

## **Psalm 21:27-28 LXX (22:27-28 MT) — The Nations Worship**

### **NETS:**

> All the ends of the earth shall remember and turn to the Lord, and all the paternal families of the nations shall do obeisance *(worship)* before him, because kingship is the Lord’s, and it is he who is master over the nations. — **Psalm 21:27-28 LXX (22:27-28 MT), NETS**
---

### Observation

The Psalm closes with the **nations turning to the LORD** and worshipping — the global outcome of the Son's suffering. This is consistent with Ps 2:8 (*"the nations as Thine inheritance"*) and with the worldwide proclamation of the Gospel.

# **PART TWO: ISAIAH 52:13 — 53:12 — The Suffering Servant**

This is **the most extensive and explicit prophecy of the Son's atoning death in the OT.** We must take it slowly.

---

## **Isaiah 52:13-15 LXX — The Servant's Exaltation and Disfigurement**

### **NETS:**

> See, my servant shall understand, and he shall be exalted and glorified exceedingly. Just as many shall be astonished at you— so shall your appearance be without glory from men, and your glory be absent from the men— so shall many nations be astonished at him, and kings shall shut their mouth, because those who were not informed about him shall see and those who did not hear shall understand. — **Isaiah 52:13-15 LXX, NETS**
---

### Observations

**(1)** **ὁ παῖς μου** — *"My Servant"* (or *"My Child"*). The LXX uses **παῖς**, which can mean both "servant" and "child/son." This same word is used of Jesus in the apostolic preaching:

> For doubtless against thine holy Son Jesus, whom thou hadst anointed, both Herod and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles and the people of Israel gathered themselves together. — **Acts 4:27, 1599 Geneva**
**Acts 3:13, 26 — same designation of Jesus as **τὸν παῖδα αὐτοῦ Ἰησοῦν** — "His Servant/Child Jesus."**

This is the apostolic identification: **Jesus is the παῖς of Isaiah 52-53.**

**(2)** **ὑψωθήσεται καὶ δοξασθήσεται σφόδρα** — *"He shall be exalted and glorified exceedingly."* The Servant's path is exaltation. This corresponds to **John 12:32**: *"And I, if I be lifted up, will draw all unto Me."* *(the 1599 print reads, "And I, if I were lifted up from the earth, will draw all men unto me.")* John explicitly connects Jesus' "being lifted up" to His glorification (John 12:23, 32; 13:31-32).

**(3)** **ἀδοξήσει ἀπὸ ἀνθρώπων τὸ εἶδός σου** — *"Your appearance shall be without glory from men."* The Servant is *disfigured* — His visible form unrecognizable. This is fulfilled in Jesus' scourging, beating, and crucifixion.

**(4)** **θαυμάσονται ἔθνη πολλὰ ἐπ᾽ αὐτῷ** — *"Many nations shall marvel at Him."* The nations are again in view (cf. Gen 49:10; Ps 21:27-28). The Servant is for the nations.

---

### **Cross-witness — explicit NT citation of Isa 52:15:**

> ... But as it is written, To whom he was not spoken of, they shall see him, and they that heard not, shall understand him. — **Romans 15:20-21, 1599 Geneva**
Paul **explicitly cites Isa 52:15 LXX** as fulfilled in his Gospel preaching to the nations. The Servant is Christ; the nations are the Gentiles.

---

## **Isaiah 53:1 — Unbelief in the Report**

### **NETS:**

> Lord, who has believed our report? And to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed? — **Isaiah 53:1 LXX, NETS**
---

### **Cross-witnesses — explicit NT citation:**

> And though he had done so many miracles before them, yet believed they not on him, That the saying of Esaias the Prophet might be fulfilled, that he said, Lord, who believed our report? and to whom is the arm of the Lord revealed? — **John 12:37-38, 1599 Geneva**
> But they have not all obeyed the Gospel: for Esaias saith, Lord, who hath believed our report? — **Romans 10:16, 1599 Geneva**
---

### Observation

**Two explicit NT citations** (John 12 and Rom 10) bind Isaiah 53:1 directly to the rejection of Christ and the Gospel. **Witnesses fully established.**

## **Isaiah 53:2-3 LXX — The Servant Despised**

### **NETS:**

> He grew up before him like a child, like a root in a thirsty land; he has no form or glory, and we saw him, and he had no form or beauty. But his form was without honor, failing beyond all men, a man being in calamity and knowing how to bear sickness; because his face is turned away, he was dishonored and not esteemed. — **Isaiah 53:2-3 LXX, NETS**
---

### Observations

**(1)** **ὡς παιδίον ἐναντίον αὐτοῦ** — *"as a little child before Him."* The Servant grows up before the LORD as a tender shoot — a humble origin.

**(2)** **ὡς ῥίζα ἐν γῇ διψώσῃ** — *"as a root in thirsty ground."* This corresponds to **Rev 22:16** — *"I am the Root..."* — and **Rev 5:5** — *"the Root of David."* The Servant's origin is humble, from dry earth, not from royal display.

**(3)** **ἄνθρωπος ἐν πληγῇ ὢν** — *"a Man in suffering."* The Servant is repeatedly identified as **a Man.** No hidden Deity unrecognizable as human — He is genuinely Man in suffering.

**(4)** **ἠτιμάσθη καὶ οὐκ ἐλογίσθη** — *"He was dishonored and not esteemed."* Fulfilled in Jesus' rejection by His own people (John 1:11).

---

## **Isaiah 53:4-6 LXX — The Substitutionary Suffering**

### **NETS:**

> This one bears our sins and suffers pain for us, and we accounted him to be in trouble and calamity and ill-treatment. But he was wounded because of our acts of lawlessness and has been weakened because of our sins; upon him was the discipline of our peace; by his bruise we were healed. All we like sheep have gone astray; a man has strayed in his own way, and the Lord gave him over to our sins. — **Isaiah 53:4-6 LXX, NETS**
---

### **Cross-witnesses — explicit NT citations:**

> ... and he healed all that were sick, That it might be fulfilled, which was spoken by Esaias the Prophet, saying, He took our infirmities, and bare our sicknesses. — **Matthew 8:16-17, 1599 Geneva**
> Who his own self bare our sins in his body on the tree ... by whose stripes ye were healed. For ye were as sheep going astray — **1 Peter 2:24-25, 1599 Geneva**
---

### Observations

**(1) Multiple explicit NT citations** of Isa 53:4-6 applied directly to Jesus' atoning work.

**(2)** **Peter's citation in 1 Pet 2:24-25 binds together vv. 4, 5, 6 of Isa 53.** This is one of the most explicit and theologically loaded apostolic uses of the OT. The Servant's stripes heal *us*; the Servant bears *our* sins on the *tree* (ξύλον — cross).

**(3)** The phrase **καὶ Κύριος παρέδωκεν αὐτὸν ταῖς ἁμαρτίαις ἡμῶν** — *"and the LORD delivered Him over for our sins"* — is significant. **The Father delivers up the Son** for our sins. This is the language Paul echoes:

> Who spared not his own Son, but gave him for us all — **Romans 8:32, 1599 Geneva**
> Who was delivered to death for our sins — **Romans 4:25, 1599 Geneva**

## **Isaiah 53:7 LXX — Silence Before His Accusers**

### **NETS:**

> And he, because he has been ill-treated, does not open his mouth; like a sheep he was led to the slaughter, and as a lamb is silent before the one shearing it, so he does not open his mouth. — **Isaiah 53:7 LXX, NETS**
---

### **Cross-witness — direct apostolic citation:**

> Now the place of the Scripture which he read, was this, He was led as a sheep to the slaughter: and like a lamb dumb before his shearer, so opened he not his mouth ... Then Philip opened his mouth, and began at the same Scripture, and preached unto him Jesus. — **Acts 8:32-35, 1599 Geneva**
---

### Observation

The Ethiopian eunuch was reading **this exact passage of Isaiah 53 LXX**, and Philip — by direct apostolic exposition — preached **Jesus** from it. **The first recorded gospel sermon from Isaiah 53 explicitly identifies the Servant as Jesus.** This is an unimpeachable witness.

**Cross-witness #2 — Jesus' fulfillment of silence:**

> And when he was accused of the chief Priests and Elders, he answered nothing ... But he answered him not to one word, in so much that the governor marveled greatly. — **Matthew 27:12-14, 1599 Geneva**
Jesus' silence before Pilate fulfills Isa 53:7.

## **Isaiah 53:8 LXX — Cut Off From the Land of the Living**

### **NETS:**

> In his humiliation his judgment was taken away. Who will describe his generation? Because his life is being taken from the earth, he was led to death on account of the acts of lawlessness of my people. — **Isaiah 53:8 LXX, NETS**
---

### **Cross-witness:**

> In his humilitie his iudgement hath bene exalted: but who shall declare his generation? for his life is taken from the earth. — **Acts 8:33, 1599 Geneva**
Verse 8 is included in the eunuch's reading, and Philip preaches Jesus from this exact text. **Witnessed.**

---

### Observation

**αἴρεται ἀπὸ τῆς γῆς ἡ ζωὴ αὐτοῦ** — *"His life is taken away from the earth."* The Servant **dies**. He is **cut off**. This is the explicit prophecy of the Christ's death — not merely suffering, but death.

## **Isaiah 53:9 LXX — Burial With the Rich**

### **NETS:**

> And I will give the wicked for his burial and the rich for his death, because he committed no lawlessness, nor was deceit found in his mouth. — **Isaiah 53:9 LXX, NETS**
---

### **Cross-witness:**

> Who did no sin, neither was there guile found in his mouth. — **1 Peter 2:22, 1599 Geneva**
Peter quotes Isa 53:9 verbatim of Jesus' sinlessness. **Witnessed.**

**Cross-witness #2 — Jesus' burial:**

> And when the even was come, there came a rich man of Arimathea, named Joseph ... He went to Pilate, and asked the body of Jesus ... and put it in his new tomb — **Matthew 27:57-60, 1599 Geneva**
Jesus is buried in the tomb of a **rich man** — Joseph of Arimathea. **The historical fulfillment is exact.**

## **Isaiah 53:10-12 LXX — The Lord's Pleasure and the Servant's Vindication**

### **NETS:**

> And the Lord desires to cleanse him from his blow *(stroke)*. If you offer for sin, your soul shall see a long-lived offspring. And the Lord wishes to take away from the pain of his soul, to show him light and fill him with understanding, to justify a righteous one who is well subject to many, and he himself shall bear their sins. Therefore he shall inherit many, and he shall divide the spoils of the strong, because his soul was given over to death, and he was reckoned among the lawless, and he bore the sins of many, and because of their sins he was given over. — **Isaiah 53:10-12 LXX, NETS**
---

### Observations

**(1)** **Κύριος βούλεται καθαρίσαι αὐτὸν τῆς πληγῆς** — *"The LORD wills/desires to cleanse Him from the stroke."* The Father's purpose is in the Servant's suffering — yet also in His vindication.

**(2)** **ἐὰν δῶτε περὶ ἁμαρτίας, ἡ ψυχὴ ὑμῶν ὄψεται σπέρμα μακρόβιον** — *"If you give for sin, your soul shall see long-living seed."* The Servant's life becomes a sin-offering (**περὶ ἁμαρτίας** — the technical term for the sin-offering in Levitical law). His **seed** survives Him — this is **resurrection language**.

**(3)** **δεῖξαι αὐτῷ φῶς** — *"to show Him light."* After His soul's labor, He sees **light**. This is **resurrection** — the Servant who was cut off from the land of the living (v. 8) now sees light.

**(4)** **δικαιῶσαι δίκαιον εὖ δουλεύοντα πολλοῖς** — *"to justify the Righteous One serving many well."* The Servant is **the Righteous One** who **justifies many**.

### **Cross-witness:**

> ... by the justifying of one, the benefit abounded toward all men to the justification of life. For as by one man's disobedience many were made sinners, so by that obedience of that one shall many also be made righteous. — **Romans 5:18-19, 1599 Geneva**
Paul's *"many shall be made righteous through the obedience of the one"* echoes Isa 53:11 LXX: *"the Righteous One serving many well... shall justify."* **Witnessed.**

**(5)** **αὐτὸς ἁμαρτίας πολλῶν ἀνήνεγκεν** — *"He bore the sins of many."*

> So Christ was once offered to take away the sins of many — **Hebrews 9:28, 1599 Geneva**
Hebrews echoes Isa 53:12 directly of Christ. **Witnessed.**

**(6)** **ἐν τοῖς ἀνόμοις ἐλογίσθη** — *"He was reckoned with the transgressors."*

> For I say unto you, That yet the same which is written, must be performed in me, Even with the wicked was he numbered: for doubtless those things which are written of me, have an end. — **Luke 22:37, 1599 Geneva**
**Jesus Himself directly cites Isa 53:12** and says it must be fulfilled **in Him**. This is the strongest possible witness — Jesus' own claim of Isa 53 as His prophecy.

# **SYNTHESIS — What is Established by the Whole Counsel**

By the most extensive and explicit OT-NT correspondence in Scripture, the following is established with overwhelming witness:

### **The Suffering, Death, Burial, and Vindication of the Son**

1. **The Son is the prophesied Servant of the LORD** — identified explicitly by the Apostles (Acts 3:13, 26; 4:27, 30; 8:32-35), by Jesus Himself (Luke 22:37), and by the Father's voice at the baptism (using Isa 42:1 language).

2. **He was forsaken by the Father on the cross**, bearing the cry of Psalm 22:1 (Matt 27:46; Mark 15:34).

3. **He was pierced in His hands and feet**, with garments divided and lots cast (Ps 22:16, 18; all four Gospels; John 19:23-24 explicit).

4. **He was silent before His accusers** as a lamb led to slaughter (Isa 53:7; Acts 8:32; Matt 27:12-14).

5. **He bore the sins of many** — making His soul a sin-offering (Isa 53:6, 10-12; 1 Pet 2:24; Rom 4:25; 8:32; Heb 9:28).

6. **He was cut off from the land of the living** — He really died (Isa 53:8).

7. **He was buried with the rich** (Isa 53:9; Matt 27:57-60).

8. **He was vindicated, seeing light and seed after His soul's travail** — He rose from the dead (Isa 53:10-11; Acts 13:33 — *"today have I begotten Thee"* fulfilled in resurrection).

9. **He justifies many by His righteousness** (Isa 53:11; Rom 5:18-19).

10. **The nations have heard and seen what was not previously declared** — the Gospel goes to the Gentiles (Isa 52:15; Rom 15:21).

---

## **A Critical Theological Observation**

Notice carefully what these prophecies establish — and what they do **not** establish.

### What they establish:

- The Son's true humanity (a Man, with form, with thirst, with sorrow, with death).
- The Son's true suffering (forsakenness, piercing, silence, death).
- The Son's substitutionary work (He bore *our* sins; the LORD laid on Him the iniquity of us all).
- The Son's vindication by the Father (resurrection, exaltation, justification of many).
- **The Father's active role** as the One who *delivers Him up* (Isa 53:6, 10; Rom 8:32) and who *vindicates* Him.

### What they do **not** establish:

- They do not, by themselves, prove the Son's pre-existence or divinity. (Other passages, which we have already surveyed, do that — Phil 2, John 17, John 8:58, Heb 1, etc.)
- They speak of the Son as **the Servant**, **the Righteous One**, **the Man** — and at no point in these passages is He called "God."

### **What this confirms:**

The pattern we have been building from the foundation upward holds firm:
- **The Father is the One God** — who delivers up His Servant for our sins.
- **The Son is the Servant of the LORD** — distinct from the Father, obedient unto death, vindicated by the Father.
- **The Son's sufferings glorify the Father** — and the Father's pleasure prospers in the Servant's hand (Isa 53:10).

This is the **Servant Christology** of the earliest apostolic preaching (Acts 3:13, 26; 4:27, 30 — all using **παῖς**), and it is bound to the Father-Source / Son-Sent pattern we have established throughout.

## **What Remains**

To complete the study of the Person of Christ from the earliest prophecies to the concluding prophecies of the Apocalypse, the major areas still to examine include:

**(A) The Son's exaltation and present session at the Father's right hand** — what He is doing *now* as priest, intercessor, and ruler (Heb 7-10; Ps 110:1-4 fully).

**(B) The Son's coming return** — the prophecies of His second appearing, His judgment of the living and the dead, and His reign (Daniel 7; Zechariah 12-14; Matt 24-25; Rev 19-22).

**(C) The Son's final relation to the Father in the consummation** — including 1 Cor 15:24-28 (the Son delivering the Kingdom to the Father, that God may be all in all), which is one of the most theologically decisive passages on the Father-Son order at the end.

**(D) The Son's titles and self-identifications in the Apocalypse** — the "I am" sayings of Revelation, the Lamb-on-the-Throne imagery, the Alpha and Omega designations, etc.
