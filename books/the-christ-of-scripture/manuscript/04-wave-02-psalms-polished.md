## **PSALM 2 (LXX) — The Anointed Son Begotten by the Father**

### **Psalm 2:1-2 (LXX):**

> Why did nations grow insolent, and peoples contemplate vain things? The kings of the earth stood side by side, and the rulers gathered together, against the Lord and against his anointed. — **Psalm 2:1-2, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observation of foundational importance

The Psalm immediately distinguishes **two parties** on the divine side:
- **ὁ Κύριος** — *the Lord* (the Father, the One God of Isaiah 44-46)
- **ὁ Χριστὸς αὐτοῦ** — ***His* Anointed** — the Christ who belongs *to* the Lord

The Anointed is **not** the Lord; He belongs to the Lord. He is the Lord's *Christ*. The genitive **αὐτοῦ** ("of Him / His") is decisive — it forbids identifying the Christ as the same Being as the Lord who anoints Him.

This is the very pattern Peter preaches at Pentecost:

**Acts 2:36 (Geneva):**

> Therefore, let all the house of Israel know for a surety, that God hath made him both Lord, and Christ, this Jesus, I say, whom ye have crucified. — **Acts 2:36, 1599 Geneva**

Peter declares that **God MADE Him** (**ἐποίησεν**) both Lord and Christ. The Father — the One God — is the One who *makes* Jesus Lord and Christ. Jesus is not the One making; He is the One *being made* into His offices by the Father.

---

### **Psalm 2:6-7 (LXX):**

> "But I was established king by him, on Sion *(Zion)*, his holy mountain, by proclaiming the Lord’s ordinance: The Lord said to me, "My son you are; today I have begotten you." — **Psalm 2:6-7, LXX (NETS)**

### Observations of the utmost weight

**(1)** **κατεστάθην βασιλεὺς ὑπ᾽ αὐτοῦ** — *"I was established as king BY HIM."* The Son does not crown Himself; He is **made** king **by the Father**. This is again the language of dependence and appointment, not co-equality.

**(2)** **Υἱός μου εἶ σύ, ἐγὼ σήμερον γεγέννηκά σε** — *"My Son art Thou; I, today, have begotten Thee."*

This is the **first explicit declaration in the Psalter** of the Father-Son relation, and it tells us two crucial things:

**(a)** The Son is **begotten** (**γεγέννηκα**) — He has an origin in the Father. This is the very meaning of **μονογενής** (only-begotten) — *singularly brought forth from the Father.*

**(b)** This begetting is fixed to a **day** — **σήμερον** (*today*) — a definite moment.

---

### When is "today"? — The two witnesses of the Apostles:

**Acts 13:32-33 (Geneva):**

> And we declare unto you, that touching the promise made unto the fathers, God hath fulfilled it unto us their children, in that he raised vp Jesus, even as it is written in the second Psalm, Thou art my Son: this day have I begotten thee. — **Acts 13:32-33, 1599 Geneva**

**Paul, by inspiration, locates the "today" of Psalm 2:7 at the *resurrection* of Jesus.** The begetting of the Son in the sense of Psalm 2 is His being **raised from the dead** and installed as King.

**Witness #2 — Romans 1:3-4 (Geneva):**

> Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord (which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh, And declared mightily to be the Son of God, touching the Spirit of sanctification by the resurrection from the dead) — **Romans 1:3-4, 1599 Geneva**

**Witness #2 confirmed:** Jesus was **ὁρισθέντος Υἱοῦ Θεοῦ** — *"declared/appointed Son of God in power"* — **ἐξ ἀναστάσεως νεκρῶν** — *"by the resurrection from the dead."*

**The two witnesses agree:** The "today I have begotten Thee" of Psalm 2:7 is **the resurrection day** — the day the Father raised His Christ and installed Him as King on Zion.

---

### **Psalm 2:8 (LXX):**

> Ask of me, and I will give you nations as your heritage, and as your possession the ends of the earth. — **Psalm 2:8, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observation

The Son **asks**; the Father **gives**. This is the consistent pattern: the Father is the Source (**ἐξ οὗ**, 1 Cor 8:6), the Son is the Recipient and Agent. The nations are **given** to the Son as an inheritance — He does not possess them by inherent right of independent deity; they are **granted** to Him by the Father.

This is identical to the language of:

**Matthew 28:18 (Geneva):**

> And Jesus came, and spake unto them, saying, All power is given unto me, in heaven, and in earth. — **Matthew 28:18, 1599 Geneva**

**ἐδόθη** — *"was given."* By whom? By the Father. The Son receives universal authority as a **gift** from the One God.

---

### **Psalm 2:12 (LXX):**

> Seize upon instruction *(grasp discipline)*, lest the Lord be angry, and you will perish from the righteous way, when his anger quickly blazes out. Happy are all who trust in him. — **Psalm 2:12, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Note on the textual difference

The LXX reads **δράξασθε παιδείας** — *"lay hold of instruction/discipline"* — where the Hebrew has *"kiss the Son"* (the wording of the 1599 Geneva and the English tradition). This is a notable variant. Either reading, the climactic line is the same: **μακάριοι πάντες οἱ πεποιθότες ἐπ᾽ αὐτῷ** — *"Blessed are all who trust in Him."* Trust is to be placed in the Anointed Son **whom the Father has set up** — yet this trust does not violate Isaiah 45:22 ("Look unto Me, and be ye saved") because the Son is the Father's appointed channel of salvation; trusting the Son is trusting the Father's provision.

---

## **What Psalm 2 establishes about the Son:**

1. He is **distinct from** the Lord — He is *the Lord's* Christ (v. 2).
2. He is **installed** as King by the Father (v. 6).
3. He is **begotten** of the Father on a definite day — fulfilled at His **resurrection** (v. 7; Acts 13:33; Rom 1:4).
4. He **asks** and **receives** the nations as a gift from the Father (v. 8).
5. He is to be **trusted** — as the Father's appointed Anointed (v. 12).

---

## **PSALM 109 LXX (110 MT) — The Lord at the Right Hand**

This is the **single most-cited Old Testament passage in the New Testament.** Jesus Himself quotes it; Peter quotes it at Pentecost; the author of Hebrews builds his entire theology of Christ's priesthood upon it.

### **Psalm 109:1 LXX (110:1 MT):**

> The Lord said to my lord, "Sit on my right *(at my right hand)* until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet." — **Psalm 109:1, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observation of supreme importance

**Two distinct figures are named:**
- **ὁ Κύριος** (capital — *the LORD*, YHWH, the Father, the One God of Isaiah 44-46)
- **τῷ κυρίῳ μου** (lowercase — *my lord*, a distinct personage who is David's Lord)

David, by the Spirit, hears the One God speaking *to another* — to David's own Lord — and inviting Him to sit at His right hand.

**This is the decisive testimony for the structure of our entire study:** The Son is **at the right hand of** the Father — meaning **He is not the Father, and the Father is not the Son**. The right hand is the place of supreme honor *granted by* the One enthroned in the center. The Son occupies the right hand; the Father occupies the throne.

---

### Jesus' own use of this Psalm — the keystone witness:

**Matthew 22:41-45 (Geneva):**

> While the Pharisees were gathered together, Jesus asked them, Saying, What think ye of Christ? whose son is he? They said unto him, Davids. He said unto them, How then doth David in spirit call him Lord, saying, The Lord said to my Lord, Sit at my right hand, till I make thine enemies thy footstool? If then David call him Lord, how is he his son? — **Matthew 22:41-45, 1599 Geneva**

Jesus' point: the Messiah is **both** David's son (according to the flesh) **and** David's Lord (according to His exalted office at the Father's right hand). The Christ is *more than* a mere descendant — He is the One *seated at the right hand of the One God.* Yet — crucially — Jesus does *not* say He is the Father, nor that He is the One who spoke. He is the One *spoken to.*

---

### Witness #2 — Peter at Pentecost:

**Acts 2:34-36 (Geneva):**

> For David is not ascended into heaven, but he saith, The Lord said to my Lord, Sit at my right hand, Until I make thine enemies thy footstool. Therefore, let all the house of Israel know for a surety, that God hath made him both Lord, and Christ, this Jesus, I say, whom ye have crucified. — **Acts 2:34-36, 1599 Geneva**

Peter explicitly identifies Psalm 110:1 as having been **fulfilled at the ascension of Jesus** — when **ὁ Θεός** (the Father) **MADE** Jesus both Lord and Christ. The Father is the actor; Jesus is the One acted upon, exalted, and seated.

**Two witnesses established:** Jesus and Peter agree — Psalm 110:1 reveals the Son seated at the Father's right hand, distinct from the Father, exalted *by* the Father.

---

### **Psalm 109:3 LXX (110:3 MT):**

> With you is rule *(dominion)* on a day of your power among the splendors of the holy ones. From the womb, before Morning-star *(the morning star)*, I brought you forth. — **Psalm 109:3, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observation — a critical LXX reading

The LXX preserves a remarkable line absent from the Hebrew (which the 1599 Geneva follows, reading *"Thy people shall come willingly at the time of assembling thine army in holy beauty: the youth of thy womb shall be as the morning dew"*): here the LXX reads, **ἐκ γαστρὸς πρὸ ἑωσφόρου ἐγέννησά σε** — *"From the womb, before the morning star."* NETS prints the Greek literally, *"I brought you forth"*; Brenton's older English renders it, *"before the morning star I have begotten thee"* *(LXX 1851)* — **ἐγέννησά σε** means *I begot you; I fathered you*.

This is the Father speaking again, as in Psalm 2:7. Here a **prior begetting** is in view — **πρὸ ἑωσφόρου** — *"before the morning star"* — that is, before the created luminaries of Genesis 1:14-16.

This is the **second witness** (alongside Psalm 2:7) to the *begetting* of the Son — but here the LXX places it **before** the cosmic order, while Psalm 2:7 places it on the day of resurrection. Scripture itself is presenting **two distinct begettings** — or more carefully, a foreordained begetting from before time and its historical fulfillment in the resurrection. We will need to examine this carefully later, particularly with Proverbs 8 and Micah 5:2.

For now, we simply note: **the Son is begotten of the Father** — never the reverse, never the Father begotten of the Son, and never a "co-eternal" begetting where the Son has no origin in the Father. The Father is *Source*; the Son is *Begotten*.

---

### **Psalm 109:4 LXX (110:4 MT):**

> The Lord swore and will not change his mind, "You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchisedek *(Melchizedek)*." — **Psalm 109:4, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observation

The Father swears an oath that the Son shall be **ἱερεὺς εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα** — *Priest forever* — after the order of Melchizedek (Gen 14). The Son is not only **King** at the right hand (vv. 1-3) — He is also **Priest** by the Father's appointment.

**Witness #1 — Hebrews 5:5-6:**

> So likewise Christ took not to himself this honor, to be made the high Priest, but he that said unto him, Thou art my Son, this day begat I thee, gave it him. As he also in another place speaketh, Thou art a Priest for ever, after ye order of Melchisedek. — **Hebrews 5:5-6, 1599 Geneva**

The author of Hebrews binds Psalm 2:7 and Psalm 110:4 together — the Son does **not glorify Himself**; the Father glorifies Him with both Sonship and Priesthood. **The Son receives every office He holds *from* the Father.**

**Witness #2 — Hebrews 7:21:**

> But this is made with an oath by him that said unto him, The Lord hath sworn, and will not repent, Thou art a Priest for ever, after the order of Melchisedek) — **Hebrews 7:21, 1599 Geneva**

**Two witnesses agree:** The Father is the One who **swears** and **appoints**; the Son is the One who **receives** the eternal priesthood by oath.

---

### **Psalm 109:5 LXX (110:5 MT):**

> The Lord at your right *(your right hand)* shattered kings on a day of his wrath. — **Psalm 109:5, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observation — a remarkable inversion

In v. 1 the Son sits at the Father's right hand. In v. 5 *the Lord is at* **the Son's** *right hand*. This is **not** a contradiction; it is the picture of the **Father and the Son operating in perfect coordination** — wherever the Son acts in judgment, the Father stands with Him; wherever the Father is enthroned, the Son sits at His right.

The Father and Son are **two distinct figures**, working together — never collapsed into one Person, never elevated to two Gods. **One God, the Father; one Lord, His Christ — at one another's right hand.**

## **SUMMARY: Psalms 2 and 109/110 Together**

| Truth Established | Psalm 2 | Psalm 109/110 |
|---|---|---|
| Two distinct figures: the Lord and His Anointed/Lord | v. 2 | v. 1 |
| The Father appoints, the Son is appointed | v. 6 | vv. 1, 4 |
| The Son is **begotten** of the Father | v. 7 | v. 3 (LXX) |
| The Son **receives** authority/inheritance | v. 8 | v. 1 |
| The Father swears an oath concerning the Son | — | v. 4 |
| The Son is enthroned at the right hand | (v. 6 implied) | v. 1 |
| Fulfilled in Jesus' **resurrection and ascension** | Acts 13:33 | Acts 2:34-36 |

---

## **The Doctrinal Foundation Now Standing**

We may now state, by two-or-more witnesses from Scripture itself, what the Son is:

1. **He is begotten of the Father** — He has an origin in the Father; the Father has no origin in Him.
2. **He is appointed, made, and given** — every title, office, and authority He holds is **conferred upon Him by the Father**.
3. **He is distinct from the Father** — David's Lord, but not the LORD; the Lord's Christ, but not the Lord Himself.
4. **He is seated at the Father's right hand** — supreme in honor, but second in order; the Father remains enthroned in the center.
5. **He is both King (Ps 2) and Priest (Ps 110)** — combining the offices that were separated in Israel under David and Aaron.
6. **He is the resurrected One** — His installation as Son-in-power, Lord, and Christ is bound to His being **raised from the dead** by the Father.

This is the apostolic gospel, and it preserves intact the Shema and the declarations of Isaiah 44-46: **the Father is the One God; Jesus is His Christ, His Lord, His Son, His Priest, His King — all by the Father's appointment.**
