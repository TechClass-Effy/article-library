# **ROMANS 8 — LIFE IN THE SPIRIT: THE MOUNTAIN PEAK**

We now ascend to **Romans 8** — what many have rightly called the **mountain peak** of the New Testament. Having traced the indicative-imperative pattern of Romans 6, we now see how Paul develops it into the full **doctrine of life in the Spirit** — the practical outworking of who we are in Christ through the indwelling Spirit's empowering work.

Per our refined modus operandi, we will:

1. Briefly note the transition through Romans 7 — which sets up the necessity of life in the Spirit.
2. Walk carefully through Romans 8 in its major sections.
3. Cross-reference all relevant NT witnesses on life in the Spirit.
4. Draw out the practical implications for the modern believer.

Let us proceed.

---

# **THE TRANSITION: WHY ROMANS 8 IS NECESSARY**

Romans 6 established that the believer is **dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus** — and that on this basis, the believer must **reckon** the truth and **walk** accordingly.

Romans 7 then confronts a question that any honest believer must face: **if I am dead to sin and freed from the law, why do I still struggle so painfully with sin?**

Paul's answer in Romans 7 — and we will not exposit it fully here — is profound. The law is good, holy, and spiritual (7:12, 14). But the law cannot give life. The believer who tries to fight sin through self-effort, even from his new position in Christ, **discovers his own helplessness:**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"For I know, that in me, that is, in my flesh, dwelleth no good thing: for to will is preset *(present)* with me: but I find no means to perform that which is good. For I do not the good thing, which I would, but the evil, which I would not, that do I."*


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"O wretched man that I am, who shall deliver me from the body of this death!"*

---

### The Crisis Romans 7 Establishes

The believer who tries to walk out his new identity **by self-effort, even with the help of the law, fails.** The flesh — that remaining principle of sin in our mortal members — frustrates every attempt at self-generated righteousness. The will is willing; the flesh is unable.

**Romans 7 ends with a cry of helpless desperation:** *"Who shall deliver me?"*

And **Romans 8 is the answer.** The deliverance comes not through more effort, not through better law-keeping, not through stricter discipline — but through **the indwelling Spirit who imparts the very life of Christ to the believer.**

This is critical to understand: **Romans 8 does not contradict Romans 6; it provides the operating mechanism by which the reality of Romans 6 becomes lived experience.** Romans 6 gives the position; Romans 8 gives the power.

---

# **ROMANS 8:1-2 — NO CONDEMNATION; THE SPIRIT'S LAW**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Now then there is no condemnation to them that are in Christ Jesus, which walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit. For the law of the Spirit of life, which is in Christ Jesus, hath freed me from the law of sin and of death."*

---

### Observations of foundational importance

**(1)** **Οὐδὲν ἄρα νῦν κατάκριμα** — *"Therefore now no condemnation."*

The word **ἄρα** (*therefore*) connects back to all that has preceded — both the established reality of Romans 6 and the demonstrated helplessness of Romans 7. The conclusion: **there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.**

The word **νῦν** — *now* — is significant. **At this present time**, the believer stands without condemnation. Not someday, not after enough good works, not when sanctification is complete. **Now.** This is the believer's present standing.

The phrase **τοῖς ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ** — *"to those in Christ Jesus"* — is the qualifier. The freedom from condemnation belongs to those who are **in Him.** This is the same identification we have traced throughout — being in Christ is the basis of every blessing.

**(2)** **ὁ γὰρ νόμος τοῦ Πνεύματος τῆς ζωῆς ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ** — *"For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus..."*

Paul uses the word **νόμος** (*law*) in a different sense here — not the Mosaic Law, but **a governing principle, an operating order.** The "law of the Spirit of life" is **the principle by which the Spirit operates in the believer to produce life.**

This stands in contrast to the **νόμος τῆς ἁμαρτίας καὶ τοῦ θανάτου** — *"the law of sin and death"* — the principle by which sin operates in the flesh to produce death (described in Romans 7).

Two competing principles, two competing operations. The Spirit's law is liberating; the flesh's law is enslaving. **And in Christ Jesus, the Spirit's law has freed the believer from the flesh's law.**

**(3)** **ἠλευθέρωσέν με** — *"has freed me."* Aorist tense — completed past action. The freedom has been accomplished. The believer does not need to *achieve* this freedom — he stands in it.

---

# **ROMANS 8:3-4 — WHAT THE LAW COULD NOT DO, GOD DID**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"For (that that was impossible to the Law, in as much as it was weak, because of the flesh) God sending his own Son, in the similitude of sinful flesh, and for sin, condened sin in the flesh, That that righteousness of the Law might be fulfilled in us, which walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit."*

---

### Observations of decisive importance

**(1)** The law was **weak through the flesh.** The law was righteous (Rom 7:12), but it could not produce righteousness in fallen humanity because the flesh resisted it.

**(2)** **ὁ Θεὸς τὸν ἑαυτοῦ Υἱὸν πέμψας** — *"God, sending His own Son."* The Father is the actor. He sent His own Son.

**(3)** **ἐν ὁμοιώματι σαρκὸς ἁμαρτίας** — *"in the likeness of sinful flesh."* The Son took on **the likeness** of sinful flesh — true humanity, but without sin (Heb 4:15). He was made like His brethren in all things, yet without sin.

**(4)** **κατέκρινεν τὴν ἁμαρτίαν ἐν τῇ σαρκί** — *"condemned sin in the flesh."* At the cross, the Father **condemned sin** — passed judicial sentence on it — **in the flesh** of His Son. Sin's claim on humanity was exhausted in the body of the Son who bore it.

**(5)** **The purpose: ἵνα τὸ δικαίωμα τοῦ νόμου πληρωθῇ ἐν ἡμῖν** — *"that the righteous requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us."*

**This is profound.** The law's righteous demand — which the law itself could not produce — **is now fulfilled IN US.** Not by our effort. Not by our discipline. Not by our resolution. **But by the Spirit's work in those who walk after the Spirit.**

The believer who walks by the Spirit fulfills the righteous demand of the law — not by trying to keep the law, but by being indwelt by the One who fulfilled it. **The Spirit produces in us what the law could only demand.**

This is the transition from Romans 7 to Romans 8 in compressed form: the law's demand, which the flesh could not meet, is now met by the Spirit's life in those who are in Christ Jesus.

---

# **ROMANS 8:5-8 — THE TWO MIND-SETS**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"For they that are after the flesh, savor the things of the flesh: but they that are after the Spirit, the things of the Spirit. For the wisdom of the flesh is death: but the wisdom of the Spirit is life and peace, Because the wisdom of the flesh is enimitie *(enmity)* against God: for it is not subject to the Law of God, neither indeed can be. So then they that are in the flesh, cannot please God."*

---

### Observations

**(1)** Paul presents **two fundamental orientations of life:**
- **κατὰ σάρκα** — *"according to the flesh"* — the orientation that lives by the principles, desires, and operations of the unregenerate human nature.
- **κατὰ Πνεῦμα** — *"according to the Spirit"* — the orientation that lives by the principles, desires, and operations of the indwelling Holy Spirit.

These are not two zones the believer moves between casually; they are two fundamentally different operating systems for life.

**(2) The key word: φρόνημα** — *"mind-set, way of thinking, what one is set on."* This is more than intellectual thought; it is the deep orientation of one's affections, values, and pursuits. Where one's mind is set.

**(3)** **τὸ φρόνημα τῆς σαρκὸς θάνατος** — *"the mind-set of the flesh is death."* Not produces death — *is* death. The very orientation of the flesh-life is already in the realm of death.

**(4)** **τὸ φρόνημα τοῦ Πνεύματος ζωὴ καὶ εἰρήνη** — *"the mind-set of the Spirit is life and peace."* The Spirit's orientation **is** life and peace. The believer's Spirit-orientation is itself the experience of life and peace, not merely a means to it.

**(5)** Paul is sobering: those who are in the flesh **cannot** please God. Not "find it difficult to please" — **cannot.** The flesh, by its very nature, is unable to please God. It is enmity against God.

This sharpens the practical implication: **the believer must not attempt to please God in the energy of the flesh.** All such attempts are doomed. The believer pleases God only as he walks by the Spirit.

---

# **ROMANS 8:9-11 — THE INDWELLING SPIRIT**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Now ye are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit, because the Spirit of God dwelleth in you: but if any man hath not the Spirit of Christ, the same is not his. And if Christ be in you, the body is dead, because of sin: but the Spirit is life for righteousness sake. But if the Spirit of him that raised up Jesus from the dead, dwell in you, he that raised up Christ from the dead, shall also quicken your mortal bodies, by his Spirit that dwelleth in you."*

---

### Observations of supreme importance

**(1)** **ὑμεῖς δὲ οὐκ ἐστὲ ἐν σαρκὶ ἀλλὰ ἐν Πνεύματι** — *"But you are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit."*

**The indicative again.** Paul declares the believer's state: **you ARE in the Spirit, not in the flesh.** This is not a goal; it is your reality.

The condition: **εἴπερ Πνεῦμα Θεοῦ οἰκεῖ ἐν ὑμῖν** — *"if indeed the Spirit of God dwells in you."* As in Rom 8:17, **εἴπερ** is more *"since indeed"* than *"if"* — Paul assumes the condition is met. **The indwelling Spirit is the mark of belonging to Christ.**

**(2)** **εἰ δέ τις Πνεῦμα Χριστοῦ οὐκ ἔχει, οὗτος οὐκ ἔστιν αὐτοῦ** — *"If anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, he is not His."*

**The Spirit's indwelling is the sine qua non of belonging to Christ.** Without the indwelling Spirit, one does not belong to Christ. This is a settled NT principle.

Note the interchangeable language:
- **Πνεῦμα Θεοῦ** — *"Spirit of God"* (the Father's Spirit).
- **Πνεῦμα Χριστοῦ** — *"Spirit of Christ"* (the Son's Spirit).
- **Χριστὸς ἐν ὑμῖν** — *"Christ in you."*

These are not three different realities; they are **one indwelling Presence described from three angles** — consistent with what we established in Parts E and F. The Father (Spirit) indwells in the Son (Christ in you), and the indwelling is mediated by the Spirit Christ has poured out (Acts 2:33).

**(3)** **τὸ μὲν σῶμα νεκρὸν διὰ ἁμαρτίαν, τὸ δὲ πνεῦμα ζωὴ διὰ δικαιοσύνην** — *"the body is dead because of sin, but the spirit is life because of righteousness."*

A sober honesty. The body is still mortal — under the sentence of death because of sin. The body still bears the corruption inherited from Adam. But the spirit (the believer's regenerated inner being, indwelt by the Holy Spirit) is **life** — because of righteousness imparted in Christ.

**(4)** The promise of v. 11: **ὁ ἐγείρας τὸν Χριστὸν ἐκ νεκρῶν ζῳοποιήσει καὶ τὰ θνητὰ σώματα ὑμῶν διὰ τοῦ ἐνοικοῦντος αὐτοῦ Πνεύματος ἐν ὑμῖν** — *"He who raised Christ from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies through His Spirit who dwells in you."*

**The Spirit's indwelling work extends to our mortal bodies.** This points forward to the resurrection (Rom 8:23 — *the redemption of our body*), but it also has a **present** dimension — the Spirit who indwells gives life to our mortal bodies even now, enabling them to be presented as instruments of righteousness (Rom 6:13; 12:1).

The Father's resurrection power is at work in the believer's body through the indwelling Spirit — **the same power that raised the Son from the dead.**

---

# **ROMANS 8:12-13 — THE OBLIGATION AND THE OPERATION**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Therefore brethren, we are detters *(debtors)* not to the flesh, to live after the flesh: For if ye live after the flesh, ye shall die: but if ye mortify the deeds of the body by the Spirit, ye shall live."*

---

### Observations of decisive practical importance

**(1)** **ὀφειλέται ἐσμέν, οὐ τῇ σαρκί** — *"we are debtors, not to the flesh."*

**We owe nothing to the flesh.** The flesh has done nothing for us; we owe it no allegiance. It is a tyrant we have been freed from, not a benefactor we must honor. The believer has **no debt** to the flesh — and therefore no obligation to live according to its dictates.

**(2)** **εἰ Πνεύματι τὰς πράξεις τοῦ σώματος θανατοῦτε** — *"if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body."*

**Here is the practical command and its mechanism.**

**The command:** *"Put to death the deeds of the body."* This is the believer's responsibility. The verb **θανατοῦτε** (present active) means *"keep on putting to death."* This is ongoing, daily mortification.

**The mechanism: Πνεύματι** — *"by the Spirit."* Not by the flesh's own effort. Not by self-discipline alone. Not by law-keeping. **By the indwelling Spirit's power.**

**This is the practical answer to Romans 7's crisis.** Where the law could not produce victory because of the flesh's weakness, the indwelling Spirit produces what is impossible to the flesh.

The pattern is:
1. **The Spirit indwells (indicative, vv. 9-11).**
2. **The believer, by the Spirit, puts to death the deeds of the body (imperative, v. 13).**
3. **Life follows (promise, v. 13).**

This is parallel to the Romans 6 pattern but with the Spirit's empowering work made explicit. The believer is responsible to act (*put to death*), but the power for the action comes from the indwelling Spirit, not from self-effort.

---

# **ROMANS 8:14-17 — SONS OF GOD**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, they are the sons of God. For ye have not received the Spirit of bodage *(bondage)*, to fear again: but ye have received the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry Abba, Father. The same Spirit beareth witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God. If we be children, we are also heirs..."*

---

### Observations

We already touched on these verses in our earlier examination of co-suffering. Let us now note their place in the Spirit-led walk.

**(1)** **ὅσοι Πνεύματι Θεοῦ ἄγονται** — *"as many as are led by the Spirit of God."* The believer is **led** by the Spirit — guided in the path of righteousness, in the will of the Father, in the imitation of the Son.

**(2)** **Πνεῦμα υἱοθεσίας** — *"the Spirit of adoption."* The Spirit not only empowers but also **witnesses to** the believer's sonship. He brings the believer into the conscious relation of son to Father — crying out **"Abba, Father"** — with the same intimacy the Son Himself used (Mark 14:36).

**(3)** **αὐτὸ τὸ Πνεῦμα συμμαρτυρεῖ τῷ πνεύματι ἡμῶν** — *"the Spirit Himself bears witness with our spirit."* The Spirit's inner witness — assuring us we are God's children — is itself a means by which He empowers us. We walk in the confidence of sonship, not the fear of slavery.

---

# **ROMANS 8:18-27 — THE PRESENT SUFFERING AND THE SPIRIT'S INTERCESSION**

We have already examined the suffering theme (Rom 8:17-18) and the groaning of creation (8:18-25) in our earlier discussion. Let us now focus on what is critical for life in the Spirit:


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Likewise the Spirit also helpeth our infirmities: for we know not what to pray as we ought: but the Spirit itself maketh request for us with sighs, which cannot be expressed. But he that searcheth the hearts, knoweth what is the meaning of the Spirit: for he maketh request for the Saints, according to the will of God."*

---

### Observations of supreme practical importance

**(1)** **τὸ Πνεῦμα συναντιλαμβάνεται τῇ ἀσθενείᾳ ἡμῶν** — *"the Spirit helps our weakness."*

**συναντιλαμβάνομαι** is a vivid word: *"to take hold of together with one against something."* The Spirit takes hold of our weakness *alongside us* — as one who helps us bear what we cannot bear alone.

**The believer's weakness is honestly acknowledged.** We do not pretend to strength we do not have. We are weak. And the Spirit takes hold with us in our weakness.

**(2)** **τὸ γὰρ τί προσευξώμεθα καθὸ δεῖ οὐκ οἴδαμεν** — *"for we do not know what we ought to pray for as we should."*

**A profound admission.** Even in prayer — perhaps especially in prayer — we do not know how to pray rightly. The believer is honest about this ignorance.

**(3)** **αὐτὸ τὸ Πνεῦμα ὑπερεντυγχάνει στεναγμοῖς ἀλαλήτοις** — *"the Spirit Himself intercedes with groanings that cannot be uttered."*

The Spirit Himself intercedes for us **with groanings beyond articulation.** When we do not know how to pray, the Spirit takes our inarticulate longings and presents them to the Father in accordance with His will.

This is a tremendous comfort. Even when the believer is overwhelmed, perplexed, grieved beyond words — the Spirit prays for him in groanings that perfectly accord with the Father's will. **No prayer of the believer is wasted, even when he himself cannot find words.**

---

# **ROMANS 8:28-30 — THE GOLDEN CHAIN**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Also we know that all things work together for the best unto them that love God, even to them that are called of his purpose. For those which he knew before, he also predestinate *(predestinated)* to be made like to the image of his Son, that he might be the first born among many brethren. Moreover whom he predestinate *(predestinated)*, them also he called, and whom he called, them also he justified, and whom he justified, them he also glorified."*

---

### Observations

**(1)** **πάντα συνεργεῖ εἰς ἀγαθόν** — *"all things work together for good."*

For those who love God, **all things** — joys and sorrows, victories and defeats, gains and losses — work together (συνεργεῖ) **for good.** The Father is sovereign over the whole tapestry of the believer's life, weaving every thread toward His purpose.

**(2) The Father's purpose: συμμόρφους τῆς εἰκόνος τοῦ Υἱοῦ αὐτοῦ** — *"conformed to the image of His Son."*

**This is the Father's eternal purpose for every believer:** to be conformed to the image of His Beloved Son. Everything that happens to the believer — every blessing, every trial, every joy, every sorrow — is being used by the Father toward this end. We will examine this more fully shortly.

**(3) The golden chain:** **foreknew → predestined → called → justified → glorified.** Five aorist verbs — five completed actions in the Father's purpose. **And note: glorified is past tense.** In the Father's eternal purpose, the believer is already glorified. The future is so certain it is reckoned as past.

---

# **ROMANS 8:31-39 — THE UNBREAKABLE SECURITY**

This is the climax of the chapter — and perhaps the climax of the entire Pauline corpus. We will not exposit every verse (we have referenced it before), but let us note the central practical truth.


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"What shall we then say to these things? If God be on our side, who can be against us? Who spared not his own Son, but gave him for us all to death, how shall he not with him give us all things also?"*


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"For I am persuaded that neither death, nor life, nor Angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord."*

---

### Observation

The chapter closes with the absolute, unbreakable security of the believer in the love of God in Christ Jesus. **Nothing** can separate us. And this security is **the very ground** of confident, faithful walking by the Spirit.

The believer who knows nothing can separate him from the Father's love walks boldly, freely, courageously. He does not walk in fear of losing his salvation; he walks in the assurance that he is forever held.

This security is not antinomian — it does not produce laziness or licentiousness. **It produces grateful, Spirit-empowered obedience.** Because the believer is forever loved, he loves in return. Because he is forever secure, he serves freely. Because he is forever held, he can risk everything for the Beloved Son who first gave Himself for him.

---

# **PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS — LIFE IN THE SPIRIT FOR THE MODERN BELIEVER**

How does Romans 8 reshape the modern believer's daily walk? Let me draw out the practical implications systematically.

---

## **1. WALKING BY THE SPIRIT IS THE NORMAL CHRISTIAN LIFE**

Paul does not present Spirit-led living as an advanced or elite experience for special saints. **It is the normal pattern for every believer.** The Spirit indwells every believer (v. 9). Every believer is led by the Spirit (v. 14). Every believer cries "Abba, Father" by the Spirit (v. 15). Every believer is helped by the Spirit in weakness (v. 26).

This means the modern believer is not waiting for some special experience to "begin" walking by the Spirit. **The Spirit indwells him now.** The question is whether he is **yielding to the Spirit's leading** or quenching it.

---

## **2. THE WAR AGAINST THE FLESH IS BY THE SPIRIT, NOT BY SELF-EFFORT**

This is the great practical lesson of Romans 7-8 together. Romans 7 demonstrates that self-effort cannot defeat the flesh. Romans 8 reveals that **the Spirit's empowering presence is what produces victory.**

The modern believer who struggles with besetting sin must learn this lesson. The pattern of attack is:
- *"Try harder."*
- *"Make more rules for yourself."*
- *"Resolve more firmly."*
- *"Discipline yourself more strictly."*

And the pattern ends, as Romans 7 ends, in *"O wretched man that I am!"*

**The Spirit-empowered pattern is different:**
- *"By the Spirit, I put to death the deeds of the body (v. 13)."*
- *"I yield my members as instruments of righteousness, depending on the Spirit's power (Rom 6:13)."*
- *"I walk by the Spirit, and shall not fulfill the lust of the flesh (Gal 5:16)."*
- *"I am strengthened with might by His Spirit in the inner man (Eph 3:16)."*

The Spirit does not replace human responsibility — we are still commanded to "put to death" — but He empowers what we are commanded to do. The believer works; the Spirit enables. Both are real; neither is absent.

---

## **3. THE FRUIT OF THE SPIRIT IS THE EVIDENCE OF SPIRIT-LIFE**

Paul will spell this out more fully in Galatians 5, but it is implicit throughout Romans 8. The Spirit's life in the believer produces the character of Christ — what Paul will call "the fruit of the Spirit."


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, long suffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, Meekness, temperance: against such there is no Law."*

The fruit is **the Spirit's** fruit — not the believer's manufactured product. **The believer's responsibility is to walk by the Spirit; the fruit emerges as the Spirit's work.**

This is liberating. The modern believer is not commanded to *manufacture* love, joy, peace, etc. He is commanded to **walk by the Spirit**, and these qualities will emerge from his life as fruit from a tree. The fruit cannot be forced; it grows from a healthy union with the indwelling Spirit.

Practically, the believer asks not *"How do I become more patient?"* but *"How do I walk more closely by the Spirit?"* — knowing that patience and the rest of the fruit will follow.

---

## **4. THE SPIRIT'S WITNESS TO SONSHIP IS THE GROUND OF CONFIDENCE**

Many modern believers struggle with assurance — *"Am I really a child of God?"* The Spirit Himself is the answer. He **bears witness with our spirit** that we are children of God (v. 16).

This is not produced by introspection or by examining the quality of our works. It is the Spirit's own witness — the deep, confident, settled sense that we belong to the Father, that we can call Him "Abba," that we are loved as beloved children.

When the believer doubts, he can ask: *"Spirit, bear witness with my spirit again that I am a child of God."* And the Spirit's gracious witness can rekindle assurance.

---

## **5. THE SPIRIT INTERCEDES WHEN THE BELIEVER CANNOT PRAY**

The believer faces moments when prayer is impossible — when grief overwhelms, when confusion clouds the mind, when temptation roars, when the future is opaque. In such moments, the believer can take comfort: **the Spirit intercedes with groanings beyond words** (v. 26).

The modern believer can rest in this. He need not produce eloquent prayers in his weakest moments. The Spirit prays for him in those very moments — and the Father, who searches hearts, knows the mind of the Spirit and answers according to His will.

---

## **6. EVERY CIRCUMSTANCE SERVES THE FATHER'S PURPOSE**

The Father is conforming the believer to the image of His Son. **Every circumstance — without exception — serves this purpose.** The good things prepare us; the hard things refine us; the joyful things equip us; the painful things deepen us. All things work together for our good (v. 28) — the good defined as conformity to Christ (v. 29).

This reshapes how the modern believer responds to circumstances:
- **Loss:** *"How is the Father using this to free me from idols and to make me more like the Son?"*
- **Trial:** *"What patience, faith, or hope is the Spirit growing in me through this?"*
- **Blessing:** *"How is the Father blessing me to enable me to serve and glorify Him?"*
- **Persecution:** *"How is this conforming me to the sufferings of Christ that I may share His glory?"*

The believer does not always know the specific purpose. But he knows the overall purpose: **conformity to the image of the Son.** And he can trust that the Father is sovereign over every circumstance to bring this purpose to fulfillment.

---

## **7. NOTHING CAN SEPARATE US — THEREFORE WE WALK BOLDLY**

The unbreakable love of God in Christ Jesus is the ground of the believer's bold, confident walk. He is not earning love; he is **walking in love that cannot be lost.** He is not striving for acceptance; he is **walking in acceptance that is already complete.** He is not fearing the future; he is **walking toward a glory that has been guaranteed.**

This produces a certain kind of believer:
- **Unhurried** — because no opportunity can be lost when our times are in the Father's hand.
- **Unanxious** — because no threat can separate us from the Father's love.
- **Unintimidated** — because if God is for us, who can be against us?
- **Unbound** — because we have no debt to the flesh and no fear of death.
- **Sustained in suffering** — because the present is not worthy to be compared with the coming glory.
- **Free in service** — because we serve from love, not from need.
- **Generous** — because all things are ours in Christ, and we have nothing to lose.

This is the practical fruit of Romans 8: **the free, confident, Spirit-empowered walk of the child of the Father.**

---

## **8. THE BELIEVER'S CONTRIBUTION: KEEP YIELDING, KEEP RECKONING, KEEP PRESENTING**

If we ask what the believer's responsibility actually is in light of all the Spirit does, the answer is consistent:

- **Reckon** (Rom 6:11) — keep agreeing with the truth of who you are in Christ.
- **Present** (Rom 6:13; 12:1) — keep yielding your members and your whole being to God.
- **Put to death** by the Spirit (Rom 8:13) — keep refusing the deeds of the body.
- **Walk by the Spirit** (Gal 5:16) — keep depending on the Spirit's leading.
- **Be filled with the Spirit** (Eph 5:18) — keep being filled, an ongoing reality.
- **Set the mind on things above** (Col 3:2) — keep your φρόνημα Spirit-oriented.

These are not earning. These are **the believer's faithful response to the Spirit's empowering presence.** The Spirit does His work in those who yield, reckon, present, and depend.

# **CROSS-WITNESSES — THE PATTERN ELSEWHERE**

The Romans 8 pattern of life in the Spirit is confirmed throughout the NT.

---

### **Galatians 5:16-25 — The Walk by the Spirit**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Then I say, Walk in the Spirit, and ye shall not fulfill the lusts of the flesh. For the flesh lusteth against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh: and these are contrary one to another..."*

The same theme: the believer walks by the Spirit, and in doing so, does not fulfill the lust of the flesh. **The Spirit and the flesh are in opposition; the believer's deliverance is in walking by the Spirit.**

---

### **Ephesians 3:16 — Strengthened by His Spirit in the Inner Man**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"That he might grant you according to the riches of his glory, that ye may be strengthened by his Spirit in the inner man,"*

The Spirit strengthens the **inner man** — the regenerated core of the believer's being — with the Father's mighty power. This is the practical mechanism of Spirit-empowered living.

---

### **Ephesians 5:18 — Be Filled With the Spirit**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess: but be fulfilled *(filled)* with the Spirit,"*

The verb **πληροῦσθε** is present passive — *"keep on being filled."* This is not a one-time experience; it is an ongoing yielding to the Spirit's filling. The contrast with drunkenness is telling: as wine fills and controls a drunken person, the Spirit fills and controls the yielded believer — but unto life, not destruction.

---

### **Philippians 2:12-13 — Working Out and God Working In**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"...so make an end of your own salvation with fear and trembling. For it is God which worketh in you, both the will and the deed, even of his good pleasure."*

The perfect statement of the cooperative relationship. **The believer works out** — kατεργάζεσθε — what is already true. **God works in** — ἐνεργῶν ἐν ὑμῖν — both the willing and the working. The believer's effort and God's empowering are not opposed; they are integrated. The believer's responsibility is **to work out what God works in.**

# **A CLOSING SYNTHESIS — THE WHOLE PATTERN OF LIFE IN THE SPIRIT**

Let me bring all the threads together.

## **The Architecture of the Spirit-Filled Life**

| Stage | Reality | Believer's Response |
|---|---|---|
| **Position** | In Christ Jesus, no condemnation (Rom 8:1) | Reckon yourself accepted in the Beloved |

## **The Vital Connection: Indicative → Spirit's Power → Believer's Response**

Romans 6 gave us **indicative → reckon → act.**

Romans 8 adds the missing element: **the Spirit's empowering presence as the means by which the believer's responsive action actually succeeds.**

The full pattern is now:
1. **The indicative reality** (what is true in Christ).
2. **The indwelling Spirit** (the Father's gift, given through the Son).
3. **The believer's reckoning** (agreeing with the indicative).
4. **The believer's yielding/walking/presenting** (responsive action).
5. **The Spirit's empowering work** (producing what the believer cannot produce by himself).
6. **The fruit of the Spirit** (life, peace, conformity to Christ — the evidence).

This is **the normal Christian life.** Not flawless. Not effortless. Not without struggle. But empowered by the Spirit, oriented toward the Father, conformed progressively to the Son, secure in the Father's unbreakable love.
