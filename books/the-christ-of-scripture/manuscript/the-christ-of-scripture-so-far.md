## **The Foundational Confession: The One God**
### **Deuteronomy 6:4 — The Shema**

> And these are the statutes and the judgments, which the Lord commanded to the sons of Israel in the wilderness as they were coming out from the land of Egypt. Hear, O Israel: The Lord our God is one Lord. — **Deuteronomy 6:4, LXX (NETS)**

This is the bedrock confession. **One Lord. One God.** Not three. Not a council. Not a composite *(a mixture of parts made into one)*. **One.**

---

### **Second witness — from the lips of the Messiah Himself:**

> Jesus answered him, The first of all the commandments is, Hear, Israel, The Lord our God is the only Lord. — **Mark 12:29, 1599 Geneva**

> **A HISTORICAL NOTE:** *Nicaea — the council called by Emperor Constantine in A.D. 325 — is where the “same substance” *(homoousios)* language for the Son was first pressed upon the churches; the fully-worded “three persons, one essence” creed followed at Constantinople, A.D. 381. That is the framework this study weighs against the Scripture itself.*

Jesus Himself — centuries after Moses, and yet centuries before Nicaea — affirms the Shema *(Hebrew, “hear” — the name of Israel’s one-God confession)* as the **first** of all commandments. He does not amend it. He does not qualify it. He confesses **one God**.

---

## **The Father Identified as That One God**
### **Witness #1 — Jesus to the Father:**

> And this is life eternal, that they know thee to be the only very God *(the only true God)*, and whom thou hast sent, Jesus Christ. — **John 17:3, 1599 Geneva**

The Messiah Himself addresses the Father as **τὸν μόνον ἀληθινὸν Θεόν** — "the only true God" — and distinguishes Himself, **Ἰησοῦν Χριστόν**, as the one *sent* by that God. The Sender and the Sent are not collapsed; the One God is the Father, and Jesus is His Christ.

---

### **Witness #2 — Paul to the Corinthians:**

> Yet unto us there is but one God, which is that Father, of whom are all things, and we in him: and one Lord Jesus Christ, by whom are all things, and we by him. — **1 Corinthians 8:6, 1599 Geneva**

Paul, the Apostle, places **ὁ Πατήρ** — the Father — as the **εἶς Θεός**, the *one* God. Jesus Christ is named **εἶς Κύριος** — *one Lord* — the agent **δι’ οὗ** *(through whom)*. The Father is the Source (**ἐξ οὗ** — *of whom*); the Son is the Mediator/Instrument *(the one standing between, the one through whom He works)* (**δι’ οὗ**).

---

### **Two witnesses established:** The One God of the Shema **is the Father.** Jesus the Christ is His Sent One, His Anointed, His Lord — distinct from the One God, and subordinate *(in ordered rank beneath Him)* as the agent to the Source.

---

## **Further Confirmation From the Mouth of the Son**

**John 20:17:**

> Jesus saith unto her, Touch me not: for I am not yet ascended to my Father: but go to my brethren, and say unto them, I ascend unto my Father, and to your Father, and to my God, and your God. — **John 20:17, 1599 Geneva**

Even after the resurrection, Jesus calls the Father **Θεόν μου** — *my God*. The Risen Christ has a God. That God is the Father.

**Ephesians 1:17 — Paul agrees:**

> That the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, that Father of glory, might give unto you the Spirit of wisdom, and revelation through the acknowledging of him *(truly knowing him)*, — **Ephesians 1:17, 1599 Geneva**

Two witnesses: **The Father is the God *of* Jesus Christ.**

---

## **Therefore, our refined foundation:**

1. There is **one God** — Deut. 6:4, confirmed by Jesus in Mark 12:29.
2. That one God is **the Father** — John 17:3, 1 Cor. 8:6.
3. Jesus the Christ is the **Son** of that God, the **sent** one, the **Lord** through whom the Father acts — distinct from, and having as His own God, the Father.
4. The "Spirit of God" in Genesis 1:2 must therefore be understood not as a separate "person" of a later-invented triune *(three-persons)* godhead *(see the Historical Note at Mark 12:29)*, but according to its plain Scriptural usage — which we will examine in due course as **the breath, power, and active presence of the One God Himself** (cf. Ps 32:6 LXX — *"By the word of the Lord the heavens were made firm, and by the breath of his mouth all their host"* — where the *word* and the *breath of His mouth* are instrumental *(the means He uses)* expressions of the one Lord).

---

## **Now — returning to Genesis 1:1 in light of this:**

> In the beginning God made the sky and the earth. — **Genesis 1:1, LXX (NETS)**

**ὁ Θεός** *(the God)* here is **the Father** — the one God of the Shema. He is the Creator. He is the Source (**ἐξ οὗ τὰ πάντα**, 1 Cor 8:6).

The question we will need to address carefully — without trinitarian assumptions — is the relationship of the **Son** to this creative act, given that 1 Cor 8:6 also says **δι’ οὗ τὰ πάντα** ("by whom are all things") of Jesus Christ, and that John 1:1-3 speaks of the **Λόγος**.

That investigation belongs to the **Son's** part of the study, and we will undertake it only *after* the Father — the One God — is firmly established as the foundation.

---

## **ISAIAH 44 — "Beside Me there is no God"**

Isaiah 44-46 contains some of the most thunderous, uncompromising declarations of monotheism *(the teaching that there is one God)* in all of Scripture. The One God speaks for Himself here — in the first person — and leaves no room for any rival, partner, council, or co-equal.

### **Isaiah 44:6**

> Thus says God, the king of Israel, who delivered him, God Sabaoth *(Hebrew, "of the armies" — the Lord of hosts)*: I am first, and I am after these things; besides me there is no god. — **Isaiah 44:6, LXX (NETS)**

**Observation:** The speaker — **ὁ Θεός**, the King of Israel, the Redeemer, the Lord of Hosts — declares: **"I am first, and I am after these things; besides me there is no god."** Not "no other God like Me," not "no God of equal rank" — **οὐκ ἔστιν Θεός** *(a god there is not)*. There is no God. Full stop.

---

### **Isaiah 44:8**

> Do not cover yourselves; did you not give ear *(listen)* from the beginning, and I declared it to you? You are witnesses whether there is a god *(any god)* besides me, and they were not formerly. — **Isaiah 44:8, LXX (NETS)**

**Observation:** The God of Israel calls His people as **μάρτυρες** — *witnesses* — to testify whether any other god exists. His own answer: **οὐκ ἦσαν** — *they were not; they are not; they shall not be.*

---

### **Isaiah 44:24**

> Thus says the Lord, who redeems you, who forms you from the womb: I am the Lord, who accomplishes all things; I alone stretched out heaven, and I bolstered *(made firm)* earth. Who else? — **Isaiah 44:24, LXX (NETS)**

**Observation of supreme importance for our study:**

The Lord declares: **ἐξέτεινα τὸν οὐρανὸν μόνος** — *"I stretched out the heaven ALONE."* And **ἐστερέωσα τὴν γῆν** — *"I made the earth firm"* (note the **στερεόω** root — connecting to **στερέωμα**, the firmament of Gen 1:6; this will matter when cosmology becomes relevant). And then the challenge: **τίς ἕτερος;** — *"Who else?"*

This single verse forbids any reading of Genesis 1:1 that places a second divine person beside the Father as co-creator. **The Father stretched out the heavens ALONE.** When we come to examine the Son's role and the meaning of John 1:3 / Col 1:16 / 1 Cor 8:6 ("through Him"), we must reconcile those texts with **this** declaration — not the other way around. **God speaks first; men interpret after.**

---

## **ISAIAH 45 — "I am the Lord, and there is none else"**

### **Isaiah 45:5-6**

> because I am the Lord God, and there is no other god besides me, and you did not know me, — **Isaiah 45:5, LXX (NETS)**

> so that they who are from the rising of the sun and from its going down may know that there is no one besides me; I am the Lord God, and there is no other. — **Isaiah 45:6, LXX (NETS)**

**Observation:** Five times in two verses the singularity is hammered: **"I am the Lord, and there is none else."** This is not the language of a tri-personal *(three persons in one being)* being veiling part of His identity. This is plain, unambiguous, first-person testimony of **one Being, one God**.

---

### **Isaiah 45:18**

> Thus says the Lord, who made heaven— this is the God who displayed the earth and made it; he himself marked its limits; he did not make it to be empty but to be inhabited: I am, and there is no other. — **Isaiah 45:18, LXX (NETS)**

**Observation:** **οὗτος ὁ Θεός** — *"This is the God"* — the One who made the heaven, formed the earth, prepared it, created it for habitation. **Ἐγώ εἰμι, καὶ οὐκ ἔστιν ἔτι** — *"I AM, and there is none else."*

Note the **Ἐγώ εἰμι** — the same self-disclosure formula that runs through Isaiah and which Jesus will later use of Himself (John 8:58). We will mark this and return to it: the question is *how* the Son can speak with the voice of the One God, when the One God has declared that there is no other.

---

### **Isaiah 45:21-22**

> If they will declare it, let them draw near so that they may know together who made from the beginning these things that are to be heard. Then it was declared to you, I am God, and there is no other besides me; there is no righteous one or savior except me. — **Isaiah 45:21, LXX (NETS)**

> Turn to me, and you shall be saved, you who are from the end of the earth! I am God, and there is no other. — **Isaiah 45:22, LXX (NETS)**

**Observation:** **δίκαιος καὶ σωτήρ οὐκ ἔστιν παρέξ ἐμοῦ** — *"There is no righteous one and Saviour besides Me."* The Father claims the title **σωτήρ** *(Saviour)* for Himself, exclusively. This too will require careful handling when we treat the Son, who is also called Saviour in the New Testament. The resolution — as we shall see — is *not* by collapsing them into one Being, but by recognizing the Son as the Father's appointed agent *(the one He sends and works through)* of salvation, executing the will of the One Saviour.

---

### **Isaiah 45:23**

> By myself I swear, "Verily *(truly)* righteousness shall go forth from my mouth; my words shall not be turned back, because to me every knee shall bow and every tongue shall acknowledge God, — **Isaiah 45:23, LXX (NETS)**

**Observation of great importance for the Son's study:** The Father swears **by Himself** that **to Him** every knee shall bow. Paul cites this exact text in Philippians 2:10-11 of Jesus Christ — *"That at the Name of Jesus should every knee bow, both of things in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth; And that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is the Lord, unto the glory of God the Father."* (1599 Geneva) — and the bowing is **εἰς δόξαν Θεοῦ Πατρός** — *"to the glory of God the Father."* The Son receives homage **as the Father's appointed Lord**, and the result is the Father's glory. The monotheism is preserved.

---

## **ISAIAH 46 — "I am God, and there is none like Me"**

### **Isaiah 46:9**

> and remember the former things of old, because I am God, and there is no other besides me, — **Isaiah 46:9, LXX (NETS)**

### **Isaiah 46:5**

> To whom have you likened me? See, act with cunning *(plot craftily)*, you who are going astray! — **Isaiah 46:5, LXX (NETS)**

**Observation:** The God of Israel asks: **τίνι με ὡμοιώσατε;** — *"To whom have you likened Me?"* The implicit answer: *no one.* He is incomparable. Any theology that posits another Person *equal* in being to the Father — co-eternal, co-equal, of the same substance *(of one being)* — has fallen under this rebuke.

---

## **SUMMARY OF THE FOUNDATION**

From Isaiah 44-46, the One God declares — in His own voice, repeatedly, emphatically, and without qualification:

| Declaration | Reference |
|---|---|
| "I am the first and the last; besides Me there is no God." | 44:6 |
| "Is there any God besides Me? There is none." | 44:8 |
| "I stretched out the heavens ALONE." | 44:24 |
| "I am the Lord, and there is none else; no God besides Me." | 45:5-6 |
| "I AM, and there is none else." | 45:18 |
| "There is no righteous one and Saviour besides Me." | 45:21 |
| "I have sworn by Myself... to Me every knee shall bow." | 45:23 |
| "I am God, and there is none like Me." | 46:9 |
| "To whom will you liken Me?" | 46:5 |

## **The Foundation Established**

1. **The One God is numerically one Being** *(one in number* — not a family, not a committee)* — not a council, not a triad *(a group of three)*, not a multiplicity of persons sharing essence *(one shared being)*.
2. **He created the heavens and the earth ALONE** (44:24) — this stands as the controlling text for Genesis 1:1.
3. **He alone is Saviour** (45:21).
4. **To Him alone every knee shall bow** (45:23).
5. **He is incomparable** — no being is *like* Him (46:5, 9).

This is the God whom Jesus called **τὸν μόνον ἀληθινὸν Θεόν** — *the only true God* (John 17:3) — and whom Jesus called **τὸν Πατέρα μου καὶ Θεόν μου** — *my Father and my God* (John 20:17).

**The Father is the One God of Isaiah 44-46.** This is our foundation. Any study of the Son must be built upon — and reconciled with — this declaration, never against it.

## **A Question Now Properly Framed**

With this foundation firm, we may now ask the right question — the question that drives the rest of our study:

> **Who, then, is the Son of this One God? In what sense is He called "the Word," "the Image," "the Firstborn," "the Lord through whom are all things" — without violating the absolute singularity of the Father?**

We now turn to the Old Testament's earliest posits of the Son — the Father's **μονογενής** *(only-begotten)* — built strictly upon the foundation we have laid: **the Father is the One God, and there is none beside Him.**

We will proceed chronologically as the prophecies are given in the canonical order, beginning at the earliest.

---

## **POSIT #1 — Genesis 3:15: The Seed of the Woman**

> And I will put enmity between you and between the woman and between your offspring and between her offspring; he will watch your head, and you will watch his heel." — **Genesis 3:15, LXX (NETS)**

The Lexham English Septuagint keeps the older English for **σπέρμα** *(seed)* — and carries the same masculine singular:

> "I will place enmity between you and the woman, and between your seed and her seed; he will watch your head carefully, and you will watch his heel carefully." — **Genesis 3:15, LES**

### Observations

**(1)** The pronoun **αὐτός** is masculine singular — "**He**, He Himself" — not *it*, not *they*. The LXX is grammatically remarkable here: the antecedent **σπέρμα** (*seed*) is **neuter**, which would normally demand **αὐτό**. Yet the translators of the LXX (3rd-2nd century BC, *centuries before any Christian influence*) deliberately rendered **αὐτός** — masculine — pointing to a singular **male person** as the Seed.

> **A HISTORICAL NOTE:** *The Greek Old Testament was rendered in the third through second centuries before Christ, for Greek-speaking Jews — long before any Christian influence could have shaped its words.*

This is the first prophetic posit of the coming **One** — a single human Person, born of a woman, who will crush the head of the serpent *(the Hebrew wording as Geneva renders it; the LXX seat above reads, "he will watch your head")*.

**(2)** "**The seed of the woman**" — not of the man. In the entire patriarchal framework of Scripture, seed is reckoned through the father. This phrase is anomalous — it anticipates a birth not according to the line of a human father. The two witnesses for this anomaly being fulfilled:

**Isaiah 7:14 (LXX):**

> Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign. Look, the virgin shall be with child and bear a son, and you shall name him Emmanouel *(Immanuel)*. — **Isaiah 7:14, LXX (NETS)**

The LXX explicitly says **ἡ παρθένος** — *the virgin* — not merely *a young woman*. This is **Witness #1** that the "seed of the woman" is a virgin-born Son.

**Galatians 4:4:**

> But when the fulness of time was come, God sent forth his Son made of a woman, and made under the Law, — **Galatians 4:4, 1599 Geneva**

**Witness #2:** Paul confirms — **γενόμενον ἐκ γυναικός** — "made of a woman" — the very language of Genesis 3:15.

---

### What Genesis 3:15 establishes about the Son:
- He is a **single, masculine Person** (αὐτός).
- He is **born of a woman** without a human father in view.
- He is **sent by the One God** as the conqueror of the serpent.
- He is, at this earliest point, a **promised man** — the prophecy says nothing yet of pre-existence or divine status. We are at the *beginning* of the revelation; we will not retroject later disclosures into this verse.

---

## **POSIT #2 — Genesis 22: The Beloved Son Offered by the Father**

This is the first time Scripture uses the word **ἀγαπητός** *(beloved — the father's cherished one)* — and it uses it of a **father offering his only son**.

**Genesis 22:2 (LXX):**

> And he said, "Take your beloved son Isaak *(Isaac)*, whom you love, and go into the high land, and offer him as a whole burnt offering *(burned entire)* on one of the mountains, whichever I mention to you." — **Genesis 22:2, LXX (NETS)**

**Genesis 22:8 (LXX):**

> And Abraam said, "God will see to a sheep as a whole burnt offering for himself, child." — **Genesis 22:8, LXX (NETS)**

### Observations

**(1) The Father-Son typology is unmistakable:**
- A father offering his **beloved only son** (**τὸν υἱόν σου τὸν ἀγαπητόν**).
- On a mountain (later called Moriah — the same range as Jerusalem, where the Christ will be slain).
- The son **carries the wood of his own sacrifice** (v. 6) — as Christ carried His own cross.
- The father declares *"God will see to a sheep as a whole burnt offering for himself" *(NETS)*, which Brenton prints, *"God will provide himself a sheep for a whole-burnt-offering, my son"* — the very title John the Baptist will give Jesus: **"ἴδε ὁ ἀμνὸς τοῦ Θεοῦ"** *(behold, the Lamb of God)* (John 1:29).

**(2) The two witnesses confirming Genesis 22 as a posit of the Son:**

The author of Hebrews calls Isaac **τὸν μονογενῆ** — *the only-begotten* — the very title given to Jesus:

> By faith Abraham offered up Isaac, when he was tried, and he that had received the promises, offered his only begotten son. (To whom it was said, In Isaac shall thy seed be called.) For he considered that God was able to raise him up even from the dead: from whence he received him also after a sort. — **Hebrews 11:17-19, 1599 Geneva**

**Witness #2 — the Voice from heaven at the Jordan and at the Mount of Transfiguration:**

> And lo, a voice came from heaven, saying, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. — **Matthew 3:17, 1599 Geneva**

The Father's own designation of Jesus — **ὁ Υἱός μου ὁ ἀγαπητός** — *"My beloved Son"* — uses the identical phrasing as Genesis 22:2 LXX. **Two witnesses established.**

---

### What Genesis 22 adds to our portrait of the Son:

- He is the **Father's beloved only-begotten**.
- He is **offered up by the Father** as a sacrifice.
- The Father Himself **provides the lamb**.
- The Son is figuratively **received back from the dead** (Heb 11:19) — a typological resurrection.

---

## **POSIT #3 — Genesis 49:10: The Scepter and Shiloh**

The first prophecy of the Son's **royalty** and the **gathering of the nations** to Him.

**Genesis 49:10 (LXX):**

> A ruler shall not be wanting *(shall not fail)* from Ioudas *(Judah)* and a leader from his thighs *(his own descendants)* until the things stored up for him come, and he is the expectation of nations. — **Genesis 49:10, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observations

**(1)** The LXX reading is profound: **"ἕως ἂν ἔλθῃ τὰ ἀποκείμενα αὐτῷ"** — *"until there come the things laid up / reserved for Him."* The LXX translators understood this messianically — a *Person* for whom all things are reserved.

**(2)** **"αὐτὸς προσδοκία ἐθνῶν"** — *"HE shall be the expectation of nations."* Not Israel only — **the nations**. The Son of the One God is to be the focal hope of all peoples.

---

### Two witnesses for this posit:

**Haggai 2:7 (LXX):**

> …and I will shake all the nations, and the choice things of all the nations shall come, and I will fill this house with splendor, says the Lord Almighty. — **Haggaios 2:7, LXX (NETS)**

The clause as it rings in the older English — *"the desire of all nations shall come, and I will fill this house with glory"* — is the 1599 Geneva's rendering of this very verse (*Geneva 2:8; the Greek verse is 2:7*).

**Witness #1:** The "Desire/Expectation of the nations" theme is confirmed.

**Revelation 5:5:**

> And one of the Elders said unto me, Weep not: behold, that Lion which is of the tribe of Judah, that root of David, hath obtained to open the book, and to loose the seven seals thereof. — **Revelation 5:5, 1599 Geneva**

**Witness #2:** The promised Ruler of Judah is identified as the slain and risen Lamb in the Apocalypse.

---

### What Genesis 49:10 adds:

- The Son is from the tribe of **Judah**.
- He is a **Ruler** (ἄρχων), holding the scepter.
- He is the **expectation of the nations** — universal in scope.

---

## **POSIT #4 — Numbers 24:17: The Star Out of Jacob**

**Numbers 24:17 (LXX):**

> I will point to him, and not now; I deem him happy *(I count him blessed)*, but he is not at hand. A star shall dawn out of Iakob *(Jacob)*, and a person shall rise up out of Israel, and he shall crush the chiefs of Moab, and he shall plunder all Seth's sons. — **Numbers 24:17, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observations

**(1)** **ἀνατελεῖ ἄστρον** — *"a Star shall rise."* The Son is figured as a rising celestial light from Jacob's line.

**(2)** **ἀναστήσεται ἄνθρωπος ἐξ Ἰσραήλ** — *"a person shall rise up out of Israel."* The Hebrew has "scepter"; the LXX clarifies: **a person** *(a Man)*. The Son is here unambiguously declared to be a *human being* arising from Israel.

---

### Two witnesses:

**Matthew 2:2 (Geneva):**

> Saying, Where is that King of the Jews that is born? for we have seen his star in the East, and are come to worship him. — **Matthew 2:2, 1599 Geneva**

**Witness #1:** The Magi — who had access to Balaam's prophecy through Eastern preservation — identify the literal star with the birth of the King.

**Revelation 22:16:**

> I Jesus have sent mine Angel, to testify unto you these things in the Churches: I am the root and the generation of David, and the bright morning star. — **Revelation 22:16, 1599 Geneva**

**Witness #2:** Jesus Himself, in the Apocalypse, claims the title **ὁ ἀστὴρ ὁ λαμπρὸς ὁ πρωϊνός** — *the bright morning Star.* The earliest prophecy and the concluding prophecy meet in His person.

---

### What Numbers 24:17 adds:

- The Son is the **Star** that rises out of Jacob.
- He is a **Man** (**ἄνθρωπος**) — true and complete humanity.
- He is the conqueror of the enemies of God's people.

---

## **SUMMARY: The Earliest Posits of the Son**

From these four foundational texts — given centuries before any council, any creed, any patristic speculation — we have, by *plain reading* and *two-or-more witnesses*:

| Text | What is revealed of the Son |
|---|---|
| **Gen 3:15** | A single Man, Seed of the woman, crusher of the serpent |
| **Gen 22** | The Beloved Only-Begotten Son, offered by the Father, the Lamb God provides |
| **Gen 49:10** | The Ruler from Judah, expectation of the nations |
| **Num 24:17** | The Star and Man arising from Israel |

**Notice carefully what these earliest prophecies do *not* say:** They do not call the Son "God." They do not say He pre-existed. They do not place Him at creation. They speak of Him as a **Man** — beloved, only-begotten, born of a woman, of the tribe of Judah, rising from Israel.

This is the **proper foundation**. Whatever further glories Scripture reveals about the Son must be built upon **this** foundation — not in violation of the Father's exclusive deity declared in Isaiah 44-46, but in the manner Scripture itself unfolds.

Psalm 2 and Psalm 110 (LXX 109) are the most quoted Old Testament texts in the New Testament concerning the Son. They are the keystone of the apostolic preaching about who Jesus is and what office He holds in relation to the One God, the Father.

---


## **PSALM 2 (LXX) — The Anointed Son Begotten by the Father**

### **Psalm 2:1-2 (LXX):**

> Why did nations grow insolent, and peoples contemplate vain things? The kings of the earth stood side by side, and the rulers gathered together, against the Lord and against his anointed. — **Psalm 2:1-2, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observation of foundational importance

The Psalm immediately distinguishes **two parties** on the divine side:
- **ὁ Κύριος** — *the Lord* (the Father, the One God of Isaiah 44-46)
- **ὁ Χριστὸς αὐτοῦ** — ***His* Anointed** — the Christ who belongs *to* the Lord

The Anointed is **not** the Lord; He belongs to the Lord. He is the Lord's *Christ*. The genitive **αὐτοῦ** ("of Him / His") is decisive — it forbids identifying the Christ as the same Being as the Lord who anoints Him.

This is the very pattern Peter preaches at Pentecost:

**Acts 2:36 (Geneva):**

> Therefore, let all the house of Israel know for a surety, that God hath made him both Lord, and Christ, this Jesus, I say, whom ye have crucified. — **Acts 2:36, 1599 Geneva**

Peter declares that **God MADE Him** (**ἐποίησεν**) both Lord and Christ. The Father — the One God — is the One who *makes* Jesus Lord and Christ. Jesus is not the One making; He is the One *being made* into His offices by the Father.

---

### **Psalm 2:6-7 (LXX):**

> "But I was established king by him, on Sion *(Zion)*, his holy mountain, by proclaiming the Lord’s ordinance: The Lord said to me, "My son you are; today I have begotten you." — **Psalm 2:6-7, LXX (NETS)**

### Observations of the utmost weight

**(1)** **κατεστάθην βασιλεὺς ὑπ᾽ αὐτοῦ** — *"I was established as king BY HIM."* The Son does not crown Himself; He is **made** king **by the Father**. This is again the language of dependence and appointment, not co-equality.

**(2)** **Υἱός μου εἶ σύ, ἐγὼ σήμερον γεγέννηκά σε** — *"My Son art Thou; I, today, have begotten Thee."*

This is the **first explicit declaration in the Psalter** of the Father-Son relation, and it tells us two crucial things:

**(a)** The Son is **begotten** (**γεγέννηκα**) — He has an origin in the Father. This is the very meaning of **μονογενής** (only-begotten) — *singularly brought forth from the Father.*

**(b)** This begetting is fixed to a **day** — **σήμερον** (*today*) — a definite moment.

---

### When is "today"? — The two witnesses of the Apostles:

**Acts 13:32-33 (Geneva):**

> And we declare unto you, that touching the promise made unto the fathers, God hath fulfilled it unto us their children, in that he raised vp Jesus, even as it is written in the second Psalm, Thou art my Son: this day have I begotten thee. — **Acts 13:32-33, 1599 Geneva**

**Paul, by inspiration, locates the "today" of Psalm 2:7 at the *resurrection* of Jesus.** The begetting of the Son in the sense of Psalm 2 is His being **raised from the dead** and installed as King.

**Witness #2 — Romans 1:3-4 (Geneva):**

> Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord (which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh, And declared mightily to be the Son of God, touching the Spirit of sanctification by the resurrection from the dead) — **Romans 1:3-4, 1599 Geneva**

**Witness #2 confirmed:** Jesus was **ὁρισθέντος Υἱοῦ Θεοῦ** — *"declared/appointed Son of God in power"* — **ἐξ ἀναστάσεως νεκρῶν** — *"by the resurrection from the dead."*

**The two witnesses agree:** The "today I have begotten Thee" of Psalm 2:7 is **the resurrection day** — the day the Father raised His Christ and installed Him as King on Zion.

---

### **Psalm 2:8 (LXX):**

> Ask of me, and I will give you nations as your heritage, and as your possession the ends of the earth. — **Psalm 2:8, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observation

The Son **asks**; the Father **gives**. This is the consistent pattern: the Father is the Source (**ἐξ οὗ**, 1 Cor 8:6), the Son is the Recipient and Agent. The nations are **given** to the Son as an inheritance — He does not possess them by inherent right of independent deity; they are **granted** to Him by the Father.

This is identical to the language of:

**Matthew 28:18 (Geneva):**

> And Jesus came, and spake unto them, saying, All power is given unto me, in heaven, and in earth. — **Matthew 28:18, 1599 Geneva**

**ἐδόθη** — *"was given."* By whom? By the Father. The Son receives universal authority as a **gift** from the One God.

---

### **Psalm 2:12 (LXX):**

> Seize upon instruction *(grasp discipline)*, lest the Lord be angry, and you will perish from the righteous way, when his anger quickly blazes out. Happy are all who trust in him. — **Psalm 2:12, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Note on the textual difference

The LXX reads **δράξασθε παιδείας** — *"lay hold of instruction/discipline"* — where the Hebrew has *"kiss the Son"* (the wording of the 1599 Geneva and the English tradition). This is a notable variant. Either reading, the climactic line is the same: **μακάριοι πάντες οἱ πεποιθότες ἐπ᾽ αὐτῷ** — *"Blessed are all who trust in Him."* Trust is to be placed in the Anointed Son **whom the Father has set up** — yet this trust does not violate Isaiah 45:22 ("Look unto Me, and be ye saved") because the Son is the Father's appointed channel of salvation; trusting the Son is trusting the Father's provision.

---

## **What Psalm 2 establishes about the Son:**

1. He is **distinct from** the Lord — He is *the Lord's* Christ (v. 2).
2. He is **installed** as King by the Father (v. 6).
3. He is **begotten** of the Father on a definite day — fulfilled at His **resurrection** (v. 7; Acts 13:33; Rom 1:4).
4. He **asks** and **receives** the nations as a gift from the Father (v. 8).
5. He is to be **trusted** — as the Father's appointed Anointed (v. 12).

---

## **PSALM 109 LXX (110 MT) — The Lord at the Right Hand**

This is the **single most-cited Old Testament passage in the New Testament.** Jesus Himself quotes it; Peter quotes it at Pentecost; the author of Hebrews builds his entire theology of Christ's priesthood upon it.

### **Psalm 109:1 LXX (110:1 MT):**

> The Lord said to my lord, "Sit on my right *(at my right hand)* until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet." — **Psalm 109:1, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observation of supreme importance

**Two distinct figures are named:**
- **ὁ Κύριος** (capital — *the LORD*, YHWH, the Father, the One God of Isaiah 44-46)
- **τῷ κυρίῳ μου** (lowercase — *my lord*, a distinct personage who is David's Lord)

David, by the Spirit, hears the One God speaking *to another* — to David's own Lord — and inviting Him to sit at His right hand.

**This is the decisive testimony for the structure of our entire study:** The Son is **at the right hand of** the Father — meaning **He is not the Father, and the Father is not the Son**. The right hand is the place of supreme honor *granted by* the One enthroned in the center. The Son occupies the right hand; the Father occupies the throne.

---

### Jesus' own use of this Psalm — the keystone witness:

**Matthew 22:41-45 (Geneva):**

> While the Pharisees were gathered together, Jesus asked them, Saying, What think ye of Christ? whose son is he? They said unto him, Davids. He said unto them, How then doth David in spirit call him Lord, saying, The Lord said to my Lord, Sit at my right hand, till I make thine enemies thy footstool? If then David call him Lord, how is he his son? — **Matthew 22:41-45, 1599 Geneva**

Jesus' point: the Messiah is **both** David's son (according to the flesh) **and** David's Lord (according to His exalted office at the Father's right hand). The Christ is *more than* a mere descendant — He is the One *seated at the right hand of the One God.* Yet — crucially — Jesus does *not* say He is the Father, nor that He is the One who spoke. He is the One *spoken to.*

---

### Witness #2 — Peter at Pentecost:

**Acts 2:34-36 (Geneva):**

> For David is not ascended into heaven, but he saith, The Lord said to my Lord, Sit at my right hand, Until I make thine enemies thy footstool. Therefore, let all the house of Israel know for a surety, that God hath made him both Lord, and Christ, this Jesus, I say, whom ye have crucified. — **Acts 2:34-36, 1599 Geneva**

Peter explicitly identifies Psalm 110:1 as having been **fulfilled at the ascension of Jesus** — when **ὁ Θεός** (the Father) **MADE** Jesus both Lord and Christ. The Father is the actor; Jesus is the One acted upon, exalted, and seated.

**Two witnesses established:** Jesus and Peter agree — Psalm 110:1 reveals the Son seated at the Father's right hand, distinct from the Father, exalted *by* the Father.

---

### **Psalm 109:3 LXX (110:3 MT):**

> With you is rule *(dominion)* on a day of your power among the splendors of the holy ones. From the womb, before Morning-star *(the morning star)*, I brought you forth. — **Psalm 109:3, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observation — a critical LXX reading

The LXX preserves a remarkable line absent from the Hebrew (which the 1599 Geneva follows, reading *"Thy people shall come willingly at the time of assembling thine army in holy beauty: the youth of thy womb shall be as the morning dew"*): here the LXX reads, **ἐκ γαστρὸς πρὸ ἑωσφόρου ἐγέννησά σε** — *"From the womb, before the morning star."* NETS prints the Greek literally, *"I brought you forth"*; Brenton's older English renders it, *"before the morning star I have begotten thee"* *(LXX 1851)* — **ἐγέννησά σε** means *I begot you; I fathered you*.

This is the Father speaking again, as in Psalm 2:7. Here a **prior begetting** is in view — **πρὸ ἑωσφόρου** — *"before the morning star"* — that is, before the created luminaries of Genesis 1:14-16.

This is the **second witness** (alongside Psalm 2:7) to the *begetting* of the Son — but here the LXX places it **before** the cosmic order, while Psalm 2:7 places it on the day of resurrection. Scripture itself is presenting **two distinct begettings** — or more carefully, a foreordained begetting from before time and its historical fulfillment in the resurrection. We will need to examine this carefully later, particularly with Proverbs 8 and Micah 5:2.

For now, we simply note: **the Son is begotten of the Father** — never the reverse, never the Father begotten of the Son, and never a "co-eternal" begetting where the Son has no origin in the Father. The Father is *Source*; the Son is *Begotten*.

---

### **Psalm 109:4 LXX (110:4 MT):**

> The Lord swore and will not change his mind, "You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchisedek *(Melchizedek)*." — **Psalm 109:4, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observation

The Father swears an oath that the Son shall be **ἱερεὺς εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα** — *Priest forever* — after the order of Melchizedek (Gen 14). The Son is not only **King** at the right hand (vv. 1-3) — He is also **Priest** by the Father's appointment.

**Witness #1 — Hebrews 5:5-6:**

> So likewise Christ took not to himself this honor, to be made the high Priest, but he that said unto him, Thou art my Son, this day begat I thee, gave it him. As he also in another place speaketh, Thou art a Priest for ever, after ye order of Melchisedek. — **Hebrews 5:5-6, 1599 Geneva**

The author of Hebrews binds Psalm 2:7 and Psalm 110:4 together — the Son does **not glorify Himself**; the Father glorifies Him with both Sonship and Priesthood. **The Son receives every office He holds *from* the Father.**

**Witness #2 — Hebrews 7:21:**

> But this is made with an oath by him that said unto him, The Lord hath sworn, and will not repent, Thou art a Priest for ever, after the order of Melchisedek) — **Hebrews 7:21, 1599 Geneva**

**Two witnesses agree:** The Father is the One who **swears** and **appoints**; the Son is the One who **receives** the eternal priesthood by oath.

---

### **Psalm 109:5 LXX (110:5 MT):**

> The Lord at your right *(your right hand)* shattered kings on a day of his wrath. — **Psalm 109:5, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observation — a remarkable inversion

In v. 1 the Son sits at the Father's right hand. In v. 5 *the Lord is at* **the Son's** *right hand*. This is **not** a contradiction; it is the picture of the **Father and the Son operating in perfect coordination** — wherever the Son acts in judgment, the Father stands with Him; wherever the Father is enthroned, the Son sits at His right.

The Father and Son are **two distinct figures**, working together — never collapsed into one Person, never elevated to two Gods. **One God, the Father; one Lord, His Christ — at one another's right hand.**

---

## **SUMMARY: Psalms 2 and 109/110 Together**

| Truth Established | Psalm 2 | Psalm 109/110 |
|---|---|---|
| Two distinct figures: the Lord and His Anointed/Lord | v. 2 | v. 1 |
| The Father appoints, the Son is appointed | v. 6 | vv. 1, 4 |
| The Son is **begotten** of the Father | v. 7 | v. 3 (LXX) |
| The Son **receives** authority/inheritance | v. 8 | v. 1 |
| The Father swears an oath concerning the Son | — | v. 4 |
| The Son is enthroned at the right hand | (v. 6 implied) | v. 1 |
| Fulfilled in Jesus' **resurrection and ascension** | Acts 13:33 | Acts 2:34-36 |

---

## **The Doctrinal Foundation Now Standing**

We may now state, by two-or-more witnesses from Scripture itself, what the Son is:

1. **He is begotten of the Father** — He has an origin in the Father; the Father has no origin in Him.
2. **He is appointed, made, and given** — every title, office, and authority He holds is **conferred upon Him by the Father**.
3. **He is distinct from the Father** — David's Lord, but not the LORD; the Lord's Christ, but not the Lord Himself.
4. **He is seated at the Father's right hand** — supreme in honor, but second in order; the Father remains enthroned in the center.
5. **He is both King (Ps 2) and Priest (Ps 110)** — combining the offices that were separated in Israel under David and Aaron.
6. **He is the resurrected One** — His installation as Son-in-power, Lord, and Christ is bound to His being **raised from the dead** by the Father.

This is the apostolic gospel, and it preserves intact the Shema and the declarations of Isaiah 44-46: **the Father is the One God; Jesus is His Christ, His Lord, His Son, His Priest, His King — all by the Father's appointment.**

---


## **A QUESTION ARISING — Was the Son Brought Forth Before the World Was?**

Psalm 109:3 LXX has opened the question of the Son's **pre-temporal begetting** — *"from the womb, before the morning star."* Two passages must now be examined to see whether and how Scripture testifies to the Son's existence *before* His historical birth in Bethlehem and His being declared Son in power at the resurrection: **Proverbs 8**, where **Wisdom** speaks in the first person, and **Micah 5:2** — *"whose goings forth have been from of old."*

We will proceed carefully — letting the text speak, and not retrojecting later "eternal generation" formulations.

---

## **PROVERBS 8 — Wisdom Brought Forth Before the Foundation of the World**

The chapter is spoken in the **first person** by **Σοφία** — *Wisdom* — personified throughout Proverbs 1-9. The question we must ask: **Is this Wisdom the Son? And if so, what does the text say about His origin?**

We will not assume the identification; we will let Scripture establish it by two witnesses.

---

### **Proverbs 8:22-25 (LXX):**

> The Lord created me as the beginning of his ways, for the sake of his works. Before the present age he founded me, in the beginning. Before he made the earth and before he made the depths, before he brought forth the springs of the waters, before the mountains were established and before all the hills, he begets me. — **Proverbs 8:22-25, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observations of decisive importance

**(1)** The LXX reads **Κύριος ἔκτισέν με** — *"The Lord created/founded me."* The verb is **κτίζω**. The Geneva follows the Hebrew *qanah*, which can mean *possess, acquire, or beget.* This is the verse over which the great Arian controversy raged in the 4th century — and it is one of the texts the post-Nicene party labored most strenuously to suppress or reinterpret, precisely because the LXX (which Jesus and the Apostles used — per our maxims) reads **ἔκτισέν με**.

**Let God be true.** The LXX says: **The Lord brought-forth/founded Wisdom as the beginning (ἀρχήν) of His ways, for His works.**

**(2)** **πρὸ τοῦ αἰῶνος ἐθεμελίωσέν με ἐν ἀρχῇ** — *"Before the age He established me in the beginning."* Wisdom is founded by the Lord **before time itself**, before the **αἰών**.

**(3)** **πρὸ δὲ πάντων βουνῶν γεννᾷ με** — *"Before all hills He BEGETS me."* The verb **γεννάω** — *to beget* — is the same root as **γεγέννηκά σε** of Psalm 2:7 and **ἐγέννησά σε** of Psalm 109:3 LXX. **Three witnesses now agree on the same vocabulary**: the Son is **begotten** of the Father.

---

### **Proverbs 8:27, 30 (LXX):**

> When he prepared the sky *(the heavens)*, I was present with him, and when he marked out his own throne on the winds. … I was beside him, fitting together; it is I who was the one in whom he took delight. And each day I was glad in his presence at every moment, — **Proverbs 8:27, 30, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observations

**(1)** **συμπαρήμην αὐτῷ** — *"I was present alongside Him."* Wisdom is **with** the Lord at the creation. This is precisely the language John 1:1 will echo: **ὁ Λόγος ἦν πρὸς τὸν Θεόν** — *"the Word was with God."*

**(2)** **ἤμην παρ᾽ αὐτῷ ἁρμόζουσα** — *"I was beside Him, fitting [things] together / harmonizing."* The Son, as Wisdom, is the Father's *Agent of arrangement* in creation. This now begins to reconcile Isaiah 44:24 ("I stretched out the heavens **alone**") with 1 Cor 8:6 ("through whom are all things"): **the Father alone is the Source; the Son is His Wisdom by whom He fits the works together.** The Father does it; He does it *by* His Wisdom — His Wisdom is not a second God, but His own Begotten One acting as His instrument.

**(3)** **καθ᾽ ἡμέραν δὲ εὐφραινόμην ἐν προσώπῳ αὐτοῦ** — *"daily I was rejoicing in His face/presence."* The mutual delight between the Father and His Begotten One **before** the world was made.

---

### Is "Wisdom" in Proverbs 8 the Son? — The two witnesses:

**Witness #1 — 1 Corinthians 1:24, 30 (Geneva):**

> But unto them which are called, both of the Jews and Grecians, we preach Christ, the power of God, and the wisdom of God. … But ye are of him in Christ Jesus, who of God is made unto us wisdom and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption, — **1 Corinthians 1:24, 30, 1599 Geneva**

Paul explicitly identifies Christ as **Θεοῦ σοφίαν** — *the Wisdom of God* — and says He was **made** (**ἐγενήθη**) Wisdom **to us** **ἀπὸ Θεοῦ** — *from God.* The Wisdom of Proverbs 8 is fulfilled in the Person of Christ.

**Witness #2 — Colossians 1:15-17 (Geneva):**

> Who is the image of the invisible God, the first begotten of every creature. For by him were all things created, which are in heaven, and which are in earth, things visible and invisible: whether they be Thrones or Dominions, or Principalities, or Powers, all things were created by him, and for him, And he is before all things, and in him all things consist. — **Colossians 1:15-17, 1599 Geneva**

**πρωτότοκος πάσης κτίσεως** — *"firstborn of all creation"* — corresponds precisely to **ἀρχὴν ὁδῶν αὐτοῦ** of Proverbs 8:22 LXX — the Beginning of God's ways. And note the verb in both texts: **κτίζω** — the very word used of Wisdom in Prov 8:22 and of all things being created **in** Christ in Col 1:16.

**Two witnesses established:** Proverbs 8 is speaking of the Son in His pre-temporal relation to the Father.

---

### What Proverbs 8 establishes about the Son:

1. He is **brought forth / founded** by the Lord as **the beginning of His ways** (v. 22).
2. He is **begotten** before the creation of the earth, depths, mountains (vv. 24-25).
3. He is **with** the Father **at** the work of creation, serving as the Father's Wisdom by which all things are fitted together (vv. 27-30).
4. He is the Father's **delight** before the world is made (v. 30).
5. **He is not the Father; He is brought forth from the Father, distinct from Him, beside Him.**

---

### A doctrinal observation per our maxims

Notice carefully what Proverbs 8 does **not** say:

- It does **not** say Wisdom is co-eternal in the sense of having no beginning. It explicitly says she was **founded / begotten** by the Lord.
- It does **not** say Wisdom is *another God*. She is the Lord's Wisdom — belonging to Him, brought forth from Him.
- It does **not** violate Isaiah 44:24. The Lord stretches out the heavens *alone* — by His own Wisdom, which is His. The Wisdom acting is *His*, not a separate Deity.

This perfectly preserves the Shema and Isaiah 44-46 while honestly testifying to the Son's pre-temporal begetting. **The Son has an origin in the Father; the Father has no origin.** This is the consistent biblical witness, and it is the testimony of the LXX our Lord used.

---

## **MICAH 5:2 — The Goings Forth From of Old**

### **Micah 5:2 (LXX):**

> And you, O Bethleem *(Bethlehem)*, house of Ephratha *(Ephrathah)*, are very few in number to be among the thousands of Ioudas *(Judah)*; one from you shall come forth for me to become a ruler in Israel, and his goings forth are from of old, from days of yore *(long ago)*. — **Micah 5:2, LXX (NETS)**

*(The verse is numbered 5:2 in the NETS print and 5:1 in the older LXX verse-numbering; the words are the same.)*

---

### Observations

**(1)** **ἐκ σοῦ μοι ἐξελεύσεται** — *"Out of you, for Me, He shall come forth."* The Ruler **comes forth** from Bethlehem **for the Father** (**μοι** — *to/for Me*). The Son's appearing in Bethlehem is **for the Father's purpose**, given over to the Father's mission.

**(2)** **αἱ ἔξοδοι αὐτοῦ ἀπ᾽ ἀρχῆς ἐξ ἡμερῶν αἰῶνος** — *"His goings-forth are from the beginning, from days of old/eternity."*

The Greek phrasing is plural — **αἱ ἔξοδοι** — *the goings-forth.* This indicates **multiple comings-forth** of the Son:
- His **begetting** before the foundations of the earth (Prov 8:24-25; Ps 109:3 LXX).
- His subsequent **appearances** in Old Testament theophanies (which we will examine next).
- His ultimate **going forth from Bethlehem** in the incarnation.

The phrase **ἀπ᾽ ἀρχῆς ἐξ ἡμερῶν αἰῶνος** — *"from the beginning, from days of the age"* — places His origin far back, before days as men count them. This corresponds exactly to **πρὸ τοῦ αἰῶνος ἐθεμελίωσέν με** of Proverbs 8:23.

---

### Two witnesses for Micah 5:2 as a Messianic prophecy of the Son:

**Witness #1 — Matthew 2:3-6 (Geneva):**

> When King Herod heard this, he was troubled, and all Jerusalem with him. And gathering together all the chief Priests and Scribes of the people, he asked of them, where Christ should be born. And they said unto him, At Beth-leem in Judea: for so it is written by the Prophet, And thou Beth-leem in the land of Judah, art not the least among the Princes of Judah: For out of thee shall come the governor that shall feed that my people Israel. — **Matthew 2:3-6, 1599 Geneva**

The scribes of Israel — independently of Christian interpretation — already understood Micah 5:2 as referring to **the Christ.** Matthew, by inspiration, confirms it.

**Witness #2 — John 7:42 (Geneva):**

> Saith not the Scripture that that Christ shall come of the seed of David, and out of the town of Beth-leem, where David was? — **John 7:42, 1599 Geneva**

**Witness #2 established:** The common Jewish expectation, from Micah 5:2, was that the Christ would come out of Bethlehem.

---

### What Micah 5:2 adds to our portrait of the Son:

1. He is to be born in **Bethlehem** — a definite, prophesied earthly location.
2. He is a **Ruler** for Israel — given by the Father, **for** the Father (**μοι**).
3. His **goings-forth** (plural) extend back **from days of old / of the age** — He has a prior history before His birth in Bethlehem.

---

## **CONVERGENCE: What We Now Have Established**

Putting our four pillars together — **Psalm 2 + Psalm 109/110 + Proverbs 8 + Micah 5:2** — Scripture testifies that the Son:

| Truth | Witnesses |
|---|---|
| Is **begotten** of the Father | Ps 2:7; Ps 109:3 LXX; Prov 8:25 |
| Has an **origin** in the Father (no co-eternity in the Nicene sense) | Prov 8:22-25; Mic 5:2 (LXX) |
| Existed **before the world was made** | Prov 8:22-30; Mic 5:2 |
| Was the Father's **Wisdom** in fitting the creation together | Prov 8:27-30; 1 Cor 1:24; Col 1:16 |
| Has **multiple goings-forth** culminating in Bethlehem | Mic 5:2 |
| Is **made Lord and Christ** by the Father at His resurrection | Ps 2:7 + Acts 13:33; Ps 110:1 + Acts 2:36 |
| Is **forever Priest** by the Father's oath | Ps 110:4 + Heb 5:5-6 |
| Sits at the Father's **right hand**, distinct from Him | Ps 110:1; Matt 22:44 |

---

## **The Doctrinal Picture — Strictly From Scripture**

The Son of the One God:
- **Was brought forth** by the Father before time and the creation (**γεννᾷ με**, **ἔκτισέν με**, **ἐθεμελίωσέν με**).
- **Was with the Father** as His Wisdom at the work of creation (Prov 8; cf. John 1:1 — **πρὸς τὸν Θεόν**).
- **Came forth** in the fullness of time, born of the virgin in Bethlehem (Mic 5:2; Gen 3:15; Isa 7:14; Gal 4:4).
- **Was begotten in power** as Son of God by the resurrection (Ps 2:7; Rom 1:4; Acts 13:33).
- **Was seated** at the Father's right hand, made both Lord and Christ (Ps 110:1; Acts 2:36).
- **Is Priest forever** by oath of the Father (Ps 110:4; Heb 7:21).

**At every point, the Father is the Source, the Sender, the Appointer, the Anointer, the Begetter, the Glorifier.** The Son is the brought-forth, the sent, the appointed, the anointed, the begotten, the glorified — **and acts always for the Father's purposes** (Mic 5:2: **μοι** — *for Me*).

The Shema stands. Isaiah 44-46 stands. **The Father is the One God.** The Son is His Only-Begotten — distinct, beloved, exalted, but never a second God, never a co-equal, never the Father.

---

## **A Question Now Properly Opened**

Micah 5:2 spoke of the Son's **goings-forth** (plural) **from the beginning**. We have accounted for:
- His begetting (Prov 8 / Ps 109:3 LXX).
- His birth in Bethlehem (Mic 5:2 / Isa 7:14).

But what of the **intermediate goings-forth** — His appearances in the Old Testament between His begetting and His birth?

Scripture testifies to a Person called **the Angel of the LORD** (**ὁ ἄγγελος Κυρίου** — literally, *the Messenger of the LORD*) who appears repeatedly — to Hagar, Abraham, Jacob, Moses, Gideon, Manoah — and who *speaks as God*, is *worshipped*, yet is *sent from God*. Is this Person the pre-incarnate Son — one of the **ἔξοδοι** of Micah 5:2?

---


## **A QUESTION ARISING — Who Is the Angel of the LORD?**

One of the most consequential threads in all of Scripture now opens — the appearances of **ὁ ἄγγελος Κυρίου** (*the Messenger of the LORD*). The texts must establish, by two-or-more witnesses, whether this Messenger is: (a) merely a created angel acting as God's spokesman, or (b) the One God Himself appearing, or (c) a distinct Person who speaks *as* God yet is *sent by* God — i.e., the pre-incarnate Son in one of His **ἔξοδοι** *(the goings-forth)* (Micah 5:2).

Per our maxims, we will not impose the answer; we will let Scripture deliver it.

## **THE FIRST APPEARANCE — Genesis 16: Hagar in the Wilderness**

> But the Lord God's angel found her by the spring of water in the wilderness, by the spring on the way to Sour *(Shur)*. And the angel of the Lord said to her, "Hagar, slave-girl of Sara *(Sarai)*, where are you coming from, and where are you going?" — **Genesis 16:7-8, LXX (NETS)**

> The angel of the Lord also said to her, "I will multitudinously *(greatly)* multiply your offspring, and it will not be counted for multitude." — **Genesis 16:10, LXX (NETS)**

### Observation of decisive importance

The Messenger says: **"I will multiply your seed."** This is a promise that **only God can make and fulfill** — and it is the **same promise** the LORD Himself makes directly to Abraham (Gen 13:16; 15:5; 22:17). The Messenger does not say *"The LORD will multiply"*; He says **"I will multiply."** He speaks in the first person *as God*.

> And Hagar called the name of the Lord who was speaking to her, "You-are-the-God-who-looksupon-me *(You are the God who sees me)*," because she said, "For truly I saw him face to face when he appeared to me." — **Genesis 16:13, LXX (NETS)**

### Observation of the utmost weight

Hagar identifies the Messenger as **Κύριος** — *the LORD* — and addresses Him as **Σὺ ὁ Θεός** — *"You are the God."* Furthermore, she marvels that she has **seen** Him and lived: **ἐνώπιον εἶδον ὀφθέντα μοι** — *"I have seen face-to-face the One who appeared to me."*

The narrator (Moses, by inspiration) does **not** correct her. Scripture *confirms* her identification: **"she called the name of the LORD who spoke to her: You are the God who sees me."**

So here is our first datum: **The Messenger speaks as God, is identified as God, and is seen — yet remains called "the Messenger of the LORD," not the LORD without qualification.**

## **THE SECOND APPEARANCE — Genesis 22: The Akedah**

> And the Lord's angel called him from the sky *(heaven)* and said to him, "Abraam *(Abraham)*, Abraam!" And he said, "Here I am." And he said, "Do not lay your hand on the youngster *(the boy)* nor do anything to him. For now I know that you do fear God, and for my sake you have not spared your beloved son." — **Genesis 22:11-12, LXX (NETS)**

### Observation

The Messenger says: **οὐκ ἐφείσω τοῦ υἱοῦ σου τοῦ ἀγαπητοῦ δι᾽ ἐμέ** — *"you did not spare your beloved son **for My sake**."*

But the entire test had been at the command of **ὁ Θεός** (v. 1): *"God tested Abraham."* So the One who is testing — God — is the same One **for whose sake** Abraham did not spare his son. The Messenger speaks as **the God** who commanded the test.

> And the Lord's angel called Abraam *(Abraham)* a second time from the sky *(heaven)*, saying, "By myself I have sworn, says the Lord: Inasmuch as you have carried out this matter and for my sake have not spared your beloved son, I will indeed bless you with blessings, and I will make your offspring as numerously numerous *(greatly multiplied)* as the stars of the sky and as the sand that is by the seashore, …" — **Genesis 22:15-18, LXX (NETS)**

### Observation of decisive weight

The Messenger says: **"Κατ᾽ ἐμαυτοῦ ὤμοσα, λέγει Κύριος"** — *"BY MYSELF I have sworn, says the LORD."*

This is identical to Isaiah 45:23 LXX: **κατ᾽ ἐμαυτοῦ ὀμνύω** — *"By Myself I swear,"* which we examined as the Father's exclusive self-oath. The Messenger speaks **as YHWH** swearing by Himself. He is not merely conveying a message — He is the One swearing, in the first person.

**Yet observe carefully** — the Messenger speaks **as YHWH swearing**, and yet the formula **λέγει Κύριος** *("says the LORD")* is used. This is the prophetic formula — the Messenger speaks for the LORD and as the LORD simultaneously. He is *of* the LORD and yet speaks *as* the LORD.

---

### The Apostolic confirmation — Hebrews 6:13-14

> For when God made the promise to Abraham, because he had no greater to swear by, he sware by himself, Saying, Surely I will abundantly bless thee and multiply thee marvelously. — **Hebrews 6:13-14, 1599 Geneva**

The author of Hebrews identifies the swearer as **ὁ Θεός** Himself. Therefore the **ἄγγελος Κυρίου** of Genesis 22 was speaking *as* God Himself swearing — yet without being called the LORD without qualification.

## **THE THIRD APPEARANCE — Genesis 31: Jacob and the God of Bethel**

> And the angel of God said to me during sleep, ‘Iakob *(Jacob)*.’ And I said, ‘What is it?’ … I am the God who appeared to you at a divine place *(the place of God, Bethel)*, there where you anointed a stele *(pillar)* to me and made a vow to me there. … — **Genesis 31:11-13, LXX (NETS)**

### Observation of supreme importance

The Messenger says plainly: **ἐγώ εἰμι ὁ Θεὸς** — *"I AM the God"* — the very **ἐγώ εἰμι** formula of Isaiah 45:18! He claims to be **ὁ Θεὸς ὁ ὀφθείς σοι** — *"the God who appeared to you"* at Bethel — referring back to Genesis 28 where Jacob saw the ladder and **YHWH** stood above it.

So the same Person who appeared to Jacob as **YHWH** at Bethel now appears as **ὁ ἄγγελος τοῦ Θεοῦ** — *the Messenger of God.* He is both: **the appearing God**, and **the sent Messenger**.

## **THE FOURTH APPEARANCE — Genesis 32: Wrestling at Peniel**

> And Iakob *(Jacob)* was left alone, and a man was wrestling with him until morning. … Then he said to him, “Your name shall no longer be called Iakob *(Jacob)*, but Israel shall be your name, because you have prevailed with a god, and with humans you are powerful.” … And Iakob *(Jacob)* called the name of that place Divine-form *(Peniel, “the face of God”)*, “For I have seen a god face to face, and my life has been preserved.” — **Genesis 32:24-30, LXX (NETS; the Greek editions number these verses 25-31)**

### Observation of the highest weight

The One who wrestles is called **ἄνθρωπος** — *a man.* He is **physically embodied**, wrestling all night. Yet:

- He has authority to **rename** Jacob to **Israel** (only God renames covenant figures).

- He declares Jacob has **striven with God** (**μετὰ Θεοῦ**).

- Jacob identifies Him as **Θεός** — *"I have seen God face to face."*

Yet this God is in the form of a **Man**. Here is a pre-incarnate visible manifestation of a Person who is both *with God* (whom Jacob strove with) and *appears as Man* — a foreshadowing of the Incarnation itself.

---

### The Prophet Hosea confirms — Hosea 12:3-4 (LXX 12:4-5):

> In the womb he kicked his brother with the heel and in his struggles prevailed against God. And he prevailed with an angel and predominated *(prevailed, overpowered)* …  — **Hosea 12:3-4, LXX (NETS; the Greek editions number these verses 4-5)**

**The Prophet's testimony is the second witness:** In one breath, Jacob's adversary is called **Θεός** *(God)* and **ἄγγελος** *(Angel/Messenger).* **Hosea, under inspiration, identifies the Messenger as God.** The two designations refer to the same Person.

## **THE FIFTH APPEARANCE — Exodus 3: The Burning Bush**

> Now an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a fire of flame out of the bush, … Now when the Lord saw that he was drawing near to see, the Lord called him from the bush, saying, “Moyses *(Moses)*, Moyses.” And he said, “What is it?” … And he said to him, “I am the God of your father, God of Abraam *(Abraham)* and God of Isaak *(Isaac)* and God of Iakob *(Jacob)*.” …  — **Exodus 3:2-6, LXX (NETS)**

### Observation of the highest order

The text moves seamlessly between three designations:

- **ἄγγελος Κυρίου** *(the Messenger of the LORD)* — v. 2

- **Κύριος** *(the LORD)* — v. 4

- **ὁ Θεός** *(God)* — v. 4, 6

**The Messenger, the LORD, and God are presented as one and the same speaker** — yet the text begins by calling Him "the Messenger of the LORD," not "the LORD" simpliciter. Once again: **He speaks as God, is identified as God, yet is named the Messenger sent by God.**

### **Exodus 3:14 (LXX) — The "I AM" Declaration:**

> And God said to Moyses *(Moses)*, “I am The One Who Is.” And he said, “Thus shall you say to the sons of Israel, ‘The One Who Is has sent me to you.’” — **Exodus 3:14, LXX (NETS)**

### Observation

The Messenger declares Himself: **Ἐγώ εἰμι ὁ ὤν** — *"I AM the One Who Is."* This is the divine Name itself.

Now hear Jesus, in the New Testament, in John 8:58 (Greek):

*"εἶπεν αὐτοῖς Ἰησοῦς· Ἀμὴν ἀμὴν λέγω ὑμῖν, πρὶν Ἀβραὰμ γενέσθαι ἐγὼ εἰμί."*

> Jesus said unto them, Verily, verily I say unto you, before Abraham was, I am. — **John 8:58, 1599 Geneva**

The Jews understood this and sought to stone Him for blasphemy. **Jesus claims the very designation the Messenger spoke at the burning bush.** This is a strong witness — though not yet decisive — that the Messenger of the LORD is the same Person who later was named Jesus.

## **THE APOSTOLIC IDENTIFICATION — Two Decisive Witnesses**

We have seen the pattern — the Messenger speaks as God, is called God, is seen as God, yet is sent by God and remains "the Messenger." Who, then, is He? The Apostles, by inspiration, supply the answer.

---

### **Witness #1 — 1 Corinthians 10:1-4, 9:**

> Moreover, brethren, I would not that ye should be ignorant, that all our fathers were under that cloud, and all passed through that sea, … And did all drink the same spiritual drink (for they drank of the spiritual Rock that followed them: and the Rock was Christ) … Neither let us tempt Christ, as some of them also tempted him, and were destroyed of serpents. — **1 Corinthians 10:1-4, 9, 1599 Geneva**


**Paul declares that the One who led Israel through the wilderness — the One they tempted, the Rock that gave them water (which the Old Testament names as **YHWH**) — was Christ.** This is Paul's unambiguous identification of the One who appeared, spoke, led, and acted in the Exodus narrative as **the pre-incarnate Christ**.

---

### **Witness #2 — Jude 5:**

> I will therefore put you in remembrance, forasmuch as ye once knew this, how that *(that)* the Lord, after that he had delivered the people out of Egypt, destroyed them afterward which believed not *(those who did not believe)*. — **Jude 5, 1599 Geneva**


**Note:** The earliest and best-attested reading is **Ἰησοῦς** — *"Jesus saved a people out of Egypt."* Jude, by inspiration, identifies the **Deliverer of Israel from Egypt** — who in Exodus is called **YHWH** and **the Messenger of the LORD** — as **Jesus**. Even taking the Geneva's variant "the Lord," the witness still holds, since Paul has already established (1 Cor 10) that "the Lord" of the wilderness is Christ.

**Two witnesses established:** The Messenger of the LORD — the One who appeared to Hagar, swore the oath to Abraham, wrestled with Jacob, spoke from the burning bush, led Israel through the sea, gave the water from the Rock — is **the pre-incarnate Christ, the Son of the One God**, in one of His **ἔξοδοι** (Mic 5:2).

## **HOW IS THIS CONSISTENT WITH OUR FOUNDATION? — A Critical Reconciliation**

Per our maxims, we must reconcile this finding with Isaiah 44-46 and the absolute monotheism of the Shema. **How can the Son be "seen as God" when the Father said, "No man can see Me and live" (Ex 33:20) and "To whom will you liken Me?" (Isa 46:5)?**

The answer is given by Jesus Himself and confirmed by John:

### **John 1:18:**

> No man hath seen God at any time: that only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared *(revealed)* him. — **John 1:18, 1599 Geneva**


### **John 6:46:**

> Not that any man hath seen the Father, save *(except)* he which is of God, he hath seen the Father. — **John 6:46, 1599 Geneva**


---

### The reconciliation, by Scripture itself:

- **No man has ever seen the Father.** Hagar did not see the Father. Abraham did not see the Father. Jacob did not see the Father. Moses did not see the Father. *Period.*
- **The One whom they all saw — and called "God" — was the Only-Begotten Son**, the visible Image (**εἰκὼν τοῦ Θεοῦ τοῦ ἀοράτου**, Col 1:15) of the invisible Father.


**Therefore Isaiah 44-46 is not violated.** The Father remains unseen, unique, the sole God. The Son is the visible Messenger of that God — His Begotten, sent forth from the Father from of old, bearing the Father's authority, Name, and message.

## **A Crucial Textual Note: Exodus 23:20-21**

### **Exodus 23:20-21, LXX (NETS):**

> And look, I am sending my angel in front of you in order to guard you on the way in order to bring you into the land that I prepared for you. Mind yourself *(take care)*, and listen to him, and do not disobey him. For he shall not hold you in undue awe *(he will not spare you)*, for my name is upon him. — **Exodus 23:20-21, LXX (NETS)**


### Observation

The Father says of this Messenger: **τὸ γὰρ ὄνομά μού ἐστιν ἐπ᾽ αὐτῷ** — *"My Name is in Him."* This is the Father's own explanation for why this Messenger speaks as God, acts as God, must be obeyed as God, and forgives or refuses to forgive sins — because **the Father's Name dwells in Him.**

Geneva *(1599)* prints the same clause of the Angel at verse 21: “for he will not spare your misdeeds, because my Name is in him.” (NETS reads “my name is upon him”; the Greek ἐπ᾽ αὐτῷ carries either rendering.)


This is the very same theme Jesus declared in His high-priestly prayer:

### **John 17:11-12:**

> And now am I no more in the world, but these are in the world, and I come to thee. Holy Father, keep them in thy Name, even them whom thou hast given me, that they may be one, as we are. While I was with them in the world, I kept them in thy Name: those that thou gavest me, have I kept, and none of them is lost, but the child of perdition, that the Scripture might be fulfilled. — **John 17:11-12, 1599 Geneva**


**The Name of the Father was given to the Son** — exactly as it was placed in the Messenger at Exodus 23:21. This is the **same Person**.

The contrast Paul is drawing is **the Father's wisdom vs. the Greeks' wisdom (man's wisdom)**:

- **To those who are called**, the crucified Christ *is* **the power and wisdom OF God** — that is, **the Father's power and the Father's wisdom embodied / enacted in Him**.

The phrase **Θεοῦ σοφίαν** is a **genitive of source/possession**: ***God's*** **wisdom** — wisdom **belonging to the Father**, manifested **in** the crucified Christ. It is not saying "Christ is a personified pre-existent Wisdom-being." It is saying: **the Father's wisdom is displayed in what He has done through His Son's cross.**

## **1 Corinthians 1:30 — A Closer Look**

### **1 Corinthians 1:30, 1599 Geneva:**

> But ye are of him in Christ Jesus, who of God is made unto us wisdom and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption, — **1 Corinthians 1:30, 1599 Geneva**

### Careful observation

- **ἐξ αὐτοῦ** — *"of Him"* (the Father) — *you are.* The Father is the source.

- **ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ** — *"in Christ Jesus"* — the sphere/locus.

- **ὃς ἐγενήθη σοφία ἡμῖν ἀπὸ Θεοῦ** — *"who was made [or became] wisdom **for us** **from God**."*

This is **not** saying "Christ is the pre-existent personified Wisdom of Proverbs 8." It is saying: **Christ was *made* wisdom *for us* — from the Father.** The Father is the Source; Christ, in His work, is *made* to be wisdom *to us* — that is, **for our benefit, the embodiment of the Father's saving plan.**

The four items listed — wisdom, righteousness, sanctification, redemption — are all **soteriological functions** Christ fulfills **for us**, given **from the Father**. None of them are pre-existence claims. The whole verse is about what Christ *is to us* by the Father's appointment, not about an ontological identification with Proverbs 8's Wisdom figure.

## **The Retraction**

I therefore must retract my use of 1 Corinthians 1:24, 30 as a witness identifying Christ with the personified Wisdom of Proverbs 8.

What Paul is saying:

- **The Father is wise** (the Source of all true wisdom).

- **The Father's wisdom is displayed in the cross of Christ** — a wisdom the world rejects.

- **Christ has been made to us wisdom from God** — meaning, the Father has appointed Christ to be the embodiment of His saving plan **for our benefit**.

What Paul is **not** saying:

- That Christ is ontologically identical with the **Σοφία** personified in Proverbs 8.

## **What Remains Standing About the Son's Pre-Temporal Existence**

If we strip out the inferences from Proverbs 8, what direct OT-and-NT-witnessed evidence do we have for the Son existing **before** His birth in Bethlehem?

**Micah 5:2** — *"whose goings-forth are from the beginning, from days of old/eternity."* The NT (Matthew 2:5-6) cites Micah 5:2 in reference to the birthplace, but does not explicitly quote the "goings-forth" phrase. So the "goings-forth from of old" is in the OT text but is not specifically expounded in the NT.

**Therefore even the Son's pre-temporal existence** — which I had treated as firmly established — rests on **less explicit NT confirmation** than I had presented. The OT phrases are *there* in the LXX text the Apostles used, but their explicit handling of those specific phrases is sparse.
