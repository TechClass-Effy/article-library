## **The Foundational Confession: The One God**
### **Deuteronomy 6:4 — The Shema**

> And these are the statutes and the judgments, which the Lord commanded to the sons of Israel in the wilderness as they were coming out from the land of Egypt. Hear, O Israel: The Lord our God is one Lord. — **Deuteronomy 6:4, LXX (NETS)**

This is the bedrock confession. **One Lord. One God.** Not three. Not a council. Not a composite *(a mixture of parts made into one)*. **One.**

---

### **Second witness — from the lips of the Messiah Himself:**

> Jesus answered him, The first of all the commandments is, Hear, Israel, The Lord our God is the only Lord. — **Mark 12:29, 1599 Geneva**

> **A HISTORICAL NOTE:** *Nicaea — the council called by Emperor Constantine in A.D. 325 — is where the “same substance” *(homoousios)* language for the Son was first pressed upon the churches; the fully-worded “three persons, one essence” creed followed at Constantinople, A.D. 381. That is the framework this study weighs against the Scripture itself.*

Jesus Himself — centuries after Moses, and yet centuries before Nicaea — affirms the Shema *(Hebrew, “hear” — the name of Israel’s one-God confession)* as the **first** of all commandments. He does not amend it. He does not qualify it. He confesses **one God**.

---

## **The Father Identified as That One God**
### **Witness #1 — Jesus to the Father:**

> And this is life eternal, that they know thee to be the only very God *(the only true God)*, and whom thou hast sent, Jesus Christ. — **John 17:3, 1599 Geneva**

The Messiah Himself addresses the Father as **τὸν μόνον ἀληθινὸν Θεόν** — "the only true God" — and distinguishes Himself, **Ἰησοῦν Χριστόν**, as the one *sent* by that God. The Sender and the Sent are not collapsed; the One God is the Father, and Jesus is His Christ.

---

### **Witness #2 — Paul to the Corinthians:**

> Yet unto us there is but one God, which is that Father, of whom are all things, and we in him: and one Lord Jesus Christ, by whom are all things, and we by him. — **1 Corinthians 8:6, 1599 Geneva**

Paul, the Apostle, places **ὁ Πατήρ** — the Father — as the **εἶς Θεός**, the *one* God. Jesus Christ is named **εἶς Κύριος** — *one Lord* — the agent **δι’ οὗ** *(through whom)*. The Father is the Source (**ἐξ οὗ** — *of whom*); the Son is the Mediator/Instrument *(the one standing between, the one through whom He works)* (**δι’ οὗ**).

---

### **Two witnesses established:** The One God of the Shema **is the Father.** Jesus the Christ is His Sent One, His Anointed, His Lord — distinct from the One God, and subordinate *(in ordered rank beneath Him)* as the agent to the Source.

---

## **Further Confirmation From the Mouth of the Son**

**John 20:17:**

> Jesus saith unto her, Touch me not: for I am not yet ascended to my Father: but go to my brethren, and say unto them, I ascend unto my Father, and to your Father, and to my God, and your God. — **John 20:17, 1599 Geneva**

Even after the resurrection, Jesus calls the Father **Θεόν μου** — *my God*. The Risen Christ has a God. That God is the Father.

**Ephesians 1:17 — Paul agrees:**

> That the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, that Father of glory, might give unto you the Spirit of wisdom, and revelation through the acknowledging of him *(truly knowing him)*, — **Ephesians 1:17, 1599 Geneva**

Two witnesses: **The Father is the God *of* Jesus Christ.**

---

## **Therefore, our refined foundation:**

1. There is **one God** — Deut. 6:4, confirmed by Jesus in Mark 12:29.
2. That one God is **the Father** — John 17:3, 1 Cor. 8:6.
3. Jesus the Christ is the **Son** of that God, the **sent** one, the **Lord** through whom the Father acts — distinct from, and having as His own God, the Father.
4. The "Spirit of God" in Genesis 1:2 must therefore be understood not as a separate "person" of a later-invented triune *(three-persons)* godhead *(see the Historical Note at Mark 12:29)*, but according to its plain Scriptural usage — which we will examine in due course as **the breath, power, and active presence of the One God Himself** (cf. Ps 32:6 LXX — *"By the word of the Lord the heavens were made firm, and by the breath of his mouth all their host"* — where the *word* and the *breath of His mouth* are instrumental *(the means He uses)* expressions of the one Lord).

---

## **Now — returning to Genesis 1:1 in light of this:**

> In the beginning God made the sky and the earth. — **Genesis 1:1, LXX (NETS)**

**ὁ Θεός** *(the God)* here is **the Father** — the one God of the Shema. He is the Creator. He is the Source (**ἐξ οὗ τὰ πάντα**, 1 Cor 8:6).

The question we will need to address carefully — without trinitarian assumptions — is the relationship of the **Son** to this creative act, given that 1 Cor 8:6 also says **δι’ οὗ τὰ πάντα** ("by whom are all things") of Jesus Christ, and that John 1:1-3 speaks of the **Λόγος**.

That investigation belongs to the **Son's** part of the study, and we will undertake it only *after* the Father — the One God — is firmly established as the foundation.

---

## **ISAIAH 44 — "Beside Me there is no God"**

Isaiah 44-46 contains some of the most thunderous, uncompromising declarations of monotheism *(the teaching that there is one God)* in all of Scripture. The One God speaks for Himself here — in the first person — and leaves no room for any rival, partner, council, or co-equal.

### **Isaiah 44:6**

> Thus says God, the king of Israel, who delivered him, God Sabaoth *(Hebrew, "of the armies" — the Lord of hosts)*: I am first, and I am after these things; besides me there is no god. — **Isaiah 44:6, LXX (NETS)**

**Observation:** The speaker — **ὁ Θεός**, the King of Israel, the Redeemer, the Lord of Hosts — declares: **"I am first, and I am after these things; besides me there is no god."** Not "no other God like Me," not "no God of equal rank" — **οὐκ ἔστιν Θεός** *(a god there is not)*. There is no God. Full stop.

---

### **Isaiah 44:8**

> Do not cover yourselves; did you not give ear *(listen)* from the beginning, and I declared it to you? You are witnesses whether there is a god *(any god)* besides me, and they were not formerly. — **Isaiah 44:8, LXX (NETS)**

**Observation:** The God of Israel calls His people as **μάρτυρες** — *witnesses* — to testify whether any other god exists. His own answer: **οὐκ ἦσαν** — *they were not; they are not; they shall not be.*

---

### **Isaiah 44:24**

> Thus says the Lord, who redeems you, who forms you from the womb: I am the Lord, who accomplishes all things; I alone stretched out heaven, and I bolstered *(made firm)* earth. Who else? — **Isaiah 44:24, LXX (NETS)**

**Observation of supreme importance for our study:**

The Lord declares: **ἐξέτεινα τὸν οὐρανὸν μόνος** — *"I stretched out the heaven ALONE."* And **ἐστερέωσα τὴν γῆν** — *"I made the earth firm"* (note the **στερεόω** root — connecting to **στερέωμα**, the firmament of Gen 1:6; this will matter when cosmology becomes relevant). And then the challenge: **τίς ἕτερος;** — *"Who else?"*

This single verse forbids any reading of Genesis 1:1 that places a second divine person beside the Father as co-creator. **The Father stretched out the heavens ALONE.** When we come to examine the Son's role and the meaning of John 1:3 / Col 1:16 / 1 Cor 8:6 ("through Him"), we must reconcile those texts with **this** declaration — not the other way around. **God speaks first; men interpret after.**

---

## **ISAIAH 45 — "I am the Lord, and there is none else"**

### **Isaiah 45:5-6**

> because I am the Lord God, and there is no other god besides me, and you did not know me, — **Isaiah 45:5, LXX (NETS)**

> so that they who are from the rising of the sun and from its going down may know that there is no one besides me; I am the Lord God, and there is no other. — **Isaiah 45:6, LXX (NETS)**

**Observation:** Five times in two verses the singularity is hammered: **"I am the Lord, and there is none else."** This is not the language of a tri-personal *(three persons in one being)* being veiling part of His identity. This is plain, unambiguous, first-person testimony of **one Being, one God**.

---

### **Isaiah 45:18**

> Thus says the Lord, who made heaven— this is the God who displayed the earth and made it; he himself marked its limits; he did not make it to be empty but to be inhabited: I am, and there is no other. — **Isaiah 45:18, LXX (NETS)**

**Observation:** **οὗτος ὁ Θεός** — *"This is the God"* — the One who made the heaven, formed the earth, prepared it, created it for habitation. **Ἐγώ εἰμι, καὶ οὐκ ἔστιν ἔτι** — *"I AM, and there is none else."*

Note the **Ἐγώ εἰμι** — the same self-disclosure formula that runs through Isaiah and which Jesus will later use of Himself (John 8:58). We will mark this and return to it: the question is *how* the Son can speak with the voice of the One God, when the One God has declared that there is no other.

---

### **Isaiah 45:21-22**

> If they will declare it, let them draw near so that they may know together who made from the beginning these things that are to be heard. Then it was declared to you, I am God, and there is no other besides me; there is no righteous one or savior except me. — **Isaiah 45:21, LXX (NETS)**

> Turn to me, and you shall be saved, you who are from the end of the earth! I am God, and there is no other. — **Isaiah 45:22, LXX (NETS)**

**Observation:** **δίκαιος καὶ σωτήρ οὐκ ἔστιν παρέξ ἐμοῦ** — *"There is no righteous one and Saviour besides Me."* The Father claims the title **σωτήρ** *(Saviour)* for Himself, exclusively. This too will require careful handling when we treat the Son, who is also called Saviour in the New Testament. The resolution — as we shall see — is *not* by collapsing them into one Being, but by recognizing the Son as the Father's appointed agent *(the one He sends and works through)* of salvation, executing the will of the One Saviour.

---

### **Isaiah 45:23**

> By myself I swear, "Verily *(truly)* righteousness shall go forth from my mouth; my words shall not be turned back, because to me every knee shall bow and every tongue shall acknowledge God, — **Isaiah 45:23, LXX (NETS)**

**Observation of great importance for the Son's study:** The Father swears **by Himself** that **to Him** every knee shall bow. Paul cites this exact text in Philippians 2:10-11 of Jesus Christ — *"That at the Name of Jesus should every knee bow, both of things in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth; And that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is the Lord, unto the glory of God the Father."* (1599 Geneva) — and the bowing is **εἰς δόξαν Θεοῦ Πατρός** — *"to the glory of God the Father."* The Son receives homage **as the Father's appointed Lord**, and the result is the Father's glory. The monotheism is preserved.

---

## **ISAIAH 46 — "I am God, and there is none like Me"**

### **Isaiah 46:9**

> and remember the former things of old, because I am God, and there is no other besides me, — **Isaiah 46:9, LXX (NETS)**

### **Isaiah 46:5**

> To whom have you likened me? See, act with cunning *(plot craftily)*, you who are going astray! — **Isaiah 46:5, LXX (NETS)**

**Observation:** The God of Israel asks: **τίνι με ὡμοιώσατε;** — *"To whom have you likened Me?"* The implicit answer: *no one.* He is incomparable. Any theology that posits another Person *equal* in being to the Father — co-eternal, co-equal, of the same substance *(of one being)* — has fallen under this rebuke.

---

## **SUMMARY OF THE FOUNDATION**

From Isaiah 44-46, the One God declares — in His own voice, repeatedly, emphatically, and without qualification:

| Declaration | Reference |
|---|---|
| "I am the first and the last; besides Me there is no God." | 44:6 |
| "Is there any God besides Me? There is none." | 44:8 |
| "I stretched out the heavens ALONE." | 44:24 |
| "I am the Lord, and there is none else; no God besides Me." | 45:5-6 |
| "I AM, and there is none else." | 45:18 |
| "There is no righteous one and Saviour besides Me." | 45:21 |
| "I have sworn by Myself... to Me every knee shall bow." | 45:23 |
| "I am God, and there is none like Me." | 46:9 |
| "To whom will you liken Me?" | 46:5 |

## **The Foundation Established**

1. **The One God is numerically one Being** *(one in number* — not a family, not a committee)* — not a council, not a triad *(a group of three)*, not a multiplicity of persons sharing essence *(one shared being)*.
2. **He created the heavens and the earth ALONE** (44:24) — this stands as the controlling text for Genesis 1:1.
3. **He alone is Saviour** (45:21).
4. **To Him alone every knee shall bow** (45:23).
5. **He is incomparable** — no being is *like* Him (46:5, 9).

This is the God whom Jesus called **τὸν μόνον ἀληθινὸν Θεόν** — *the only true God* (John 17:3) — and whom Jesus called **τὸν Πατέρα μου καὶ Θεόν μου** — *my Father and my God* (John 20:17).

**The Father is the One God of Isaiah 44-46.** This is our foundation. Any study of the Son must be built upon — and reconciled with — this declaration, never against it.

## **A Question Now Properly Framed**

With this foundation firm, we may now ask the right question — the question that drives the rest of our study:

> **Who, then, is the Son of this One God? In what sense is He called "the Word," "the Image," "the Firstborn," "the Lord through whom are all things" — without violating the absolute singularity of the Father?**

We now turn to the Old Testament's earliest posits of the Son — the Father's **μονογενής** *(only-begotten)* — built strictly upon the foundation we have laid: **the Father is the One God, and there is none beside Him.**

We will proceed chronologically as the prophecies are given in the canonical order, beginning at the earliest.

---

## **POSIT #1 — Genesis 3:15: The Seed of the Woman**

> And I will put enmity between you and between the woman and between your offspring and between her offspring; he will watch your head, and you will watch his heel." — **Genesis 3:15, LXX (NETS)**

The Lexham English Septuagint keeps the older English for **σπέρμα** *(seed)* — and carries the same masculine singular:

> "I will place enmity between you and the woman, and between your seed and her seed; he will watch your head carefully, and you will watch his heel carefully." — **Genesis 3:15, LES**

### Observations

**(1)** The pronoun **αὐτός** is masculine singular — "**He**, He Himself" — not *it*, not *they*. The LXX is grammatically remarkable here: the antecedent **σπέρμα** (*seed*) is **neuter**, which would normally demand **αὐτό**. Yet the translators of the LXX (3rd-2nd century BC, *centuries before any Christian influence*) deliberately rendered **αὐτός** — masculine — pointing to a singular **male person** as the Seed.

> **A HISTORICAL NOTE:** *The Greek Old Testament was rendered in the third through second centuries before Christ, for Greek-speaking Jews — long before any Christian influence could have shaped its words.*

This is the first prophetic posit of the coming **One** — a single human Person, born of a woman, who will crush the head of the serpent.

**(2)** "**The seed of the woman**" — not of the man. In the entire patriarchal framework of Scripture, seed is reckoned through the father. This phrase is anomalous — it anticipates a birth not according to the line of a human father. The two witnesses for this anomaly being fulfilled:

**Isaiah 7:14 (LXX):**

> Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign. Look, the virgin shall be with child and bear a son, and you shall name him Emmanouel *(Immanuel)*. — **Isaiah 7:14, LXX (NETS)**

The LXX explicitly says **ἡ παρθένος** — *the virgin* — not merely *a young woman*. This is **Witness #1** that the "seed of the woman" is a virgin-born Son.

**Galatians 4:4:**

> But when the fulness of time was come, God sent forth his Son made of a woman, and made under the Law, — **Galatians 4:4, 1599 Geneva**

**Witness #2:** Paul confirms — **γενόμενον ἐκ γυναικός** — "made of a woman" — the very language of Genesis 3:15.

---

### What Genesis 3:15 establishes about the Son:
- He is a **single, masculine Person** (αὐτός).
- He is **born of a woman** without a human father in view.
- He is **sent by the One God** as the conqueror of the serpent.
- He is, at this earliest point, a **promised man** — the prophecy says nothing yet of pre-existence or divine status. We are at the *beginning* of the revelation; we will not retroject later disclosures into this verse.
