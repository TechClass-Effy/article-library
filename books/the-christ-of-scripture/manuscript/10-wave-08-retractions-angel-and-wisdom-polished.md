## **Your Reading Is Correct**

The contrast Paul is drawing is **the Father's wisdom vs. the Greeks' wisdom (man's wisdom)**:

- **The Greeks seek wisdom** (σοφίαν ζητοῦσιν) — *their* wisdom, the world's wisdom.

- **God** (the Father) reveals **His wisdom** (τῇ σοφίᾳ τοῦ Θεοῦ) — which the world cannot grasp through its own wisdom.

- **The Father's wisdom** is His **plan of salvation through the crucified Christ** — which appears foolish to the Greeks and a scandal to the Jews.

- **To those who are called**, the crucified Christ *is* **the power and wisdom OF God** — that is, **the Father's power and the Father's wisdom embodied / enacted in Him**.

The phrase **Θεοῦ σοφίαν** is a **genitive of source/possession**: ***God's*** **wisdom** — wisdom **belonging to the Father**, manifested **in** the crucified Christ. It is not saying "Christ is a personified pre-existent Wisdom-being." It is saying: **the Father's wisdom is displayed in what He has done through His Son's cross.**

## **1 Corinthians 1:30 — A Closer Look**

### **1 Corinthians 1:30, 1599 Geneva:**

> But ye are of him in Christ Jesus, who of God is made unto us wisdom and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption, — **1 Corinthians 1:30, 1599 Geneva**

### Careful observation

- **ἐξ αὐτοῦ** — *"of Him"* (the Father) — *you are.* The Father is the source.

- **ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ** — *"in Christ Jesus"* — the sphere/locus.

- **ὃς ἐγενήθη σοφία ἡμῖν ἀπὸ Θεοῦ** — *"who was made [or became] wisdom **for us** **from God**."*

This is **not** saying "Christ is the pre-existent personified Wisdom of Proverbs 8." It is saying: **Christ was *made* wisdom *for us* — from the Father.** The Father is the Source; Christ, in His work, is *made* to be wisdom *to us* — that is, **for our benefit, the embodiment of the Father's saving plan.**

The four items listed — wisdom, righteousness, sanctification, redemption — are all **soteriological functions** Christ fulfills **for us**, given **from the Father**. None of them are pre-existence claims. The whole verse is about what Christ *is to us* by the Father's appointment, not about an ontological identification with Proverbs 8's Wisdom figure.
