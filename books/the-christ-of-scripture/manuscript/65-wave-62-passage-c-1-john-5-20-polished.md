# **PASSAGE C — 1 JOHN 5:20**

## **1 John 5:20 — Geneva 1599:**

*"But we know that that Son of God is come, and hath given us a mind to know him, which is true: and we are in him that is true, that is, in that his Son Jesus Christ: this same is that very God, and that eternal life."*

---

## **The Grammatical Question**

**οὗτός ἐστιν ὁ ἀληθινὸς Θεὸς** — *"This one is the true God."*

**The critical question:** what is the antecedent of **οὗτός** ("this one")?

**Two possibilities:**

**Reading A:** The antecedent is **Jesus Christ** (the last-named person in the previous clause). Under this reading, John is calling Jesus *"the true God."*

**Reading B:** The antecedent is **τὸν ἀληθινόν** — *"the True One"* — from earlier in the verse, referring to **the Father** (whom the Son has come to reveal). Under this reading, John is saying **the Father** is the true God, and eternal life is in Him through the Son.

The Geneva translation *"this same is that very God"* is ambiguous but tends toward Reading A.

---

## **Careful Contextual Examination**

### **The verse begins with a clear distinction:**

*"...that we may know **Him that is true** (τὸν ἀληθινόν), and we are in **Him that is true** (ἐν τῷ ἀληθινῷ), even in **His Son Jesus Christ**."*

**John distinguishes:**
- **τὸν ἀληθινόν** — *"the True One"* — the antecedent object of knowledge.
- **Ἰησοῦ Χριστῷ** — *"Jesus Christ"* — the Son through whom we are in the True One.

**Grammatically, the more natural antecedent of "οὗτός" is "τὸν ἀληθινόν" — the True One — the Father.**

Why? Because **οὗτός** typically points back to the earlier and dominant referent in the discourse. The Father is the subject of knowledge throughout the verse. Jesus Christ is the means by which we come to know the True One.

**Under this reading (Reading B):**

*"We know the Son has come, and has given us understanding to know the True One (the Father); and we are in the True One (the Father), through His Son Jesus Christ. This One (the True One, the Father) is the true God and eternal life."*

**This reading is grammatically defensible and theologically coherent** with everything John has written elsewhere:

- John 17:3 — *"this is life eternal, that they know **thee** the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom thou hast sent."* *(the 1599 print reads, "that they know thee to be the only very God, and whom thou hast sent, Jesus Christ")*

**John himself defines "the true God" as the Father in his own Gospel.** This is decisive.

---

## **Under Our Framework**

### **Reading B (Preferred):**

The Father is **the true God**. Eternal life is in Him. We come to Him through His Son Jesus Christ. This preserves everything we have established:

- The Father as the One True God (John 17:3; 1 Cor 8:6; 1 Tim 2:5).
- Jesus as the Son through whom we access the Father.
- Eternal life as knowing the Father through the Son.

### **Reading A (if the antecedent is Jesus):**

Even if the antecedent is Jesus, under your Name-authority framework this can be honestly held:

Jesus, as the Only-Begotten bearing the Father's Name and manifesting His fullness bodily (Col 2:9), can be called **"the true God"** in the sense that He is the perfect embodied manifestation of the true God (the Father). This does not make Him identical Person with the Father — it acknowledges His unique bearing of the Father's Name and nature.

**But Reading B is the more natural grammatical reading** and coheres more directly with John's own definition in John 17:3.

**Under our maxims, we can honestly hold Reading B as the primary reading** — but neither reading requires the post-Nicene metaphysical formulation.
