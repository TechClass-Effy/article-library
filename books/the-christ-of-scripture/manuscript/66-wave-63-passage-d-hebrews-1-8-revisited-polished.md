# **PASSAGE D — HEBREWS 1:8 REVISITED**

We examined this passage earlier, but under our new combined framework (Spirit-begotten pre-existence + Name-authority), we can now see it even more clearly.

## **Hebrews 1:8 — Geneva 1599:**

*"But unto the Son he saith, O God, thy throne is for ever and ever..."*

---

## **Under Our Combined Framework**

Recall the citation is from **Psalm 45:6-7 LXX (Ps 44:7-8 LXX):**

The King is addressed as **ὁ Θεός** in v. 6, and in v. 7 the very same passage says: **ἔχρισέν σε ὁ Θεός ὁ Θεός σου** — *"God, even Thy God, has anointed Thee."*

**The One addressed as "God" has a God who anointed Him.**

Under your two insights:

**The Son, begotten of the Father in Spirit and bearing the Father's Name, can be addressed as "ὁ Θεός" — because He bears the Father's divine Name as the Only-Begotten.** This is Name-authority at its highest expression.

**But — and this is critical — the same passage insists that the Father is His God.** The Son's Name-bearing does not make Him identical to the Father; the Father remains the Son's God.

**This is exactly the pattern of Ex 23:21:** *"My Name is IN him."* The Messenger bears the Name; but the LORD remains the Sender. The Son bears the Father's Name; but the Father remains His God.

**Under our combined framework, Heb 1:8 reads coherently:**

The Son can legitimately be addressed with divine titles because He bears the Father's Name given to Him. His throne is forever because the Father has established it. His scepter is righteous because the Father has anointed Him. **He is genuinely divine in the sense that He bears the Father's Name and shares His Spirit-nature — but the Father remains His God, His Anointer, His Source.**

**No metaphysical co-equal substance is required.** Only the biblical framework of Name-authority is required.

**Hebrews 1:9 immediately clarifies:** *"Thou hast loved righteousness, and hated iniquity; therefore God, even thy God, hath anointed thee..."* — preserving the Father as the Son's God.

**Hebrews 1:8-9 together, honestly read under our maxims, present:**
- **The Son who bears the divine title** (v. 8) — as the Only-Begotten bearing the Father's Name.
- **The Father who remains His God** (v. 9) — the ultimate supremacy of the Father.

This is precisely the framework you have proposed.
