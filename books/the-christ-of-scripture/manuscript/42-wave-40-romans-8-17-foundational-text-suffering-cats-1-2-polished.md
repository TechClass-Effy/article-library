# **ROMANS 8:17 — THE FOUNDATIONAL TEXT**

### **1599 Geneva:**
*"If we be children, we are also heirs, even the heirs of God, and heirs annexed with Christ: if so be that we suffer with him, that we may also be glorified with him."*

---

### Careful examination of the conditional

**εἴπερ συμπάσχομεν** — *"if indeed / since indeed we suffer together."*

**εἴπερ** is a strengthened form of **εἰ** (if). It can be translated *"if indeed"* or *"inasmuch as"* — sometimes carrying the sense of *"seeing that"* (assuming the condition is in fact met). It is **not** a doubtful "if" — it is more like *"if, as is the case"* or *"given that."*

**συμπάσχομεν** — present active indicative — *"we are suffering together [with Christ]."* The present tense indicates **an ongoing reality**, not a future possibility. Paul presents co-suffering as the present experience of the believer.

**ἵνα καὶ συνδοξασθῶμεν** — *"in order that we may also be glorified together [with Him]."* Aorist passive subjunctive — the purpose / result clause. **The co-suffering is the path to the co-glorification.**

---

### What this verse establishes

**(1) Suffering with Christ is normative for the believer.** Paul does not say *"some believers suffer with Him."* He presents co-suffering as the present experience of those who are joint-heirs. The εἴπερ does not introduce a possibility — it confirms an actual condition that characterizes the believer's life.

**(2) Co-suffering is the path to co-glorification.** The Father's design is that the path to glory runs through fellowship with the Son's sufferings. **There is no co-glorification without co-suffering.** This is not punishment for being a child of God; it is the **pattern of the Beloved Son**, into whose image we are being conformed (Rom 8:29).

**(3) This is corporate to all believers, not an elite calling.** Paul includes himself and his Roman readers — ordinary believers — in the *"we"* who suffer together with Christ.

---

# **WHAT DOES "SUFFERING WITH CHRIST" MEAN?**

Per our maxims, we must let Scripture itself define this — not project our own assumptions. The NT identifies several distinct yet overlapping categories of suffering that constitute the believer's fellowship with Christ's sufferings.

Let us survey them carefully.

---

## **CATEGORY 1 — SUFFERING FOR RIGHTEOUSNESS' SAKE**

This is the most explicit form of suffering with Christ: enduring opposition, hostility, slander, persecution, or harm specifically *because of one's union with Him and obedience to His word.*

---

### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Blessed are they which suffer persecution for righteousness' sake: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. Blessed shall ye be when men revile you, and persecute you, and say all manner of evil against you for my sake, falsely. Rejoice and be glad, for great is your reward in heaven: for so persecuted they the Prophets which were before you."*

---

### Observations

**(1)** Three distinct experiences are identified:
- **ὀνειδίσωσιν** — being reviled / reproached (verbal contempt).
- **διώξωσιν** — being persecuted (active hostility, possibly physical).
- **εἴπωσιν πᾶν πονηρὸν καθ᾽ ὑμῶν ψευδόμενοι** — having all manner of evil spoken against you falsely (slander, defamation).

**(2)** The qualifier is critical: **ἕνεκεν ἐμοῦ** — *"for My sake."* Not all suffering qualifies as "suffering with Christ." It must be **for His sake** — because of fidelity to Him and His word.

**(3)** The pattern continues that of the prophets — those who spoke for God and suffered for it.

---

### **1599 Geneva:**
*"If the world hate you, ye know that it hated me before you. If ye were of the world, the world would love his own: but because ye are not of the world, but I have chosen you out of the world, therefore the world hateth you. Remember the word that I said unto you, The servant is not greater than his master. If they have persecuted me, they will persecute you also..."*

---

### Observations of foundational importance

**(1) The world's hatred is a settled disposition.** Jesus presents it as a constant — the world has hated Him, and will hate those who are His.

**(2) The reason: ὅτι ἐκ τοῦ κόσμου οὐκ ἐστέ** — *"because you are not of the world."* The believer's separation from the world is the cause of the world's hostility. Identification with Christ removes us from the world's love.

**(3) The principle: Οὐκ ἔστιν δοῦλος μείζων τοῦ κυρίου αὐτοῦ** — *"The servant is not greater than his master."* What He endured, His servants will endure. This is a settled pattern of the Christian life, not an aberration.

---

### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Yea, and all that will live godly in Christ Jesus, shall suffer persecution."*

---

### Observation of decisive importance

**πάντες οἱ θέλοντες εὐσεβῶς ζῆν ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ διωχθήσονται** — *"All who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus shall be persecuted."*

**πάντες** — *"all."* No exception. **διωχθήσονται** — future passive — *"will be persecuted."* A settled, prophetic certainty.

This is a sobering text. Paul, writing to Timothy near the end of his own life, declares it as a universal principle: **godly living in Christ Jesus brings persecution.** The form may vary; the reality is constant.

---

## **CATEGORY 2 — SUFFERING THE WORLD'S HATRED FOR HIS NAME**

A related but distinct facet: suffering specifically because one bears the Name of Christ.

---

### **1599 Geneva:**
*"But all these things will they do unto you for my Name's sake, because they have not known him that sent me."*

### **1599 Geneva:**
*"So they departed from the council, rejoicing, that they were counted worthy to suffer rebuke for his Name."*

### **1599 Geneva:**
*"If ye be railed upon for the Name of Christ, blessed are ye: for the spirit of glory, and of God resteth upon you... But if any man suffer as a Christian, let him not be ashamed: but let him glorify God in this behalf."*

---

### Observations

**(1)** Bearing the Name itself can be a cause of suffering. The world's quarrel is ultimately not with the believer personally but **with the Name and the One whose Name it is** (because they do not know the Father — John 15:21).

**(2)** The apostles' response to this suffering is **rejoicing** — counted worthy to bear shame for the Name. This is not a stoic endurance but a joyful identification: *"if this is what comes from being identified with Him, blessed are we."*

**(3) 1 Peter 4 carefully distinguishes the suffering that pleases God** (suffering as a Christian, for His Name) **from suffering that does not** (suffering as a murderer, thief, evildoer, busybody — v. 15). Not all suffering counts as fellowship with Christ; only suffering that arises from identification with Him and His ways.
