# **PASSAGE A — JOHN 1:1-3 REVISITED**

## **John 1:1-3 — Geneva 1599:**

*"In the beginning was that Word, and that Word was with God, and that Word was God. This same was in the beginning with God. All things were made by it, and without it was made nothing that was made."*

---

## **Examining Under Our Proposal**

### **Phrase 1: Ἐν ἀρχῇ ἦν ὁ Λόγος** — *"In the beginning was the Word"*

**Under your proposal:**

*"In the beginning"* points back to Gen 1:1 LXX (**Ἐν ἀρχῇ**) — the beginning of creation. **The Word (ὁ Λόγος) was already existing at that beginning.** Not created at that beginning — **already there.**

The imperfect verb **ἦν** (*was existing*) denotes continuous existence at that point. When creation began, the Word already **was.**

**This coheres with your proposal:** The Son, begotten of the Father in Spirit, existed with the Father before creation. When "the beginning" of Gen 1:1 dawned, He was already there — the Only-Begotten, existing in the Spirit-mode of being with the Father.

---

### **Phrase 2: καὶ ὁ Λόγος ἦν πρὸς τὸν Θεόν** — *"and the Word was with God"*

**πρὸς τὸν Θεόν** — *"toward / in relation to / face-to-face with the God."*

The preposition **πρός** with the accusative denotes **orientation, relation, presence-toward.** The Word was **facing** God, in relational presence with Him.

**τὸν Θεόν** — with the definite article — clearly refers to **the Father** (as we established under John 17:3; 1 Cor 8:6).

**Under your proposal:**

The Word — the Son begotten of the Father in Spirit — existed **in personal, face-to-face communion** with the Father before creation. Two distinct figures in living relation. This is precisely what John 17:5 confirms: *"the glory I had with You before the world was."*

**The Word was with God — meaning the Only-Begotten Son was in living, personal communion with the Father in the Spirit-realm before creation.**

---

### **Phrase 3: καὶ Θεὸς ἦν ὁ Λόγος** — *"and the Word was God"*

**This is the most contested clause.** We must handle it with the utmost care under our maxims.

**Critical Greek grammar:**

- **Θεός** is **anarthrous** (no article) — placed first in the clause for emphasis.
- **ὁ Λόγος** has the article — clearly the subject.

The word order is: *"and God was the Word"* — but grammatically, the subject is **ὁ Λόγος** (with the article), and the predicate is **Θεός** (without the article).

**Why does John write anarthrous Θεός here?**

If John had written **καὶ ὁ Θεὸς ἦν ὁ Λόγος** — *"and THE God was the Word"* — this would identify the Word as *identical* to *the God* just mentioned (the Father). This would be a flat contradiction of v. 1b (the Word was WITH the God) and would collapse Father and Son into one Person.

**John deliberately avoided this.** By writing anarthrous **Θεός**, he indicates something different: **the Word was of divine quality / nature / mode of being** — but not identical Person with **ὁ Θεός** of v. 1b.

**Under your proposal:**

The Word — the Only-Begotten Son begotten of the Father in Spirit — shared **the Father's Spirit-nature / divine mode of being.** He was **Θεός in quality** (divine, of the Father's essence, sharing His Spirit-nature) — because He was the Father's Only-Begotten, brought forth from the Father's own being.

This is not identity of Person (v. 1b prevents that reading). This is not "co-equal substance" in the post-Nicene sense (which the text does not deliver). **This is the shared Spirit-nature of the Only-Begotten with the Father.**

**Some careful translations reflect this:**
- *"the Word was divine"* (some translations) — captures qualitative sense.
- *"the Word was God"* (Geneva, most traditional translations) — captures the qualitative claim but risks collapsing Persons.

**The Geneva's rendering is defensible** if understood in the qualitative sense — the Word was of God's nature — not identifying Him as Person-identical to the Father.

**Under your proposal, this resolves beautifully:** The Word shared the Father's Spirit-nature as the Only-Begotten, brought forth from Him — genuinely divine in mode of being, yet distinct in Person from the Father with whom He was face-to-face.

---

### **Phrase 4: οὗτος ἦν ἐν ἀρχῇ πρὸς τὸν Θεόν** — *"This same was in the beginning with God"*

Repetition for emphasis. **The Word was with the Father at the beginning.** Two distinct figures. Personal communion in the Spirit-realm before creation.

---

### **Phrase 5: πάντα δι᾽ αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο** — *"All things came to be through Him"*

Under your proposal:

The Father, who is Spirit (John 4:24), created all things **through** His Only-Begotten Son — the Word — who existed with Him in the Spirit-realm before creation. This is exactly the pattern of 1 Cor 8:6: *"from the Father are all things... through the Lord Jesus Christ are all things."*

The Father is Source (**ἐξ οὗ τὰ πάντα**); the Son is Agent (**δι᾽ οὗ τὰ πάντα**). **The Son as the Father's Word — His creative Utterance, His Spirit-begotten Only-Son — was the means through which the Father brought all things into being.**

This coheres with Ps 32:6 LXX (33:6 MT) — *"By the Word of the LORD were the heavens made, and all the host of them by the breath of His mouth."* The Word (Λόγος) and the Breath (Spirit) — both belong to the LORD (the Father), and through both He creates.

**Under your proposal, John 1:1-3 reads coherently:**

*"In the beginning, the Only-Begotten Son — the Word — was already existing. He was in face-to-face communion with the Father. He shared the Father's Spirit-nature as the Only-Begotten. He was there at the beginning with the Father. All things came into being through Him — the Father's Word, His Spirit-begotten Agent — and without Him nothing came into being that has come into being."*

**This reading:**
- Honors the Greek grammar (anarthrous Θεός preserves distinction from ὁ Θεός of v. 1b).
- Coheres with John 17:5 (glory with the Father before the world was).
- Coheres with Heb 10:5 (a body was prepared for Him later).
- Coheres with 1 Cor 8:6 (Father as Source, Son as Agent).
- Preserves the Shema (Father as the One God).
- Illuminates "only-begotten" (John 1:14, 18).
- Requires no post-Nicene metaphysical accretions.

---

# **PASSAGE B — JOHN 8:58**

## **John 8:58 — Geneva 1599:**

*"Jesus said unto them, Verily, verily I say unto you, before Abraham was, I am."*

---

## **The Context (John 8:56-59)**

Jesus is in dispute with the Jews. He has said: *"Your father Abraham rejoiced to see My day, and he saw it, and was glad"* (v. 56). The Jews mock: *"Thou art not yet fifty years old, and hast thou seen Abraham?"* (v. 57).

Jesus answers: **πρὶν Ἀβραὰμ γενέσθαι ἐγὼ εἰμί.**

The Jews immediately take up stones to kill Him (v. 59) — clearly perceiving that He has made a claim they regard as blasphemy.

---

## **Careful Greek Examination**

### **The Verb Contrast**

**γενέσθαι** — aorist middle infinitive of **γίνομαι** — *"to come to be, to become, to be born."* Used of Abraham — *"before Abraham came to be / was born."*

**εἰμί** — present active — *"I am."* Used of Jesus — *"I am."*

**The contrast is deliberate:** Abraham *came to be* (at a point in time — his birth). Jesus *is* (in continuous existence).

**Jesus is claiming existence that pre-dates Abraham's birth and is not defined by a "coming to be."**

---

### **The Echo of Isaiah 43:10, 46:4 LXX and Exodus 3:14 LXX**

The absolute **ἐγώ εἰμι** (*"I am"*) echoes:

- **Exodus 3:14 LXX:** *"Ἐγώ εἰμι ὁ ὤν"* — *"I am the One who is"* — the LORD's self-identification to Moses at the burning bush.
- **Isaiah 43:10 LXX:** *"ἐγώ εἰμι"* — *"I am He."*
- **Isaiah 46:4 LXX:** *"ἐγώ εἰμι"* — *"I am He."*

**The Jews' violent reaction indicates they perceived Jesus as invoking the divine self-identification of YHWH.**

---

## **Examining Under Our Proposal**

**Under your proposal:**

The Son, begotten of the Father in Spirit, existed as spirit with the Father before creation. Certainly, therefore, He existed before Abraham was born.

**But there is more here than mere pre-existence.**

Jesus says **ἐγώ εἰμι** — invoking the LORD's own self-designation. **How is this possible?**

**Under our foundation:**

**The Father's Name is IN the Son** (Ex 23:21: *"My Name is in Him"*; John 17:11-12: *"I kept them in Thy Name which Thou hast given Me"*).

**The Son, as the Father's Only-Begotten, sharing His Spirit-nature, bears the Father's Name and speaks with the Father's voice.** When the Son uses **ἐγώ εἰμι**, He speaks in the very self-identifying voice of the Father — because He is the Father's Only-Begotten Word, the perfect Image, in whom the Father's Name dwells.

**This is not:**
- The Son claiming to be the Father in Person (which would contradict John 17:3 and 1 Cor 8:6).
- The Son claiming metaphysical co-equality in the post-Nicene sense.

**This is:**
- The Son claiming genuine pre-existence before Abraham (established by your proposal).
- The Son claiming to bear the Father's Name and to speak with the Father's self-identifying voice as the Only-Begotten.

**The Jews' violent reaction was to the second claim** — that Jesus, standing before them as a Man, was claiming the LORD's own voice. To their mind (which did not know Him as the Only-Begotten), this was blasphemy.

**But from Jesus' side, this was truth:** He, as the Only-Begotten Son of the Father, bearing the Father's Name and sharing His Spirit-nature, could legitimately speak **ἐγώ εἰμι** — because the Father's Name dwelt in Him.

**Under your proposal, John 8:58 reads coherently:**

Jesus, the Only-Begotten Son begotten of the Father in Spirit before creation, existed with the Father long before Abraham was born. Bearing the Father's Name (given to Him by the Father), He could speak with the Father's self-identifying voice: *"I am."* This was not a claim to be the Father, but a claim to be **the One in whom the Father's Name dwells** — the Only-Begotten who existed with the Father from before Abraham's birth, indeed from before creation itself.

---

# **PASSAGE C — COLOSSIANS 2:9**

## **Colossians 2:9 — Geneva 1599:**

*"For in him dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily."*

---

## **The Context**

Paul is warning the Colossians against being deceived by philosophy and human tradition (v. 8) — likely including proto-Gnostic ideas that Christ was one of many spiritual mediators. Paul's response: **In Christ dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily.**

Note the context. Paul is not writing a treatise on metaphysics; he is answering a specific error that would diminish Christ. His statement must be understood in its polemical context.

---

## **Careful Greek Examination**

### **πλήρωμα** — *"fullness"*

**πλήρωμα** means *fullness, that which fills, completeness.* It can indicate:
- The full sum of something.
- That which fills a space completely.
- The complete manifestation of something.

### **Θεότης** — *"Godhead"*

**Θεότης** is a rare word — used only here in the NT. It comes from **Θεός** and denotes **the essence, nature, or state of being God** — *"deity, divine nature."*

It should be distinguished from **Θειότης** (Rom 1:20) — *"divinity, divine attributes"* — which speaks more of visible divine qualities.

**Θεότης** is the stronger, more essential term.

### **κατοικεῖ** — *"dwells"*

**κατοικέω** — *"to dwell, to make one's home."* Present active — *"is dwelling."* Continuous state.

### **σωματικῶς** — *"bodily"*

The adverb **σωματικῶς** is critical. It means **"bodily, in bodily form, in a body."**

**This is the decisive word for our examination.** Paul says the fullness of the Godhead dwells in Christ **σωματικῶς** — *bodily*.

---

## **Examining Under Our Proposal**

**Under your proposal:**

The Son was begotten of the Father in Spirit and existed as spirit with the Father before creation. At His incarnation, **the Father prepared a body for Him** (Heb 10:5), and the Son descended into that body.

**Colossians 2:9 speaks precisely to what happened at the incarnation** — and continues in the exalted state:

**The Only-Begotten Son, who eternally shared the Father's Spirit-nature, now — in the prepared body that the Father made for Him — bears the fullness of the Godhead BODILY.**

**Notice what this means:**

- **Before the incarnation**, the Son shared the Father's fullness in the Spirit-mode of being — the Only-Begotten existing with the Father as spirit.
- **At the incarnation**, that same fullness came to dwell **σωματικῶς** — *bodily* — in the prepared body of Jesus.
- **In His present exalted state**, He still bears that fullness bodily — as the glorified Man Christ Jesus at the Father's right hand.

**The word σωματικῶς is decisive.** Paul is not making an abstract metaphysical claim about co-equal substance. He is making a specific incarnational claim: **the fullness of the divine nature has been embodied in the Man Christ Jesus.**

**Under your proposal, this makes complete sense:**

The Son, begotten of the Father in Spirit, shared the Father's Spirit-nature (Θεότης) in His pre-incarnate existence. When the body was prepared for Him and He descended, **the Father's fullness came to dwell in that body — through the Son who bore it.** The Word made flesh **tabernacled** among us (John 1:14 — **ἐσκήνωσεν** — *tabernacled*).

**This is why Paul can say the fullness dwells σωματικῶς** — because the incarnation was a genuine embodiment of what the Son already bore as the Father's Only-Begotten. Not a new nature; a new mode of manifestation of what had always been His as the Only-Begotten.

**And this dwelling continues** — because the exalted Son retains His glorified humanity forever (1 Tim 2:5; Rev 5:6). The fullness of the Father's nature is now permanently manifest bodily in the exalted Man Christ Jesus.

---

## **What Col 2:9 Does NOT Establish**

**Per our maxims, we must be honest about what the verse does not require:**

**It does not require:**
- That the Son is metaphysically identical in essence with the Father in the post-Nicene sense.
- That the Son is co-equal Person of the Godhead in the specific creedal formulation.
- That "Godhead" here refers to a tri-personal essence that Christ shares with the Father and Spirit as one substance.

**It does require:**
- That the fullness of the divine nature dwells in Christ bodily.
- That Christ is more than a mere mediator or spiritual being.
- That the incarnation is genuine — the divine fullness took bodily form in Him.

**Under your proposal:**

The Son, as the Only-Begotten begotten of the Father in Spirit, shares the Father's Spirit-nature (Θεότης). When He was incarnated, the fullness of that nature came to dwell in the prepared body — and continues to dwell in His glorified humanity forever. **He is the perfect embodiment of the invisible Father — the Image made visible (Col 1:15) — bearing the fullness of the Father's own divine nature in bodily form.**

**This is the profound wonder of the incarnation: the Only-Begotten Son, who shared the Father's Spirit-nature from all pre-existence, now bears that nature σωματικῶς in the glorified Man Christ Jesus.**

# **PASSAGE D — HEBREWS 1:8**

## **Hebrews 1:8 — Geneva 1599:**

*"But unto the Son he saith, O God, thy throne is for ever and ever: the scepter of thy kingdom is a scepter of righteousness."*

---

## **This is a Direct Citation of Psalm 45:6-7 LXX**

Let us examine the Psalm in its original context.

### **Psalm 44:7-8 LXX (Psalm 45:6-7 MT):**

### **NETS:**

*"Your throne, O God, is forever and ever. A rod of equity is the rod of your rule; you loved righteousness and hated lawlessness. Therefore God, your God, anointed you with oil of rejoicing beyond your partners."*

*"ὁ θρόνος σου, ὁ Θεός, εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα τοῦ αἰῶνος· ῥάβδος εὐθύτητος ἡ ῥάβδος τῆς βασιλείας σου. ἠγάπησας δικαιοσύνην καὶ ἐμίσησας ἀνομίαν· διὰ τοῦτο ἔχρισέν σε, ὁ Θεός, ὁ Θεός σου, ἔλαιον ἀγαλλιάσεως παρὰ τοὺς μετόχους σου."*

### **1599 Geneva (Ps 45:6-7):**

*"Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever: the scepter of thy kingdom is a scepter of righteousness. Thou lovest righteousness, and hatest wickedness, because God, even thy God hath anointed thee with the oil of gladness above thy fellows."*

---

## **Careful Examination**

### **The Original Psalm**

Psalm 45 is a **royal wedding psalm** — celebrating the king and his bride. It is applied by the author of Hebrews to Christ as the Messianic King.

**Two critical observations:**

**(1)** The psalm addresses the king as **ὁ Θεός** — *"O God"* — a striking address.

**(2)** In the very next verse, the same King is told: **ἔχρισέν σε ὁ Θεός ὁ Θεός σου** — *"God, even Thy God, hath anointed Thee."*

**This is the decisive contextual observation.** The One addressed as **ὁ Θεός** in v. 6 is Himself said to have **a God who anointed Him** in v. 7.

**The King has a God — the God who anointed Him.**

---

## **Examining Under Our Proposal**

**Under your proposal:**

The Son, as the Only-Begotten begotten of the Father in Spirit, shares the Father's Spirit-nature. In this shared divine nature and by virtue of being the Father's Only-Begotten who bears the Father's Name, He can legitimately be addressed as **ὁ Θεός** — *"O God"* — because He bears the divine nature.

**But — and this is critical — the same passage immediately identifies the Father as *"God, even Thy God"* who anointed the Son.**

**The Father is the Son's God.**

This is entirely consistent with:
- **John 20:17** — Jesus after the resurrection: *"I ascend unto My Father, and your Father; and to My God, and your God."*
- **Eph 1:17** — *"the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory."*
- **1 Pet 1:3** — *"the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ."*
- **Rev 3:12** — Jesus speaks of *"My God"* four times.

**The Son has a God. That God is the Father.**

**How, then, is the Son addressed as *"O God"* in Ps 45:6 / Heb 1:8?**

Under your proposal:

The Son, as the Father's Only-Begotten sharing His Spirit-nature and bearing His Name (Ex 23:21), can be addressed with divine titles that reflect His shared nature with the Father. He is genuinely divine — begotten of the Father's own Spirit-essence.

**But this does not make Him identical Person with the Father.** The Father remains His God — the One who anointed Him, sent Him, exalted Him, and to whom He ultimately delivers all things (1 Cor 15:24-28).

**The address "O God" reflects the Son's shared Spirit-nature with the Father. The following clause "God, even Thy God, hath anointed Thee" preserves the Father's supremacy.**

**Under our maxims, this reading:**
- Honors the text (which does address the Son as "O God").
- Preserves the Father's supremacy (which the immediate context establishes).
- Coheres with the whole counsel (the Father as the Son's God throughout the NT).
- Requires no post-Nicene metaphysical formulation to hold together.

**The Son can bear divine titles as the Father's Only-Begotten who shares His Spirit-nature and bears His Name — without displacing the Father as the One God who is Himself the Son's God.**

---

## **A Careful Note on Textual Variants**

There is a translational question in Heb 1:8. The Greek **ὁ θρόνος σου, ὁ Θεός** can be read in two ways:

**Reading 1 (most translations, Geneva):** *"Thy throne, O God, is forever and ever"* — treating **ὁ Θεός** as a vocative address to the Son.

**Reading 2 (some scholars):** *"God is Thy throne forever and ever"* — treating **ὁ Θεός** as the subject.

**Under Reading 2, the theological weight shifts significantly** — the Son is not being addressed as God, but the Father is being called the Son's throne (His foundation, His authority).

**Per our maxims, we do not need to force Reading 2.** Reading 1 (the traditional and most natural rendering) can be honestly held under our proposal — the Son bears divine titles as the Only-Begotten sharing the Father's Spirit-nature, while the Father remains His God.

**Either reading, the essential truth is preserved:** the Son is uniquely honored, and the Father is His God.

# **PASSAGE E — TITUS 2:13 AND 2 PETER 1:1**

I will treat both together since they involve the same grammatical question.

## **Titus 2:13 — Geneva 1599:**

*"Looking for that blessed hope, and appearing of the glory of the mighty God, and of our Saviour Jesus Christ,"*

---

## **2 Peter 1:1 — Geneva 1599:**

*"Simon Peter a servant and an Apostle of Jesus Christ, to you which have obtained like precious faith with us by the righteousness of our God and Saviour Jesus Christ:"*

---

## **The Grammatical Question**

Both verses have the same construction: **τοῦ [μεγάλου] Θεοῦ καὶ Σωτῆρος [ἡμῶν] Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ.**

The question is whether this construction:

**Reading A:** Refers to **one Person** — *"our great God and Savior, Jesus Christ"* (Jesus is called both God and Savior).

**Reading B:** Refers to **two Persons** — *"the great God, and our Savior Jesus Christ"* (the Father is called God, and Jesus is called Savior separately).

The Geneva translates Titus 2:13 in a way that allows Reading B: *"the mighty God, and of our Saviour Jesus Christ"* — treating them as two.

The Geneva translates 2 Pet 1:1 in a way that could allow Reading A: *"our God and Saviour Jesus Christ"* — but the "of our" is somewhat ambiguous.

---

## **The Granville Sharp Rule**

Some Greek grammarians argue (per what is called "Granville Sharp's rule") that when two nouns are joined by **καί** with the article only before the first noun (**τοῦ Α καὶ Β**), they refer to the same person. Under this rule, both Titus 2:13 and 2 Pet 1:1 would be Reading A.

However, this rule has exceptions and is not universally accepted as strict. The construction can bear either reading depending on context.

---

## **Examining Under Our Maxims**

**Per maxim #2** (every doctrine established by two or more witnesses), we must ask: **does the whole counsel establish that Jesus is directly called "God"?**

**What is established beyond dispute:**

- Jesus is the **Beloved Son** (Matt 3:17).
- Jesus is the **Only-Begotten** (John 1:14, 18).
- Jesus is **Lord and Christ** (Acts 2:36).
- Jesus is the **Image of the invisible God** (Col 1:15).
- Jesus bears **the Father's Name** (Ex 23:21; John 17:11).
- Jesus is our **Saviour** (Matt 1:21; Luke 2:11; and many others).
- The **fullness of the Godhead** dwells in Him bodily (Col 2:9).

**What is less common but present:**

- Direct address of Jesus as *"my God"* by Thomas (John 20:28).
- Address of the Son as *"O God"* in Heb 1:8 / Ps 45:6.
- Possible identification of Jesus as *"God and Savior"* in Titus 2:13 and 2 Pet 1:1 (depending on reading).

---

## **Under Your Proposal**

**Both readings can be held under your proposal:**

**If Reading A (one Person — Jesus as our God and Savior):**

The Son, as the Father's Only-Begotten begotten in Spirit and bearing the Father's Name, can be called **Θεός** in the qualitative sense — because He shares the Father's Spirit-nature and is the perfect embodiment of the Father's divine essence (per Col 2:9). This does not make Him identical Person with the Father, but it does mean He can be addressed with divine titles as the Father's Only-Begotten who bears His Name.

**If Reading B (two Persons — the great God, and our Savior Jesus):**

The Father is *"the great God"* whose glory will be revealed at the appearing, and Jesus is *"our Savior"* who is coming to reveal that glory. This preserves the Father as the One God supreme, with Jesus as His Sent Savior.

**Under our maxims, both readings are permissible.** The Greek genuinely admits both. What is decisive is that whichever reading we adopt, we must honor the whole counsel:

- The Father is the One God (John 17:3; 1 Cor 8:6; 1 Tim 2:5).
- Jesus is the Father's Beloved Only-Begotten who bears the Father's Name and shares His Spirit-nature.
- The Father has anointed and exalted Him.
- All glory ultimately returns to the Father.

**Under your proposal:**

Whether Titus 2:13 addresses Jesus as *"our great God and Savior"* (in the sense of the Only-Begotten who bears the divine nature) or whether it distinguishes *"the great God"* (Father) from *"our Savior Jesus Christ"* — either reading is coherent with the framework you have proposed.

**What is not permissible under our maxims is to press these verses beyond what they establish** — to force them to prove co-equal metaphysical substance in the post-Nicene sense that would displace the Father's supremacy or collapse Persons into one.
