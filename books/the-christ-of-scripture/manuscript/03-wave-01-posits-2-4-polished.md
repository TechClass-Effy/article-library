## **POSIT #2 — Genesis 22: The Beloved Son Offered by the Father**

This is the first time Scripture uses the word **ἀγαπητός** *(beloved — the father's cherished one)* — and it uses it of a **father offering his only son**.

**Genesis 22:2 (LXX):**

> And he said, "Take your beloved son Isaak *(Isaac)*, whom you love, and go into the high land, and offer him as a whole burnt offering *(burned entire)* on one of the mountains, whichever I mention to you." — **Genesis 22:2, LXX (NETS)**

**Genesis 22:8 (LXX):**

> And Abraam said, "God will see to a sheep as a whole burnt offering for himself, child." — **Genesis 22:8, LXX (NETS)**

### Observations

**(1) The Father-Son typology is unmistakable:**
- A father offering his **beloved only son** (**τὸν υἱόν σου τὸν ἀγαπητόν**).
- On a mountain (later called Moriah — the same range as Jerusalem, where the Christ will be slain).
- The son **carries the wood of his own sacrifice** (v. 6) — as Christ carried His own cross.
- The father declares *"God will see to a sheep as a whole burnt offering for himself" *(NETS)*, which Brenton prints, *"God will provide himself a sheep for a whole-burnt-offering, my son"* — the very title John the Baptist will give Jesus: **"ἴδε ὁ ἀμνὸς τοῦ Θεοῦ"** *(behold, the Lamb of God)* (John 1:29).

**(2) The two witnesses confirming Genesis 22 as a posit of the Son:**

The author of Hebrews calls Isaac **τὸν μονογενῆ** — *the only-begotten* — the very title given to Jesus:

> By faith Abraham offered up Isaac, when he was tried, and he that had received the promises, offered his only begotten son. (To whom it was said, In Isaac shall thy seed be called.) For he considered that God was able to raise him up even from the dead: from whence he received him also after a sort. — **Hebrews 11:17-19, 1599 Geneva**

**Witness #2 — the Voice from heaven at the Jordan and at the Mount of Transfiguration:**

> And lo, a voice came from heaven, saying, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. — **Matthew 3:17, 1599 Geneva**

The Father's own designation of Jesus — **ὁ Υἱός μου ὁ ἀγαπητός** — *"My beloved Son"* — uses the identical phrasing as Genesis 22:2 LXX. **Two witnesses established.**

---

### What Genesis 22 adds to our portrait of the Son:

- He is the **Father's beloved only-begotten**.
- He is **offered up by the Father** as a sacrifice.
- The Father Himself **provides the lamb**.
- The Son is figuratively **received back from the dead** (Heb 11:19) — a typological resurrection.

## **POSIT #3 — Genesis 49:10: The Scepter and Shiloh**

The first prophecy of the Son's **royalty** and the **gathering of the nations** to Him.

**Genesis 49:10 (LXX):**

> A ruler shall not be wanting *(shall not fail)* from Ioudas *(Judah)* and a leader from his thighs *(his own descendants)* until the things stored up for him come, and he is the expectation of nations. — **Genesis 49:10, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observations

**(1)** The LXX reading is profound: **"ἕως ἂν ἔλθῃ τὰ ἀποκείμενα αὐτῷ"** — *"until there come the things laid up / reserved for Him."* The LXX translators understood this messianically — a *Person* for whom all things are reserved.

**(2)** **"αὐτὸς προσδοκία ἐθνῶν"** — *"HE shall be the expectation of nations."* Not Israel only — **the nations**. The Son of the One God is to be the focal hope of all peoples.

---

### Two witnesses for this posit:

**Haggai 2:7 (LXX):**

> …and I will shake all the nations, and the choice things of all the nations shall come, and I will fill this house with splendor, says the Lord Almighty. — **Haggaios 2:7, LXX (NETS)**

The clause as it rings in the older English — *"the desire of all nations shall come, and I will fill this house with glory"* — is the 1599 Geneva's rendering of this very verse (*Geneva 2:8; the Greek verse is 2:7*).

**Witness #1:** The "Desire/Expectation of the nations" theme is confirmed.

**Revelation 5:5:**

> And one of the Elders said unto me, Weep not: behold, that Lion which is of the tribe of Judah, that root of David, hath obtained to open the book, and to loose the seven seals thereof. — **Revelation 5:5, 1599 Geneva**

**Witness #2:** The promised Ruler of Judah is identified as the slain and risen Lamb in the Apocalypse.

---

### What Genesis 49:10 adds:

- The Son is from the tribe of **Judah**.
- He is a **Ruler** (ἄρχων), holding the scepter.
- He is the **expectation of the nations** — universal in scope.

## **POSIT #4 — Numbers 24:17: The Star Out of Jacob**

**Numbers 24:17 (LXX):**

> I will point to him, and not now; I deem him happy *(I count him blessed)*, but he is not at hand. A star shall dawn out of Iakob *(Jacob)*, and a person shall rise up out of Israel, and he shall crush the chiefs of Moab, and he shall plunder all Seth's sons. — **Numbers 24:17, LXX (NETS)**

---

### Observations

**(1)** **ἀνατελεῖ ἄστρον** — *"a Star shall rise."* The Son is figured as a rising celestial light from Jacob's line.

**(2)** **ἀναστήσεται ἄνθρωπος ἐξ Ἰσραήλ** — *"a person shall rise up out of Israel."* The Hebrew has "scepter"; the LXX clarifies: **a person** *(a Man)*. The Son is here unambiguously declared to be a *human being* arising from Israel.

---

### Two witnesses:

**Matthew 2:2 (Geneva):**

> Saying, Where is that King of the Jews that is born? for we have seen his star in the East, and are come to worship him. — **Matthew 2:2, 1599 Geneva**

**Witness #1:** The Magi — who had access to Balaam's prophecy through Eastern preservation — identify the literal star with the birth of the King.

**Revelation 22:16:**

> I Jesus have sent mine Angel, to testify unto you these things in the Churches: I am the root and the generation of David, and the bright morning star. — **Revelation 22:16, 1599 Geneva**

**Witness #2:** Jesus Himself, in the Apocalypse, claims the title **ὁ ἀστὴρ ὁ λαμπρὸς ὁ πρωϊνός** — *the bright morning Star.* The earliest prophecy and the concluding prophecy meet in His person.

---

### What Numbers 24:17 adds:

- The Son is the **Star** that rises out of Jacob.
- He is a **Man** (**ἄνθρωπος**) — true and complete humanity.
- He is the conqueror of the enemies of God's people.

---

## **SUMMARY: The Earliest Posits of the Son**

From these four foundational texts — given centuries before any council, any creed, any patristic speculation — we have, by *plain reading* and *two-or-more witnesses*:

| Text | What is revealed of the Son |
|---|---|
| **Gen 3:15** | A single Man, Seed of the woman, crusher of the serpent |
| **Gen 22** | The Beloved Only-Begotten Son, offered by the Father, the Lamb God provides |
| **Gen 49:10** | The Ruler from Judah, expectation of the nations |
| **Num 24:17** | The Star and Man arising from Israel |

**Notice carefully what these earliest prophecies do *not* say:** They do not call the Son "God." They do not say He pre-existed. They do not place Him at creation. They speak of Him as a **Man** — beloved, only-begotten, born of a woman, of the tribe of Judah, rising from Israel.

This is the **proper foundation**. Whatever further glories Scripture reveals about the Son must be built upon **this** foundation — not in violation of the Father's exclusive deity declared in Isaiah 44-46, but in the manner Scripture itself unfolds.

Psalm 2 and Psalm 110 (LXX 109) are the most quoted Old Testament texts in the New Testament concerning the Son. They are the keystone of the apostolic preaching about who Jesus is and what office He holds in relation to the One God, the Father.
