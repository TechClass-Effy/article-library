# **PART 5 — THE LORD'S SUPPER**

The Lord's Supper is the appointed act of remembrance and communion that the Lord Himself instituted on the night of His betrayal.

## **The Institution**

### **1 Corinthians 11:23-26 — Geneva 1599:**

*"...That the Lord Jesus in the night when he was betrayed, took bread: And when he had given thanks, he brake it, and said, Take, eat: this is my body, which is broken for you: this do ye in remembrance of me. After the same manner also he took the cup, when he had supped, saying, This cup is the New Testament in my blood: this do as oft as ye drink it, in remembrance of me. For as often as ye shall eat this bread, and drink this cup, ye show the Lord's death till he come."*

---

### Observations

**(1) Three elements:**
- **The Bread** — representing His body **broken for us.**
- **The Cup** — representing **the New Covenant in His blood.**
- **The Remembrance** — *"in remembrance of Me"* (εἰς τὴν ἐμὴν ἀνάμνησιν).

**(2) The proclamation** — *"you show the Lord's death till He come."* The Supper is both a remembrance of the past (His death) and a proclamation toward the future (until He comes).

**(3) The frequency** — *"as often as you drink it."* The NT does not prescribe a specific frequency. The early church appears to have observed it frequently — perhaps weekly (Acts 20:7).

---

## **The Self-Examination**

### **1 Corinthians 11:27-29 — Geneva 1599:**

*"Wherefore whosoever shall eat this bread, and drink the cup of the Lord unworthily, shall be guilty of the body and blood of the Lord. Let every man therefore examine himself, and so let him eat of this bread, and drink of this cup. For he that eateth and drinketh unworthily, eateth and drinketh his own damnation, because he discerneth not the Lord's body."*

---

### Observations

**(1) Self-examination is commanded** — **δοκιμαζέτω ἑαυτόν** — *"let a man examine himself."* Before partaking, the believer is to examine his own heart.

**(2) Eating unworthily** is the danger — not because the believer is worthy in himself (none are), but because **the manner** of partaking can be unworthy — without faith, without discernment, in unconfessed sin, in division from the brethren.

**(3) Discerning the body** — recognizing the significance of the broken body of the Lord, and (in context of 1 Cor 11) recognizing the Body of Christ which is the Church (the Corinthians were divided and treating one another shamefully at the Supper).

---

## **Practical Implications**

**1. Approach the Supper with reverence.** It is not a casual meal but a sacred remembrance.

**2. Examine yourself before partaking.** Confess known sin. Reconcile with brethren. Come with faith.

**3. Use the Supper as an occasion of fresh consecration.** Each remembrance is an opportunity to renew your dedication to the One who died for you.

**4. Look both backward and forward.** Backward to His death; forward to His return.

**5. Recognize the Body.** The Supper is a corporate act — celebrated with the Body of Christ.
