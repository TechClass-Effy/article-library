# **GALATIANS 2:20 — THE FOUNDATIONAL DECLARATION**

## **The Context**

Paul has just rebuked Peter at Antioch for compromising the Gospel by drawing back from table fellowship with Gentile believers (Gal 2:11-14). He has declared that justification is by faith in Christ, not by works of the law (vv. 15-16). And then, in vv. 19-20, he gives the most personal and profound statement of the believer's union with Christ in the entire NT.

---

## **Galatians 2:19-20**

### **1599 Geneva:**
*"For I through the Law am dead to the Law, that I might live unto God. I am crucified with Christ, but I live, yet not I any more, but Christ liveth in me: and in that that I now live in the flesh, I live by the faith in the Son of God, who hath loved me, and given himself for me."*

---

## **CLAUSE-BY-CLAUSE EXAMINATION**

Let me unfold this carefully — phrase by phrase — for it contains, in compressed form, the entire doctrine of identification with Christ.

---

### **Clause 1: ἐγὼ γὰρ διὰ νόμου νόμῳ ἀπέθανον ἵνα Θεῷ ζήσω**

*"For I through the law died to the law, that I might live unto God."*

**(1)** **διὰ νόμου** — *"through the law."* The law itself was the means by which Paul died to the law. How?
- The law condemned him as a sinner (Rom 3:19-20; 7:7-13).
- The law's curse fell on Christ as our substitute (Gal 3:13).
- In Christ, the law's demand was met and its condemnation exhausted.

**(2)** **νόμῳ ἀπέθανον** — *"I died to the law."* Paul's relationship to the law as a means of righteousness was terminated. The law no longer has claim upon him as the basis of his standing before God.

**(3)** **ἵνα Θεῷ ζήσω** — *"that I might live unto God."* The purpose of dying to the law is **to live unto God**. The law's killing function led to a new resurrection life **for God** — a life oriented toward the Father, lived in His presence and for His glory.

---

### **Clause 2: Χριστῷ συνεσταύρωμαι**

*"I have been crucified with Christ."*

**This is the foundational declaration of identification.** Let us examine every element.

**(1)** **Χριστῷ** — dative case — *"with Christ"* or *"in Christ."* The dative of association.

**(2)** **συνεσταύρωμαι** — perfect passive of **συσταυρόω** — *"I have been co-crucified."*

The prefix **σύν-** means *"together with."* The believer is **crucified together with Christ.**

The tense is **perfect** — a completed past action with continuing present effect. **It happened, and its effects remain in force.** Paul did not say *"I am being crucified"* (present) or *"I will be crucified"* (future). He says *"I have been crucified, and remain in that crucified state."*

The voice is **passive** — *"I was crucified"* — i.e., **God did this to me.** The Father, through the Son's cross, included me in the Son's death. I did not crucify myself; the Father united me with the Son's cross.

**(3) What does this mean — that the believer was crucified with Christ?**

It does not mean Paul was historically nailed to Christ's cross. It means **the believer's old life — his identity in Adam, his identity as a sinner, his identity under condemnation — was put to death when Christ died.** The Father reckoned the believer into the Son's death.

This is what theologians have called **federal headship** or **representative union** — but Scripture's own language is **identification**: the believer is **identified with Christ** in His death so completely that what happened to Christ happened to him.

**Cross-witness — Romans 6:6:**

**1599 Geneva:** *"Knowing this, that our old man is crucified with him, that the body of sin might be destroyed, that henceforth we should not serve sin."*

The same verb — **συνεσταυρώθη** — *"was crucified together with."* Paul's individual testimony in Galatians 2:20 is universalized in Romans 6: **our old man is crucified with Christ.** Two witnesses establish: **every believer was co-crucified with Christ at the cross.**

---

### **Clause 3: ζῶ δὲ οὐκέτι ἐγώ**

*"I live; yet no longer I."*

**This is the death of the self.** Paul lives — but the "I" that lives is no longer the old Paul. The old man, the Adam-self, is gone. The Pharisee Saul of Tarsus has been put to death.

The phrasing is paradoxical: **"I live, yet not I."** Paul does not deny his ongoing existence; he denies that the ongoing existence is the old self. The man who lives now is not who he was.

**Cross-witness — 2 Corinthians 5:17:**

**1599 Geneva:** *"Therefore if any man be in Christ, let him be a new creature. Old things are passed away: behold, all things are become new."*

The believer in Christ is **a new creation.** The old has passed away. **This is established by two witnesses (Gal 2:20 and 2 Cor 5:17).**

---

### **Clause 4: ζῇ δὲ ἐν ἐμοὶ Χριστός**

*"but Christ lives in me."*

**This is the indwelling we examined in Part F**, now stated in Paul's own personal experience.

**(1)** **ζῇ** — present active — *"He lives, continually lives."* Christ's living in Paul is **ongoing**, not a past event.

**(2)** **ἐν ἐμοί** — *"in me."* The same preposition Jesus used in John 14:20 — *"and I in you."* The indwelling promised by Jesus is the lived reality of the apostle.

**(3) The chain:**
- The believer **died with Christ** (Clause 2).
- The believer's old self is **no longer living** (Clause 3).
- **Christ now lives in the believer** (Clause 4).

**The death of the old self made room for Christ's indwelling life.** This is the spiritual mechanism: the old must die before the new can live. The believer's ongoing existence is now the vehicle of Christ's life.

---

### **Clause 5: ὃ δὲ νῦν ζῶ ἐν σαρκί, ἐν πίστει ζῶ τῇ τοῦ Υἱοῦ τοῦ Θεοῦ**

*"And the life which I now live in the flesh, I live by faith of the Son of God."*

**(1)** **νῦν ζῶ ἐν σαρκί** — *"I now live in the flesh."* Paul acknowledges that he still lives in his physical body. The death of the old self does not mean physical death; the body remains. But the principle of life in the body has changed.

**(2)** **ἐν πίστει ζῶ τῇ τοῦ Υἱοῦ τοῦ Θεοῦ** — *"I live in faith of the Son of God."*

The genitive **τοῦ Υἱοῦ τοῦ Θεοῦ** can be read either as:
- **Objective genitive:** *"faith in the Son of God"* (the Son is the object of the believer's faith).
- **Subjective genitive:** *"the faith of the Son of God"* (the Son's own faithfulness as the basis of our life).

**Both readings carry profound truth, and both are likely intended.** The believer lives by faith in the Son, *and* the believer lives by the Son's own faithful self-giving. The Geneva preserves the ambiguity: *"by the faith in the Son of God."*

---

### **Clause 6: τοῦ ἀγαπήσαντός με καὶ παραδόντος ἑαυτὸν ὑπὲρ ἐμοῦ**

*"who loved me, and gave Himself for me."*

**(1)** **τοῦ ἀγαπήσαντός με** — *"the One who loved me."* Aorist participle — a definite past act of love. The Son **loved** Paul (and every believer) with a specific, personal love.

**(2)** **παραδόντος ἑαυτὸν ὑπὲρ ἐμοῦ** — *"and gave Himself for me."* Aorist participle — again a definite past act. The Son **gave Himself** (His own person, His own life) **for me** (on my behalf, in my place).

**The personal pronouns are striking.** Paul does not say *"who loved us"* or *"who gave Himself for us"* — but **"who loved ME and gave Himself for ME."** Each believer can say this personally. **The Son's love and self-giving is for me, individually, by name.**

---

## **SYNTHESIS OF GALATIANS 2:20**

Paul has compressed into a single verse the following established truths:

1. **The believer has been co-crucified with Christ** (perfect tense — completed and abiding).
2. **The believer's old self no longer lives** (the Adam-identity is terminated).
3. **Christ now lives in the believer** (present, continuous indwelling).
4. **The believer's ongoing physical life is lived by faith in / of the Son of God.**
5. **This is grounded in the Son's personal love and self-giving for the believer.**

This is the **doctrinal seed** from which the entire NT teaching on the believer's identification with Christ unfolds. We must now examine every other major witness.

---

# **THE FULL COUNSEL — THE BELIEVER'S IDENTIFICATION IN CHRIST**

The NT establishes that the believer is identified with Christ in **every major redemptive act** of the Son's work. Let us examine each in order.

---

## **POINT 1 — CO-CRUCIFIED WITH CHRIST**

### **Galatians 2:20** — already examined.

### **Romans 6:6:**

### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Knowing this, that our old man is crucified with him, that the body of sin might be destroyed, that henceforth we should not serve sin."*

### **Galatians 5:24:**

### **1599 Geneva:**
*"For they that are Christ's, have crucified the flesh with the affections and the lusts."*

### **Galatians 6:14:**

### **1599 Geneva:**
*"But God forbid that I should rejoice, but in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, whereby the world is crucified unto me, and I unto the world."*

---

### Observation

**Multiple witnesses establish:** The believer's old man has been crucified with Christ. The flesh (the principle of sin in our nature) has been crucified. The world has been crucified to us, and we to the world. **The cross of Christ is the believer's cross.** What Christ accomplished there, we are reckoned as accomplishing in Him.
