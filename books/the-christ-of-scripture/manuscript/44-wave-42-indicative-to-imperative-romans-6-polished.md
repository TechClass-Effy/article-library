# **THE INDICATIVE-TO-IMPERATIVE PATTERN**

Before we enter Romans 6, we must understand what Paul is doing.

Throughout the NT — but especially in Paul — there is a consistent structure:

- **The Indicative:** A declaration of what *is true* of the believer in Christ. "You *are* dead to sin." "You *have been* crucified with Christ." "You *are* in Christ Jesus." "You *have* been raised with Him."

- **The Imperative:** A command based on the indicative. "Therefore, do not let sin reign." "Walk worthy." "Put off the old man." "Put on the new."

**The order is critical.** Paul never commands without first establishing the indicative. He does not say *"Try to become dead to sin"* — he says *"Reckon yourselves to be dead to sin, because you ARE dead to sin."* The command flows from the reality, not toward it.

This is the **heart of the Gospel's ethical power.** The Christian life is not *"work hard to become what you wish you were."* It is *"walk in the reality of what you already are in Christ."* Sanctification is not the achievement of an unreal status; it is the working out, in daily life, of what is already true positionally.

Romans 6 is the chapter where Paul most explicitly lays this pattern bare.

---

# **THE CONTEXT — Romans 5-6**

Paul has just declared in Romans 5:20-21 that **where sin abounded, grace did much more abound.** He has just declared that grace reigns through righteousness unto eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

This sets up the question that opens Romans 6 — the question of every shallow critic of grace: *"If grace abounds where sin abounds, shall we not sin more, that grace may abound more?"*

Paul's answer to this question is the entire chapter — and his answer is grounded entirely in **the believer's identification with Christ.**

---

# **ROMANS 6:1-2 — THE QUESTION AND THE PIVOT**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"What shall we say then? Shall we continue still in sin, that grace may abound? God forbid. how shall we, that are dead to sin, live yet therein?"*

---

### Observations of foundational importance

**(1)** Paul's response to the question *"shall we continue in sin?"* is **μὴ γένοιτο** — *"God forbid"* / *"may it never be"* — the strongest negation in Greek. The very thought is unthinkable.

**(2)** But notice carefully **why** Paul says it is unthinkable. He does not say *"Because sin is bad."* He does not say *"Because God's law forbids it."* He says: **οἵτινες ἀπεθάνομεν τῇ ἁμαρτίᾳ, πῶς ἔτι ζήσομεν ἐν αὐτῇ;** — *"We who died to sin, how shall we still live in it?"*

**This is the indicative.** Before Paul will give a single command in this chapter, he establishes the **fact** of what has happened to the believer: **we died to sin.** The aorist tense **ἀπεθάνομεν** points to a definite past event — **the believer's death to sin at the cross of Christ.**

The argument is **ontological, not merely behavioral.** Paul does not argue that the believer *should not* continue in sin because it would be wrong. He argues that the believer *cannot consistently* continue in sin because **he has died to it.** Continuing in sin is **a contradiction of the new reality of his being.**

This is the indicative-to-imperative pattern in its purest form: the moral question is answered by a declaration of fact.

---

# **ROMANS 6:3-5 — THE FOUNDATIONAL INDICATIVE: BAPTIZED INTO HIS DEATH**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Know ye not, that all we which have been baptized into Jesus Christ, have been baptized into his death? We are buried then with him by baptism into his death, that like as Christ was raised up from the dead to the glory of the Father, so we also should walk in newness of life. For if we be planted with him to the similitude of his death, even so shall we be to the similitude of his resurrection."*

---

### Observations of foundational importance

**(1)** **ἢ ἀγνοεῖτε** — *"or are you ignorant?"* Paul appeals to knowledge they should already have. The indicative is something the believer **must know**. Ignorance of one's identification with Christ leaves the believer powerless to walk in the imperative.

**(2)** **ὅσοι ἐβαπτίσθημεν εἰς Χριστὸν Ἰησοῦν, εἰς τὸν θάνατον αὐτοῦ ἐβαπτίσθημεν** — *"as many as were baptized into Christ Jesus, were baptized into His death."*

**Baptism here is more than an outward rite** — it is the visible enactment of the believer's identification with Christ. To be baptized **into Christ** is to be baptized **into His death.** The two cannot be separated. We are not joined to a sin-bearing Christ at one point and detached from His death-bearing reality at another. **To be in Him is to be in His death.**

**(3)** **συνετάφημεν** — *"we were buried together with Him."* The aorist passive — completed past action done to us. **We have been buried together with Christ.** Burial is the seal of death — what is buried is settled as dead.

**(4)** **The purpose: ἵνα... ἐν καινότητι ζωῆς περιπατήσωμεν** — *"that we should walk in newness of life."*

**Here we encounter the first imperative implied — though stated as purpose.** The believer's burial with Christ has a stated purpose: that we should walk in newness of life. The indicative serves a practical end.

**The walk is grounded in the resurrection** — *"like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life."* Our walk corresponds to His resurrection. As He was raised to a new mode of life, so we walk in a new mode of life.

**(5)** **σύμφυτοι γεγόναμεν** — *"we have become grown-together / united / planted together."* The word **σύμφυτος** is striking — it means *"grown together"* (as a graft grows together with its stock). **The believer is grown-together with Christ — in the likeness of His death, and also in the likeness of His resurrection.**

This is the indicative reality: we are organically united with Christ in both His death and His resurrection. The resurrection-union is as certain as the death-union.

---

# **ROMANS 6:6-7 — THE OLD MAN CRUCIFIED**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Knowing this, that our old man is crucified with him, that the body of sin might be destroyed, that henceforth we should not serve sin. For he that is dead, is freed from sin."*

---

### Observations of supreme importance

**(1)** **τοῦτο γινώσκοντες** — *"knowing this."* Again, the appeal to knowledge. The indicative must be known before it can be lived.

**(2)** **ὁ παλαιὸς ἡμῶν ἄνθρωπος συνεσταυρώθη** — *"our old man was crucified together [with Him]."*

**The "old man"** (ὁ παλαιὸς ἄνθρωπος) is the person we were in Adam — the sinner, the rebel, the bondservant of sin, the one under condemnation. **That person was crucified with Christ.** The verb is aorist passive — a definite past action done to us by the Father at the cross.

**This is one of the most liberating truths in all Scripture.** The person who lived before conversion — who was a slave to sin, dominated by the flesh, alienated from God — **does not exist anymore.** He was crucified. The believer is not the old man trying to be good; the believer is **a new man** in whom **the old man no longer lives.**

**(3) The purpose:** **ἵνα καταργηθῇ τὸ σῶμα τῆς ἁμαρτίας, τοῦ μηκέτι δουλεύειν ἡμᾶς τῇ ἁμαρτίᾳ** — *"that the body of sin might be rendered powerless, that we should no longer serve sin."*

**καταργέω** — *"to render inoperative, to nullify, to deprive of power."* The body of sin (the entire structure of sinful patterns, habits, and identity that ruled the old man) **has been rendered powerless** by the crucifixion of the old man with Christ.

**The result:** **τοῦ μηκέτι δουλεύειν ἡμᾶς τῇ ἁμαρτίᾳ** — *"that we should no longer be slaves to sin."*

**This is the foundation of Christian freedom from sin's dominion.** Not that sin is gone (we will see it can still be yielded to), but that **the slavery is broken.** The old chains have been severed. We are no longer compelled to obey sin's commands. We are now free to obey righteousness.

**(4)** **ὁ γὰρ ἀποθανὼν δεδικαίωται ἀπὸ τῆς ἁμαρτίας** — *"For he who has died has been justified / set free from sin."*

A legal principle: death pays sin's claim and ends sin's jurisdiction. A dead man cannot be summoned to obey his old master. The believer, having died with Christ, **has been judicially set free** from sin's claim. The court has no jurisdiction over a corpse.

---

# **ROMANS 6:8-10 — LIVING WITH HIM**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Wherefore, if we be dead with Christ, we believe that we shall live also with him, Knowing that Christ being raised from the dead, dieth no more: death hath no more dominion over him. For in that he died, he died once to sin but in that he liveth, he liveth to God."*

---

### Observations

**(1)** **εἰ δὲ ἀπεθάνομεν σὺν Χριστῷ, πιστεύομεν ὅτι καὶ συζήσομεν αὐτῷ** — *"If we died with Christ, we believe we shall also live with Him."*

The death-union guarantees the life-union. Paul presents this as an article of faith — **πιστεύομεν** — we *believe* this. The believer's confidence in his new life with Christ is grounded in his death with Christ.

**(2)** **Χριστὸς ἐγερθεὶς ἐκ νεκρῶν οὐκέτι ἀποθνῄσκει** — *"Christ, having been raised from the dead, dies no more."*

The risen Christ is in a **once-for-all** state. He will never die again. Death has no more dominion over Him. **And we are in Him.** Therefore, the believer's new life, like Christ's, is one over which death has no more dominion. Sin cannot kill us again; we are alive forever in Him.

**(3)** **ὃ ἀπέθανεν τῇ ἁμαρτίᾳ ἀπέθανεν ἐφάπαξ· ὃ δὲ ζῇ ζῇ τῷ Θεῷ** — *"What He died, He died to sin once for all; what He lives, He lives to God."*

Christ's death was a death **to sin** — not that He sinned, but that He died in relation to sin (bearing it, exhausting its claim, ending its power over Him as our representative). Christ's life now is **a life unto God** — entirely oriented to the Father.

**This is the pattern for the believer.** As Christ died once for all to sin and now lives unto God, so we, in Him, died once for all to sin and now live unto God. The pattern of His existence is the pattern of ours.

---

# **ROMANS 6:11 — THE FIRST IMPERATIVE: RECKON!**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Likewise think ye also, that ye are dead to sin, but are alive to God in Jesus Christ our Lord."*

---

### Observations of supreme importance

**Here is the first explicit imperative of the chapter — and it is breathtakingly precise.**

**(1)** **οὕτως καὶ ὑμεῖς** — *"so also you."* The pattern that has just been established for Christ (died to sin, lives to God) is now applied to the believer.

**(2)** **λογίζεσθε** — present imperative — *"reckon, count, calculate, consider."* This is a continuing, present action. **Keep on reckoning yourselves.**

The word **λογίζομαι** is a bookkeeping term. It means *to make a careful calculation and act on it as true.* The believer is commanded to **add up the facts of his identification with Christ** and treat the resulting reality as the operative truth of his life.

**The reckoning does not create the reality.** The reality is already true (vv. 1-10 have established it). The reckoning is the believer's daily, moment-by-moment **agreement** with the reality the Father has already established.

**(3) Two facts to be reckoned:**

**νεκροὺς μὲν τῇ ἁμαρτίᾳ** — *"dead to sin."* The believer is to count himself as dead to sin. When sin makes its claim — "obey me, you have always obeyed me" — the believer says: *"You speak to a dead man. I am dead to your claims."*

**ζῶντας δὲ τῷ Θεῷ ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ** — *"alive to God in Christ Jesus."* The believer is to count himself as alive — to the Father — in Christ Jesus. **The new identity is Godward.** The orientation of the new life is toward the Father, through the Son.

**(4) This is the heart of the indicative-to-imperative pattern.** The reality has been established by the Father at the cross and resurrection. The believer's task is to *reckon it true* and walk accordingly.

This is **faith applied to identity.** It is not pretending; it is agreeing with the truth. The Father reckons us dead with Christ — we reckon ourselves dead with Christ. Our reckoning aligns with His.

---

# **ROMANS 6:12-13 — THE PRACTICAL IMPERATIVES**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"Let not sin reign therefore in your mortal body, that ye should obey it in the lusts thereof: Neither give ye your members, as weapons of unrighteousness unto sin: but give yourselves unto God, as they that are alive from the dead, and give your members as weapons of righteousness unto God."*

---

### Observations of decisive importance

**Now the imperatives multiply.** Note the careful logic: because v. 11 has been reckoned (the indicative agreed to), now the practical commands follow.

**(1)** **Μὴ οὖν βασιλευέτω ἡ ἁμαρτία** — *"Let sin not reign."* Present imperative — a continuing prohibition. **Stop letting sin reign. Do not allow it to take the throne.**

The verb is **βασιλεύω** — *to reign as king.* Sin **wants to reign.** Sin's ambition is dominion. **And the believer's task is to refuse it the throne.**

Notice: Paul does not say *"sin cannot be present in you."* He says *"do not let it reign."* Sin may still attempt to assert itself; the question is whether you grant it sovereignty. The believer has the standing to refuse — because he died to sin's slavery (v. 6).

**(2)** **ἐν τῷ θνητῷ ὑμῶν σώματι** — *"in your mortal body."* This is honest. The body remains mortal — still subject to its passions, its appetites, its weariness, its corruption. The body has not yet been redeemed (Rom 8:23). But sin's dominion in the body is to be refused.

**(3)** **εἰς τὸ ὑπακούειν αὐτῇ ταῖς ἐπιθυμίαις αὐτοῦ** — *"so as to obey it in its desires."* Sin reigns through the body's desires when we obey them. To refuse sin's reign is to refuse to obey the body's sinful desires.

**(4)** **μηδὲ παριστάνετε τὰ μέλη ὑμῶν ὅπλα ἀδικίας τῇ ἁμαρτίᾳ** — *"nor present your members as weapons of unrighteousness to sin."*

**ὅπλα** — *weapons / instruments.* The body's members (hands, feet, eyes, tongue, mind) are tools that can be used as **weapons** — for sin or for righteousness. The believer is commanded **not to make his members available to sin as weapons against God and against righteousness.**

**(5)** **ἀλλὰ παραστήσατε ἑαυτοὺς τῷ Θεῷ** — *"but present yourselves to God."*

Now the **positive command:** present yourselves **to God.** The verb is **παρίστημι** — *to present, to hand over, to place at someone's disposal.* The aorist tense here suggests a definitive presentation — make this transfer.

**ὡσεὶ ἐκ νεκρῶν ζῶντας** — *"as those alive from the dead."* The basis of the self-presentation: **you are alive from the dead.** Your reckoning of vv. 11 informs your presentation of vv. 13.

**(6)** **καὶ τὰ μέλη ὑμῶν ὅπλα δικαιοσύνης τῷ Θεῷ** — *"and your members as weapons of righteousness to God."*

The same members that were weapons of unrighteousness are now presented as **weapons of righteousness to God.** The hands that took are now hands that give. The tongue that lied is now the tongue that speaks truth. The mind that imagined evil is now the mind that meditates on what is true, honorable, just, pure, lovely, and of good report (cf. Phil 4:8).

---

# **ROMANS 6:14 — THE PROMISE THAT SUSTAINS THE COMMANDS**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"For sin shall not have dominion over you: for ye are not under the Law, but under grace."*

---

### Observations of foundational importance

**(1)** **ἁμαρτία γὰρ ὑμῶν οὐ κυριεύσει** — *"For sin shall not have dominion over you."*

**This is a promise, not merely a command.** Future indicative — **shall not.** Paul declares that sin **will not** rule over the believer.

But notice: this comes right after the commands of vv. 12-13. The order is profound:
- The believer is commanded to refuse sin's reign (v. 12).
- The believer is commanded to present himself to God (v. 13).
- And then: sin **shall not** have dominion (v. 14).

**The promise is grounded in the obedience to the command — and the obedience to the command is grounded in the indicative reality of co-crucifixion and resurrection.** The Christian life flows like this:
1. **What is true** (indicative — vv. 1-10).
2. **What we reckon** (v. 11).
3. **How we act** (vv. 12-13).
4. **What is then experientially real** (v. 14).

**(2)** **οὐ γάρ ἐστε ὑπὸ νόμον ἀλλὰ ὑπὸ χάριν** — *"For you are not under law but under grace."*

This is the foundation of the promise. Under the law, sin had power because the law condemned the sinner and provoked the flesh (Rom 7:8-11). Under grace, the believer stands in the favor of God, possessing the righteousness of Christ, indwelt by the Spirit, free from condemnation. **Sin cannot rule a person who stands in grace** — for grace has broken sin's grip and provided the resources for victory.

---

# **ROMANS 6:15-23 — THE SLAVERY MOTIF**

The chapter continues with Paul addressing a second objection — *"Shall we sin because we are not under law but under grace?"* — with a parallel structure: indicative, then imperative.

### **Romans 6:15-18 (selections):**

### **1599 Geneva:**
*"What then? shall we sin, because we are not under the Law, but under grace? God forbid. Know ye not, that to whomsoever ye give yourselves as servants to obey, his servants ye are to whom ye obey... But God be thanked, that ye have been the servants of sin, but ye have obeyed from the heart unto the form of the doctrine, whereunto ye were delivered. Being then made free from sin, ye are made the servants of righteousness."*

---

### Observation

Paul presents two slaveries: slavery to sin (which leads to death) and slavery to righteousness/God (which leads to sanctification and eternal life). The believer has been **set free** from the first and **made a slave** to the second.

**The indicative:** You **were** slaves of sin; you **have been** set free; you **are now** slaves of righteousness (v. 18). These are settled facts.

**The imperative implied:** Therefore present your members to righteousness, just as before you presented them to sin (v. 19).

---

# **ROMANS 6:23 — THE CONCLUDING TRUTH**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"For the wages of sin is death: but the gift of God is eternal life, through Jesus Christ our Lord."*

---

### Observation

The chapter closes with the great contrast. Sin **pays wages** — and its wage is death. God **gives** — and His gift is eternal life. Sin earns; grace gives. The two paths are utterly different in source and destination.

And the gift is **ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ τῷ Κυρίῳ ἡμῶν** — *"in Christ Jesus our Lord."* Once again, the locus of the new life is identification with Christ.

---

# **THE FULL PATTERN — SYNTHESIS OF ROMANS 6**

| Section | Indicative or Imperative | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **vv. 1-2** | Indicative declared | We died to sin |
| **vv. 3-5** | Indicative expanded | Baptized into His death; buried with Him; will share His resurrection |
| **vv. 6-7** | Indicative explained | Old man crucified; body of sin rendered powerless; freed from sin |
| **vv. 8-10** | Indicative applied | Living with Him in His resurrection life pattern |
| **v. 11** | **First imperative** | **Reckon yourselves dead to sin, alive to God** |
| **vv. 12-13** | Practical imperatives | Do not let sin reign; do not yield members to sin; yield to God |
| **v. 14** | Promise + indicative | Sin shall not have dominion; you are under grace |
| **vv. 15-22** | Indicative + imperative | Slaves of righteousness now; yield to righteousness |
| **v. 23** | Concluding contrast | Wages of sin = death; gift of God = eternal life in Christ |

# **THE GOVERNING PATTERN: INDICATIVE → RECKON → ACT**

Romans 6 establishes the fundamental Christian operation:

1. **Know what God has done** (the indicative — vv. 1-10).
2. **Reckon it true of yourself** (v. 11).
3. **Act on the reckoning** (vv. 12-13).

This is the precise reverse of the world's pattern, which is:
1. *Try to act righteously.*
2. *Hope to become righteous through your acts.*
3. *Believe in your status only after performance.*

**The Gospel pattern is the opposite:**
1. *You ARE righteous in Christ (indicative).*
2. *Reckon it true (faith).*
3. *Act consistently with what is true (obedience).*

The Christian does not act in order to become; he acts because he has become.

---

# **CROSS-WITNESSES — THE PATTERN ELSEWHERE**

This indicative-to-imperative pattern is not unique to Romans 6 — it is the fundamental pattern of all NT ethics. Let us confirm by examining the most explicit parallels.

---

## **EPHESIANS — THE INDICATIVE OF CHAPTERS 1-3, THE IMPERATIVE OF 4-6**

The entire epistle to the Ephesians displays this pattern on a grand scale.

**Chapters 1-3 are pure indicative:**
- Chosen in Him before the foundation of the world (1:4).
- Predestined to adoption (1:5).
- Accepted in the Beloved (1:6).
- Redeemed through His blood (1:7).
- Sealed with the Holy Spirit (1:13).
- Made alive together with Christ (2:5).
- Raised up together (2:6).
- Seated together in the heavenly places in Christ Jesus (2:6).
- Fellow-citizens with the saints (2:19).
- Members of God's household (2:19).
- Built together for a habitation of God (2:22).
- Filled with the fullness of God (3:19).

**Chapters 4-6 are pure imperative — and they begin with the pivot word.**

### **Ephesians 4:1:**

### **1599 Geneva:**
*"I therefore, being prisoner in the Lord, pray you that ye walk worthy of the vocation whereunto ye are called,"*

The word **οὖν** — *"therefore"* — is the pivot. Three chapters of indicative produce one *"therefore"* that launches three chapters of imperative.

**The imperative of Ephesians 4-6 is grounded entirely in the indicative of Ephesians 1-3.** Walk worthy of the calling — *because of all that Christ has already accomplished in you.*

---

## **COLOSSIANS 3 — THE EXPLICIT CONNECTION**

### **Colossians 3:1-5:**

### **1599 Geneva:**
*"If ye then be risen with Christ, seek those things which are above, where Christ sitteth at the right hand of God. Set your affections on things which are above, and not on things which are on the earth. For ye are dead, and your life is hid with Christ in God. When Christ which is our life, shall appear, then shall ye also appear with him in glory. Mortify therefore your members which are on the earth..."*

---

### Observation

**The pattern is perfectly visible:**

- **Indicative (v. 1):** *"If you have been raised with Christ..."* — you have been.
- **Indicative (v. 3):** *"For you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God."*
- **Indicative (v. 4):** *"You shall appear with Him in glory."*
- **Imperative (v. 1):** *"Seek those things which are above."*
- **Imperative (v. 2):** *"Set your affection on things above."*
- **Imperative (v. 5):** *"Mortify therefore your members which are on the earth..."*

The word **οὖν** — *"therefore"* — appears again as the pivot in v. 5. **Because you have died (indicative), therefore mortify your members (imperative).**

This is the same Romans 6 pattern, restated.

---

## **GALATIANS 5:24-25 — A SUCCINCT STATEMENT**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"For they that are Christ's, have crucified the flesh, with the affections and the lusts. If we live in the Spirit, let us also walk in the Spirit."*

---

### Observation

**v. 24 — Indicative:** Those who belong to Christ **have crucified** the flesh. The verb is aorist — completed past action.

**v. 25 — Imperative based on the indicative:** *"If we live in the Spirit, let us also walk in the Spirit."* Since we live by the Spirit (an established reality), let us walk by the Spirit (the corresponding action).

The same pattern in compressed form.

---

## **EPHESIANS 4:22-24 — PUTTING OFF AND PUTTING ON**


### **1599 Geneva:**
*"That is, that ye cast off, concerning the conversation in time past, that old man, which is corrupt through the deceivable lusts, And be renewed in the spirit of your mind, And put on the new man, which after God is created unto righteousness, and true holiness."*

---

### Observation

The believer is to **put off** the old man and **put on** the new man. But notice: **the new man has already been created — κτισθέντα — perfect tense.** The new man is not something the believer creates by his obedience; the new man has been created by God in Christ. The believer **puts on** what already exists in him through new creation.

This is the same indicative-imperative pattern: **the new man exists (indicative); put him on (imperative).**

# **THE PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS FOR THE MODERN BELIEVER**

Now we must bring this home to daily life. How does the Romans 6 pattern reshape the practical walk of the modern believer?

---

## **1. The Reckoning Is the Daily Foundation**

The believer's daily life begins with **the reckoning of v. 11.** This is not a one-time event at conversion; it is a moment-by-moment posture of mind.

When the believer wakes in the morning, he must reckon:
- *"I am dead to sin. I am alive to God in Christ Jesus."*
- *"I have been crucified with Christ; the life I now live in the flesh, I live by faith of the Son of God."*
- *"My life is hidden with Christ in God."*

This reckoning is **faith applied to identity** — and it is the basis on which the day's obedience proceeds.

When temptation comes during the day, the believer's first response is not *"I must resist this"* but *"I am dead to this. Sin no longer has dominion over me. I am alive to God in Christ Jesus. I refuse the throne to sin."*

**The believer fights from victory, not for victory.** The victory has been won at the cross. The fight is to walk in what has been won.

---

## **2. The Body Is to Be Presented, Not Indulged**

The body is mortal and still bears the desires that can be exploited by sin. **But the body is not to be allowed to dictate.** The believer presents his body to God as a living sacrifice (Rom 12:1).

This means practically:
- **The eyes:** what we look at — are they weapons of righteousness or unrighteousness?
- **The ears:** what we listen to — does it build us up or pull us toward sin?
- **The tongue:** what we speak — does it bless or curse, edify or tear down?
- **The hands:** what we do — are they instruments of service to God or to self/sin?
- **The feet:** where we go — are they leading us toward righteousness or temptation?
- **The mind:** what we dwell on — are our thoughts being taken captive to obey Christ (2 Cor 10:5)?
- **The heart's affections:** what we love — are they set on things above (Col 3:1-2)?

The believer presents each member of his body, daily and moment by moment, to God as a weapon of righteousness.

---

## **3. Sin's Authority Is Refused, Not Negotiated With**

The believer does not negotiate with sin. He does not say, *"Maybe a little bit is okay."* He refuses sin's authority entirely.

When sin makes its claim — *"You always do this; it's who you are; you might as well give in"* — the believer responds:
- *"You are speaking to a dead man. I died to you at the cross."*
- *"My identity is not in you. I am in Christ Jesus."*
- *"I refuse you the throne. Christ is Lord."*

The fight is to **agree with reality** — to refuse the lie that we are still slaves and to stand in the freedom Christ has purchased.

---

## **4. The Walk Is in Newness of Life**

The believer's walk is **καινότης ζωῆς** — *newness of life* (Rom 6:4). This is not an effort to keep old rules; it is a new mode of being.

Practically, this means:
- A new orientation: toward the Father, in the Son, by the Spirit.
- New affections: loving what the Father loves, hating what He hates.
- New motivations: not for self-preservation or self-glory, but for the glory of the Father in the Son.
- New patterns: turning from the courses of the world to the path of Christ.
- New companions (where possible): seeking fellowship with the saints rather than communion with the world.
- New speech, new habits, new disciplines: increasingly conforming to the pattern of Christ.

**The new walk flows from the new identity.** It is not a list of behaviors to perform; it is a life to live out of who we are in Christ.

---

## **5. Failure Is Met with Reckoning, Not Despair**

When the believer fails — when sin gains a momentary victory, when the old patterns reassert themselves — the response is **not** to abandon the indicative.

The response is to **return to the reckoning.**
- *"I have failed, but I have not become what I once was."*
- *"My identity is in Christ, not in this failure."*
- *"I confess and turn, knowing that the blood of Christ cleanses me from all sin (1 John 1:7-9)."*
- *"I reckon myself again dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus."*
- *"I present myself again to God."*

**The reckoning is repeated, not abandoned.** The believer does not lose his identity through failure; he reaffirms his identity in confession and continues to walk.

---

## **6. The Spirit Enables What the Reckoning Affirms**

The reckoning is not a self-help technique. **The reckoning is faith in the work of the Father, accomplished in the Son, and applied by the Spirit.**

The indwelling Spirit (whom we examined in Parts E and F) is the agent who **makes the reckoning real in experience.** The Spirit witnesses with our spirit that we are children of God (Rom 8:16). The Spirit produces the fruit of conformity to Christ (Gal 5:22-23). The Spirit puts to death the deeds of the body (Rom 8:13).

**The believer reckons; the Spirit empowers.** These are not two competing realities but one — the Father's work in us, mediated by the Son, applied by the Spirit, received by our faithful reckoning.

---

## **7. The Pattern Is Repeated in Every Sphere**

The Romans 6 pattern (indicative → reckon → act) applies to every sphere of the Christian life:

| Sphere | Indicative | Reckoning | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sin in general** | Dead to sin in Christ | Reckon yourself dead | Refuse sin's reign |
| **Particular sins** | Crucified with Christ | Reckon the old pattern broken | Replace with godly pattern |
| **The flesh** | The flesh crucified with its passions (Gal 5:24) | Reckon it powerless | Walk by the Spirit |
| **The world** | Crucified to the world (Gal 6:14) | Reckon its claims null | Set affection on things above |
| **The law's condemnation** | Justified in Christ | Reckon yourself in His righteousness | Stand in grace, not legalism |
| **Fear of death** | Co-raised with Christ | Reckon yourself alive forever | Live without the slavery of fear (Heb 2:14-15) |
| **Identity** | In Christ, a child of God | Reckon yourself accepted in the Beloved | Live as a beloved child |
| **Service** | Created in Christ for good works (Eph 2:10) | Reckon the works prepared | Walk in them |
| **Future** | Glory laid up, inheritance reserved | Reckon the hope sure | Live with eternity in view |

---

## **8. The Pattern Distinguishes Christian Sanctification From All Other Moralisms**

This is critical. The Christian's pursuit of holiness is **fundamentally different** from every other moral system.

- **The Law-based moralist says:** *"You must obey, or you will not be accepted. Try harder."*
- **The performance-based religion says:** *"Do enough good, and you may earn favor."*
- **The self-help approach says:** *"You can become the best version of yourself through discipline."*

**Christian sanctification, by contrast, says:**
- *"You ARE accepted in the Beloved (Eph 1:6). Now walk worthy of it (Eph 4:1)."*
- *"You ARE dead to sin (Rom 6:2). Now reckon it true and refuse sin's reign (Rom 6:11-12)."*
- *"You ARE seated with Christ in the heavenlies (Eph 2:6). Now set your affection above (Col 3:1-2)."*

**The Christian works *from* acceptance, not *for* acceptance.** This is the radical difference. The motivation is **gratitude and identity**, not earning and fear.

---

# **A CLOSING OBSERVATION — THE BEAUTY OF THIS PATTERN**

Brother, the Romans 6 pattern is one of the most beautiful and liberating realities in all of Scripture.

The Father has done in His Son everything necessary for our complete salvation — not only forgiving us, but **uniting us with His Son in His death, burial, resurrection, ascension, and session.** Our position is settled, complete, unchangeable.

Our daily walk is not to *achieve* this position; it is to *live out* what has been freely given. The reckoning is the daily faith-act by which we agree with God's reckoning of us. The presenting is the daily yielding of our members to the One who has bought us. The walking in newness is the practical outworking of the new creation we have already become.

**The Christian life is not striving to become; it is living out of who we already are.**

And the indwelling Spirit makes this possible — strengthening us in the inner man, putting to death the deeds of the body, conforming us progressively to the image of the Son.

The old man is dead. The new man is alive. The Spirit indwells. The Father is for us. The Son intercedes. **Therefore — walk worthy.**
