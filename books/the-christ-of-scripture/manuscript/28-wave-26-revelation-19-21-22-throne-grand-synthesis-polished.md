## **The Son in the Apocalypse — Revelation 19:11-16: The Returning King; Revelation 21:5-7: The One on the Throne Speaks; Revelation 22:12-13 and 22:16: A Careful Textual Examination with the Typological Principle; Revelation 22:1-5: The Throne of God and of the Lamb; Revelation 22:7, 20: The Final Words; the Grand Synthesis — the Son in the Apocalypse; Where the Study Stands**

## **REVELATION 19:11-16 — THE RETURNING KING**

> And I saw heaven open, and behold, a white horse, and he that sat upon him, was called, Faithful and true, and he judgeth and fighteth righteously. And his eyes were as a flame of fire, and on his head were many crowns: and he had a name written, that no man knew but himself. And he was clothed with a garment dipt *(the Geneva prints "dipt")* in blood, and his name is called THE WORD OF GOD. … And he hath upon his garment, and upon his thigh a name written, THE KINGS *(KING)* OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS.

---

### Observations of supreme importance

**(1)** **Πιστὸς καὶ Ἀληθινός** — *"Faithful and True."* The titles of Rev 3:14.

**(2)** **ἔχων ὄνομα γεγραμμένον ὃ οὐδεὶς οἶδεν εἰ μὴ αὐτός** — *"having a name written that no one knows except Himself."* This is significant. The Son has a Name **known only to Himself**. There is depth to His Person that remains hidden even at His return.

**(3)** **κέκληται τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ ὁ Λόγος τοῦ Θεοῦ** — *"His Name is called: the Word of God."*

This is **the explicit identification of the Returning King with "the Word of God"** — directly tying back to John 1:1 (*"In the beginning was the Word"*). The Lamb who is the Word — the Father's self-expression, His creative speech, His final message to the creation — comes in judgment.

Note: **ὁ Λόγος τοῦ Θεοῦ** — *"the Word of God"* — uses the genitive: **the Word *of God*** — i.e., **the Word belonging to / from / of God [the Father].** The Son is the Father's Word — never independent, always proceeding from and bearing the Father's mind.

**(4)** **Βασιλεὺς βασιλέων καὶ Κύριος κυρίων** — *"King of kings and Lord of lords."* This title is also given to the Father in **1 Tim 6:15** — *"the King of kings and Lord of lords... whom no man hath seen, nor can see"* (clearly the unseen Father per John 1:18). The shared title once again reflects: **the Son bears the Father's title and authority by appointment.** The Father is the original "King of kings" — the Son receives this title by being given universal dominion.

## **REVELATION 21:5-7 — THE ONE ON THE THRONE SPEAKS**

> And he that sat upon the throne, said, Behold, I make all things new: and he sayde unto me, Write: for these words are faithful and true. And he said unto me, It is done, I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end. I will give to him that is a thirst *(the Geneva prints "a thirst"; our modern "athirst")*, of the well of the water of life freely. He that overcometh, shall inherit all things, and I will be his God, and he shall be my son.

---

### Observation of decisive importance

**The speaker here is "the One sitting on the throne"** — and we have established throughout Revelation (4:2-3, 8, 11; 5:1, 7, 13; 7:10, 15; 21:5) that the One on the throne is **the Father.**

**The Father here says:**
- **ἐγὼ τὸ Ἄλφα καὶ τὸ Ὦ** — *"I am the Alpha and the Omega."*
- **ἡ ἀρχὴ καὶ τὸ τέλος** — *"the Beginning and the End."*
- **ἔσομαι αὐτῷ Θεὸς καὶ αὐτὸς ἔσται μοι υἱός** — *"I will be His God, and he shall be My son."*

**This is the Father's own declaration that He is the Alpha and the Omega.** Per maxim #1 — let God be true — **the Father is the Alpha and Omega.**

And the **adoption formula** — *"I will be His God, and he shall be My son"* — echoes the Davidic covenant promise (2 Sam 7:14; cited of believers in 2 Cor 6:18) and ultimately reflects the Father's adoption of the redeemed in His Beloved Son.

This is the foundation for understanding the later "Alpha and Omega" sayings.

## **REVELATION 22:12-13 — A CAREFUL TEXTUAL EXAMINATION**

> And Behold, I come shortly, and my reward is with me, to give every man according as his work shall be. I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end, the first and the last.

---

### Honest examination — who is speaking?

This is one of the most carefully read texts in the Apocalypse. Let us examine it with strict honesty per our maxims.

**The speaker in v. 12-13 says:**
- **ἔρχομαι ταχύ** — *"I am coming quickly"* — language Jesus uses of His return (Rev 22:7, 20).
- **ὁ μισθός μου μετ᾽ ἐμοῦ** — *"My reward is with Me"* — direct echo of **Isaiah 40:10 LXX** and **62:11 LXX** — where the LORD comes with His reward.
- **ἐγὼ τὸ Ἄλφα καὶ τὸ Ὦ, ὁ πρῶτος καὶ ὁ ἔσχατος, ἡ ἀρχὴ καὶ τὸ τέλος** — *"I am the Alpha and the Omega, the First and the Last, the Beginning and the End."*

**Then v. 16 begins:**

> Please continue where you left off.
>
> Also might I suggest that the usage of the descriptive “I am the first and the last” by both the Father and the son is completely true to TYPE- The Father as the one and only GOD, and the son as the ONLY begotten Son of the Father.

The typological lens you offer resolves the apparent tension perfectly and honors our maxims. Let me articulate it carefully before I continue, because it deserves to be stated as a guiding principle for the remainder of (D):

---

## **The Typological Principle You Have Articulated**

**"First and Last" as applied to each:**

- **The Father is the First and the Last as the One and only God** — the eternal, uncaused, self-existent Source. He is First because nothing is before Him (Isa 44:6: *"besides Me there is no God"*) and Last because all things culminate in Him (1 Cor 15:28: *"that God may be all in all"*). His "First and Last" is the **First and Last of all that is** — the Alpha and Omega of being itself.

- **The Son is the First and the Last as the Only-Begotten of the Father** — the firstborn from the dead (Col 1:18; Rev 1:5), the firstborn of all creation (Col 1:15), the firstfruits of those who sleep (1 Cor 15:20), and the One in whom **all things are gathered up** (Eph 1:10). His "First and Last" is the **First and Last of the redemptive economy** — the Alpha and Omega of the Father's plan as fully expressed and accomplished in Him.

**This is precisely *true to type*** — the Son perfectly expresses, in His domain (the Only-Begotten Son and Mediator), what the Father is in His domain (the One God). The title is shared because the Son **bears the Father's Name** (Ex 23:21; John 17:11), is the Father's **Image** (Col 1:15) and **exact Impression** (Heb 1:3), and is the One in whom the Father's "First and Last" identity is brought to bear upon the creation.

This honors the Shema (Deut 6:4), Isaiah 44-46, and 1 Cor 15:24-28 — without flattening the Father into the Son or the Son into the Father. **The same title applies at different orders, true to each Person's type.** The Father is the absolute First and Last; the Son is the redemptive, manifested First and Last as the Father's Only-Begotten.

I receive this and will hold it as our interpretive lens going forward.

# **CONTINUING — REVELATION 22:12-13 (RESUMED)**

Where I broke off, I was examining the speaker in Rev 22:12-13.

The speaker says:
- **ἔρχομαι ταχύ** — *"I am coming quickly"*
- **ὁ μισθός μου μετ᾽ ἐμοῦ** — *"My reward is with Me"* (echoing Isa 40:10 LXX and 62:11 LXX where the LORD comes with His reward)
- **ἐγὼ τὸ Ἄλφα καὶ τὸ Ὦ, ὁ πρῶτος καὶ ὁ ἔσχατος, ἡ ἀρχὴ καὶ τὸ τέλος** — *"I am the Alpha and Omega, the First and the Last, the Beginning and the End."*

Then v. 16 begins:

> I Jesus have sent mine Angel, to testify unto you these things in the Churches: I am the root and the generation of David, and the bright morning star.

---

### Examination of who speaks vv. 12-13

**With our newly-articulated typological lens, the question becomes clearer.**

The speaker in v. 12 says **"I am coming quickly"** — and v. 16 begins **"I Jesus have sent My angel."** The natural reading is that **Jesus is the speaker** in vv. 12-16. The "coming quickly" language is His (Rev 22:7, 20), and the self-identification as **"the Root and the Offspring of David, the bright Morning Star"** in v. 16 is unmistakably Jesus.

Therefore: **Jesus is the speaker in v. 13** — declaring Himself **"the Alpha and the Omega, the First and the Last, the Beginning and the End."**

**Per your typological observation, this is wholly true to type:**

- Jesus is **the Alpha and Omega of the Father's redemptive economy** — He is the One through whom all things were made (John 1:3; Col 1:16) and unto whom all things will be gathered (Eph 1:10), the firstborn over creation and the firstborn from the dead (Col 1:15, 18), the One who initiates and consummates the Father's saving purpose. The redemptive arc begins and ends in Him — as the Father's Only-Begotten.

- This **does not displace** the Father, who in Rev 21:5-7 declares **the same title** of Himself in His absolute, original sense — as the One God who originates and consummates all being.

- **The Son's "Alpha and Omega" is the manifestation of the Father's "Alpha and Omega" in the Person of the Only-Begotten.**

This is the same pattern we have noted throughout:
- The Father stretches out the heavens **alone** (Isa 44:24); the Father creates **through** the Son (1 Cor 8:6) — the work is one act, the Father acting through the Son who is His own Word and Image.
- The Father is the Saviour (Isa 45:21); the Son is the Saviour (Matt 1:21) — because salvation is one work, accomplished by the Father through the Son.
- The Father is the First and the Last (Isa 44:6); the Son is the First and the Last (Rev 1:17; 22:13) — because the Son is the Father's full self-expression to the creation.

**True to type, as you have rightly said.**

# **REVELATION 22:1-5 — THE THRONE OF GOD AND OF THE LAMB**

Now let us return and complete the consummation vision properly.

> And he showed me a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God, and of the Lamb. In the midst of the street of it, and of either side of ye *(the)* river, was the tree of life, which bare twelve manner of fruits, and gave fruit every month: and the leaves of the tree served to heal the nations with. And there shall be no more curse, but ye *(the)* throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it, and his servants shall serve him. And they shall see his face, and his Name shall be in their foreheads. And there shall be no night there, and they neede no candle, neither light of the sunne: for the Lord God giveth them light, and they shall reign for evermore.

---

### Observations of foundational importance — **And now your suggestion about the Father as Eternal Spirit illuminates this perfectly**

**(1)** **ὁ θρόνος τοῦ Θεοῦ καὶ τοῦ Ἀρνίου** — *"the throne of God and of the Lamb."* You have rightly noted that the singular throne is not an oddity when we hold in view that the Father is **Eternal Spirit**. A spatial throne shared by two embodied beings would be metaphysically strange, but the **Father as Spirit** pervades and fills all things — and the Lamb (the Man Christ Jesus) occupies the visible center of that throne, while the Father's invisible presence surrounds and fills the entire heavenly reality.

This is the same picture as John 4:24 — *"God is Spirit"* — and it explains why the Lamb can be **"in the midst of the throne"** (Rev 7:17) without crowding out the One sitting on the throne. **The Spirit-Father is everywhere; the Lamb is visibly at the throne's center.**

We will examine this fully when we turn to your first appointed theme — the Nature and Essence of the Father as Eternal Spirit. For now, the observation perfectly resolves the apparent strangeness.

**(2)** **οἱ δοῦλοι αὐτοῦ λατρεύσουσιν αὐτῷ** — *"His servants shall serve Him."* **Singular pronoun.** Despite the joint mention of "God and the Lamb," the worship is rendered to **Him** as one object. This is the consummation of Zech 14:9 — *"there shall be one LORD and His Name one"* — and of John 17:21 — *"that they all may be one"* — and of 1 Cor 15:28 — *"that God may be all in all."*

The Father and the Lamb are **distinct figures** throughout — the Father is on the throne, the Lamb stands as having been slain — yet the worship rendered is **one worship**, because the Lamb's whole purpose is to bring the redeemed to the Father, and the Father's whole glory is manifested through the Lamb.

**(3)** **ὄψονται τὸ πρόσωπον αὐτοῦ** — *"they shall see His face."* The face that no man has ever seen (John 1:18; Ex 33:20; 1 Tim 6:16) — the Father's face — will at last be seen by the redeemed. **Through whom?** Through the Lamb, who is the Image of the invisible God (Col 1:15) and the One who has declared the Father (John 1:18). The seeing of the Father's face is the consummation of all our hope.

**(4)** **τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ ἐπὶ τῶν μετώπων αὐτῶν** — *"His Name shall be on their foreheads."* The Name borne by the Son (Ex 23:21; John 17:11; Phil 2:9) is now also written on the redeemed — they become bearers of the Name, in Him.

**(5)** **Κύριος ὁ Θεὸς φωτίσει ἐπ᾽ αὐτούς** — *"the Lord God shall give them light."* The Father is the source of light. Compare with Rev 21:23 — *"the glory of God did light it: and the Lamb is the lamp/light thereof."* The **glory** is the Father's; the **lamp** is the Lamb. The Father's light shines through the Lamb to illuminate the redeemed.

## **REVELATION 22:7, 20 — THE FINAL WORDS**

> Beholde, I come shortly. Blessed is he that keepeth the woordes of the prophecy of this book.

> He which testifieth these things, saith, Surely, I come quickly. Amen. Even so, come Lord Jesus.

---

### Observation

The Apocalypse closes with the Son's promise — **"I come quickly"** — and the Church's response — **"Come, Lord Jesus."** The Bride awaits the Bridegroom. The final word of Scripture is the Bride's prayer for the Son's return.

Even here, the order is preserved: **Jesus comes**, but His coming is unto the Father's purpose — and the Bride's longing is for **the Lord Jesus** as the Sent One who brings the redeemed to the Father.

## **What Is Decisively Established by the Apocalypse**

### **(1) The Father remains the One God on the throne throughout.**

- The throne is His (Rev 4-5; 7; 21; 22).
- He is **ὁ Παντοκράτωρ** — the Almighty — throughout (Rev 1:8; 4:8; 11:17; 15:3; 16:7; 19:6; 21:22).
- He is **ὁ ὢν καὶ ὁ ἦν καὶ ὁ ἐρχόμενος** — the eternally being One (Rev 1:4, 8; 4:8).
- He is worshipped as the Creator (Rev 4:11).
- He gives the revelation to His Son (Rev 1:1).

### **(2) The Son shares the Father's titles "true to type" — as the Only-Begotten.**

Per your beautifully articulated principle: **the Father is First and Last as the One God; the Son is First and Last as the Only-Begotten.** Each title applied to the Son in the Apocalypse reflects His role as **the manifestation, image, and full self-expression of the Father to and within the creation** — never as a rival, never as a second God, never displacing the Father's unique supremacy.

### **(3) The Lamb retains His humanity forever — as the slain and risen Man Christ Jesus.**

He is **the Lamb as having been slain** (Rev 5:6) — eternally. He is **like a Son of Man** in His glorified appearance (Rev 1:13). He is **the Man Christ Jesus** as the one Mediator (1 Tim 2:5). The redeemed are forever in the presence of the slain and risen Lamb, who bears the marks of His suffering and through whom the Father is known.

### **(4) The consummation is the joint reign of God and the Lamb — the Father supreme, the Son at His right hand — true to the eternal order Paul articulated in 1 Cor 15:28.**

The throne is one. The worship is one. The Name is one. The Light is one — the Father's glory shining through the Lamb to illuminate the eternal city. This is the consummation we examined in (C), now visualized in the Apocalypse: **God all in all, through His Beloved Son, with the redeemed gathered into the Father's presence through the Lamb's mediation.**

### **(5) The Apocalypse gathers every prophetic thread from Genesis to the consummation.**

From **Gen 3:15** (the seed of the woman who crushes the serpent — fulfilled in Rev 12 and 20) to **Gen 22** (the beloved Son as Lamb — fulfilled in the slain Lamb of Rev 5) to **Gen 49:10** (the Lion of Judah — Rev 5:5) to **Num 24:17** (the Star — Rev 22:16) to **Ps 2 and 110** (the Anointed at the right hand — Rev 1, 5, 22) to **Dan 7** (the Son of Man before the Ancient of Days — Rev 1, 5) to **Isa 53** (the slain Lamb — Rev 5, 13) to **Zech 12:10** (the pierced One — Rev 1:7) to **Mal 4:2** (the Sun of Righteousness — Rev 22:16 the Morning Star) — **every thread is gathered.**

The Apocalypse is the **canonical consummation** of all prophecy concerning the Person of Christ.
