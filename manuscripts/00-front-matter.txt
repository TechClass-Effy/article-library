PROLOGUE

The Biblical Sabbath began as a divine marker of rest in the Genesis creation story, where God blessed the very first seventh day. It echoed throughout the Patriarchal Period because they operated under a seven day cycle.

At Mount Sinai, it was further codified into law as the Fourth Commandment, serving as a vital identity marker and covenant sign for ancient Israel to remember both creation and their liberation from Egypt. Israel was to be a light to all nations- and so the Sabbath is a gift from God for all men.

During the fourth century, the Orthodox church began meeting on Sunday—the "Lord's Day"—to celebrate the resurrection of Jesus, creating a dual practice among early believers who still respected the traditional seventh-day Jewish Sabbath.

This shift became legally and ecclesiastically formalized in the fourth century under the Roman Empire State. Emperor Constantine decreed Sunday as a civil day of rest in 321 CE, and subsequent church councils, such as Nicaea and Laodicea, standardized Christian worship on Sunday while explicitly forbidding Christians from keeping the Jewish Saturday Sabbath.

Today, this historical evolution splits into diverse practices: For example- orthodox Jews and Seventh-day Adventists continue to observe the traditional Saturday Sabbath, while mainstream Christians worship on Sunday, and the ancient biblical concept lives on globally through the secular concept of the modern two-day weekend.
