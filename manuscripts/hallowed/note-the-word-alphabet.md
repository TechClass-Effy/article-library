# NOTE FOR THE BOOK — How We Got Our Word "Alphabet"
*(for Hallowed Be Thy Name — the aleph-and-bet line's fuller telling; use one, use it whole, or leave on the shelf)*

Draft as it might stand, right after the aleph-and-bet line in The Old Hand:

---

Say our word for it. We say **alphabet** — and the saying remembers: the Greek tongue
held their word *alphabetos*, born of the first two letters spoken together — **alfa** and
**bēta** — just as a man might say "to ABC" of anything learned from the very first mark.
The Hebrews said it long before: **aleph-bet** — the ox and the house, the first two of
the twenty-two — and the word meant simply *the letters*, the twenty-two in their order.
The Greek borrowed the letters themselves from Phoenicia, in their shapes and their
names and their order, and the name of the order came with them: alpha-beta. Then the
Latin carried the word to Rome, and Rome went on carrying it to every nation of Europe.
So the word in your mouth this morning is as old as the letters it names — and every time
we say it, we name no one who invented, but two old pictures: **the ox and the house.**
Aleph-bet. Alphabet.

---

Companion note (if the book wants the older sister-word):
*The Hebrews kept their own name for the order —* **aleph-bet** *— and the Jewish
writers use it to this day for the Hebrew's own letters. The Greek word and the Hebrew
word are twin inventions of the same habit: name the first two, and the whole rolls out
behind them.*
