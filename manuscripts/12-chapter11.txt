CHAPTER 11

The Sabbath Rest That Remains — Hebrews and Revelation

We're almost done with this biblical journey. Two more books to hear from. And what they say puts a crown on top of everything we've studied.

Hebrews 4 — A Sabbath-Keeping Remains

Here's one of the most direct statements in the whole New Testament about the ongoing Sabbath. Look what the writer of Hebrews says:

Hebrews 4:9–11 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"So there remains a Sabbath-keeping for the people of God. For the one who has entered into his rest has also himself rested from his works, just as God from his own. Let us therefore be diligent to enter into that rest, lest anyone fall in the same example of disobedience."

— Hebrews 4:9–11, Codex Sinaiticus

That Word "Sabbath-Keeping"

Look at verse 9 carefully. "There remains a Sabbath-keeping for the people of God."

The Greek word for "Sabbath-keeping" here is sabbatismos. This is the ONLY time this word appears in the entire New Testament. Once. Right here.

Now watch this. The verb form of that same word — sabbatizo — appears in the Greek Old Testament (LXX) at Exodus 16:30 (the manna Sabbath), Leviticus 23:32 (the Day of Atonement Sabbath), Leviticus 26:34-35 (the land keeping Sabbath), and 2 Chronicles 36:21 (the land's Sabbath-rest during exile).

In every single one of those Old Testament uses, it means literal Sabbath-keeping. Actual, observed, weekly Sabbath practice.

So when the writer of Hebrews picks the noun form of that same word — sabbatismos — he's picking the word that specifically means Sabbath-observance. Literal keeping of the Sabbath.

He could have used a more general word for "rest." In fact, he uses one — "katapausis" — throughout verses 1-8 of this same chapter. But when he gets to verse 9, he switches. He deliberately chooses sabbatismos. Why? Because he's naming the thing specifically: a Sabbath-keeping.

"Remains"

The verb "remains" is in the present tense. "Is left over." "Continues to be reserved." It hasn't been removed. It hasn't been transferred. It remains.

"For the People of God"

Not "for Israel." Not "for the Jews." Not "for the Old Covenant remnant." For the people of God — which is the exact phrase Hebrews uses for New Covenant believers. That's YOU. That's the church.

Read verse 9 one more time in plain English:

"A Sabbath-keeping remains for the people of God."

That's the Bible's own statement. Under our maxim — take God at His word — that settles it. The Sabbath continues. For God's people. Now.

Two Witnesses on the Ongoing Sabbath

Remember our rule — two witnesses minimum. Do we have them?

Yes. And more.

• Matthew 24:20 — Jesus tells His followers to pray their FUTURE flight not fall on a Sabbath (a Sabbath they'll still be keeping)
• Hebrews 4:9 — A Sabbath-keeping remains for the people of God

Two direct witnesses. From Jesus Himself and from the writer of Hebrews.

Plus confirming witnesses:
• Luke 23:56 — the women rested the day after Calvary "according to the commandment"
• Acts 13, 16, 17, 18 — Paul's weekly Sabbath practice
• Colossians 2:16-17 — Paul defends Sabbath-keeping against outside criticism
• Matthew 5:17-19 — Jesus forbids loosing any commandment until heaven and earth pass away

The witness is overwhelming. The Sabbath continues.

What About the "Rest in Christ" Argument?

Some folks read Hebrews 4 and say, "See? We enter rest by faith in Christ. That IS the Sabbath. No need for a weekly day."

That's a spiritual-sounding argument, but it doesn't hold up to the actual text. Let me show you why.

Hebrews 4 talks about THREE rests:

1. Creation Rest (verse 4) — "And God rested on the seventh day from all his works." That's Genesis 2. The original Sabbath.

2. Promised Land Rest (verse 8) — "For if Joshua had given them rest, he would not have spoken about another day after these things." Joshua brought Israel into Canaan, but that wasn't the final rest, or David wouldn't have written Psalm 95 centuries later inviting people to enter God's rest.

3. The Sabbath-Keeping That Remains (verse 9) — for the people of God, right now.

The writer's whole argument is that neither the creation rest nor the Promised Land rest was the FINAL rest. There's still one to come. And a weekly Sabbath-keeping remains, both to participate in the rest we already have by faith and to point to the eternal rest still to come.

The weekly Sabbath isn't ABOLISHED by the "spiritual rest in Christ." The weekly Sabbath is HOW we live out and anticipate that rest, one day every week.

Every Sabbath, you experience by faith the rest Christ has given you AND you look forward to the eternal rest still to come. That's the beauty of it.

A Real-Life Picture

Imagine a soldier deployed overseas. He's got a wife and kids at home. Every night he Skypes with them. That's real connection. That's real love. That's real relationship.

Does that mean he doesn't need to come home for good? Of course not. The nightly Skype call is a taste of the reunion. Not a replacement for it. When he finally comes home, THEN he's fully home. But every Skype call was real too.

Sabbath is like that. Every week, you enter God's rest — really, truly, by faith. And every week, you're reminded there's a FULLER rest coming. The eternal Sabbath. The final home-coming. Sabbath-keeping now and eternal rest later are not opposed. The one prepares you for the other.

Revelation — The Eternal Sabbath and the "Lord's Day"

Now to the last book of the Bible. Two big questions here.

First: What is "the Lord's Day" in Revelation 1:10? Sunday? Or something else?

Second: Does the Sabbath continue into eternity?

Revelation 1:10 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"I came to be in spirit on the Lordly day, and I heard behind me a great voice as of a trumpet."

(Codex Vaticanus doesn't have the book of Revelation, so we rely on Sinaiticus and other early witnesses here.)

What Is "The Lord's Day"?

Here's a fact most people don't know. The phrase "the Lord's Day" appears exactly ONCE in the entire Bible. Right here. Nowhere else.

Under our maxim — two or more witnesses to establish doctrine — a single occurrence can't itself define "the Lord's Day." So we have to ask: what does Scripture ELSEWHERE tell us is "the Lord's day"?

The answer is really clear. Multiple witnesses.

The Sabbath is called "the Lord's" over and over:
• Exodus 20:10 — "but on the seventh day is a sabbath to the Lord your God"
• Exodus 31:15 — "on the seventh day is a sabbath, a holy rest to the Lord"
• Leviticus 23:3 — "on the seventh day is a sabbath, a rest, a holy assembly to the Lord"
• Deuteronomy 5:14 — "but on the seventh day is a sabbath to the Lord your God"
• Isaiah 58:13 — "you shall call the sabbaths delightful, holy to your God"

At least five witnesses in the Old Testament — the Sabbath is God's day.

And Jesus specifically claimed lordship over one day — the Sabbath:

Mark 2:28 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"So then the Son of Man is Lord also of the Sabbath."

Jesus never claimed lordship over any other day. Not once. The one day He specifically called Himself Lord of is the Sabbath.

And here's what the first day of the week is called in the Gospels:

Not "the Lord's Day." Not once. The Gospels always call it "the first of the Sabbaths" — Matthew 28:1, Mark 16:2, Luke 24:1, John 20:1, Acts 20:7. Never "the Lord's Day."

Putting It Together
• Scripture repeatedly calls the Sabbath "the Lord's day."
• Jesus specifically claims lordship over the Sabbath.
• The first day of the week is NEVER called "the Lord's Day" anywhere in the Bible.

When John says he was "in the spirit on the Lord's day," the Bible-honest reading is that he was in the spirit on the Sabbath. The day Scripture has always called God's. The day Jesus specifically Lorded over.

Where "Sunday = Lord's Day" Came From

This equation — Sunday = Lord's Day — doesn't appear in the Bible. It shows up in second-century writings from Church Fathers. Under our Maxim 6, we set that aside. If it contradicts what Scripture already established, it goes.

The plainest, Scripture-consistent reading of Revelation 1:10: John, a Sabbath-keeping Apostle exiled on Patmos, was in the spirit on the Sabbath when he received the Revelation. That harmonizes with every other Scripture. That's the OLD path.

The Eternal Sabbath — Isaiah 66 and Revelation 21

Here's the crown of the whole study. Watch this.

Isaiah 66:22–23 (LXX, NETS)

"For just as the new sky and the new earth, which I make, remain before me, says the Lord, so shall your offspring and your name stand. And it shall be, month by month and sabbath by sabbath, all flesh shall come to do obeisance before me in Ierousalem, said the Lord."

Read That Again

In the new heavens and new earth — that's the eternal state — ALL FLESH will come month by month and Sabbath by Sabbath to worship God.

Let that sink in.
• The eternal state — the age to come, the world without end.
• All flesh — not just Jews. Every people. Every nation. Every believer.
• Sabbath by Sabbath — literally, week by week, every seventh day.
• New Moon by New Moon — every month.
• Coming to worship God.

The Sabbath is kept in eternity.

If the Sabbath is kept in the eternal state — forever — then how could it possibly have been abolished for a brief 2,000-year period called the church age?

It can't. That's absurd. The Sabbath was made at creation. It was codified at Sinai. It was defended by Jesus. It was practiced by the Apostles. It remains for God's people now (Hebrews 4:9). And it will be kept forever in the new creation (Isaiah 66:23).

The Sabbath is eternal.

Revelation Confirms Isaiah 66

Revelation describes the same new heavens and new earth that Isaiah prophesied.

Revelation 21:1–3 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"And I saw a new heaven and a new earth; for the first heaven and the first earth passed away, and the sea is no more. And the holy city, new Jerusalem, I saw coming down out of heaven from God, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband. And I heard a great voice from the throne saying, 'Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he shall tabernacle with them, and they shall be his peoples, and God himself shall be with them.'"

Same new heavens. Same new earth. Same eternal state. Revelation confirms the vision Isaiah saw. And Isaiah told us what happens Sabbath by Sabbath in that state. All flesh comes to worship.

The Saints in the End Times

Revelation gives us one more picture — of the faithful in the last days.

Revelation 12:17 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"And the dragon was enraged against the woman, and he went off to make war against the rest of her seed — those keeping the commandments of God and having the testimony of Jesus."

Revelation 14:12 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"Here is the endurance of the saints — those keeping the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus."

Two Witnesses in Revelation

Twice — Revelation 12:17 and Revelation 14:12 — the end-times faithful are marked by TWO things joined together:

1. Keeping the commandments of God.
2. Having / keeping the faith of Jesus.

Not one or the other. Both.

The commandments of God include the Fourth Commandment. Keeping the Sabbath. This is what marks the endurance of the saints in the last days.

The dragon rages specifically against those who KEEP the commandments and have the testimony of Jesus. These are joined. Neither is dropped.

Where We Are So Far

From Hebrews and Revelation:
• A Sabbath-keeping remains for the people of God — the New Testament's own direct statement (Heb 4:9)
• Jesus expected His followers to still be keeping the Sabbath in the future (Matt 24:20)
• The "Lord's Day" in Revelation 1:10 is most naturally the Sabbath — the day Scripture has always called God's, the day Jesus specifically Lorded over
• All flesh will keep the Sabbath in the new heavens and new earth — forever (Isa 66:22-23)
• End-times saints are marked by keeping God's commandments AND the faith of Jesus — both, joined (Rev 12:17, 14:12)

What the Modern Saint Should Take from This Section
• Rest in the finished work of Christ. Every Sabbath you experience the rest He purchased.
• Anticipate the eternal Sabbath. Every weekly Sabbath points to the forever rest coming.
• Keep God's commandments AND keep faith in Jesus. Both. Together. That's what marks the saints who endure.
• You are practicing now what you'll do forever. Sabbath-keeping isn't a temporary chore. It's your eternal calling.

Now let's put it all together.
