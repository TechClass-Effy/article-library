CHAPTER 10

The Hard Paul Passages — Colossians, Galatians, Romans

Okay. Here's where the rubber meets the road. If you've ever tried to talk to another Christian about the Sabbath, you've probably heard something like this:

"But Paul said in Colossians 2 that we don't have to keep the Sabbath anymore."

Or:

"Paul said in Galatians we're not under law, we're under grace."

Or:

"Paul said in Romans 14 every man decides for himself which days he keeps."

These three passages are the big guns. Every argument against Sabbath-keeping runs through them. So we're going to take them one at a time. In full context. And see what Paul actually said.

Here's the anchor to hold onto through this whole section. Paul kept the Sabbath every week of his ministry. We just watched him do it — in Acts 13, 16, 17, and 18. In four different cities. For eighteen months in Corinth. As his personal custom — Luke's word.

A Paul who kept the Sabbath cannot be a Paul who abolishes the Sabbath. If our reading of any Paul passage contradicts what Paul actually did, our reading is wrong. Not Paul. Our private interpretation.

Let's find the reading that harmonizes Paul with Paul.

Colossians 2:16-17 — The Big One

Colossians 2:16–17 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"Let no one therefore judge you in eating and in drinking, or in respect of a feast or of a new moon or of sabbaths — which things are a shadow of the things about to come, but the body is of Christ."

(Vaticanus reads the same.)

Read That Carefully

Look at what Paul actually says. What's the command? What's the direction of the command?

"Let no one judge YOU."

Paul is NOT saying, "Don't observe these things." He's saying, "Don't let anybody judge you for observing these things."

Read that again until it clicks. Paul is defending the ones observing these things. He's telling them: someone's judging you for keeping the Sabbath, the New Moons, and the Feasts — let no one judge you for it.

[The foods, Sabbaths, New Moons, and annual festivals listed in Colossians 2:16–17 act like a divine calendar. YHVH, the one true God, designed this calendar from the beginning to reveal His master plan. Every celebration was given as an early preview to show exactly how His Only Begotten Son would carry out YHVH's will to save, rule, and restore mankind.

Hear and understand this truth: YHVH our God is one, and He alone is the author of salvation. The Son does not work apart from the Father, but is the exact hand of YHVH executing the Father's purposes. Instead of being random rules, each of the following category(s) points directly to a specific part of YHVH's single, unified plan carried out through His Son:

1. The Food and Drink (Accomplished)
This does not just mean everyday meals. In the Old Covenant, specific clean foods and drink offerings were brought to the altar to cleanse the temple. This previewed how the Son is the Bread of Life and as such would cleanse and feed our inner selves. He became our true spiritual nourishment by giving His life as the ultimate sacrifice. Through Him, YHVH washes us clean from sin and keeps us spiritually alive.

2. The Seven Annual Festivals / (YHVH's "Moadim") — Partly Done, Partly Future
These seven holy feast times are YHVH's appointed timeline. They lay out the exact order of the Son's redemptive work, showing what He has completed and what YHVH has promised He will finish.

The Spring Feasts (Accomplished Works)
• Passover (Pesach): This Feast foreshadowed the Only Begotten Son who died as the true Lamb of YHVH on the exact day this Feast is celebrated from ancient times. His blood cleanses us of ALL sin and delivers us from the power of sin and death.
• Unleavened Bread (Chag HaMatzot): The Son was buried in the earth, completely WITHOUT SIN. His perfect sacrifice breaks the power of sinful corruption and bridges the Gulf between fallen man and his Holy God. Thus setting YHVH's redeemed people apart.
• Firstfruits (Yom HaBikkurim): The Son rose from the dead on this exact day also. He became the firstfruits of the resurrection, guaranteeing eternal life to all who believe and belong to Him.
• Pentecost (Shavuot): The Son returned from Glory bearing Gift for men by pouring out YHVH's Holy Spirit from heaven. He did this to write YHVH's law directly onto human hearts, filling His vessels with His own presence.

The Fall Feasts (Future Works)
• Trumpets (Yom Teruah): The Son will return with a shout and the sound of a shofar. (A goat's horn used like a trumpet to blast a distinctive sound.) He will gather YHVH's chosen people from the four corners of the earth in a grand assembly.
• Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur): The Son will execute YHVH's final judgment. He will completely cleanse creation, banish evil, and reconcile the heavens and the earth back to the Father.
• Tabernacles (Sukkot): The Son will establish a New Heaven and a New Earth for YHVH's visible Kingdom. YHVH- in- Christ Jesus will dwell among mankind, bringing His people into perfect peace, harmony, and rest.

3. The New Moons and Sabbaths (Future)
The New Moons (which reset the Lunar months) and the weekly Sabbaths (the days of complete rest) preview the final steps of the plan. They show that the Son will eventually cleans this broken world with fervent heat and reset our God's governance. He will bring His people into the permanent, peaceful rest of YHVH's Kingdom rule, where all creation will worship the One True God forever.

Simply put, the Old Covenant celebrations were the blueprint drawn by YHVH, and the Son's actions—both past and future—are the actual fulfillment of what was prophesied from the very beginning when YHVH created the Moon and set her in motion to establish the very appointed times and seasons foretelling the vital steps to achieving His Plan to bring His Chosen People Home.]

That's not abolition. That's protection. Paul is protecting the Sabbath-keepers, not shutting them down.

But Who Was Judging Them?

To answer that, we need to see what Paul was warning about in the CHAPTER. Look at verse 8.

Colossians 2:8 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"Beware lest anyone shall be the one taking you captive through philosophy and empty deceit, according to the tradition of men, according to the elements of the world, and not according to Christ."

What's Paul warning against? Watch the words:
• Philosophy — Greek pagan philosophy. Not Covenant sabbath keeping.
• Empty deceit — false teaching.
• The tradition of men — human religious inventions.
• The elements of the world — pagan spiritual systems.
• NOT according to Christ.

And look at verse 22:

Colossians 2:22–23 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"…according to the commandments and teachings of men? Which things have indeed a word of wisdom in self-imposed worship and humility and unsparing treatment of the body, not in any honor, unto satisfaction of the flesh."

"Commandments and teachings of MEN." "Self-imposed worship." Not God's commandments. Not what God said. MAN-MADE religious rules.

Paul is not attacking Torah. He's attacking human religious traditions that people were adding on top of Torah — a Gnostic-pagan mix at Colossae that was piling extra dietary and calendar rules on believers.

Read it again in context:

"Don't let outsiders judge you for keeping God's Feasts, God's New Moons, and God's Sabbaths — these are shadows of Messiah's coming works, and the body of Christ is your judge, not these outsiders with their human traditions."

That's what Paul is saying. He's defending Sabbath-keeping. Not abolishing it.

"A Shadow of Things About to Come"

One more piece. Paul says these things are "a shadow of the things about to come."

Look at the Greek word for "about to come" — it's a future participle. Not past. Not "shadows of things that already came." Shadows of things STILL COMING.

The High Sabbath Feasts, marked by the New Moons, are prophetic. Three have already been fulfilled and three point forward. They still teach us of Messiah's ongoing and future work.

To abandon them is to abandon prophetic instruction. It is to cease the acknowledgment and celebration of what has been accomplished and what is yet to be fulfilled for our complete redemption from sin guiltiness, and our restoration to living in the direct presence of our God forever in eternity.

The weekly Sabbaths points to the eternal Sabbath rest — Hebrews 4 will hammer that home. It's a foreshadowing of what's still coming. Which is why we still keep it. Because it still reminds and teaches.

"But the Body Is of Christ"

Now — this phrase. Most translations render it "the substance belongs to Christ." That's fine as a translation choice. But the Greek word Paul actually uses is "soma" — which means body. Same word Paul uses in 1 Corinthians 12:27 — "you are the body of Christ." Same word he uses in Ephesians 5:23. Same word he uses in Colossians 1:24 — "his body, which is the church."

Paul's meaning: The judgment on these matters belongs to the body of Christ — the assembly of believers — not to the outsiders. Let the body of Christ judge these matters. Let no one outside the body judge you.

Colossians 2:16-17 is defending Sabbath-keeping. Not abolishing it.

A Real-Life Picture

Imagine your neighbor tells your wife she shouldn't wear a wedding ring because his religious group says wedding rings are pagan. Your wife is faithful. She loves her ring. It's a covenant sign between you and her.

You tell her: "Don't let him judge you for wearing that ring. That ring is yours. The people who love you get to weigh in on your marriage. Not that guy."

That's not you telling your wife to take off her ring. That's you telling her to keep wearing it and to ignore the critic.

Same thing Paul is doing at Colossae. "Don't let outside critics judge you for keeping God's Sabbaths. Keep them. Their opinion doesn't matter."

Galatians 4:9-11 — "Days and Months and Seasons and Years"

Galatians 4:8–11 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"But then indeed, not knowing God, you were enslaved to those which by nature are not gods; but now having known God, or rather having been known by God, how are you turning back again to the weak and beggarly elements, to which you desire again anew to be enslaved? You observe days and months and seasons and years. I fear for you, lest somehow I have labored for you in vain."

Context Is Everything

Paul is writing to Gentile converts. Galatians is his letter fighting the Judaizers — people who were telling Gentile converts they had to be circumcised and take on the whole Law of Moses to be saved.

Paul's argument isn't against Torah itself. Paul's argument is against using Torah as your way of getting saved and forgetting that you are already saved through Faith.

That's the whole letter.

Now look at verse 9. Paul says the Galatians are "turning back again" to weak elements. Back to what?

Look at verse 8: "Not knowing God, you were enslaved to those which by nature are not gods."

Before their conversion, the Galatians were pagans. Not Jews. They weren't keeping Torah before Christ. They were serving pagan gods.

So when Paul says they're "turning back again" — back to what? Back to paganism. Not back to Sabbath-keeping. They never kept Sabbath before Christ. They CAN'T "turn back" to what they never did.

"Days and Months and Seasons and Years"

So what days is Paul talking about?

Pagan religious calendars. The Roman festival days. The astrological "lucky days" and "unlucky days." The seasonal pagan feasts. The imperial cult celebrations. The birthdays of the gods. All the pagan time-keeping the Galatians left when they came to Christ.

That's what Paul is warning them about. Going BACK to their old pagan calendar.

Look — the word "elements" in verse 9 is the same word Paul used in Colossians 2:8 and 2:20, where it clearly meant pagan spiritual systems, not God's Law. Paul uses his own words consistently.

Paul is not attacking God's Sabbath, New Moons, or Feasts in Galatians 4. He's attacking pagan time-keeping. Which is exactly what the Galatians left when they came to Christ.

Romans 14:5-6 — "One Man Esteems One Day"

Romans 14:5–6 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"One indeed judges a day above a day, but another judges every day; let each be fully persuaded in his own mind. The one minding the day, to the Lord he minds; and the one eating, to the Lord he eats, for he gives thanks to God; and the one not eating, to the Lord he does not eat, and he gives thanks to God."

What Days Is Paul Talking About?

Watch how carefully Paul writes. He does NOT name any specific day. He does NOT mention the Sabbath. He does NOT mention the New Moon or the Feasts.

Romans 14 is about disputable matters — things Scripture hasn't clearly settled. The chapter is mainly about food (verse 2 — some eat all things, some eat only vegetables) and days (verse 5).

What kind of days could Paul mean?
• Rabbinic fast days. The Pharisees fasted twice a week (Luke 18:12). Some Jewish-background believers kept these fasts, others didn't. Fasting on non-biblical days isn't commanded in Torah.
• Days of ritual purification — rabbinic add-ons.
• Roman civic days — days considered lucky or unlucky, days of remembrance for family events.
• Personal fasts or memorials.

In our day we could point to "Christianized" festivals such as Christmas and Easter for instance- both of which have occult pagan origins. Any of those Paul could leave to individual conscience.

What Paul is NOT talking about:
• The Sabbath. Because Paul kept it every single week (Acts 13, 16, 17, 18). If Paul were saying the Sabbath is optional in Romans 14, he'd be contradicting his own practice.
• The Feasts of the Lord. Paul explicitly rushed to be at Jerusalem for Pentecost (Acts 20:16). He kept the Feasts.

The Sabbath and God's Feasts are doctrinally settled in Scripture. They're not "disputable matters" — they're commanded by God's own voice. Romans 14 doesn't touch them. Paul is talking about matters where Scripture hasn't clearly spoken. There are lots of such matters. But Sabbath isn't one of them.

An Important Textual Note

Here's something worth mentioning. In verse 6, some later manuscripts add: "and he that does not regard the day, to the Lord he does not regard it." Some Bibles include this. But in Codex Sinaiticus, this second clause is missing.

What Sinaiticus actually says is: "The one minding the day, to the Lord he minds it." Just that. Paul only affirms the day-observer. He doesn't equalize the two.

That's not a huge argument, but it's worth noting — the oldest manuscripts don't give as strong a "either way is fine" reading as later manuscripts do.

Putting Paul Together

Let's line all three passages up. What was Paul actually teaching?

Colossians 2:16-17: Don't let outsiders judge you for keeping God's Sabbath, New Moons, and Feasts. These are prophetic shadows of Messiah's still-coming works. The body of Christ is your judge, not outside critics.

Galatians 4:8-11: Don't return to pagan religious calendars from which you were delivered. And don't take on Torah thinking it saves you. Christ's finished work saves you.

Romans 14:5-6: In matters not clearly settled by Scripture (personal fasts, non-biblical observances), let each be fully persuaded in his own mind and don't judge each other.

All three together:
• God's Sabbath, New Moons, and Feasts are to be observed (Colossians defends them)
• They're NOT the means of justification — that's Christ's finished work alone (Galatians)
• Rabbinic and pagan additions are to be rejected (Colossians and Galatians both)
• Genuinely disputable matters are left to individual conscience (Romans 14)

This harmonizes with:
• Paul's own weekly Sabbath practice (Acts)
• Jesus' teaching that He didn't come to abolish the Law (Matthew 5:17-19)
• Jesus' own Sabbath custom (Luke 4:16)
• The women resting the day after Calvary "according to the commandment" (Luke 23:56)

No abolition of the Sabbath can be pulled from Paul without contradicting Paul… period.

A Word on Church History

Brother and sister, here's the sad truth about how we got here. Within a hundred years of Paul's death, the Roman church started reinterpreting Colossians 2 and Galatians 4 as if they abolished the Sabbath. By the 200s, Sunday-keeping was becoming common. By 321 AD, Emperor Constantine made Sunday the legal day of rest by imperial decree. By 325 AD, the Council of Nicaea condemned keeping Passover on the biblical date.

Every one of these developments happened AFTER the Apostles were dead. None of it is in the New Testament. All of it came from "the tradition of men" — the exact thing Paul warned against in Colossians 2:8.

Under our maxims, we set the traditions of men aside. We return to what Paul actually kept and taught. We seek the OLD path.

What the Modern Saint Should Take from This Section
• Paul kept the Sabbath. So do you. His own practice interprets his own letters.
• Don't let anyone judge you for keeping God's days. Colossians 2:16 protects Sabbath-keepers, not those who abandon it.
• Don't try to earn salvation by law-keeping. Christ finished that work. Rest in His work. Then obey out of love.
• Don't add pagan or rabbinic time-keeping. God's calendar is enough.
• In genuinely disputable matters, extend grace. But the Sabbath isn't disputable. It's commanded.

Now we come to two more witnesses — Hebrews and Revelation — that seal the whole thing.
