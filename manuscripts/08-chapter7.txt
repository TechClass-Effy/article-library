CHAPTER 7

Jesus and the Sabbath — What He Actually Did and Taught

Now we come to what might be the most important section of the whole study for a Christian. Because if you're going to follow Jesus, you need to know: What did Jesus Himself do with the Sabbath?

Some folks will tell you Jesus "abolished" the Sabbath. Some will tell you He "changed it to Sunday." Some will tell you He was constantly getting in trouble for breaking it.

Let's put all the traditions aside and just read what the Bible says. From the oldest manuscripts. What did Jesus actually do?

Here's the summary before we start: Jesus kept the Sabbath every single week of His life. He fought the Pharisees over how to keep it rightly, not whether to keep it. He never once said it was abolished. He never once changed it. He never once told anybody to stop keeping it. And when He talked about the future — decades and centuries ahead — He told His followers they'd still be keeping it.

That's what the text says. Now let me show you.

Jesus' Own Sabbath Habit

Let's start at the very beginning of Jesus' ministry. Luke tells us what His custom was.

Luke 4:16 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"And he came to Nazareth, where he had been brought up, and entered according to his custom on the day of the Sabbaths into the synagogue, and stood up to read."

"According to his custom." His habit. His regular practice. Every Sabbath, into the synagogue, hearing the Word read, teaching it.

Now let's be clear on what the synagogue was. The synagogue is not the Temple. The Temple was way off in Jerusalem, only priests worked there, and it was where sacrifices were offered. The synagogue was the local neighborhood meeting-house. Any town with even a small Jewish population had one. Regular folks came together there every Sabbath. Someone read the Scriptures out loud. Somebody taught what they meant. Everybody discussed.

That's what Jesus did. Every Sabbath. From childhood on. It was His custom.

Let's see two more witnesses to Jesus' Sabbath habit.

Mark 1:21 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"And they enter into Capernaum; and immediately on the Sabbaths, having entered into the synagogue, he was teaching."

Mark 6:2 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"And when Sabbath had come, he began to teach in the synagogue, and the many hearing were astonished, saying, 'From where did these things come to this one?'"

Three witnesses. Multiple Gospels. Same pattern. Jesus went to the synagogue every Sabbath. He read the Word. He taught it. That was His custom.

If Jesus is your Lord, and Jesus kept the Sabbath every week of His life, that alone should stop a lot of arguments about whether the Sabbath is for us.

The Seven Sabbath Controversies

Now here's where it gets interesting. The Gospels record about seven big fights Jesus had with the Pharisees over the Sabbath. Every single one of them, the Pharisees accused Him or His disciples of "breaking" the Sabbath.

Look carefully at what Jesus did in every single one. Not once did He agree with them. Not once did He say, "You're right, I broke it, but I'm going to abolish it anyway." Every single time, He defended what He or His disciples did, and He rebuked the Pharisees for adding rabbinic rules that God never gave.

This is critical. Jesus was NOT abolishing the Sabbath. Jesus was stripping off the rabbinic corruption and restoring the Sabbath to what God actually commanded. That's a huge difference.

Let's look at the biggest ones.

Controversy 1 — The Disciples Plucking Grain

Mark 2:23–28 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"And it came about, on the Sabbaths, that he was passing through the grain fields, and his disciples began to make a way, plucking the heads of grain. And the Pharisees were saying to him, 'Look — why are they doing on the Sabbaths what is not lawful?' And he says to them, 'Have you never read what David did when he had need and hungered, he and those with him — how he entered into the house of God under Abiathar the high priest and ate the loaves of the presentation, which it is not lawful to eat except for the priests, and gave also to those who were with him?' And he was saying to them, 'The Sabbath came into being on account of the man, and not the man on account of the Sabbath. So then the Son of Man is Lord also of the Sabbath.'"

Let's Get This Straight

The Pharisees are accusing the disciples of doing something "not lawful." So — was the disciples' plucking grain actually against God's Law?

No it wasn't. Look what God actually said in the Law:

Deuteronomy 23:25 (LXX, NETS)

"Now if you should enter your neighbor's grain field, and you shall gather with your hands ears of grain, but you shall not put a sickle to your neighbor's grain field."

Torah explicitly permits what the disciples did. Grabbing a handful of grain to eat as you walk through a field is allowed by God. What Torah forbids is bringing a sickle and starting a harvest.

So where did the Pharisees get their objection? From rabbinic tradition. They had built up rules on top of God's Law — categorizing "plucking a few heads of grain" as "reaping" and thus forbidden on Sabbath. That was a rabbi's invention. Not God's.

Jesus doesn't say, "Yeah, that rule is dumb, and by the way, the whole Sabbath is off." He points out that they're adding to Scripture. And He teaches two massive truths:

Truth 1: "The Sabbath came into being on account of the man, and not the man on account of the Sabbath."

The Sabbath was made for people. For your benefit. For your good. For your rest. For your restoration. God made it for you. He didn't make you to serve the Sabbath — He made the Sabbath to serve you.

The Pharisees had it backwards. They had turned the Sabbath into a heavy burden that people had to serve, with a thousand fussy rules. Jesus said, no — the Sabbath is God's gift to you. Not your master.

Truth 2: "The Son of Man is Lord also of the Sabbath."

This is huge. Jesus claims Himself as Lord of the Sabbath. He rules it. He owns it. He has final say on what it means and what it forbids.

Now — think about this. A Lord doesn't abolish his own kingdom. When Jesus says "I am Lord of the Sabbath," He's saying, "This day is Mine. I rule it. I get to say what it's for."

And what does He do next? He heals people on the Sabbath. Because that's what a Lord of a day of rest and restoration does. He rests. He restores. He shows mercy.

Jesus is not abolishing the Sabbath. He's asserting His rightful lordship over it and stripping away the rabbinic corruption.

Controversy 2 — The Man with the Withered Hand

Mark 3:1–6 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"And he entered again into the synagogue, and there was a man there having a withered hand. And they were watching him closely to see if he would heal him on the Sabbaths, so that they might accuse him. And he says to the man having the withered hand, 'Rise up into the middle.' And he says to them, 'Is it lawful on the Sabbaths to do good or to do evil, to save a life or to kill?' But they were silent. And having looked around at them with anger, grieved at the hardening of their heart, he says to the man, 'Stretch out the hand.' And he stretched it out, and his hand was restored. And having gone out, the Pharisees immediately with the Herodians were making counsel against him, how they might destroy him."

— Mark 3:1–6, Codex Sinaiticus

The Question That Settles the Matter

Jesus asks the question that settles it once and for all: "Is it lawful on the Sabbaths to do good or to do evil, to save a life or to kill?"

The Pharisees can't answer. Because they know. Doing good is lawful. Saving life is lawful. Sabbath is not a day for withholding mercy. It's a day for pouring it out.

Notice Jesus is angry. He's grieved. Why? Because the Pharisees' tradition had made them prefer a man to keep suffering rather than have him healed on the Sabbath. That's what religious tradition can do to your heart. It can make you hate mercy in the name of "keeping the rules."

Jesus wasn't having it. He healed the man. And nothing about that healing broke God's actual Law. Jesus didn't fire up a forge. He didn't buy or sell. He didn't carry burdens. He just spoke, and the hand was healed. Divine mercy freely poured out on God's holy day.

That's what Sabbath is FOR.

Controversies 3 & 4 — The Bent Woman and the Man with Dropsy

Let me give you two more, showing the same pattern.

Luke 13:15–16 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"But the Lord answered him and said, 'Hypocrites — does not each of you on the Sabbath loose his ox or his donkey from the manger and lead it away to water? And this one, being a daughter of Abraham, whom Satan bound — behold, eighteen years — was it not necessary that she be loosed from this bond on the day of the Sabbath?'"

Luke 14:3–5 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"And Jesus answering said to the lawyers and Pharisees, saying, 'Is it lawful to heal on the Sabbath, or not?' But they were silent. And having taken hold of him, he healed him and released him. And to them he said, 'Which of you shall have a son or ox fall into a well, and will not immediately pull him up on the day of the Sabbath?'"

Jesus keeps making the same argument: You yourselves would rescue an animal on the Sabbath. How much more should a suffering human being be freed?

Multiple witnesses now (Mark 3, Luke 13, Luke 14) tell us the same truth:
• Acts of mercy on the Sabbath are lawful — even required
• Sabbath is the right day for healing and restoration
• Rescuing what has fallen, freeing what is bound, healing what is broken — all of that fits Sabbath perfectly
• Sabbath ceases the labor of self-provision, not the labor of love for others

Controversy 5 — The Man at Bethesda

John 5:8–10, 16–17 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"Jesus says to him, 'Rise, take up your mat and walk.' And immediately the man became well, and he took up his mat and was walking. But it was Sabbath on that day. So the Jews were saying to the one having been healed, 'It is Sabbath, and it is not lawful for you to take up your mat.'… And for this reason the Jews were persecuting Jesus, because he was doing these things on the Sabbath. But he answered them, 'My Father is working until now, and I also am working.'"

"My Father Is Working Until Now"

This one gets misread all the time. Some people read Jesus' words — "My Father is working until now, and I also am working" — and say, "See? Jesus said God works on the Sabbath, so we can too. Sabbath abolished."

That's not what Jesus said. Read carefully.

God rested from creating on the seventh day (Genesis 2). He stopped making new things. He stopped bringing new stuff into existence.

But God never rested from sustaining what He made. He never rested from hearing prayer. He never rested from healing the broken. He never rested from pouring out mercy. If He had, the universe would fall apart every seventh day.

Jesus is saying: The Father's ongoing works of mercy, sustaining, healing, and life-giving don't stop on the Sabbath. And neither do Mine.

That's not abolishing the Sabbath. That's clarifying what Sabbath rest is and isn't. You rest from the work of your six days — the making, the producing, the earning. You don't rest from mercy, healing, and doing good.

Which is exactly what Jesus taught in every single Sabbath controversy.

The Big One — Matthew 24:20

Now watch this. This one verse alone would settle the whole question of whether the Sabbath continues for Jesus' followers after His resurrection.

Jesus is teaching about the future. He's talking about a time of tribulation. A time His followers will need to flee. And listen to what He tells them:

Matthew 24:20 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"But pray that your flight may not happen in winter, nor on a Sabbath."

Read That Again

Jesus is speaking of a future event — either the fall of Jerusalem in 70 AD (decades after His resurrection) or the end-times tribulation. Either way, it's after His death, after His resurrection, after Pentecost.

And He commands His followers to pray that their flight not fall on the Sabbath.

Why? Because on the Sabbath, His followers would still be keeping the Sabbath rest. They wouldn't be walking long distances. They wouldn't be carrying burdens. Fleeing on Sabbath would violate the very Sabbath they'd be keeping.

Jesus expected His followers to still be keeping the Sabbath — years, decades, maybe centuries after His ascension. If He had planned to abolish the Sabbath, this was the perfect place to say so. Right? He was talking about the future. He was warning them.

Instead, He assumes they'll be keeping it.

You cannot honestly read Matthew 24:20 and think Jesus intended to abolish the Sabbath. He explicitly plans for His followers to still be keeping it, and He tells them to pray they don't have to flee on it.

This one verse, all by itself, would settle the matter. But it doesn't stand alone. It stands with everything else we've seen.

The Day After Calvary

Here's one more that will establish you.

Jesus has just been crucified. He's dead. He's in the tomb. The women who love Him — who followed Him from Galilee, who saw where He was laid — they have spices. They want to anoint His body. They're broken with grief The promised Messiah was DEAD! Surely, this is a time when God would make an exception to His Rule? But not so. There are NEVER exceptions to the Laws of God.

Look what they do.

Luke 23:54–56 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"And it was day of Preparation, and Sabbath was dawning. And the women, who had come with him from Galilee, having followed after, beheld the tomb and how his body was laid. And having returned, they prepared spices and myrrh. And on the Sabbath they rested according to the commandment."

"According to the Commandment"

Read that last line one more time. "And on the Sabbath they rested according to the commandment."

Jesus has just been killed. The New Covenant has been inaugurated in His blood. The Temple veil has been torn. And what do the women closest to Him do?

They rest. On the Sabbath. According to the commandment.

Time was of the essence. They had spices to bring. They had a body to anoint. They had the deepest grief a human heart can carry. Every reason in the world to work. And they didn't.

Because the Sabbath commandment still applied. Even the day after Calvary. And Luke — writing his Gospel decades later, under inspiration of the Holy Spirit, for a mostly Gentile audience — describes their rest as obedience to the commandment, with not a single hint that this commandment has been superseded.

Think about it. If the Sabbath had been abolished by the cross, THIS would be the exact spot in the New Testament where you'd expect Luke to say something. But he doesn't. He describes their Sabbath-keeping as normal, right, faithful, "according to the commandment."

The women closest to Jesus — who had heard His teaching for years — believed that even the day after His crucifixion, the Sabbath was still to be kept. And Luke, under inspiration, confirms it.

Where We Are So Far

From the Gospels, with multiple witnesses at every point:
• Jesus kept the Sabbath every week — it was His custom (Luke 4, Mark 1, Mark 6)
• He went to the synagogue for the reading and teaching of Scripture
• He fought the Pharisees over rabbinic add-ons, never over the commandment itself
• The Sabbath was made for people, not people for the Sabbath (Mark 2:27)
• Jesus is Lord of the Sabbath — He rules it, doesn't abolish it (Mark 2:28)
• Acts of mercy, healing, and rescue are lawful — even required — on the Sabbath (Mark 3, Luke 13, Luke 14, John 5)
• The Father's sustaining works never stop, and neither do the Son's (John 5:17)
• Jesus expected His followers to be keeping the Sabbath decades after His ascension (Matthew 24:20)
• The women closest to Him kept the Sabbath the day after Calvary "according to the commandment" (Luke 23:56)
• Jesus explicitly said He didn't come to abolish the Law (Matthew 5:17-19)

What Jesus NEVER Did

Look at what the Gospels never show:
• Jesus never said the Sabbath was abolished
• Jesus never changed the Sabbath to Sunday
• Jesus never said His resurrection replaced the Sabbath
• Jesus never told anyone to start gathering on the first day of the week
• Jesus never rebuked anyone for keeping the Sabbath — only for keeping it the wrong way (with rabbinic add-ons)

Every single Sabbath dispute in the Gospels is about HOW to keep it rightly — never WHETHER to keep it.

What the Modern Saint Should Take from This Section
• Follow Jesus. If He kept the Sabbath every week of His life, you keep the Sabbath every week of yours.
• Gather with other Saints when you can. Not at a Temple — you don't have one — but in fellowship. In someone's home. Around the open Bible. That was Jesus' custom, and it should be ours.
• Pour out mercy on Sabbath. Visit the sick. Comfort the grieving. Help the needy. That's not Sabbath-breaking. That's Sabbath-keeping at its highest.
• Don't add rabbinic rules. If God commanded it, do it. If a man added it, drop it.
• Receive the day from Jesus. He is Lord of the Sabbath. Take it from His hand.

Now let's watch what the Apostles did after Jesus rose.
