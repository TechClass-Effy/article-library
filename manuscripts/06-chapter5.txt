CHAPTER 5

Called Together, and a Warning

Two more Torah passages before we move to the prophets. One tells us the Sabbath is a "called-together" day. The other is a sobering story about what happened when a man broke the Sabbath in the wilderness.

Both of these need careful handling. Let's take them in order.

Leviticus 23 — Called Together, But Where?

Leviticus 23:1–3 (LXX, NETS)

"And the Lord spoke to Moyses, saying: Speak to the sons of Israel, and you shall say to them: The feasts of the Lord which you shall call holy assemblies — these are my feasts. Six days you shall do works, but on the seventh day is a sabbath, a rest, a holy assembly to the Lord. You shall not do any work; it is a sabbath to the Lord in all your dwellings."

The "Holy Assembly" Question

The phrase "holy assembly" in Greek is klētē hagia — literally, a "called-holy" gathering. A summons. God is calling His people to Him on the Sabbath.

Now here's where we need to think carefully.

What the text does say:
• The Sabbath is a called-together, holy day
• It's placed at the top of God's list of "appointed times" (His feasts)
• It's kept "in all your dwellings" — in your homes

What the text does NOT say:
• It doesn't say "come to the Tabernacle"
• It doesn't say "find a priest"
• It doesn't require a sacrifice
• It doesn't require a special service

That phrase "in all your dwellings" is the key. God's saying: this Sabbath is kept at home. In your dwelling place.

Look at what happens right after in Leviticus 23. God starts talking about Passover, Unleavened Bread, and the other annual Feasts. These are called High Sabbaths, the "MOADIM" in Hebrew meaning- YHVH's Appointed Seasons. These are very special annual events for which YHVH designed and set the Moon in motion to track when these Sacred Feasts occur in the course of each New Year. I will reveal all this in another study.

So Does That Mean Christians Shouldn't Gather?

No. Not at all. Let me explain.

Leviticus 23 is saying the baseline Sabbath is a home Sabbath. Family. Household. Wherever you live. That's the minimum requirement, and it doesn't need a building or a leader to happen.

But when we get to the Gospels, we're going to see that Jesus went to the synagogue every Sabbath. Synagogues aren't the Temple. They're the local gathering places where regular folks read the Scriptures out loud together and taught each other. Think of it as the neighborhood meeting-house where believers came together to hear God's Word.

The synagogue was practicable then. It's practicable now. That's how believers can gather to hear the Word — in someone's living room, in a house church, in a rented space, in a park — anywhere Saints can come together around the open Bible.

But — and this is important — even if you have no other believers around, no fellowship to join, nobody to gather with, you still keep the Sabbath at home. The home is where the Sabbath happens first. Everything else is added blessing.

A Real-Life Picture

Imagine a truck driver crossing the country. He's a believer. He's out on the road Wednesday night through Sunday. When Saturday morning dawns he knows of no home fellowship he can attend. There's no local church in the area that meets on the Sabbath.

Does that mean he can't keep Sabbath? No. He parks the truck. He rests. He reads the Word. He eats cold cuts, cheese, fruit and some fig Newtons he gathered the day before. He calls his family and reads with them over the phone. He prays. He turns his cab into his dwelling place — and there he keeps Sabbath, alone with God. What God Himself sees you do to obey Him in secret… when nobody is looking…He rewards you openly.

Or imagine a stay at home mom whose husband is overseas in the military, and whose whole family thinks she's crazy for keeping Sabbath. She's alone. Does that mean she can't keep it? No. Her kitchen table becomes her Sabbath place. Her Bible open. Her children reading with her. Her home is the sanctuary.

That's the point. The Sabbath's home base is your dwelling. Fellowship is a beautiful addition when God provides it. But until He does… home comes first.

The Sabbath Is a Summons

Here's something worth pausing on. The Sabbath isn't just a day off. It's a calling. A summons. God is calling you to Him every seventh day.

That means Sabbath isn't just about what you don't do. It's about answering God's call to come and be with Him. Whether you answer that call in your kitchen or in a house fellowship or in a huge gathering — you're answering God's summons. That's what makes it holy.

Numbers 15 — The Sabbath-Breaker

Now we come to a hard passage. A sobering story. It shows us just how seriously God takes the Sabbath.

Numbers 15:32–36 (LXX, NETS)

"And the sons of Israel were in the wilderness, and they found a man gathering wood on the day of the sabbaths. And those who found him gathering wood on the day of the sabbaths brought him to Moyses and Aaron and to the whole congregation of the sons of Israel. And they put him in custody, for they had not decided what they should do to him. And the Lord spoke to Moyses, saying: 'By death let the man be put to death. Stone him with stones — the whole congregation, outside the camp.' And the whole congregation brought him outside the camp and stoned him with stones outside the camp, as the Lord instructed Moyses."

Let that sit for a minute. It sounds terrifying. It may even seem unjust to most of us today. But consider with me what has happened. For a long time now our modern society has come to view civil laws as mere suggestions. It's all good, just don't get caught … right? We deem obedience to civil laws with casual self permissiveness.

To do so is not right, and we most certainly should never do so with YHVH's Laws! EVERY man owes a duty of worshipful obedience to his Creator and GOD. If YHWH were to fail to punish such direct disobedience to His Commandments HE WOULD HAVE BEEN UNJUST… He would cease to be righteous in ALL HIS WAYS.

Ever hear the phrase "three strikes and your out?" Observe:

Strike one: A man was gathering wood he was Commanded not to on that day. Strike two: He was Commanded to stay home and rest… he did not. Strike three: His urgent self imposed necessity implies he most certainly intended to kindle a fire- that he was Commanded not to- on that day… By Almighty GOD.

And now he had been brought in front of the whole of Israel! Everyone in Israel rushed together to see what would happen. Think about it. If YHVH failed to punish this egregious behavior in front of- nay, but also BY THE VERY HANDS of- all of Israel… then a root of Sin would spring up and ALL of Israel would also quickly go astray with complete disregard for ALL of Gods Commandments. You know I'm right.

Such overt disobedience HAD TO BE SEVERELY PUNISHED AND PUT AWAY… once and for ALL. Now with all these things being wisely considered- our God said, "Put him to death…"

My hope and prayer here and now is that all His people may now think about both the GOODNESS… and SEVERITY… of our God.

Goodness- this refers to God's Mercy, Grace and Loving Kindness shown to those who trust and abide in Him,
Severity- This refers to God's Holiness, Justice, Judgement and disciplinary action against sin and rebellion.

This calls for God's people to soberly recognize both sides of God's Character rather than taking His Grace for Granted- warning that persistent unbelief and refusal to obey will incur His decisive Judgement! See Romans 11.12

The Bible speaks about the "fear of God" or the "fear of the Lord" hundreds of times across both the Old and New Testaments.

In the Bible, this "fear" does not usually mean being terrified of God like you would be of a monster. Instead, it means having deep respect, awe, and reverence for His immense power and holiness.

Here are the most famous places where the Bible talks about it:

The Old Testament (The Foundation) The Old Testament views the fear of God as the starting point for a good and wise life.

Proverbs 1:7 "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge, but fools despise wisdom and instruction."
Proverbs 9:10 "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom, and the knowledge of the Holy One is understanding."
Ecclesiastes 12:13: After searching for the meaning of life, King Solomon concludes: "Fear God and keep His commandments, for this is man's all."
Psalm 111:10 "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom; a good understanding have all those who do His commandments."

The New Testament also emphasizes fearing God, pairing it with His grace and love.

Luke 12:5: Jesus tells His followers not to fear humans who can only kill the body, but to "Fear Him who, after He has been killed, has power to cast him into hell; yes, I say to you, fear Him!"
Acts 9:31 The early church is described as "walking in the fear of the Lord and in the comfort of the Holy Spirit."
Philippians 2:12: The Apostle Paul tells believers to "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling."
1 Peter 2:17 A simple command for daily life: "Honor all people. Love the brotherhood. Fear God. Honor the king."

What Does It Actually Mean?

To help understand how the Bible balances the "fear of God" with His "goodness," think of it like a massive thunderstorm or the ocean. They are beautiful and life-giving, but they are also incredibly powerful and dangerous if you do not properly respect them.

Fearing God means recognizing His awesome power while trusting in His love.

Now put the two passages together:

• Exodus 35:3 — Don't kindle a fire in your dwellings on the Sabbath
• Numbers 15:32–36 — God executes a man for gathering fuel for such a fire… on the SABBATH. Two witnesses. Same principle. Sabbath is a day when the ordinary labor of provisioning — including preparing the fuel and firing up the kitchen — is put away.

What "Work" Really Means

Here's what this story settles for us. Some folks want to argue that "work" in the Fourth Commandment only means "commercial work" or "hard labor for money." "Gathering sticks doesn't count," they'd say.

God said it counts. He treated it as a capital offense. Gathering sticks is:
• Small-scale
• Domestic (household)
• For personal use, not for money
• Not hard labor by any modern standard

And God said: that's forbidden work on Sabbath.

So "work" in the Sabbath command isn't just your commercial job. It's the whole labor of household provisioning. No- gathering, running errands, doing laundry, mowing the lawn — the ordinary labor that keeps daily life going. That's what stops on Sabbath.

As I have said before- We Don't Execute People Today

Let me be clear about this, because it matters. God had this man executed by the whole congregation of Israel — Israel as a theocratic nation-state, under God's direct government.

We do not have that authority today. We are not a theocratic nation. We hold no civil sword. Jesus himself refused to be a civil Arbitrator (Luke 12:14). Paul told the Romans that the civil sword belongs to civil governments (Romans 13), not to the church.

So what do we take from Numbers 15? Two things:

We take the weight. God takes Sabbath-breaking seriously. Deadly seriously. Don't play with this. Don't treat it as optional.

We take the definition. "Work" means the ordinary labor of daily living — not just commercial employment.

We do NOT take:
• Any authority to execute Sabbath-breakers
• Any duty to enforce Sabbath on people outside the faith
• Any assumption that we can wield the civil sword in God's name

The commandment weighs on us. The execution does not belong to us.

But What About Mercy?

Here's something we need to be clear on, especially before we get to Jesus. The same God who executed the wood-gatherer also commanded mercy on the Sabbath. Look:

Deuteronomy 22:4 (LXX, NETS)

"You shall not see the donkey of your brother or his calf fallen in the way, and you shall disregard them; you shall raise them up with him."

If you're walking down the road and your brother's donkey is in the ditch, you help him get it out. Doesn't matter what day it is. Mercy doesn't stop on Sabbath.

Jesus is going to build on this when we get to the Gospels. He'll ask, "Which of you, if his son or his ox falls into a pit on the Sabbath day, will not immediately pull him out?" (Luke 14:5).

Sabbath rest is not a rest FROM mercy. It's a rest from the labor of provision — the work you do to make your living. Mercy, rescue, and relieving suffering are not the work Sabbath forbids. In fact, as we'll see, Jesus taught that Sabbath is the perfect day for mercy.

Where We Are So Far

Adding from Leviticus 23 and Numbers 15:
• The Sabbath is a called-together, holy day — home base is your dwelling; God summons His people to Himself
• Weekly Sabbath does not require the Temple or a priest — it's kept in your dwellings
• "Work" on the Sabbath means all ordinary labor of provision, not just commercial employment
• God takes Sabbath-breaking with the utmost seriousness
• We don't enforce the civil death penalty today, but we take the weight of it
• Mercy is not "work" — the Sabbath doesn't put mercy on hold

What the Modern Saint Should Take from This Section
• Your home is your Sabbath sanctuary. Whether you gather with others or not, your dwelling is where the Sabbath happens.
• Answer God's summons. He's calling you every seventh day. Come to Him. He will meet you then. Open the Word. Pray. Rest with Him.
• Take Sabbath seriously. God does. Don't reduce it to a suggestion.
• Cease from ALL the ordinary work of daily life — not just your job. Cooking, errands, laundry, home projects — all of it.
• Don't withhold mercy. If someone needs help, help them. That's not Sabbath-breaking. That's exactly what Sabbath is for.

Now let's hear from the prophets. Because the prophets are going to show us what Sabbath-keeping looks like in real life — no priest, no temple, no sacrifice required.
