CHAPTER 8

What the Apostles Did — The Book of Acts

Here's the question that matters. After Jesus rose from the dead, after Pentecost, after the New Covenant was fully in force — did the Apostles keep the Sabbath, or did they abandon it?

If they kept it, that tells us something huge. Because these men were closest to Jesus. Paul was personally trained by the risen Christ (Galatians 1:12). Whatever they did with the Sabbath tells us what Jesus intended.

Let's watch what actually happened.

Paul at Pisidian Antioch

Acts 13:14–15, 42–44 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"But they, having gone through from Perga, arrived at Antioch of Pisidia, and having entered into the synagogue on the day of the Sabbaths, they sat down. And after the reading of the Law and the Prophets, the synagogue rulers sent to them, saying, 'Men, brothers, if there is among you a word of encouragement toward the people, speak.'"

"And as they were going out, they were urging that on the between Sabbath these words be spoken to them. And when the synagogue had been dismissed, many of the Jews and of the devout proselytes followed Paul and Barnabas, who, speaking to them, were persuading them to continue in the grace of God. And on the coming Sabbath almost the whole city was gathered together to hear the word of the Lord."

What Just Happened

Paul enters the synagogue on the Sabbath — same custom Jesus had. He teaches. And when the Gentiles want more, look what he tells them:

"Come back next Sabbath."

Read verse 42 again. The Gentiles wanted to hear more of the Word. Paul did not say, "Come tomorrow, on the Lord's Day." He did not say, "Come Sunday morning to the first-day service." He said: come back next Sabbath.

And by verse 44, almost the whole city — a Gentile city — is gathered on the Sabbath to hear the Word of the Lord.

If the Sabbath had been changed or abolished, this is exactly where you'd expect Luke to note it. He doesn't. Because it wasn't.

Paul at Philippi

Acts 16:13 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"And on the day of the Sabbaths we went out outside the gate beside a river where we were considering there to be a place of prayer, and having sat down we were speaking to the women who had come together."

Watch This Carefully

Philippi was a Roman colony. Almost no Jews there. Not enough for even a synagogue.

Paul and his team — including Luke, who's writing this — went out on the Sabbath to find a place of prayer. In a Gentile city. With no Jewish community pressuring them.

They kept the Sabbath because they kept the Sabbath. Not because they were trying to blend in with anybody.

And who's there? Lydia, a Gentile woman. Sabbath-keeping at a Gentile place of prayer with the Apostolic team. This is New Testament, post-Pentecost, Gentile Sabbath practice.

Paul at Thessalonica

Acts 17:1–2 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"And having passed through Amphipolis and Apollonia, they came to Thessalonica, where there was a synagogue of the Jews. And according to what was customary to Paul, he went in to them, and on three Sabbaths he reasoned with them from the Scriptures."

The Same Phrase Luke Used for Jesus

Look at what Luke wrote: "According to what was customary to Paul."

Now look back at Luke 4:16 — how Luke described Jesus:

"He entered according to his custom on the day of the Sabbaths into the synagogue."

Same author. Same phrase. Luke deliberately uses the same words for Jesus and for Paul.

Why? Because he's showing us: Paul's Sabbath custom is the same as Jesus' Sabbath custom. The Apostle imitates his Master. Same day. Same practice. Same synagogue. Same reasoning from the Scriptures.

Paul at Corinth

Acts 18:4, 11 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"And he was reasoning in the synagogue every Sabbath, and he was persuading both Jews and Greeks."

"And he settled for a year and six months, teaching among them the word of God."

Every Sabbath. For a Year and a Half.

"Every Sabbath." Not "sometimes." Not "when it worked out." Every.

"Jews and Greeks." Both. Paul is teaching Gentiles on the Sabbath.

A year and a half. That's approximately 78 Sabbaths in one location, all in the synagogue, teaching Jews and Greeks the Word of God.

If Paul had believed the Sabbath was transferred to Sunday, this is bizarre behavior. But he didn't. So he did what he always did — kept the Sabbath, taught the Word, and welcomed Gentiles into that practice.

Four Witnesses, Same Pattern

Let's put them together:
• Pisidian Antioch — Paul teaches Gentiles on the Sabbath (Acts 13)
• Philippi — Paul keeps Sabbath in a Gentile city with no Jewish community (Acts 16)
• Thessalonica — Sabbath is called Paul's custom, same word Luke used for Jesus (Acts 17)
• Corinth — Every Sabbath for 18 months, Jews AND Greeks (Acts 18)

Four cities. Four witnesses. Same practice.

This is settled Apostolic doctrine: Paul kept the Sabbath. Paul taught Gentiles the Word on the Sabbath. Paul's Sabbath custom mirrored Jesus' Sabbath custom exactly.

But What About Acts 20:7?

Now some folks will point to Acts 20:7 and say, "See? The disciples met on the first day of the week. That's the change to Sunday."

Let's look at it carefully.

Acts 20:7 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"And on the first of the Sabbaths, when we were gathered together to break bread, Paul was reasoning with them, being about to depart on the next day, and he prolonged the word until midnight."

What This Actually Says

Read carefully. What does the text say happened?
• The believers at Troas gathered on the first day of the week
• They broke bread (which in Acts often just means shared a meal — see Acts 2:46 for the pattern)
• Paul was reasoning with them
• Paul was leaving the next day
• The gathering went into the night — until midnight

Now look at what the text does NOT say:
• It does NOT say this was a regular weekly practice
• It does NOT call this day "the Lord's Day"
• It does NOT say the Sabbath was moved
• It does NOT command any Sabbath change
• It does NOT establish any doctrine
• It does NOT even necessarily describe a "worship service" — "breaking bread" often just meant sharing a meal in Acts

Why Was Paul There That Night?

Paul was leaving the next day. This was a farewell gathering. Paul had been at Troas for a full week (Acts 20:6). During that week there was at least one Sabbath. But Luke doesn't say what happened on that Sabbath — because nothing unusual happened. What Luke narrates is this particular gathering: the one where Paul was leaving and where the miracle of Eutychus happened (verses 8–12, where the young man fell out of a window and Paul raised him from the dead).

This is a special-occasion farewell, not a weekly Sunday service. One event doesn't establish doctrine. Remember our rule — two witnesses minimum. Acts 20:7 is one event.

What About 1 Corinthians 16:2?

The other "first day" Sunday folks bring up.

1 Corinthians 16:1–2 (Codex Sinaiticus)

"Now concerning the collection for the saints, as I directed the churches of Galatia, so also you do. On the first of the Sabbaths, let each one of you set aside by himself, storing up whatever he may prosper, so that when I come, collections may not then be made."

Read Every Word

Paul is talking about a special one-time collection for famine relief in Jerusalem — not a weekly church offering.

And look what he says:

"Let each one of you set aside by himself" — the phrase means "at his own place," at home. Individually. Not in a corporate gathering. Not "when you come together to worship, take up a collection." Just — each person, at home, sets aside what he's prospered.

Paul's stated reason: "So that when I come, collections may not then be made." He wants each believer to have his gift ready privately so no last-minute gathering is needed.

Why the first day of the week? Most likely because the Sabbath had just ended. The Sabbath was the day when believers weren't handling money or counting income. First day of the week was the earliest chance to review the week's income and set aside what God had provided.

This actually presupposes Sabbath-keeping. It doesn't overthrow it.

And read again what the text does NOT say:
• It does NOT command a first-day assembly
• It does NOT mention worship
• It does NOT call it "the Lord's Day"
• It does NOT indicate any change to the Sabbath
• It does NOT even describe a gathering — it describes individual home setting-aside

The Two "First Day" Texts, Weighed Honestly

These are the ONLY two "first day" texts in all the New Testament used to argue for a Sabbath change to Sunday:
• Acts 20:7 — one farewell gathering, extending from Saturday evening into the night, before Paul's Sunday departure. Cannot establish a weekly pattern.
• 1 Corinthians 16:2 — each believer individually setting aside money at home for a one-time collection. Not a corporate gathering. Not worship. Not doctrine.

Neither text abolishes the Sabbath. Neither commands first-day worship. Neither establishes a pattern of first-day assembly.

Meanwhile, Paul's Sabbath practice is documented in four different cities, over years, with the phrase "according to Paul's custom," including Gentile audiences, in cities without Jewish presence.

The witnesses aren't close. The Sabbath witness stands. Overwhelmingly.

A Real-Life Picture

Imagine your grandpa left a will. In it, he divides his farm four different ways in four different clauses, spread over the whole document. And in one paragraph he mentions he had dinner at your uncle's house one evening. Then someone comes along and argues that the dinner mention proves grandpa left the farm to your uncle.

You'd laugh. Four clear passages divide the farm four ways. One mention of dinner doesn't overrule them.

Same here. Four clear passages show Paul keeping every Sabbath in synagogue, teaching Jews and Gentiles, in city after city, for years. Two passages mention the "first day" — one is a farewell gathering, one is home savings. Neither one is a worship command. Neither can overrule Paul's clear, repeated, documented Sabbath practice.

Where We Are So Far

From Acts:
• Paul kept the Sabbath every week (Acts 13, 16, 17, 18)
• Paul's custom was the same as Jesus' custom — Luke, who personally knew both Jesus Christ and Paul- and writing under the unction of the Holy Spirit, uses identical phrasing (Luke 4:16, Acts 17:2)
• Paul kept Sabbath even in Gentile cities with no Jewish community (Philippi, Acts 16)
• Paul taught Gentiles the Word on the Sabbath — Corinth, every Sabbath for 18 months (Acts 18)
• Almost whole Gentile cities gathered on Sabbath to hear the Word (Acts 13:44)
• The two "first day" texts do NOT establish Sunday worship — one is a farewell (Acts 20:7), one is individual home savings for a special collection (1 Cor 16:2)
• No Apostolic text abolishes, changes, or replaces the Sabbath

What the Modern Saint Should Take from This Section
• Do what the Apostles did. They kept the Sabbath. So do you.
• Gather when you can. Home fellowship. House church. Wherever Saints can come together around the open Bible on Sabbath.
• Teach the Word on the Sabbath. In your home. To your kids. To your family. To whoever will listen.
• Don't let anyone tell you the Apostles moved worship to Sunday. The text doesn't say that. The text shows the exact opposite.
• If Church leaders in your circle don't teach this, you now know why. They inherited a tradition, not a Scripture. That's on them. Your job is to know the Word.
