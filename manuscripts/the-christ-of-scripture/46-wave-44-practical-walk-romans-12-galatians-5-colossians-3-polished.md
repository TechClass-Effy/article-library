# **PART 1 — ROMANS 12: THE LIVING SACRIFICE AND THE RENEWED MIND**

## **The Transition**

Paul has just concluded eleven chapters of doctrinal foundation — sin, justification, sanctification, life in the Spirit, the Father's sovereign plan in Israel and the nations. Then comes the great **οὖν** (*therefore*) at Romans 12:1 — the pivot from indicative to imperative on a grand scale.

---

## **Romans 12:1 — THE LIVING SACRIFICE**


### **1599 Geneva:**
***"I beseech you therefore brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye give up your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable serving of God."***

---

### Observations of foundational importance

**(1)** **Παρακαλῶ οὖν** — *"I therefore beseech you."* The word **οὖν** (*therefore*) gathers up all eleven preceding chapters. The imperative is grounded in everything Paul has established. The believer is being asked to act **on the basis of all that God has done.**

**(2)** **διὰ τῶν οἰκτιρμῶν τοῦ Θεοῦ** — *"by the mercies of God."* The motivation for the imperative is **the Father's mercies** — the entire chain of mercy Paul has unfolded (chosen, called, justified, indwelt, sealed, secured). The believer is not motivated by fear or by the threat of loss, but by gratitude for mercy received.

**(3)** **παραστῆσαι τὰ σώματα ὑμῶν** — *"present your bodies."* This is the same verb **παρίστημι** Paul used in Romans 6:13 — *"present yourselves to God."* Now he specifies: **present your bodies.** Not your minds only, not your spirits only — your bodies. The physical, mortal, fleshly bodies in which we live our daily lives.

**This is striking after Romans 7-8.** Paul has just established that the body is mortal, weak, and the seat of competing desires. Yet **this very body** is to be presented as a sacrifice. The Father does not despise the body; He receives it as a holy offering.

**(4)** **θυσίαν ζῶσαν** — *"a living sacrifice."* A paradox. OT sacrifices were dead. The believer's sacrifice is **alive** — continually being offered, not slain once and consumed. The believer's life itself is the offering.

**(5)** **ἁγίαν εὐάρεστον τῷ Θεῷ** — *"holy, well-pleasing to God."* The sacrifice is **holy** (set apart, dedicated) and **well-pleasing** to the Father. This is the believer's life when wholly given to the Father — a fragrant offering received with delight.

**(6)** **τὴν λογικὴν λατρείαν ὑμῶν** — *"your reasonable service / spiritual worship."*

**λογικός** can mean *"rational, reasonable"* (related to **λόγος** — reason, word) or *"spiritual."* The Geneva translates *"reasonable serving."* Either way, the meaning converges: in light of God's mercies, this whole-life presentation is the **only reasonable response.** It is not extreme; it is not optional; it is the proper, fitting, rational service of those who have received what the believer has received.

---

### Practical Implications

**(1) The believer's body is the place of worship.** Not the church building. Not a sacred space. **The body itself, presented to God, is the temple of acceptable worship.** This dovetails with what we established in Part F — the believer's body is the temple of the Holy Spirit (1 Cor 6:19).

**(2) Worship is comprehensive, not compartmentalized.** What we eat, what we wear, what we do with our hands, where we go with our feet, what we say with our tongues, what we think with our minds — **all of this is the sphere of worship.** There is no secular/sacred division in the believer's life. **Every act of the body is potentially an act of worship.**

**(3) The presentation is to be definite.** The aorist infinitive **παραστῆσαι** suggests a decisive act. The believer is to make a deliberate, conscious presentation of his body to God — not as a vague aspiration, but as a settled transaction. *"Father, this body is Yours. Use it as You will."*

---

## **Romans 12:2 — THE RENEWED MIND**


### **1599 Geneva:**
***"And fashion not yourselves like unto this world, but be ye changed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what that good, that acceptable and perfect will of God is."***

---

### Observations of decisive practical importance

**(1)** **μὴ συσχηματίζεσθε τῷ αἰῶνι τούτῳ** — *"do not be conformed to this age."*

The verb **συσχηματίζεσθε** is from **σχῆμα** — *outward form, fashion, appearance.* Present passive imperative — *"stop being shaped to fit the pattern of this age."*

The believer is **shaped by something** — the question is what. The world (this age) is constantly pressing its mold upon the believer through media, education, culture, fashion, expectation, ideology, advertising, conversation. To live without resistance is to be molded into the world's shape.

Paul commands: **do not conform.** Refuse the world's mold. Do not let this age dictate your outward form — your values, your priorities, your habits, your appearance, your patterns.

**(2)** **ἀλλὰ μεταμορφοῦσθε** — *"but be transformed."*

**μεταμορφοῦσθε** is from **μορφή** — *inward, essential form.* This is the same root as the **μεταμόρφωσις** (*metamorphosis*) of the caterpillar to butterfly. **A complete inward transformation of essential form.** Present passive imperative — *"keep being transformed."*

The contrast is precise: do not let your outward **σχῆμα** be shaped by the world; let your inward **μορφή** be transformed by God. **Outward form follows inward form.** When the inner being is being transformed, the outer life is increasingly conformed not to this age but to Christ.

**(3)** **τῇ ἀνακαινώσει τοῦ νοός** — *"by the renewing of the mind."*

**The mechanism of transformation is the renewing of the mind (νοῦς).**

The **νοῦς** is the believer's faculty of understanding, reasoning, perceiving, evaluating. **It is the mind, but not in a narrow intellectual sense — it is the seat of one's whole orientation, values, and worldview.**

The mind is renewed (**ἀνακαίνωσις**) — made new, fresh, reconstituted — by the Spirit working through the Word. As the believer feeds on Scripture, meditates on the Father's truth, beholds the Son's glory (2 Cor 3:18), and walks in the Spirit, his mind is progressively renewed.

**The renewed mind is the inner mechanism of outer transformation.** As the believer thinks differently — sees the world differently, evaluates differently, desires differently — his outward life is correspondingly transformed.

**(4)** **εἰς τὸ δοκιμάζειν ὑμᾶς τί τὸ θέλημα τοῦ Θεοῦ** — *"that you may prove / discern what the will of God is."*

The result of the renewed mind: **the ability to discern the Father's will.** The unrenewed mind cannot discern God's will — it is set on the things of the flesh (Rom 8:5-7). The renewed mind, oriented by the Spirit, can recognize, evaluate, and confirm what is the Father's good, well-pleasing, and perfect will.

**This is critically important for practical decision-making.** The believer does not need a special revelation for every decision — though he must be open to such. **The renewed mind, saturated with Scripture and yielded to the Spirit, can discern the Father's will in the ordinary course of life.**

---

### Practical Implications

**(1) The believer must be actively engaged in mind-renewal.** This requires:

- **Saturation in Scripture** — reading, meditating, memorizing, studying the Word so that the renewed mind has the material from which to think God's thoughts after Him.
- **Resistance to worldly inputs** — being discerning about what media, entertainment, conversation, and influences shape the mind.
- **Cultivation of holy thought patterns** — *"Whatsoever things are true, whatsoever things are honest, whatsoever things are just, whatsoever things are pure, whatsoever things are lovely, whatsoever things are of good report... think on these things"* (Phil 4:8 Geneva).
- **Walking in the Spirit** — for it is the Spirit who applies the Word to renew the mind.
- **Fellowship with the saints** — being shaped by godly community rather than by worldly community.

**(2) The renewing of the mind is an ongoing process.** The present tense **μεταμορφοῦσθε** suggests continuous action. The believer never reaches a point of completion in this life. He is always being transformed, always being renewed.

**(3) Discernment grows.** As the mind is renewed, the believer's ability to recognize the Father's will sharpens. What was once unclear becomes clearer. What was once a difficult judgment becomes an instinctive perception. The mature believer increasingly walks in the Father's will not by anguished deliberation but by Spirit-formed instinct.

---

## **Romans 12:3-21 — THE RENEWED MIND IN ACTION**

The remainder of Romans 12 spells out what the renewed mind looks like in practice. We will not exposit every verse, but let us note the major themes.

### **Romans 12:3-8 — Sober Self-Assessment and Gifts**

The renewed mind:
- **Does not think more highly of itself than it ought** (v. 3) — humility before God and others.
- **Recognizes itself as a member of one body** with diverse gifts (vv. 4-5).
- **Uses its gifts faithfully** — prophecy, ministry, teaching, exhortation, giving, ruling, mercy (vv. 6-8).

This anticipates the corporate dimension we will examine later. But notice: **the renewed mind has a sober estimation of self** and **a clear sense of place within the Body.**

---

### **Romans 12:9-13 — Love in Practice**


### **1599 Geneva:**
***"Let love be without dissimulation. Abhor that which is evil, and cleave unto that which is good. Be affectioned to love one another with brotherly love: in giving honor, go one before another. ..."***

The believer's love is to be **unhypocritical** — genuine, without pretense. The renewed mind hates evil and clings to good. It honors others. It is fervent in spirit, serving the Lord. It rejoices in hope, is patient in tribulation, continues steadfastly in prayer.

These are not isolated virtues; they are **the natural fruit of a renewed mind** living in the Spirit.

---

### **Romans 12:14-21 — Response to Enemies**


### **1599 Geneva:**
***"Bless them which persecute you: bless, I say, and curse not. ... Recompense to no man evil for evil: procure things honest in the sight of all men. ... Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine, I will repay, saith the Lord. ... Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with goodness."***

The most counter-cultural section of the chapter. The renewed mind:
- **Blesses persecutors** rather than cursing them.
- **Does not repay evil for evil.**
- **Does not avenge itself** but leaves vengeance to the Father.
- **Overcomes evil with good.**

This is impossible to the flesh. **Only the renewed mind, empowered by the Spirit, can love enemies in this way.** It is the very pattern of Christ on the cross.

---

# **PART 2 — GALATIANS 5: THE WALK BY THE SPIRIT AND THE WAR WITH THE FLESH**

## **The Context**

Paul has spent the first four chapters of Galatians defending the Gospel of grace against the Judaizers who insisted that Gentile believers must keep the Mosaic Law to be saved. Now, in chapters 5-6, he turns to the practical implications: **how is the believer to live who is free from the law?**

The answer is not antinomianism (lawlessness) but **walking by the Spirit.** The believer is free from the law as a system of justification, but he is by no means free to indulge the flesh. He is freed *from* law and sin *into* the Spirit's lordship.

---

## **Galatians 5:1 — STAND FAST IN FREEDOM**


### **1599 Geneva:**
***"Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage."***

---

### Observation

**Χριστὸς ἠλευθέρωσεν** — *"Christ has freed us."* Aorist tense — the freedom is accomplished. The believer **stands** in this freedom. He does not negotiate it, does not earn it, does not re-create it. He stands in what Christ has won.

**στήκετε οὖν** — *"stand therefore."* The command is to stand firm. Not to revert to the yoke of bondage (the law as a system of justification or the flesh as a master).

---

## **Galatians 5:13-15 — FREEDOM, NOT LICENSE**


### **1599 Geneva:**
***"For brethren, ye have been called unto liberty: only use not your liberty as an occasion unto the flesh: but by love serve one another. For all the Law is fulfilled in one word, which is this, Thou shalt love thy neighbor as thyself. But if ye bite and devour one another, take heed least *(lest)* ye be consumed one of another."***

---

### Observation

The believer is called to freedom — but freedom is not license. The believer is freed **from** the flesh's tyranny, not freed **for** the flesh's gratification. The believer's freedom is fulfilled in **loving service** to one another.

**The whole law is fulfilled in love** (citing Lev 19:18) — not because the believer is under the law for justification, but because the believer, walking by the Spirit, produces what the law required.

---

## **Galatians 5:16-17 — THE FOUNDATIONAL COMMAND**


### **1599 Geneva:**
***"Then I say, Walk in the Spirit, and ye shall not fulfill the lusts of the flesh. For the flesh lusteth against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh: and these are contrary one to another, so that ye cannot do the same things that ye would."***

---

### Observations of decisive importance

**(1)** **Πνεύματι περιπατεῖτε** — *"walk by the Spirit."* Present imperative — continuous walking. The Christian life is not a one-time decision; it is a continuous walk.

**The dative Πνεύματι is instrumental** — *"by means of the Spirit."* The Spirit is both the **path** and the **power** of the walk. The believer walks **by** the Spirit's leading and **in** the Spirit's empowering.

**(2) The promise:** **ἐπιθυμίαν σαρκὸς οὐ μὴ τελέσητε** — *"you shall by no means fulfill the lust of the flesh."*

**οὐ μή** with the aorist subjunctive is the strongest possible negation in Greek — *"absolutely not, by no means, in no way."* The promise is absolute: **the believer who walks by the Spirit will not fulfill the flesh's lust.**

This is the practical answer to every besetting sin. The believer does not defeat the flesh by attacking it directly; he defeats it by **walking in the Spirit** — at which point the flesh's lust is bypassed.

**(3)** **ἡ γὰρ σὰρξ ἐπιθυμεῖ κατὰ τοῦ Πνεύματος, τὸ δὲ Πνεῦμα κατὰ τῆς σαρκός** — *"the flesh lusts against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh."*

**Two opposing principles within the believer.** This is honest. The flesh (the remaining principle of sin in our mortal members) does not disappear at conversion. It lusts (ἐπιθυμεῖ — *desires, craves*) against the Spirit. And the Spirit lusts against the flesh — opposes it, contradicts it, fights it.

**These are contrary** (ἀλλήλοις ἀντίκειται). The believer cannot serve both. The believer's life is a constant choice — who will I walk with today, this hour, this moment? The Spirit or the flesh?

---

## **Galatians 5:19-21 — THE WORKS OF THE FLESH**


### **1599 Geneva:**
***"Moreover the works of the flesh are manifest, which are adultery, fornication, uncleanness, wantonness, Idolatry, witchcraft, hatred, debate, emulations, wrath, contentions, seditions, heresies, Envy, murthers *(murders)*, drunkenness, gluttony, and such like, whereof I tell you before, as I also have told you before, that they which do such things, shall not inherit the kingdom of God."***

---

### Observations

**(1)** **φανερά** — *"manifest, plain to see."* The works of the flesh are obvious. They are not hidden mysteries; they are visible in the lives of those who walk by the flesh.

**(2) The list is comprehensive** — covering:
- **Sexual immorality** (πορνεία, ἀκαθαρσία, ἀσέλγεια).
- **False religion** (εἰδωλολατρία, φαρμακεία — idolatry, sorcery).
- **Relational disorder** (ἔχθραι, ἔρις, ζῆλος, θυμοί, ἐριθεῖαι, διχοστασίαι, αἱρέσεις, φθόνοι — hatred, strife, jealousy, outbursts of wrath, selfish ambition, dissensions, factions, envy).
- **Lifestyle excess** (μέθαι, κῶμοι — drunkenness, revelries).

**The list is not exhaustive** — *"and such like"* (καὶ τὰ ὅμοια τούτοις) — but illustrative. The flesh produces these kinds of things wherever it reigns.

**(3) The warning:** *"They which do such things shall not inherit the kingdom of God."*

This is sobering. **A pattern of life characterized by the works of the flesh** is incompatible with citizenship in the kingdom. Paul is not saying a believer who falls into one of these sins is lost; he is saying that **those whose lives are characterized by these patterns** demonstrate they are not in the Spirit's possession.

The mark of a true believer is not the absence of all sin (we still battle the flesh), but the **direction** of life — being increasingly turned away from these patterns by the Spirit's work.

---

## **Galatians 5:22-23 — THE FRUIT OF THE SPIRIT**


### **1599 Geneva:**
***"But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, long suffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, Meekness, temperance: against such there is no Law."***

---

### Observations of supreme importance

**(1) Note: ὁ καρπός — singular, not "the fruits."** It is **one fruit** with nine facets. The Spirit's fruit is **a single integrated character** — not a buffet from which we can select preferences.

**(2)** Compare with the works of the flesh. The flesh produces "works" (ἔργα — plural, fragmented, disordered acts). The Spirit produces "fruit" (καρπός — singular, organic, integrated growth).

**This contrast is profound:** the flesh manufactures (works); the Spirit grows (fruit). The flesh produces by effort; the Spirit produces by organic life within. **The fruit cannot be forced; it grows naturally from a healthy union with the Spirit.**

**(3) The nine facets:**

- **ἀγάπη** — love (sacrificial, willed love)
- **χαρά** — joy (the deep joy of the Lord)
- **εἰρήνη** — peace (the peace of God that surpasses understanding)
- **μακροθυμία** — long-suffering / patience with people
- **χρηστότης** — kindness, useful service
- **ἀγαθωσύνη** — goodness, moral excellence
- **πίστις** — faith / faithfulness
- **πραΰτης** — meekness, gentle strength
- **ἐγκράτεια** — self-control

**These are the character of Christ.** Each one was perfectly displayed in Him. As the Spirit conforms the believer to the Son's image (Rom 8:29), these qualities increasingly appear in the believer's life — not by his manufacturing them, but by the Spirit's growing them.

**(4)** **κατὰ τῶν τοιούτων οὐκ ἔστιν νόμος** — *"against such things there is no law."*

A wonderful note. No law condemns these qualities. The believer who walks in the Spirit and produces this fruit is automatically in fulfillment of every law's righteous demand — without being under the law.

---

## **Galatians 5:24-25 — THE CRUCIFICATION OF THE FLESH AND THE WALK BY THE SPIRIT**


### **1599 Geneva:**
***"For they that are Christ's, have crucified the flesh, with the affections and the lusts. If we live in the Spirit, let us also walk in the Spirit."***

---

### Observations

**(1)** **οἱ τοῦ Χριστοῦ Ἰησοῦ τὴν σάρκα ἐσταύρωσαν** — *"those who belong to Christ Jesus have crucified the flesh."* Aorist tense — a completed past act.

This recapitulates Romans 6 in compressed form. **The flesh has been crucified** with its passions and lusts. The believer's identification with Christ's cross includes the crucifixion of the flesh.

**(2)** **εἰ ζῶμεν Πνεύματι, Πνεύματι καὶ στοιχῶμεν** — *"if we live by the Spirit, let us also walk by the Spirit."*

**The indicative-imperative pattern again, in compressed form:**
- **Indicative:** *"We live by the Spirit"* (an established reality).
- **Imperative:** *"Let us walk by the Spirit"* (the corresponding action).

The verb **στοιχῶμεν** is military — *"march in step, walk in line, keep in formation."* The believer who lives by the Spirit is to **march in step with the Spirit** — keeping in line with His leading, His pace, His direction.

---

# **PART 3 — COLOSSIANS 3: THE PUTTING OFF AND PUTTING ON**

## **The Context**

Paul has just spent Colossians 1-2 establishing the supremacy and fullness of Christ — Creator, Sustainer, Head of the Body, the Image of the invisible God, in whom dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily. He has warned against false philosophies that would diminish Christ. Now in chapter 3, he turns to the practical implications: **since you are united with this Christ, here is how you walk.**

---

## **Colossians 3:1-4 — THE INDICATIVE FOUNDATION**


### **1599 Geneva:**
***"If ye then be risen with Christ, seek those things which are above, where Christ sitteth at the right hand of God. Set your affections on things which are above, and not on things which are on the earth. For ye are dead, and your life is hid with Christ in God. When Christ which is our life shall appear, then shall ye also appear with him in glory."***

---

### Observations

We touched on these verses earlier in our examination of identification. Now we see them in their practical-imperative context.

**(1) The indicative reality (vv. 1, 3-4):**
- **You have been raised with Christ.**
- **You died.**
- **Your life is hidden with Christ in God.**
- **You will appear with Him in glory.**

These are the established facts of identification.

**(2) The imperatives flow from the indicative:**

- **τὰ ἄνω ζητεῖτε** — *"seek the things above."* Present imperative — keep seeking.
- **τὰ ἄνω φρονεῖτε** — *"set your mind on the things above."* Present imperative — keep your mind oriented upward.

The believer's orientation is to be **upward** — toward where Christ is. This is not other-worldly escapism; it is **eternal perspective** in the midst of earthly life.

**(3) The Connecting Word:** Notice the **οὖν** (*therefore*) in v. 1. **Because you have been raised** (indicative), **therefore seek the things above** (imperative). The pattern is exactly the same as Romans 6, 8, 12.

---

## **Colossians 3:5-9 — PUTTING OFF**


### **1599 Geneva:**
***"Mortify therefore your members which are on the earth, fornication, uncleanness, the inordinate affection, evil concupiscence, and covetousness which is idolatry. For the which things' sake the wrath of God cometh on the children of disobedience. Wherein ye also walked once, when ye lived in them. But now put ye away even all these things, wrath, anger, maliciousness, cursed speaking, filthy speaking, out of your mouth. Lie not one to another, seeing that ye have put off the old man with his works,"***

---

### Observations

**(1)** **Νεκρώσατε** — *"put to death."* Aorist imperative — a definitive action. The believer is to **put to death** the earthly members.

This is parallel to Romans 8:13 — *"if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body."* The believer's responsibility is active — to put to death — but the empowering is the Spirit's.

**(2) First list — sins of corrupt desire:**
- **πορνεία** — sexual immorality
- **ἀκαθαρσία** — uncleanness
- **πάθος** — passion (especially shameful)
- **ἐπιθυμίαν κακήν** — evil desire
- **πλεονεξίαν** — covetousness, **which is idolatry**

These are the sins of disordered desire — wanting what is not ours to have.

**(3) Second list — sins of corrupt relationship:**
- **ὀργήν** — wrath
- **θυμόν** — anger (sudden outbursts)
- **κακίαν** — malice
- **βλασφημίαν** — slander
- **αἰσχρολογίαν** — filthy/abusive speech

These are the sins that destroy relationships within the Body.

**(4)** **μὴ ψεύδεσθε εἰς ἀλλήλους** — *"do not lie to one another."* A specific application of the relational sins. Lying is incompatible with the new identity in Christ.

**(5)** **ἀπεκδυσάμενοι τὸν παλαιὸν ἄνθρωπον** — *"having put off the old man."*

**The verb ἀπεκδυσάμενοι is aorist middle — a completed action.** The old man has been put off. The believer is not striving to put off what is still part of his identity; he is **walking in the reality** that the old man has already been put off in Christ.

This is the indicative undergirding the imperative. **Because the old man has been put off** (positionally), **therefore put off these practices that belonged to him** (practically).

---

## **Colossians 3:10-11 — PUTTING ON THE NEW MAN**


### **1599 Geneva:**
***"And have put on the new, which is renewed in knowledge after the image of him that created him. Where is neither Grecian nor Jew, circumcision nor uncircumcision, Barbarian, Scythian, bond, free: But Christ is all, and in all things."***

---

### Observations

**(1)** **ἐνδυσάμενοι τὸν νέον** — *"having put on the new [man]."* Again aorist middle — a completed action. The new man has been put on.

**(2)** **τὸν ἀνακαινούμενον εἰς ἐπίγνωσιν κατ᾽ εἰκόνα τοῦ κτίσαντος αὐτόν** — *"being renewed unto knowledge according to the image of Him who created him."*

The new man is being **continually renewed** (present participle) **unto full knowledge** of the One in whose **image** he was created.

The image is Christ Himself — the perfect Image of the invisible God (Col 1:15). The new man is being progressively renewed to fully bear that image.

**(3)** **ὅπου οὐκ ἔνι...** — *"where there is not..."* — **in the new man, the old divisions are abolished:**
- Greek and Jew
- Circumcised and uncircumcised
- Barbarian, Scythian
- Slave, free

**ἀλλὰ τὰ πάντα καὶ ἐν πᾶσιν Χριστός** — *"but Christ is all and in all."*

In the new humanity that is the Body of Christ, **Christ is all** — the unifying reality that transcends every former division. This anticipates the corporate dimension we will examine later.

---

## **Colossians 3:12-17 — THE NEW MAN IN PRACTICE**


### **1599 Geneva:**
***"Now therefore as the elect of God holy and beloved, put on the bowels of mercies, kindness, humbleness of mind, meekness, long suffering, Forbearing one another, and forgiving one another, if any man have a quarrel to another: even as Christ forgave, even so do ye. And above all these things put on love, which is the bond of perfectness. And let the peace of God rule in your hearts, to the which ye are called in one body, and be ye thankful."***

---

### Observations

**(1)** **Ἐνδύσασθε οὖν** — *"therefore put on."* The imperative flows from the identity: **as elect, holy, and beloved of God**, you are to put on these characteristics.

**(2) The garments of the new man:**
- **σπλάγχνα οἰκτιρμοῦ** — *"bowels of mercies"* (deep, visceral compassion)
- **χρηστότητα** — kindness
- **ταπεινοφροσύνην** — humility
- **πραΰτητα** — meekness
- **μακροθυμίαν** — long-suffering
- **ἀνεχόμενοι ἀλλήλων** — bearing with one another
- **χαριζόμενοι ἑαυτοῖς** — forgiving one another (literally, *"granting grace"*)

**(3) The pattern:** **καθὼς καὶ ὁ Κύριος ἐχαρίσατο ὑμῖν οὕτως καὶ ὑμεῖς** — *"as the Lord forgave you, so also you."*

**The believer forgives because and as he has been forgiven.** The Father's gracious treatment in Christ becomes the pattern of our treatment of others.

**(4)** **ἐπὶ πᾶσιν δὲ τούτοις τὴν ἀγάπην, ὅ ἐστιν σύνδεσμος τῆς τελειότητος** — *"and above all these things, love, which is the bond of perfectness."*

**Love is the binding garment that holds all the others together.** Without love, kindness becomes flattery, humility becomes self-loathing, patience becomes mere endurance. **Love is the unifying principle.**

**(5)** **ἡ εἰρήνη τοῦ Χριστοῦ βραβευέτω ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις ὑμῶν** — *"let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts."*

The verb **βραβευέτω** is from **βραβεύς** — *an umpire, an arbiter.* Christ's peace is to be the **umpire** in the believer's heart — settling disputes, making decisions, ruling over conflicts. When peace departs from the heart, it is often the signal that something is wrong in our direction.

---

## **Colossians 3:18-4:1 — PRACTICAL SANCTIFICATION IN RELATIONSHIPS**


### **1599 Geneva (summary):**
***"Wives, submit yourselves unto your husbands, as it is comely in the Lord. ... Husbands, love your wives, and be not bitter unto them. ... Children, obey your parents in all thing *(things)* for that is well pleasing unto the Lord. ... Fathers, provoke not your children to anger, least *(lest)* they be discouraged. ... Servants, be obedient unto them that are your masters according to the flesh, in all things, not with eye service as men pleasers, but in singleness of heart, fearing God. ... Ye masters, do unto your servants, that which is just, and equal, knowing that ye also have a master in heaven."***

---

### Observation

The practical sanctification reaches into every sphere of relationship:
- **Marriage:** wives submit, husbands love.
- **Family:** children obey, fathers do not provoke.
- **Work/economic relationships:** servants serve as unto the Lord, masters treat servants justly.

**The renewed mind transforms every relationship.** The Father's purposes for family, work, and social relations are honored not because of law but because **the new man, indwelt by the Spirit, walks in love.**

## **Colossians 3:17 — THE GOVERNING PRINCIPLE**


### **1599 Geneva:**
***"And whatsoever ye shall do, in word or deed, do all in the Name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God even the Father by him."***

---

### Observation

**The single, governing principle of all practical Christian living:**

- **Whatever you do — in word or deed — do all in the Name of the Lord Jesus.**
- **Give thanks to God the Father through Him.**

Every action of the believer's life is to be done **in the Name of the Lord Jesus** — that is, as one belonging to Him, representing Him, acting in His authority, for His glory. And every action is to be accompanied by **thanksgiving to the Father through Him.**

**This single verse captures the comprehensive nature of Christian practice.** No part of life is excluded. No moment is secular. Every word and deed is to be in His Name and to the Father's glory.

---

# **GRAND SYNTHESIS — THE THREE PASSAGES TOGETHER**

Romans 12, Galatians 5, and Colossians 3 are not three different teachings — they are **three angles on the same reality:** how the indicative of who we are in Christ produces the imperative of how we walk.

## **The Common Pattern**

| Element | Romans 12 | Galatians 5 | Colossians 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Indicative foundation** | God's mercies (11 chapters of doctrine) | Christ has freed us (1:1-4:31) | Raised with Christ; died; life hidden with Him |
| **Pivot word** | οὖν (12:1) | οὖν (5:1, implied throughout) | οὖν (3:1, 5, 12) |
| **Body presentation** | Present your bodies (12:1) | Walk by the Spirit (5:16) | Mortify your members (3:5) |
| **Mind renewal** | Renewing of the mind (12:2) | Mind set on Spirit (implied) | Set affection on things above (3:1-2) |
| **Discernment of will** | Prove what is good will of God (12:2) | Led by the Spirit (5:18) | Walk in wisdom (3:16) |
| **Putting off** | Cast off works of darkness | Crucified the flesh (5:24) | Put off the old man (3:9) |
| **Putting on** | Christ's character | Fruit of the Spirit (5:22-23) | Put on the new man (3:10-14) |
| **Love as governing** | Love without dissimulation (12:9) | Through love serve one another (5:13) | Love is the bond of perfectness (3:14) |
| **Comprehensive scope** | All relationships, including enemies | Every part of life | Every sphere — marriage, family, work |

---

## **The Single Practical Theology**

What emerges from all three passages is **one coherent doctrine of practical sanctification:**

**The believer who has died with Christ, been raised with Him, is seated with Him at the Father's right hand, and is indwelt by the Spirit — this believer is to walk in newness of life by:**

1. **Presenting his body as a living sacrifice** (Rom 12:1) / **mortifying his earthly members** (Col 3:5) — the comprehensive yielding of the physical body to God.

2. **Being renewed in the mind** (Rom 12:2; Col 3:10) — the ongoing transformation of the inner orientation by the Word and the Spirit.

3. **Walking by the Spirit** (Gal 5:16) — depending moment by moment on the Spirit's leading and empowering.

4. **Putting off the works of the old man** (Col 3:5-9; Gal 5:19-21) — actively refusing the patterns of the flesh.

5. **Putting on the new man** (Col 3:10-14) — actively cultivating the patterns of Christ's character.

6. **Walking in love** (Rom 12:9-21; Gal 5:13-14; Col 3:14) — the unifying principle of all Christian practice.

7. **Doing all in the Name of the Lord Jesus, with thanksgiving to the Father** (Col 3:17) — the comprehensive governing principle.

---

## **The Practical Modern Application**

For the modern believer, this means:

**In the morning:** Reckon yourself dead to sin and alive to God. Present your body to the Father as a living sacrifice. Set your mind on things above. Open the Word and feed the renewing process.

**Throughout the day:** Walk by the Spirit. Yield to His leading. Put off the works of the flesh as they arise. Put on the characteristics of Christ. Do every word and deed in the Name of the Lord Jesus, with thanksgiving.

**In relationships:** Love without dissimulation. Bless those who persecute. Forgive as Christ has forgiven. Honor your spouse, parents, children, employer, employees, brethren. Be at peace with all men so far as it depends on you. Overcome evil with good.

**In the mind:** Resist the world's mold. Embrace the Spirit's transformation. Think on what is true, honorable, just, pure, lovely, of good report. Take every thought captive to obey Christ. Renew the mind through Scripture, prayer, and fellowship.

**In moments of failure:** Return immediately to the reckoning. Confess. Trust the cleansing blood. Receive forgiveness. Get up and walk again. Do not despair; do not abandon the walk; do not let one failure define the day.

**In suffering:** Recognize the Father's purpose — conformity to the Son. Trust that all things work together for good. Hold fast the unshakable hope. Look beyond present sufferings to the coming glory.

**In all things:** Live as one who belongs to the Father, walks in the Son, is indwelt by the Spirit, and is destined for glory. **You are not who you were. You are in Christ. Walk worthy.**

---

# **A CLOSING OBSERVATION**

Brother, what we have surveyed in these three passages is **the genius of the New Testament's ethic.** It is not a system of rules to be followed; it is a life to be lived out of who we already are. It is not the achievement of an unreal status; it is the working out of what is positionally true.

The modern believer who walks in this reality is:
- **Free** — from law as a means of justification, from the flesh as a master, from the world as a mold.
- **Empowered** — by the indwelling Spirit who produces what the believer cannot manufacture.
- **Oriented** — toward the things above, where Christ is seated.
- **Renewed** — in the mind, progressively conformed to the Son's image.
- **Engaged** — in active putting off and putting on, by the Spirit's power.
- **In love** — the unifying principle of all Christian practice.
- **In community** — for the new humanity transcends every former division.
- **In every sphere** — for nothing in the believer's life is exempt from the Lordship of Christ.

This is what the Father's eternal purpose looks like in daily practice. This is what it means to be **a child of the Father, in the Beloved Son, by the indwelling Spirit, walking by faith until He appears.**
