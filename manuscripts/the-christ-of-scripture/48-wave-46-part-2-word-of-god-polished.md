# **PART 2 — THE WORD OF GOD: THE RENEWING OF THE MIND**

We established under Romans 12:2 that the believer is transformed **by the renewing of the mind.** The primary instrument of this renewal is **the Word of God** applied by the Spirit.

## **The Nature of Scripture**

### **2 Timothy 3:16-17:**

*"For the whole Scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable to teach, to convince *(convict)*, to correct, and to instruct in righteousness, That the man of God may be absolute *(complete, perfect)*, being made perfect unto all good works."*

---

### Observations of foundational importance

**(1)** **θεόπνευστος** — *"God-breathed."* Scripture is the very breath of God — proceeding from Him as our own breath proceeds from us. This single word establishes the divine origin and authority of the Word.

**(2) Four practical uses:**
- **διδασκαλίαν** — *teaching* (showing what is true).
- **ἐλεγμόν** — *convicting / reproof* (showing what is wrong).
- **ἐπανόρθωσιν** — *correction / setting straight* (showing how to get right).
- **παιδείαν τὴν ἐν δικαιοσύνῃ** — *training in righteousness* (showing how to stay right).

**The Word makes the believer complete** — **ἄρτιος** (*fit, complete, fitted*) — equipped for every good work.

---

## **The Word's Active Power**

### **Hebrews 4:12:**

*"For the word of God is lively *(alive)*, and mighty in operation, and sharper than any two edged sword, and entereth through, even unto the dividing asunder of the soul and the spirit, and of the joints, and the marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts, and the intents of the heart."*

---

### Observation

The Word of God is **ζῶν** (*living*) — alive, active, not merely a printed text. It is **ἐνεργής** (*energetically working*) — accomplishing what God sends it to do. It penetrates beyond the surface to **discern thoughts and intents** of the heart.

This is why the Word renews the mind. The Spirit uses the living Word to penetrate, expose, instruct, and transform.

---

## **The Modes of Engaging the Word**

### **1. Reading**

The believer should read the Word regularly — substantial portions, not merely scattered verses. The whole counsel of God comes through engagement with the whole text.

### **2. Hearing**

### **Romans 10:17:**

*"Then faith is by hearing, and hearing by the word of God."*

Faith comes by hearing the Word. The believer benefits from preaching, teaching, and the audible reading of Scripture — both privately (reading aloud) and corporately (in the assembly).

### **3. Meditating**

### **Psalm 1:1-2 LXX (Ps 1:1-2 MT):**

> Happy the man … Rather, his will is in the law of the Lord, and on his law he will meditate day and night. — **Psalm 1:1-2, NETS**

**μελετάω** — to meditate, ponder, chew over, mutter to oneself. **The blessed man meditates on the Law day and night.** Meditation is the slow, deliberate pondering of a text — turning it over, asking what it means, applying it to one's life, allowing it to penetrate.

### **4. Memorizing**

### **Psalm 118:11 LXX (Ps 119:11 MT):**

> In my heart I hid your sayings so that I may not sin against you. — **Psalm 118:11 LXX (= MT 119:11), NETS**

The Word hidden in the heart guards against sin. Memorized Scripture becomes available to the Spirit's recall in moments of temptation, trial, and decision.

### **5. Studying**

The serious examination of Scripture — comparing passage with passage, examining the Greek and Hebrew where helpful, considering context. Per our maxims, this is the discipline of letting Scripture interpret itself by two or more witnesses.

### **6. Applying / Obeying**

### **James 1:22-25:**

*"And be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves..."*

The Word is given **to be done**, not merely heard. The believer who hears but does not do **deceives himself.** The renewing of the mind issues in renewed living.

---

## **Practical Implications**

**1. Establish a regular reading plan** — daily, substantial portions. Many find systematic reading through the entire Bible over a year or two beneficial.

**2. Meditate on key passages** — slow down. Read less, think more. Let the Spirit illuminate.

**3. Memorize strategic passages** — psalms, key NT verses on identification with Christ, promises, exhortations.

**4. Study with the LXX where you are able** — per our maxims, this is the text Jesus and the Apostles used.

**5. Apply what you read** — ask: what does this mean? what does it require? how does it call me to walk?

**6. Read in community** — the Word is read in the assembly, and small-group study deepens understanding.

**7. Pray Scripture** — let the Word shape your prayers. The Psalms are especially helpful.

**8. Beware of substituting commentary for text** — read the Bible itself first. Commentaries can help, but they are servants of the text, not replacements for it.
