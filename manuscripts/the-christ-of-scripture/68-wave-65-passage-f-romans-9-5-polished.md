# **PASSAGE F — ROMANS 9:5**

## **Romans 9:5 — Geneva 1599:**

*"Of whom are the fathers, and of whom concerning the flesh, Christ came, who is God over all, blessed for ever, Amen."*

---

## **The Punctuation Question**

Ancient Greek manuscripts had no punctuation. Romans 9:5 can be punctuated in two significantly different ways:

### **Reading A (Geneva, most traditional translations):**
*"...of whom concerning the flesh Christ came, who is God over all, blessed for ever."*

Under this reading, **"God over all, blessed for ever"** is a descriptive clause about **Christ.**

### **Reading B (some scholars):**
*"...of whom concerning the flesh Christ came. God, who is over all, be blessed for ever."*

Under this reading, the second clause is a **doxology to the Father** — a separate statement praising God (the Father) after mentioning Christ's descent.

**Both punctuations are grammatically possible.**

---

## **Contextual Considerations**

**Arguments for Reading A (Christ is called God):**
- The phrase flows continuously from "Christ came" without an obvious break.
- Some argue this is the more natural Greek grammar.

**Arguments for Reading B (doxology to the Father):**
- Paul consistently distinguishes Christ from "God" (the Father) elsewhere in Romans (1:7; 15:6; etc.).
- Paul typically ends with doxologies to God the Father.
- The word order is more natural for a doxology in Greek.

---

## **Under Our Framework**

**Per our maxims, we do not need to force this passage to bear doctrinal weight it cannot honestly carry.**

**Under Reading A (Christ is called God):**

Under your Name-authority framework, this is coherent — Christ, as the Only-Begotten bearing the Father's Name and manifesting His fullness bodily, can be called *"God over all, blessed for ever"* — because in Him the Father's supremacy is manifested. This does not make Him identical Person with the Father.

**Under Reading B (doxology to the Father):**

The Father remains "God over all, blessed for ever" — the Source and Supreme One. Christ came according to the flesh; the Father is praised as the God who accomplished this.

**Either reading is compatible with our framework.** Per our maxim #2 (two or more witnesses), we should not build doctrine on a punctuation choice in a single verse when the meaning is genuinely contested.

**The safest reading under our maxims is to acknowledge both possibilities and let the clearer texts govern our doctrine** — texts like John 17:3, 1 Cor 8:6, and 1 Tim 2:5 which unambiguously identify the Father as the One God.
