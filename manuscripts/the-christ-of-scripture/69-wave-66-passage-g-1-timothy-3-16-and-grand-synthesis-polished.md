# **PASSAGE G — 1 TIMOTHY 3:16**

## **1 Timothy 3:16 — Geneva 1599:**

*"And without controversy, great is the mystery of godliness, which is, God is manifested in the flesh, justified in the Spirit, seen of Angels, preached unto the Gentiles, believed on in the world, and received up in glory."*


## **The Textual Variant**

### **The Textus Receptus (followed by the Geneva) reads:**
*"Θεὸς ἐφανερώθη ἐν σαρκί..."*
*"God was manifest in the flesh..."*

### **The earlier Greek manuscripts (Sinaiticus, Alexandrinus, etc.) read:**
*"ὃς ἐφανερώθη ἐν σαρκί..."*
*"He/who was manifest in the flesh..."*

**The difference is a single letter in Greek:**
- **ΘΣ** (contracted form of Θεός — "God")
- **ΟΣ** (the relative pronoun "who")

---

## **Careful Textual Assessment**

Under our maxim regarding the LXX for the OT, we noted the Geneva follows a text tradition that at times differs from the earlier LXX manuscripts. For the NT, similar textual questions arise.

**The earlier Greek manuscripts consistently read "ὅς" (who) rather than "Θεός" (God).**

**Many careful scholars (including many who hold high views of Christ) acknowledge that the original reading was likely "ὅς"** — *"who was manifest in the flesh."* The change to "Θεός" appears to have happened later in the manuscript tradition, possibly through a scribal error (a small horizontal line added inside the omicron) that was then propagated.

**Under our maxim #1 (let God be true):** We should hold to what the earliest and best manuscripts reveal about the original text.

---

## **Under Both Readings**

### **Reading with "ὅς" (who):**

*"Great is the mystery of godliness: **who** was manifest in the flesh, justified in the Spirit, seen of angels, preached unto the Gentiles, believed on in the world, received up into glory."*

Under this reading, the antecedent of "who" is the **mystery** — meaning the Person of Christ is the subject of what follows. Christ was manifest in flesh (the incarnation). No claim is made that "God" (the Father) was manifest in flesh in a way that would collapse Persons.

### **Reading with "Θεός" (God):**

*"Great is the mystery of godliness: **God** was manifest in the flesh..."*

Under this reading, "God" refers to a divine reality manifested in Christ. But note: even here, under your Name-authority framework, this reads as **"the Father's fullness was manifested bodily in the Man Christ Jesus"** (Col 2:9) — not that the Father Himself became flesh, but that the divine nature the Son bore was manifested in His incarnation.

**Under our maxims, either reading is compatible with our framework** — but the textual evidence favors "ὅς" (who), which does not press any metaphysical claim that we must labor to reconcile.

# **GRAND SYNTHESIS — ALL SEVEN PASSAGES TOGETHER**

Brother, we have now examined seven of the most difficult Christological passages in the NT, along with Isaiah 9:6:

| Passage | Traditional Weight | Under Our Framework |
|---|---|---|
| **John 1:1-3** | The Word "was God" | Word shares Father's Spirit-nature qualitatively; distinct in Person from ὁ Θεός |

| **John 10:30** | *"I and the Father are one"* | Oneness in purpose, will, Spirit-communion — not Person-identity |

| **1 John 5:20** | *"This is the true God"* | Grammatically, more naturally refers to the Father (John 17:3) |
| **Isa 9:6 LXX** | *"Mighty God, Everlasting Father"* | LXX reads *"Messenger of Great Counsel"* — no metaphysical challenge |
| **Rom 9:5** | Punctuation-dependent | Compatible with either reading; not doctrinally decisive |
| **1 Tim 3:16** | *"God manifest in flesh"* | Best manuscripts read *"who"* — coherent with our framework |

**Every one of these passages is honestly coherent under your two combined insights:**

1. **The Son was begotten of the Father in Spirit** — existing as spirit with the Father before a body was prepared for Him.
2. **"ἐγώ εἰμι" functions as a legal formula of Name-authority** — the Son bearing the Father's Name and speaking with the Father's self-identifying voice.

**Under these insights:**

- **No text is strained.**
- **No text requires the post-Nicene metaphysical formulations.**
- **The Shema is preserved absolutely** — the Father remains the One God.
- **The Son is honored fully** — as the Only-Begotten, bearing the Father's Name, sharing His Spirit-nature, manifesting His fullness bodily.
- **The consistent pattern is maintained:** Father as Source, Son as Sent and Authorized, all things unto the Father's glory.
