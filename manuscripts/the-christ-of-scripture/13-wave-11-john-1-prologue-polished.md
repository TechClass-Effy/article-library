## **John 1: The Prologue**

John 1:1-18 — the Prologue — is perhaps the most cited, most contested, and most assumed-upon passage in the entire NT regarding the identity of Christ. Let us approach it with strict discipline under our maxims.

Per our second maxim, I cannot establish anything from John 1 *alone* — every doctrine requires two or more witnesses. So as we examine, we will note what John says and then ask: *does Scripture establish this same point elsewhere?*

Let us proceed phrase by phrase.

---

## **JOHN 1:1**

> In the beginning was that Word, and that Word was with God, and that Word was God. — **John 1:1, 1599 Geneva**

---

### Careful Examination — Phrase by Phrase

#### **Phrase 1: Ἐν ἀρχῇ ἦν ὁ Λόγος** — *"In the beginning was the Word."*

**What the text strictly says:**
- The phrase **Ἐν ἀρχῇ** is the same as Genesis 1:1 LXX. John is deliberately echoing Genesis.
- **ἦν** is imperfect — *"was [existing/being]"* — implies continuous existence at that point.
- **ὁ Λόγος** — *"the Word."* The definite article is present in Greek; the Geneva renders *"that Word"* to capture the definiteness.

**What "ὁ Λόγος" means:** This is the critical lexical question. **Λόγος** in Greek can mean:
- A spoken word, utterance, declaration
- A reasoned account, message, report
- A principle, plan, rational expression
- (In some philosophical usage) a personified rational principle

**John does not define the term in v. 1.** He uses it without explanation, as if his readers should know. We must ask: *does Scripture itself establish what John means by* **ὁ Λόγος**?

---

#### **Phrase 2: καὶ ὁ Λόγος ἦν πρὸς τὸν Θεόν** — *"and the Word was with God."*

**What the text strictly says:**
- **πρὸς τὸν Θεόν** — *"with/toward God."* The preposition **πρός** with the accusative often indicates orientation, presence-toward, face-to-face relation.
- **τὸν Θεόν** — *"the God"* — definite article. Per our foundation, **ὁ Θεός** in NT usage virtually always refers to **the Father** (see John 17:3; 1 Cor 8:6).

**Honest observation:** The Word is **with** the God (the Father). This phrase distinguishes **ὁ Λόγος** from **ὁ Θεός**. The Word is *toward / with* God — implying relation, not identity-of-person.

---

#### **Phrase 3: καὶ Θεὸς ἦν ὁ Λόγος** — *"and the Word was God."*

**What the text strictly says — observe carefully:**
- **Θεὸς** here has **no article** — it is *anarthrous* (without **ὁ**).
- The subject is **ὁ Λόγος** (with article); the predicate is **Θεὸς** (no article).

**The Greek grammar matters:**
- In v. 1b, the Word is **πρὸς τὸν Θεόν** — *with* **the** **God** (the Father, with article).
- In v. 1c, the Word is **Θεὸς** — anarthrous, predicate nominative.

The grammatical distinction is *deliberate* on John's part. In v. 1b, **ὁ Θεός** = the Father (identified Person). In v. 1c, **Θεός** (anarthrous) is being used qualitatively — describing the *nature*, *character*, or *category* of the Word, not identifying Him as **the God** of v. 1b.

**Compare:** If John had written **καὶ ὁ Θεὸς ἦν ὁ Λόγος** (with article), it would mean *"and the Word was [identically] the God [with whom He was]"* — a flat contradiction with v. 1b. John deliberately avoided that. He wrote **Θεὸς ἦν ὁ Λόγος** to say something different — what exactly, we must let Scripture interpret.

**Honest observation:** The text says the Word **was God** in some sense, but the *grammar itself* prevents collapsing the Word into the Person of the Father (who is **ὁ Θεός** of v. 1b).

---

### What v. 1 strictly establishes:

1. There is a figure called **ὁ Λόγος** (the Word).
2. **ὁ Λόγος** existed at "the beginning" (Gen 1:1's beginning).
3. **ὁ Λόγος** was *with* **ὁ Θεός** (the Father — distinct from the Father).
4. **ὁ Λόγος** was **Θεός** (anarthrous — qualitatively *God*, divine in nature/character, but *not identically* the **ὁ Θεός** of v. 1b).

### What v. 1 does NOT yet say:

- John has not yet said **ὁ Λόγος** is a Person.
- John has not yet said **ὁ Λόγος** is Jesus.
- John has not yet identified **ὁ Λόγος** with the Son.

These will come in later verses. We must read forward to see how John himself defines the Word.

---

## **JOHN 1:2-3**

> This same was in the beginning with God. All things were made by it, and without it was made nothing that was made. — **John 1:2-3, 1599 Geneva**

---

### Careful examination

**Notice — the Geneva translators rendered the pronouns as "it"** — treating **ὁ Λόγος** as the Word/utterance/expression, **not yet** as a personal pronoun "He." This is honest to the Greek at this stage. The Greek pronoun **αὐτοῦ** is grammatically masculine (matching **Λόγος**, which is masculine in gender), but Greek grammatical masculinity does not by itself prove personal masculinity. (E.g., **ὁ νόμος** — *the law* — is grammatically masculine; we still say "it.")

**The Geneva's choice to translate "it" reflects an honest reading of the Greek up to this point**: the Word may be a personification of God's creative utterance/plan, not yet established as a distinct personal being.

---

#### **What v. 2-3 strictly says:**

- **οὗτος** — *"this [one/thing]"* — refers back to **ὁ Λόγος**.
- **ἦν ἐν ἀρχῇ πρὸς τὸν Θεόν** — *"was in the beginning with God."* Repetition of vv. 1a-b for emphasis.
- **πάντα δι᾽ αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο** — *"all things came to be through it."* The Word is the *means* (**διά** + genitive — *through, by means of*) of all things coming into being.

**Cross-witness:** Does Scripture elsewhere say that the Father created **through** something or someone?

**Psalm 32:6 LXX (33:6):**
> By the word of the Lord the heavens were made firm, and by the breath of his mouth all their host — **Psalm 32:6 (33:6 MT), NETS**

*Geneva: "By the word of the Lord were the heavens made, and all the host of them by the breath of his mouth."*

The Psalm declares that the heavens were made **by the word of the LORD** — **τῷ λόγῳ** — *by the Word.* This is a direct OT witness to the Father creating **through His Word** — and the LXX uses the same term **λόγος**.

This is significant. The OT *itself* establishes that **the Father creates through His Word** — and the Word is the Father's own utterance/expression, not a separate God. This perfectly reconciles with Isaiah 44:24 (*"I stretched out the heavens **alone**"*) — because the Word is *the Father's own Word*, not a second Deity.

---

### Honest assessment of vv. 1-3:

The Prologue, **strictly read**, can be understood in either of two ways consistent with the grammar:

**Reading A:** **ὁ Λόγος** is a pre-existent *Person* distinct from the Father, who was "with" the Father and was "divine in nature" (Θεός qualitatively), and through whom the Father created.

**Reading B:** **ὁ Λόγος** is the Father's own *expressed Word / creative utterance / plan*, "with Him" in the sense that His Word is always His own, "divine" in the sense that it is God's own Word, and the means by which He created — exactly as Psalm 33:6 says.

**Up to v. 3, both readings fit the Greek.** John has not yet decided the question for us.

---

## **JOHN 1:14 — The Decisive Verse**

Let us jump to the verse that *does* clarify John's intent:

> And that Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we saw the glory thereof, as the glory of the only begotten Son of the Father) full of grace and truth. — **John 1:14, 1599 Geneva**

---

### Observations

**(1)** **ὁ Λόγος σὰρξ ἐγένετο** — *"the Word became flesh."* This is the decisive turning point. The Word — whatever its prior mode of existence — *became* flesh. The verb is **ἐγένετο** (came to be, became).

**(2)** **δόξαν ὡς μονογενοῦς παρὰ Πατρός** — *"glory as of the Only-Begotten from the Father."* John now explicitly connects the Word-become-flesh to **the Only-Begotten of the Father** — the title used elsewhere of Jesus.

**(3)** Notice the careful word: **παρὰ Πατρός** — *"from beside the Father."* The Only-Begotten is *from* the Father — consistent with everything Scripture has said about the Son being begotten of the Father.

---

### What v. 14 establishes:

The Word that *was* in the beginning, was with God, and was qualitatively God — **became flesh** and was the Only-Begotten of the Father. John is identifying the Word with the Son.

**However — observe carefully:** John does NOT say the Word *was* a Person before becoming flesh. He says the Word *became* flesh. This is consistent with either:

**Reading A:** A pre-existent personal Word *took on* flesh (a personal Being clothed Himself in humanity).

**Reading B:** The Father's eternal Word/plan/expression *was enacted as / became* flesh in the person of Jesus — i.e., what was previously the Father's Word now took human form.

Both readings honor v. 14.

---

## **JOHN 1:18 — Another Key Verse**

> No man hath seen God at any time: that only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him. — **John 1:18, 1599 Geneva**

---

### Observations

**(1) Textual note:** There is a manuscript variant — *"only-begotten God"* vs. *"only-begotten Son."* The earlier manuscripts (Sinaiticus, Vaticanus) read **μονογενὴς Θεὸς**; the later Byzantine tradition reads **μονογενὴς Υἱός**. The Geneva, following the Textus Receptus, reads "only begotten Son."

Per our maxims, we follow the LXX for the OT but the NT text is what it is. The Geneva text is what you have specified. Let us proceed with **"only begotten Son."**

**(2)** **Θεὸν οὐδεὶς ἑώρακεν πώποτε** — *"No man hath seen God at any time."* This is the absolute statement. **No one has ever seen God** (the Father). This is consistent with Ex 33:20, 1 Tim 6:16, and Isa 44-46's declaration that the Father is uniquely sovereign and (in this verse) unseen.

**(3)** **ὁ μονογενὴς Υἱὸς... ἐξηγήσατο** — *"the only-begotten Son... has declared/explained Him."* The Son is the One who has made the Father known. **ἐξηγήσατο** is the root of our word "exegesis" — *He has unfolded, explained, expounded* the Father.

---

### What v. 18 establishes:

- **No one has seen the Father.** (Confirms our foundation: the Father is the One God of Isa 44-46, unseen and incomparable.)
- **The Son has made Him known.** The Son is the *revealer* of the Father.

---

## **The Apostolic Definition of "the Word" — A Second Witness**

We have John's use of **ὁ Λόγος**. Does Scripture elsewhere give us a second witness as to what *the Word of God* refers to?

> That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with these our eyes, which we have looked upon, and these hands of ours have handled of that Word of life, (For that life was made manifest, and we have seen it, and bear witness, and show unto you that eternal life, which was with the Father, and was made manifest unto us) — **1 John 1:1-2, 1599 Geneva**

**Critically observe:**
- John uses **neuter relative pronouns** — **ὃ** — *"that which"* — not **ὃς** *"He who."*
- **"That which was from the beginning"** — the same phrase as **ἐν ἀρχῇ ἦν** of John 1:1.
- **"the Word of life"** — and John then equates this with **ἡ ζωὴ ἡ αἰώνιος** — *"the eternal life"* — *"which was with the Father and was manifested to us."*

This is a significant witness. John, writing later, calls the same subject **the Word of life** and **the eternal life that was with the Father.** This *can* support either reading:

**Reading A:** The pre-existent Personal Word/Life was with the Father and was manifested in flesh.

**Reading B:** The eternal life/plan/Word of the Father was manifested *in* Jesus — and "we have seen, heard, handled" *Jesus*, who is the manifestation of that eternal life.

Either way, the Father is the source (*"with the Father"* — **πρὸς τὸν Πατέρα**), and what was with Him has been manifested.

---

## **Revelation 19:13 — Another Use of "the Word of God"**

> And he was clothed with a garment dipped in blood *(the Geneva prints "dipt")*, and his name is called the Word of God. — **Revelation 19:13, 1599 Geneva**

This is a direct witness that **ὁ Λόγος τοῦ Θεοῦ** — *"the Word of God"* — is **a Name given to the returning Christ.** This strongly supports the personal identification of **ὁ Λόγος** with Christ — at least in His exalted form. The name is **given** to Him (**κέκληται** — *"has been called"*), not innately possessed apart from any conferral.

---

## **HONEST ASSESSMENT OF JOHN 1**

### What John 1 *strictly* establishes by its own text + cross-witnesses:

1. There is **the Word** — **ὁ Λόγος** — present at the beginning (echoing Gen 1:1).
2. The Word was **with God [the Father]** and was **God** (anarthrous — *qualitatively divine*, not identically the Person of the Father).
3. **All things came to be through the Word.** (Cross-witness: Ps 33:6 LXX — the Father creates by His Word.)
4. **The Word became flesh** and was beheld as the Only-Begotten from the Father — i.e., the Word is identified with Jesus the Son in His incarnation.
5. **No one has ever seen the Father** — confirmed by John 1:18. The Father remains the One Unseen God.
6. **The Son declares/exegetes the Father.** He is the Revealer.

### What remains *indeterminate* from the strict text:

- **Whether the Word was a personal pre-existent Being** *as the Son* before becoming flesh, **or whether the Word was the Father's own creative utterance/plan/expression** that took personal form *as the Son* in the incarnation.

The Greek text — strictly read — does not force the choice. Both readings are consistent with:
- Isa 44:24 (the Father creates alone — by His Word, which is His own).
- The Shema (one God — the Father).
- John 17:3 (the Father is the only true God).
- Acts 2:36, 13:33, Rom 1:4 (the Son was *made* / *declared* / *begotten* as Lord, Christ, and Son in power at His resurrection).

---

## **A Significant Implication**

Both Readings A and B *preserve* our foundation:
- The Father remains the One God.
- The Word/Son comes forth from the Father.
- The Father creates by His Word (whether personally pre-existent or as His expressed creative act).
- The Word *is* the Son in His incarnation — manifested as Jesus.

What we should **not** do is import the post-Nicene formulation of "co-eternal co-equal Persons of one substance" into John 1. The text does not say that. It says the Word was **with** the Father and was **Θεός** qualitatively — divine in character — which is something less specific than later creedal language.

---

## **What This Means for Our Study**

We can honestly say:

1. **John 1 identifies the incarnate Jesus as "the Word made flesh"** — established by v. 14.
2. **The Word was present and active at the beginning** — through whom all things came to be.
3. **The Word was with God (the Father)** and was **qualitatively divine.**
4. **The Word and the Father are distinguished** — the Word is "with" God, not identical to "the God" with whom He was.

What we **cannot** firmly say without going beyond the text:

- That the Word was a fully distinct personal Being from eternity in the Nicene sense.
- That the Word is precisely "the Angel of the LORD" or "Wisdom" of Proverbs 8 (no NT citation binds these).
