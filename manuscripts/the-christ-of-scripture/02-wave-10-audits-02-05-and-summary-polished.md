## **AUDIT OF FOUNDATION #2: The Earliest Prophetic Posits of the Son**

### Claim A: Genesis 3:15 prophesies a single male Person, born of a woman.

**Direct evidence:**
- **Gen 3:15 LXX** — the masculine **αὐτός** referring to **σπέρμα** (neuter) is grammatically remarkable and points to a male person. ✓
- **Gal 4:4** — *Geneva: "God sent forth his Son made of a woman"* — uses the same conceptual framework. ✓
- **Isa 7:14 LXX** — *NETS: "the virgin shall be with child and bear a son"* — confirms a virgin-born male. ✓

**Honest assessment:** Galatians 4:4 does not explicitly cite Genesis 3:15. The connection is by *theme* (a son born of a woman), not by *citation*. However, the Genesis text *by itself* — with its grammatical anomaly — does posit a single male Person. The NT confirms the *fulfillment* of a virgin-born son, but does not explicitly say "this is Genesis 3:15 fulfilled."

**Status: PARTIALLY ESTABLISHED.** The text plainly says what I claimed about the masculine **αὐτός**. The identification with Jesus specifically is by *thematic fulfillment* rather than by explicit NT citation. This is honest to state.

---

### Claim B: Genesis 22 (Isaac's binding) is a typological posit of the Son.

**Direct evidence:**
- **Heb 11:17-19** — calls Isaac **τὸν μονογενῆ** (only-begotten) and speaks of him being received back *Geneva: "after a sort"* (**ἐν παραβολῇ**, *in a parable*) — explicitly typological. ✓
- **Matt 3:17** — the Father's voice: *"My beloved Son"* — uses the same **ὁ Υἱός μου ὁ ἀγαπητός** language as Gen 22:2 LXX. ✓

**Honest assessment:** Hebrews 11:19 explicitly says Abraham received Isaac *"in a parable/figure"* — establishing typology by direct apostolic statement. The verbal echo at Matt 3:17 is striking but is not framed as a citation of Gen 22. Still, Hebrews *alone* is sufficient to establish typology.

**Status: ESTABLISHED**, but with the precision that Hebrews establishes **typology**, not a direct prophecy. Gen 22 is a *figure*, not a *prediction*.

---

### Claim C: Genesis 49:10 prophesies the Ruler from Judah, expectation of the nations.

**Direct evidence:**
- **Gen 49:10 LXX** — the text plainly says what I quoted. ✓
- **Rev 5:5** — *Geneva: "that Lion which is of the tribe of Judah"* echoes the Judah imagery. ✓
- **Haggai 2:7** — *NETS: "the choice things of all the nations shall come"* — thematic parallel. ✓

**Honest assessment:** Revelation 5:5 does not explicitly cite Gen 49:10. The connection is thematic. **No NT text explicitly cites Gen 49:10.** I should not have claimed this as "established by two witnesses." The text itself is plainly messianic in tone, but the bind-to-Jesus is by inference, not citation.

**Status: NOT STRICTLY ESTABLISHED by NT citation.** The OT text stands on its own as a prophecy of a future ruler from Judah; the identification with Jesus is by inference and pattern.

---

### Claim D: Numbers 24:17 (Star out of Jacob) is a posit of the Son.

**Direct evidence:**
- **Num 24:17 LXX** — the text says what I quoted. ✓
- **Matt 2:2** — the Magi see "His star." ✓
- **Rev 22:16** — Jesus says, *Geneva: "I am the root and the generation of David, and the bright morning star."* ✓

**Honest assessment:** Neither Matthew 2:2 nor Revelation 22:16 explicitly cite Numbers 24:17. Matthew narrates an event involving a star; Revelation uses star imagery for Jesus. The connection to Numbers 24:17 is inference, not citation.

**Status: NOT STRICTLY ESTABLISHED by NT citation.** The OT text is plainly messianic-prophetic on its own; the identification with Jesus is by pattern.

## **AUDIT OF FOUNDATION #3: Psalm 2 and Psalm 109/110 LXX**

### Claim A: Psalm 2 speaks of the Father and His Anointed Christ, fulfilled in Jesus.

**Direct evidence:**
- **Acts 4:25-27** — explicitly cites Psalm 2:1-2 and applies it to *Geneva: "against thine holy Son Jesus, whom thou haddest anointed"* ✓
- **Acts 13:33** — explicitly cites Psalm 2:7 — *Geneva: "Thou art my Son: this day have I begotten thee"* — and applies it to the resurrection of Jesus. ✓
- **Heb 1:5** — explicitly cites Psalm 2:7 of the Son. ✓
- **Heb 5:5** — explicitly cites Psalm 2:7 of Christ's appointment as High Priest. ✓

**Status: SOLIDLY ESTABLISHED.** Multiple explicit NT citations of Psalm 2 applied directly to Jesus. **This is one of our strongest pillars.**

---

### Claim B: Psalm 109/110 LXX speaks of the Father and the Lord at His right hand, fulfilled in Jesus.

**Direct evidence:**
- **Matt 22:41-45** — Jesus Himself cites Psalm 110:1 and applies it to the Christ. ✓
- **Acts 2:34-36** — Peter explicitly cites Psalm 110:1 and applies it to Jesus. ✓
- **Heb 1:13** — cites Psalm 110:1 of the Son. ✓
- **Heb 5:6, 7:17, 7:21** — explicitly cite Psalm 110:4 of Christ's priesthood. ✓

**Status: SOLIDLY ESTABLISHED.** Multiple explicit NT citations.

---

### Claim C (the more delicate one): Psalm 109:3 LXX — *NETS: "From the womb, before Morning-star, I brought you forth"* — Brenton: *"I have begotten thee from the womb before the morning"* — establishes a pre-temporal begetting of the Son.

**Direct evidence:**
- The LXX text plainly reads what I quoted. ✓
- **NT explicit citation of Psalm 109:3 LXX:** I cannot recall any NT passage that explicitly cites this specific verse.

**Honest assessment:** The verse is *there* in the LXX. But I cannot point to an apostolic witness that explicitly cites and interprets this specific verse. My use of it as a parallel to Psalm 2:7 was honest as a *parallel*, but I should not have presented it as a fully established witness to the doctrine of pre-temporal begetting.

**Status: PRESENT IN THE LXX TEXT, but NOT EXPLICITLY HANDLED by any NT citation I can identify.** This is significant. The verse can be noted; it should not be leaned upon as doctrinally decisive.

## **AUDIT OF FOUNDATION #4: Proverbs 8 and Micah 5:2**

### Claim A: Proverbs 8's Wisdom is the Son.

**Direct evidence:**
- **1 Cor 1:24, 30** — **WITHDRAWN per the previous correction.** Paul is contrasting the Father's wisdom with the Greeks' wisdom; he is not identifying Christ ontologically with the Wisdom-figure of Proverbs 8.
- **Col 1:15-17** — speaks of Christ as image, firstborn, before all things, agent of creation. This *parallels* Proverbs 8 conceptually but does **not cite** Proverbs 8.

**Honest assessment:** **No NT text explicitly cites Proverbs 8 and applies it to Christ.** The parallels exist; the citation does not.

**Status: NOT ESTABLISHED by NT citation.** Proverbs 8 stands as a personified Wisdom passage in the OT. Whether it speaks of the Son is not directly answered by any NT witness. The connection has been made by interpreters (including patristic ones) but not by Scripture itself.

---

### Claim B: Micah 5:2 speaks of the Son with "goings-forth from of old."

**Direct evidence:**
- **Matt 2:5-6** — explicitly cites Micah 5:2 (the part about Bethlehem and the Ruler) and applies it to Jesus' birthplace. ✓
- **John 7:42** — alludes to the Bethlehem birth expectation. ✓

**Honest assessment:** The NT cites Micah 5:2 for the **birthplace** of the Christ — *"out of Bethlehem"* — but does **not** explicitly handle the second part of the verse: *NETS: "his goings forth are from of old, from days of yore"* *(long ago)* That second clause is in the OT text; the NT cites only the Bethlehem portion explicitly.

**Status:** The **Bethlehem prophecy** is solidly established by Matt 2:5-6. The **"goings-forth from of old"** clause is **in the LXX text** but is **not explicitly cited or interpreted** in the NT. We can note the text; we should not lean on it as definitively interpreting pre-existence apart from explicit NT handling.

## **AUDIT OF FOUNDATION #5: The Angel of the LORD**

### Claim: The Messenger of the LORD is the pre-incarnate Son.

**Already retracted per our previous correction.** I conceded that:
- 1 Cor 10:4 identifies the Rock as Christ but does not identify the Messenger as Christ.
- Jude 5 identifies Jesus/the Lord as the Saviour from Egypt but does not specifically identify the Messenger figure.

**Status: NOT ESTABLISHED.** Withdrawn.

What **is** established about the Messenger texts:
- He speaks as God.
- He is seen and identified as God.
- He is sent by the LORD and bears the LORD's Name (Ex 23:21).
- He is distinguished from the LORD who sends Him.

These observations stand on the text. Their *interpretation* — whether He is the Son, a unique self-disclosure of the LORD, or something else — is not delivered by any NT witness explicitly handling these specific OT passages.

## **SUMMARY OF THE AUDIT**

### Solidly established by explicit NT citation:

1. **The Father is the One God** (Shema; Isa 44-46; John 17:3; 1 Cor 8:6; etc.)
2. **Psalm 2** speaks of the Father's Anointed Son, fulfilled in Jesus (Acts 4:25-27; 13:33; Heb 1:5; 5:5)
3. **Psalm 110** speaks of the Father and the Lord at His right hand, fulfilled in Jesus (Matt 22:44; Acts 2:34-36; Heb 1:13; 5:6; 7:17, 21)
4. **Micah 5:2 (Bethlehem clause)** prophesies the Christ's birthplace, fulfilled in Jesus (Matt 2:5-6)
5. **Genesis 22** is typological of the Father offering His Son (Heb 11:17-19 — *Geneva: "after a sort"*, Greek ἐν παραβολῇ "in a parable")

### Plausible but NOT strictly established by explicit NT citation:

6. **Genesis 3:15** — the masculine **αὐτός** posits a male Person; the *fulfillment* in Jesus is thematic, not by direct citation.
7. **Genesis 49:10** — messianic in tone; no direct NT citation.
8. **Numbers 24:17** — messianic in tone; no direct NT citation.
9. **Psalm 109:3 LXX** (*NETS: "before Morning-star"*) — in the LXX text; no direct NT citation.
10. **Micah 5:2 (goings-forth clause)** — in the LXX text; the NT cites only the Bethlehem portion.
11. **Proverbs 8** — parallels Christ thematically; no direct NT citation identifying it.

### Withdrawn:

12. **The Angel of the LORD = the pre-incarnate Son** — withdrawn for lack of explicit NT identification.

## **The Refined Foundation Standing After Audit**

What we have securely, with strict witness-establishment, is this:

**1. There is one God: the Father.** (Multiple direct witnesses, both OT and from Jesus' own lips.)

**2. There is one Lord: Jesus Christ — the Father's Anointed Son.** This is established by:
- **Psalm 2** explicitly cited as fulfilled in Jesus (Acts 4, 13; Heb 1, 5).
- **Psalm 110** explicitly cited as fulfilled in Jesus (Matt 22; Acts 2; Heb 1, 5, 7).
- **Micah 5:2 (Bethlehem)** explicitly cited as fulfilled in Jesus (Matt 2).

**3. The Son was begotten / made / declared Son of God in power by His resurrection.** Explicitly:
- **Acts 13:33** — Paul applies Psalm 2:7 to the resurrection: *Geneva: "in that he raised up Jesus, even as it is written in the second Psalm, Thou art my Son: this day have I begotten thee."*
- **Rom 1:4** — *Geneva: "declared mightily to be the Son of God ... by the resurrection from the dead"*
- **Acts 2:36** — *Geneva: "God hath made him both Lord, and Christ, this Jesus, I say, whom ye have crucified"* — at His exaltation.

**4. The Son is at the Father's right hand, made both Lord and Christ by the Father, and is forever Priest by the Father's oath.** (Ps 110:1, 4; Acts 2:36; Heb 7:21)

**5. Genesis 22 is a typological figure of the Father offering His beloved Son.** (Heb 11:17-19)

### What we do NOT have firmly established by explicit witness:

- That the Son existed personally before His conception in Mary.
- That the Son was the agent of original creation (Gen 1).
- That the Son is the personified Wisdom of Proverbs 8.
- That the Son is the Angel/Messenger of the LORD.

These are claims that have been made for centuries — by patristic writers, by creeds, by tradition — but the **strict two-witness establishment from explicit Scripture is not as firm as I had presented**. They are *possible*. They may even be probable. But they are not established with the certainty I had implied.
