## **JOHN 1:4-5**

> In it was life, and that life was the light of men. And that light shineth in the darkness, and the darkness comprehended it not. — **John 1:4-5, 1599 Geneva**
---

### Observations

**(1) The Geneva again translates "it"** — *"In it was life"; "comprehended it not"* — treating **ὁ Λόγος** as not-yet-personal at this point in John's argument. This is honest to the text.

**(2)** **ἐν αὐτῷ ζωὴ ἦν** — *"In it/Him was life."* Life is *in* the Word. This parallels Gen 1 thematically — God's creative speech brings life.

**(3)** **ἡ ζωὴ ἦν τὸ φῶς τῶν ἀνθρώπων** — *"the life was the light of men."* The same connection appears in Gen 1:3-4 — God said *"Let there be light,"* and the light was good. The Word/Life/Light pattern echoes the creation account.

**(4)** **τὸ φῶς ἐν τῇ σκοτίᾳ φαίνει** — present tense — *"the light shines [is shining] in the darkness."* This is John's transition from creation-context to the present action of the Word/Light among men.

## **JOHN 1:6-8**

> There was a man sent from God, whose name was John. This same came for a witness, to bear witness of that light, that all men through him might believe. He was not that light, but was sent to bear witness of that light. — **John 1:6-8, 1599 Geneva**
---

### Observations

**(1)** John the Baptist is introduced — **ἀπεσταλμένος παρὰ Θεοῦ** — *"sent from God."* Same construction (**ἀπεσταλμένος παρά**) will be used of the Word later. John, a mere man, is "sent from God" — so this construction by itself does not establish pre-existence; it establishes *commissioning*.

**(2)** John was *not* the Light; he came to **bear witness** of the Light. This sets up a contrast: the Light is *another* than John.

## **JOHN 1:9-11**

> This was that true light, which lighteth every man that cometh into the world. He was in the world, and the world was made by him: and the world knew him not. He came unto his own, and his own received him not. — **John 1:9-11, 1599 Geneva**
---

### Observations

**(1) Important shift in the Geneva translation:** Up to v. 8, the Geneva used "it" for the Word. **At v. 10, the Geneva shifts to "him."** Why? Because the Light/Word is now described as coming **into the world** and **into His own** — and being **received** or **not received** — which are personal actions toward a personal subject. The Geneva translators are honest: they shifted to personal pronouns at the point where the Word's actions become personal.

**(2)** **ὁ κόσμος δι᾽ αὐτοῦ ἐγένετο** — *"the world was made through Him."* Repetition of v. 3's claim, now with personal pronoun.

**(3)** **εἰς τὰ ἴδια ἦλθεν** — *"He came unto His own."* **τὰ ἴδια** — *"His own things/people."* The Word has *His own* — implying ownership, a prior relation to the world He made.

**(4)** **οἱ ἴδιοι αὐτὸν οὐ παρέλαβον** — *"His own [people] did not receive Him."* This refers to Israel — His covenant people. The Light came to Israel and was rejected.

---

### Honest assessment of vv. 9-11:

These verses speak of the Word/Light **coming into the world** and **being rejected by His own**. The language is increasingly personal. *If* one reads this as the historical Jesus coming to Israel and being rejected, the actions are clearly personal. *If* one reads "He came into the world" as referring to a pre-incarnate coming, then a personal pre-existent Word is in view.

The text moves seamlessly from creation-context (v. 10b: *"the world was made through Him"*) to incarnation-context (v. 11: *"His own did not receive Him"*) — suggesting John sees these as actions of the *same* Subject. This is **a stronger push** toward personal pre-existence than vv. 1-5 alone provide.

## **JOHN 1:12-13**

> But as many as received him, to them he gave prerogative to be the sons of God, even to them that believe in his Name. Which are born not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God. — **John 1:12-13, 1599 Geneva**
---

### Observations

**(1)** Those who **receive** the Word and **believe in His Name** are given the right to **become children of God**. This is a transition from being a creature to being a child — by faith in the Word.

**(2)** Their being born "of God" parallels — and is contrasted with — the Word's own relation to the Father. The believer is **born of God**; the Word *was with* God from the beginning. The relationships are not the same kind.

**(3)** The phrase **πιστεύουσιν εἰς τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ** — *"believing in His Name"* — is highly significant. Recall Ex 23:21: *"My Name is in Him."* Faith in the Name of the Sent One is faith in the Father whose Name He bears.

## **JOHN 1:14-18 — Now Read in Full**

> And that Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we saw the glory thereof, as the glory of the only begotten Son of the Father) full of grace and truth. John bare witness of him, and cried, saying, This was he of whom I said, He that cometh after me, was before me: for he was better than I. And of his fulness have all we received, and grace for grace. For the Law was given by Moses, but grace and truth came by Jesus Christ. No man hath seen God at any time: that only begotten Son which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him. — **John 1:14-18, 1599 Geneva**
---

### Verse 15 — John the Baptist's Testimony

**ὁ ὀπίσω μου ἐρχόμενος ἔμπροσθέν μου γέγονεν, ὅτι πρῶτός μου ἦν**

*"He that comes after me has come to be before me, because He was first/prior to me."*

This is a significant statement. John the Baptist was **born before** Jesus (six months earlier, per Luke 1:36). Yet John says Jesus **was prior** to him (**πρῶτός μου ἦν**). The natural reading: John is testifying to a *priority of being* of the One coming after him in time — i.e., a pre-existence of some kind.

**However**, the word **πρῶτος** can also mean *"of higher rank"* or *"chief"* — and this is its meaning in some contexts. The Geneva captures this by translating: *"for he was better than I."* This rendering preserves the ambiguity — John may be testifying to *priority of being* or to *priority of rank*.

**Honest assessment:** v. 15 admits both readings.

---

### Verse 16-17

**ἐκ τοῦ πληρώματος αὐτοῦ ἡμεῖς πάντες ἐλάβομεν** — *"of His fullness we all received."* The Word/Son has fullness; we receive from it. This is consistent with Acts 2:33: Jesus, exalted, *received* the promise of the Spirit from the Father and *poured it out* on the disciples. The fullness comes from the Father through the Son to us.

**ὁ νόμος διὰ Μωϋσέως ἐδόθη, ἡ χάρις καὶ ἡ ἀλήθεια διὰ Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ ἐγένετο** — *"the Law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ."* Now Jesus is named explicitly. The Word-become-flesh is **Jesus Christ**. John has fully identified His subject.

### **WITNESS A: Philippians 2:5-11**

> Let the same mind be in you that was even in Christ Jesus, Who being in the form of God, thought it no robbery to be equal with God: But he made himself of no reputation, and took on him the form of a servant, and was made like unto men, and was found in shape as a man. He humbled himself, and became obedient unto the death, even the death of the Cross. Wherefore God hath also highly exalted him, and given him a Name above every name, That at the Name of Jesus should every knee bow, both of things in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth, And that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is the Lord, unto the glory of God the Father. — **Philippians 2:5-11, 1599 Geneva**
---

### Observations

**(1)** **ἐν μορφῇ Θεοῦ ὑπάρχων** — *"existing in the form of God."* **μορφή** = form, outward expression of essential nature. **ὑπάρχων** = continuously existing, subsisting.

This is a significant witness. Paul says Christ **was existing in the form of God** *before* taking the form of a servant.

**(2)** **οὐχ ἁρπαγμὸν ἡγήσατο τὸ εἶναι ἴσα Θεῷ** — *"did not regard being equal with God a thing to be grasped/seized."* The phrase is contested in translation. Reading carefully:
- **ἁρπαγμός** = a thing seized, plunder, something to be grasped or held onto.
- **τὸ εἶναι ἴσα Θεῷ** = *"the being equal with God."*

Two main readings:
- **Reading 1:** He already was equal with God but did not consider it something to cling to (selfless renunciation).
- **Reading 2:** He did not consider equality with God something to be seized/grasped at (unlike Adam, who grasped at it in Eden).

The Geneva's *"thought it no robbery to be equal with God"* leans toward reading 1, but the Greek is genuinely contested.

**(3)** **ἑαυτὸν ἐκένωσεν** — *"He emptied Himself."* This implies a prior state from which He emptied.

**(4)** **μορφὴν δούλου λαβών, ἐν ὁμοιώματι ἀνθρώπων γενόμενος** — *"taking the form of a servant, becoming in the likeness of men."* The *taking* and *becoming* imply a transition from a prior state.

**(5)** **διὸ καὶ ὁ Θεὸς αὐτὸν ὑπερύψωσεν** — *"Therefore God has highly exalted Him."* The Father (**ὁ Θεός**) is the One who exalts. The Son is the One exalted.

**(6)** **ἐχαρίσατο αὐτῷ τὸ ὄνομα** — *"He [the Father] graciously gave Him the Name."* The Name above every name is **given** to the Son by the Father — not innately possessed.

**(7)** **εἰς δόξαν Θεοῦ Πατρός** — *"to the glory of God the Father."* Every confession of Jesus as Lord is **to the Father's glory.** The Father remains the ultimate object.

---

### What Phil 2:5-11 establishes (honestly):

- Christ existed **in the form of God** before His incarnation.
- He **emptied Himself** and **took** the form of a servant — a real transition.
- He was **exalted by the Father** after His obedience to death.
- The **Name** was **given** to Him by the Father.
- Every confession of His Lordship is **to the Father's glory** — the Father remains supreme.

This is the **strongest single NT witness to a pre-incarnate state of the Son** in some form of divine glory. The Father remains supreme; the Son acts in dependence and is exalted by the Father.

### **WITNESS B: Colossians 1:13-20**

> Who hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of his dear Son, In whom we have redemption through his blood, that is, the forgiveness of sins, Who is the image of the invisible God, the first begotten of every creature. For by him were all things created, which are in heaven, and which are in earth, things visible and invisible: whether they be Thrones or Dominions, or Principalities, or Powers, all things were created by him, and for him, And he is before all things, and in him all things consist. And he is the head of the body of the Church: he is the beginning, and the first begotten of the dead, that in all things he might have the preeminence. For it pleased the Father, that in him should all fulness dwell. And through peace made by that blood of that his cross, to reconcile to himself through him, through him, I say, all things, both which are in earth, and which are in heaven. — **Colossians 1:13-20, 1599 Geneva**
---

### Observations

**(1)** **εἰκὼν τοῦ Θεοῦ τοῦ ἀοράτου** — *"the image of the invisible God."* The Son is the **Image** of the unseen Father. (Connects with John 1:18 — *"No one has seen God; the Only-Begotten Son has declared Him."*)

**(2)** **πρωτότοκος πάσης κτίσεως** — *"firstborn of all creation."* This phrase is contested:
- **Reading 1:** "Firstborn [over] all creation" — preeminent over creation.
- **Reading 2:** "Firstborn [of/within] all creation" — first in the order of created beings.

The Greek genitive **πάσης κτίσεως** admits both. Verse 16 immediately clarifies: **ὅτι ἐν αὐτῷ ἐκτίσθη τὰ πάντα** — *"because in Him were all things created."* This **ὅτι** (because) gives the reason for the firstborn-title: He is firstborn *because* all things were created in/through/for Him.

If He is the one through/for whom all things were created, He cannot be a created thing within all things — so Reading 1 is favored by context.

**(3)** **ἐν αὐτῷ ἐκτίσθη τὰ πάντα... τὰ πάντα δι᾽ αὐτοῦ καὶ εἰς αὐτὸν ἔκτισται** — *"In Him all things were created... all things have been created through Him and unto Him."*

Three prepositions, three relations to creation:
- **ἐν αὐτῷ** — *in Him* (the sphere)
- **δι᾽ αὐτοῦ** — *through Him* (the agency)
- **εἰς αὐτόν** — *unto Him* (the goal)

**(4)** **καὶ αὐτός ἐστιν πρὸ πάντων** — *"and He is before all things."* The Son is **prior to all things.**

**(5)** **καὶ τὰ πάντα ἐν αὐτῷ συνέστηκεν** — *"and in Him all things hold together."* The Son sustains creation.

**(6)** **ὅτι ἐν αὐτῷ εὐδόκησεν πᾶν τὸ πλήρωμα κατοικῆσαι** — *"because in Him it pleased [the Father] that all fullness should dwell."* The fullness dwelling in the Son is **the Father's pleasure** — granted by the Father.

---

### What Col 1:13-20 establishes:

- The Son is the **Image** of the invisible Father.
- The Son is **firstborn over all creation** (preeminent).
- **All things were created in, through, and for Him.**
- He is **before all things**.
- **All things hold together in Him.**
- **The Father's fullness dwells in Him** by the Father's pleasure.

This passage, taken at face value, presents the Son as the agent of original creation — which seems to stand in tension with Isaiah 44:24's *"I stretched out the heavens **alone**."*

### Reconciliation honestly considered:

The reconciliation must lie in this: **the Son acts as the Father's own agent**, so that what the Father does *through* the Son is still the Father's own act, done *alone* in the sense that no other Deity participates. The Son is not "another God"; He is the Father's own Image, through whom the Father works. The Father's **Word** (Ps 33:6) creates — yet the Word is the Father's own, not a rival.

This reading honors both Isa 44:24 and Col 1:16. The Father stretches out the heavens alone — *through His own Image, His own Firstborn, His own Word.*

### **WITNESS C: Hebrews 1:1-3**

> At sundry times and in divers manners God spake in the old time to our fathers by the Prophets: in these last days he hath spoken unto us by his Son, Whom he hath made heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds, Who being the brightness of the glory, and the engraved form of his person, and bearing up all things by his mighty word, hath by himself purged our sins, and sitteth at the right hand of the Majesty in the highest places, — **Hebrews 1:1-3, 1599 Geneva**
---

### Observations

**(1)** **ὁ Θεὸς... ἐλάλησεν ἡμῖν ἐν Υἱῷ** — *"God has spoken to us in [a/the] Son."* The Father is the speaker; the Son is the medium/locus of His final speech.

**(2)** **ὃν ἔθηκεν κληρονόμον πάντων** — *"whom He appointed heir of all things."* The Son is **appointed** by the Father as heir. This is given language — the Son receives by the Father's appointment.

**(3)** **δι᾽ οὗ καὶ ἐποίησεν τοὺς αἰῶνας** — *"through whom He also made the ages/worlds."* The Father made the ages **through** the Son. Same construction as 1 Cor 8:6, Col 1:16, John 1:3.

**(4)** **ἀπαύγασμα τῆς δόξης** — *"radiance of [the Father's] glory."* The Son is the *radiance* — what proceeds from and reveals the Father's glory. Light from light, so to speak.

**(5)** **χαρακτὴρ τῆς ὑποστάσεως αὐτοῦ** — *"the exact stamp/impression of His being."* **χαρακτήρ** = the impression made by a die or seal. The Son bears the exact impression of the Father's reality.

**(6)** **φέρων τε τὰ πάντα τῷ ῥήματι τῆς δυνάμεως αὐτοῦ** — *"upholding all things by the word of His power."* The Son upholds; the word is His own / the Father's.

**(7)** **ἐκάθισεν ἐν δεξιᾷ τῆς μεγαλωσύνης** — *"He sat down at the right hand of the Majesty."* The same Psalm 110 enthronement.

---

### What Heb 1:1-3 establishes:

- The Father speaks **through** the Son in these last days.
- The Father made the ages **through** the Son.
- The Son **radiates** the Father's glory and bears the **exact impression** of the Father's being.
- The Son **upholds all things** by His powerful word.
- The Son **sat down** at the Father's right hand after purifying sins.

This is consistent with — and reinforces — the pattern: the Father is the Source; the Son is the Father's Image, Word, Agent, and exalted Heir.

### Observation

### **WITNESS F: 1 Corinthians 8:6**
