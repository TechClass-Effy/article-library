## **A Question Now Properly Framed**
With this foundation firm, we may now ask the right question — the question that drives the
rest of our study:
> **Who, then, is the Son of this One God? In what sense is He called "the Word," "the Image,"
"the Firstborn," "the Lord through whom are all things" — without violating the absolute
singularity of the Father?**
Shall we now turn to that question, beginning with the earliest prophetic posits of the Son in
the Old Testament (e.g., Genesis 3:15, Psalm 2, Proverbs 8, Psalm 110)?
Indeed we shall!
Excellent. We now turn to the Old Testament's earliest posits of the Son — the Father's
**μονογενής** (only-begotten) — built strictly upon the foundation we have laid: **the Father is
the One God, and there is none beside Him.**
We will proceed chronologically as the prophecies are given in the canonical order, beginning
at the earliest.

## **POSIT #1 — Genesis 3:15: The Seed of the Woman**
### **LXX:**
*"καὶ ἔχθραν θήσω ἀνὰ μέσον σοῦ καὶ ἀνὰ μέσον τῆς γυναικὸς καὶ ἀνὰ μέσον τοῦ σπέρματός
σου καὶ ἀνὰ μέσον τοῦ σπέρματος αὐτῆς· αὐτός σου τηρήσει κεφαλήν, καὶ σὺ τηρήσεις αὐτοῦ
πτέρναν."*
### **1599 Geneva Bible:**
*"I will also put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed. He
shall break thine head, and thou shalt bruise his heel."*
---
### Observations
**(1) The pronoun αὐτός is masculine singular** — "**He**, He Himself" — not *it*, not *they*.
The LXX is grammatically remarkable here: the antecedent **σπέρμα** (*seed*) is **neuter**,
which would normally demand **αὐτό**. Yet the translators of the LXX (3rd-2nd century BC,
*centuries before any Christian influence*) deliberately rendered **αὐτός** — masculine —
pointing to a singular **male person** as the Seed.
This is the first prophetic posit of the coming **One** — a single human Person, born of a
woman, who will crush the head of the serpent.
**(2) "The seed of the woman"** — not of the man. In the entire patriarchal framework of
Scripture, seed is reckoned through the father. This phrase is anomalous — it anticipates a
birth not according to the line of a human father. The two witnesses for this anomaly being
fulfilled:
**Isaiah 7:14 (LXX):**
*"διὰ τοῦτο δώσει Κύριος αὐτὸς ὑμῖν σημεῖον· ἰδοὺ ἡ παρθένος ἐν γαστρὶ ἕξει καὶ τέξεται υἱόν,
καὶ καλέσεις τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ Ἐμμανουήλ·"*
**1599 Geneva:**
*"Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign. Behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a
son, and she shall call his name Immanuel."*
The LXX explicitly says **ἡ παρθένος** — *the virgin* — not merely *a young woman*. This is
**Witness #1** that the "seed of the woman" is a virgin-born Son.
**Galatians 4:4 (Greek):**
*"ὅτε δὲ ἦλθεν τὸ πλήρωμα τοῦ χρόνου, ἐξαπέστειλεν ὁ Θεὸς τὸν Υἱὸν αὐτοῦ, γενόμενον ἐκ
γυναικός, γενόμενον ὑπὸ νόμον,"*

---
### What Genesis 3:15 establishes about the Son:
- He is a **single, masculine Person** (αὐτός).
- He is **born of a woman** without a human father in view.
- He is **sent by the One God** as the conqueror of the serpent.
- He is, at this earliest point, a **promised man** — the prophecy says nothing yet of pre-
existence or divine status. We are at the *beginning* of the revelation; we will not retroject later
disclosures into this verse.
