# **PASSAGE E — ISAIAH 9:6 LXX**

This is one of the most theologically weighted OT passages, and it is critical to compare the LXX and Hebrew readings — per our maxim to follow the LXX that Jesus and the Apostles used.

## **The Hebrew Reading (Reflected in Geneva)**

### **1599 Geneva (Isa 9:6):**
*"For unto us a child is born, and unto us a Son is given: and the government is upon his shoulder, and he shall call his name Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The prince of peace,"*


The Hebrew reading (as translated in the Geneva) contains four (or five) titles:
- Wonderful, Counsellor
- The mighty God (**אֵל גִּבּוֹר** — El Gibbor)
- The everlasting Father (**אֲבִיעַד** — Abi-Ad)
- The prince of peace

## **The LXX Reading**

### **Isaiah 9:6 (LXX 9:5 — the LXX numbering differs), NETS:**

> because a child was born for us, a son also given to us, whose sovereignty was upon his shoulder, and he is named Messenger of Great Counsel, for I will bring peace upon the rulers, peace and health to him. — **Isaiah 9:6 (LXX 9:5), NETS**

### **Literal translation of the LXX:**
*"For a child is born to us, and a son is given to us, whose government is upon His shoulder, and His name is called Messenger of Great Counsel; for I will bring peace upon the rulers, peace and health to him."*

---

## **Observation of Decisive Importance for Our Maxims**

**The LXX reading is remarkably different from the Hebrew.**

**The LXX simply calls the Child: "Messenger of Great Counsel" (Μεγάλης βουλῆς ἄγγελος).**

**The LXX does NOT include:**
- "Mighty God"
- "Everlasting Father"

**These titles are present in the Hebrew but absent from the LXX** that Jesus and the Apostles used.

---

## **Per Our Maxims**

**Per maxim #1 (let God be true):** We follow the God-breathed Word — and per our commitment throughout this study, we prioritize the LXX that Jesus and the Apostles used.

**Per maxim #3 (ignoring accretions):** The Hebrew reading with "Mighty God, Everlasting Father" — as translated in the Geneva — is what the Masoretic tradition preserved. But the LXX (translated by Jewish scholars centuries before Christ) reads differently.

**We should not build major Christological doctrines on the Hebrew reading of Isa 9:6 alone** — because the LXX (which Jesus and His Apostles used) does not confirm these specific titles for the Child.

---

## **What the LXX Does Confirm**

The LXX still presents this Child as:
- **The Messenger of Great Counsel** — the One bearing the Father's counsel and authority.
- **The One whose government is on His shoulder** — bearing supreme rule.
- **The One through whom peace comes** — the Prince of Peace, effectively.

**This is coherent with everything we have established:**
- The Son is the Father's Messenger — bearing the Father's Name and authority (John 5:43; Ex 23:21).
- The Son bears the government / kingdom given to Him by the Father (Dan 7:13-14; Matt 28:18).
- The Son is the Prince of Peace — through whom the Father brings peace.

**No metaphysical claim of the Son being "Mighty God, Everlasting Father" is required from the LXX text.**

---

## **A Note on the Hebrew "Everlasting Father" (אֲבִיעַד)**

Even in the Hebrew tradition, the title **"Everlasting Father"** applied to the Son has been a source of theological difficulty for post-Nicene tradition. If the Son is called "Father," how does He remain distinct from the Father?

**Answer:** The title **"Father"** in the Hebrew idiom can mean *"originator, founder, source"* — as in *"father of many nations"* (Abraham), *"father of the fatherless"* (God), etc. It does not necessarily mean identical Person with **the** Father.

**"Everlasting Father"** in Isa 9:6 (Hebrew) may mean **"Father of the age to come"** — the One who founds and sustains the everlasting age of the kingdom. This does not collapse Him into the Person of God the Father.

**But since our maxim prioritizes the LXX, we need not resolve this here.** The LXX reading (Μεγάλης βουλῆς ἄγγελος) does not contain this title, and per our discipline, we let the LXX guide us.

**Under our maxims, Isa 9:6 does not present a metaphysical challenge to our framework** — because the LXX reading is consistent with the Name-authority framework you have proposed.
